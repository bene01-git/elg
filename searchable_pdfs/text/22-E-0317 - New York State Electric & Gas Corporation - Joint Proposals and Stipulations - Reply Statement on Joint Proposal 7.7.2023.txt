ARGUMENT 
 
POINT I 
 
“REDUCTIONS” FROM UTILITY-PROPOSED REVENUE 
REQUIREMENTS ARE NOT IMPRIMATUR OF BEING IN 
THE PUBLIC INTEREST 
 
 
 
The electric and gas delivery rate increases advanced in the Joint Proposal are 
enormous, and, if adopted by the Commission, the resulting customer rate impacts would be 
nothing short of staggering.  (See MI at 15-18.)  In support of this outcome, Staff argues that the 
proposed electric and gas revenue requirements “represent a significant reduction in customer 
costs compared to what the Companies originally requested for each of the four businesses.”  
(Staff at 2.)  Staff further argues that the proposed revenue requirements compare favorably to 
the Companies’ litigation positions (see id. at 13), and then proceeds to tout the revenue 
requirement “reductions” that were realized when compared to the levels proposed in the 
Companies’ August 2022 Update to their rate case filings (id. at 16-19).  For the reasons set forth 
below, “reductions” from utility-proposed revenue requirements are not imprimatur of outcomes 
being in the public interest.  Additionally, the Commission needs to recognize that while 
settlement negotiations in utility rate proceedings should be explored wherever feasible, 
proposed settlements (in the form of Joint Proposals) are not intrinsically “better” than litigated 
outcomes and in some cases may be worse. 
 
In its June 27th submission, Staff touts that: (a) the NYSEG electric revenue 
requirement contained in the Joint Proposal is 26.8 percent less than the level proposed in the

Proposal is 68.8 percent less than the level proposed in the utility’s August 2022 Update; (c) the 
RG&E electric revenue requirement contained in the Joint Proposal is 24.8 percent less than the 
level proposed in the utility’s August 2022 Update; and (d) the RG&E gas revenue requirement 
contained in the Joint Proposal is 34.8 percent less than the level proposed in the utility’s August

in the public interest. 
 
Initially, every rate proceeding is different.  So, too, are the strengths and 
weaknesses of various utilities’ electric and gas rate filings.  Multiple Intervenors believes that it 
is not uncommon for utilities to file “aggressive” requests for rate relief in anticipation of 
experiencing “reductions” during the rate-setting process.  Even where that might not have 
occurred, circumstances may warrant outcomes very different than what utilities sought (e.g., 
because rate impact concerns compel a different weighting of priorities by the Commission than 
that favored by utilities or, for that matter, Staff).  It should be emphasized that even after Staff’s 
calculated “reductions” to the rate relief requested in the Companies’ August 2022 Update, 
customers are confronted with three consecutive years of annual, net base delivery rate increases 
of 17.1 percent for NYSEG Electric, 5.6% for NYSEG Gas, 11.0 percent for RG&E Electric, and 
10.2 percent for RG&E Gas.  (JP, App. 1 at 1.)  The fact that the Companies requested even 
greater rate relief in RY 1 this past August is of no consequence or relief to customers that could 
be forced to bear the delivery rate increases proposed in the Joint Proposal. 
 
Multiple Intervenors is concerned that the Commission and Staff have become too 
enamored with multi-year settlements of utility rate proceedings.  Multiple Intervenors 
recognizes that such settlements can – and often are – in the public interest.  Significantly,
