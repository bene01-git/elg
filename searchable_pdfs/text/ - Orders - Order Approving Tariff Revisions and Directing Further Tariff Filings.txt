BACKGROUND 
Interruptible Service 
 
 
By offering IT and TC service, the Companies can 
provide non-firm customers with delivery service during the vast 
majority of the year, while also ensuring the reliability of the 
distribution networks when demand for natural gas is highest, 
generally during cold weather when firm customers use natural 
gas for heating.  Additionally, the Companies design, construct 
and maintain their distribution networks to serve firm customers 
when the distribution system experiences peak demand.  Thus, the 
distribution networks do not include the additional 
infrastructure that would be required to serve TC and IT 
customers when the system experiences peak demand.  This 
minimizes the cost of the system that must be recovered from 
firm customers.

IT customers receive gas service unless peaking assets 
are deployed, since IT customers’ rates do not include the costs 
of those assets.  When peaking assets must be called upon to 
maintain system reliability, IT customers must switch to their 
alternative fuel.  TC customers receive gas service so long as 
the actual outside temperature remains at or above a designated 
switchover temperature, typically 15 degrees Fahrenheit, and are 
then required to switch to their alternative fuels. 
 
 
In order to ensure the reliability of the distribution 
networks, National Grid must have confidence that IT and TC 
customers will not use natural gas under certain conditions.  To 
that end, the Companies’ tariffs include provisions requiring 
that IT and TC customers have access to an alternative fuel and 
penalizes those customers for the unauthorized use of natural 
gas. 
 
 
IT and TC customers must have access to a minimum of 
ten days’ supply of an alternative fuel source, typically #2 oil 
(i.e., ultra-low sulfur diesel fuel).1  In an effort to confirm 
that customers actually maintain sufficient access to fuel and 
have a ten day supply, the Commission requires that IT and TC 
customers provide affidavits attesting to compliance with this 
requirement, which include the contact information for the 
customers’ oil suppliers.2  Due to the sheer number of IT and TC 
customers in National Grid’s service territories (approximately

Prospective Structure of IT and TC Services 
 
 
The Collaborative Parties recommend that the Companies 
create a new non-firm service class for each service territory 
that would blend the existing TC and IT services and implement 
two pricing tiers determined by a customer’s switching 
equipment.  Under Tier One, customers would utilize automatic 
switching equipment and receive service so long as the outside 
temperature remained at or above 15 degrees Fahrenheit.  Under 
Tier Two, customers would utilize manual switching equipment and 
receive service so long as the outside temperature remained at 
or above 20 degrees Fahrenheit.  Tier Two would pay a lower rate 
than Tier One, reflective of the higher frequency of 
interruption the customers would be subject to. 
 
 
The Report explains that the benefits of the proposal 
include both service flexibility and assured levels of service 
that will eliminate the situation where a customer receiving a 
lower level of service pays more for service than a customer 
receiving a higher level of service.  The proposed levels of 
service will also permit the Companies to utilize the new non-
firm service class as a demand response tool because the 
Companies will know that interruptions will occur with certainty 
when the temperature drops below certain levels.  The Report 
stated that the Collaborative Parties recognize the Companies 
have a number of critical details to work out concerning the 
revised IT/TC class, including the rate design, and thus 
proposed that the details be worked out in the next base rate 
cases for the Companies.8

TECHNICAL CONFERENCE ON INTERRUPTILE SERVICE 
 
 
On November 27, 2018, Staff convened a technical 
conference with Consolidated Edison Company of New York, Inc. 
(Con Edison), National Grid and other stakeholders to discuss 
issues related to interruptible service and gather input on the 
best approach to resolve issues related to both Con Edison’s and 
National Grid’s interruptible gas service programs.  The notable 
topics of discussion at the technical conference were: (1) what 
consequence should be imposed if a customer fails to return an 
affidavit in time; (2) communication between gas distribution 
companies and interruptible gas service customers; (3) best 
approaches to verify whether customers have adequate physical 
alternate fuel storage and replenishment contracts; (4) 
alternative enforcement actions against customers violating 
tariff requirements, such as installing tank monitoring devices 
and remote shut-off valves; (5) challenges associated with 
switching interruptible gas service customers to firm service 
because of capacity limitations; and (6) uniformity among 
downstate natural gas utility tariffs.  In addition, 
stakeholders identified the importance of IT customers in the 
operation of the gas distribution system, and also noted that a 
number of critical customers, e.g., hospitals or schools, take 
service under the interruptible gas program.

DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION 
 
 
Providing customers with IT and TC service allows the 
Companies to reduce gas demand during periods of peak usage by 
offering a reduced delivery rate to those customers who agree to 
interrupt gas service when demand peaks.  This type of demand 
response service benefits all customers by using the gas 
distribution system more efficiently, as IT and TC customers 
utilize the distribution network when the system is free of 
capacity constraints but, during times of peak demand, remove 
their usage from the system.  IT and TC services allow the 
Companies to spread the costs of operating their systems over a 
larger customer base without the need for system enhancements to 
meet a higher peak demand. 
 
 
Prior to the issuance of the Rate Order, many National 
Grid IT and TC customers were migrating to firm service.  This 
trend continues.  IT and TC customers primarily rely on fuel oil 
when their use of natural gas is interrupted.  IT and TC 
customers can face difficulty when seeking to replenish their 
fuel oil during the winter.  Moreover, while the Rate Order 
attempted to correct the price inequity that caused many IT and 
TC customers to request firm service, the Rate Order established

Unauthorized Use of Gas Penalty Tariff Modification 
 
 
Last Year, National Grid informed Staff that there 
were two separate but consecutive interruption events between 
December 28, 2017 and January 8, 2018, that interrupted TC 
customers for eleven and a half days, or a day and a half longer 
than the ten days of back-up fuel supply required by KEDNY’s 
tariff.  89 TC customers incurred penalties during the second 
interruption event because of the length of the consecutive 
interruption periods, the limited supply of oil during the 
interruption period, and the need to continue to heat premises 
such as public buildings, including schools and multi-family 
dwellings. 
 
 
The Commission granted a waiver of the tariff and 
provided that the penalty should be the lesser of the two 
options set forth in the tariff, rather than the greater option.  
Specifically, in that case, the customers were required to pay 
penalty charges of nine times the applicable sales rate.  In 
that case, the Commission noted that the affected customers’ 
unauthorized use of gas reflected the customers’ difficulty 
securing alternative fuels despite adequate planning.12  
Moreover, the limited firm gas transportation capacity downstate 
had a direct impact on the price of natural gas during the 
period of very cold weather.  For example, on January 5, 2018 
the Transco Zone 6 NY price exceeded $140 per dekatherm, whereas 
during more mild temperatures in January 2018 the price of gas 
was about $3 per dekatherm.  Due to the spike in natural gas

Prospective Structure of IT and TC Services 
 
 
The Report recommended creating a new non-firm service

related to the actual revenues generated during the rate plans.14

to end the existing moratorium on new TC customers and 
grandfathering the existing TC customers until the conclusion of 
the Companies’ next base rate proceedings.

potential additional infrastructure that might be necessary if 
those customers required firm service.  As NYC commented,
