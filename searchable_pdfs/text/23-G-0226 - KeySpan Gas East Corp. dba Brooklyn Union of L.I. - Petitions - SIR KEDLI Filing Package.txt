19

Site Background

commercial and residential area, adjacent to the Montauk branch of the LIRR. The RI was completed in

OU2-offsite groundwater plume which originated from OU1, OU3-Brightwaters Yard which was used for

Site Investigation and Remediation Progress January 2019 through December 2022
OM&M for the Bay Shore site currently includes an ozone injection groundwater treatment system in 
OU1; DNAPL monitoring and recovery in OU1; supplemental oxygen injection groundwater treatment 
systems in OU1, OU2 and OU3; and a sitewide groundwater monitoring program. Monthly and/or 
quarterly OM&M activities are ongoing for operation of these systems. As groundwater impacts are 
remediated, as demonstrated by the groundwater monitoring data, oxygen injection systems are shut 
down upon approval from the NYSDEC. The systems are abandoned when additional groundwater 
monitoring data demonstrates no remaining impacts and upon approval from the NYSDEC.

accordance with the interim SMP in OU3 in 2019, and in OU1 in 2021.  Two OU2 oxygen injection

and the system was shut down, with NYSDEC approval, in August 2021. The SVE system associated with 
the OU1 ozone system was discontinued in September 2019. Post SVE system shutdown, monthly 
sampling of soil vapor points on OU1 South were conducted beginning in October 2019.
The emerging contaminants groundwater sampling results summary report was submitted to the NYSDEC 
in February 2019.  A request for the reduction in SVI sampling at St. Patrick’s school was submitted to 
the agencies in January 2019 and approved in February 2019. Six temporary groundwater monitoring

The following tenns shall have the following meanings: 
"BPM Director": the Director of the Bureau of Program Management within the Division of

"CERCLA": the Comprehensive Environmental Response, Compensation, and Liability Act of

"Day": a calendar day. In computing any period of time under this Order & Settlement Agreement, 
where the last day would fall on a Saturday, Sunday or State holiday, the period shall run until the 
close of business of the next working day. 
"Department": the New York State Department of Environmental Conservation. 
"Director": the Division Director, Division of Environmental Remediation. 
"ECL": the Environmental Conservation Law, Chapter 43-B of the Consolidated Laws of New 
York, as amended. 
"Feasibility Study" or "FS": a study undertaken to develop and evaluate options for remedial 
action. The feasibility study emphasizes data analysis and is generally perfonned concurrently and 
in an interactive fashion with the remedial investigation, using data gathered during the remedial 
investigation. The tenn also refers to a report that describes the results of the study. (See 6 
NYCRR Part 375) 
"Force Majeure Event": an event which is brought on as a result of fire, lightning, earthquake,

reasonable control. 
"Inactive Hazardous Waste Disposal Site Remedial Program" or "Remedial Program": activities 
undertaken to eliminate, remove, abate, control, or monitor existing health hazards, existing 
environmental hazards, potential health hazards, and/or potential environmental hazards in 
connection with the Site and all activities to manage wastes and contaminated materials at or 
removed from the Site. (See ECL 27-1301(3) and 6 NYCRR Part 375) 
"Interim Remedial Measure" or "IRM": a discrete set of activities, including removal activities, to 
address both emergency and non-emergency Site conditions, which can be undertaken without 
extensive investigation or evaluation, to prevent, mitigate, or remedy environmental damage or the 
consequences of environmental damage attributable to the Site. (See 6 NYCRR Part 375)

KEDLI Examples of Value Engineering that led to Changes to DEC Remedy 
Decisions and associated Cost Savings  
From 2019 through 2022, KEDLI has documented multiple opportunities where value 
engineering and project optimizations have reduced costs or avoided additional costs to the 
projects.  Below are examples of some of these reductions.   
Babylon Former MGP Site - Remedial Action 
Alternative approach to reduce excavation footprint 
KEDLI discussed an alternative approach with New York State Department of Environmental 
Conservation (NYSDEC) to reduce the excavation footprint of the remedy.  The alternative 
included performing a four-foot cut excavation after the installation of In-Situ Stabilization (ISS) 
columns instead of prior to installation of the columns.  The columns were installed at ground 
level and following installation of the ISS the excavation was conducted.  This reduced the 
amount of ISS swell created during installation, reduced the number of trucks entering and 
exiting the Site to dispose of the material and made the site safer for workers on Site leaving 
much needed space for truck traffic.  The approach was included as part of the Technical 
Execution Plan and received approval October 2021.  Utilizing this approach resulted in 
$106,000 savings below the contractor bid.  
Incorporating Automated Survey Equipment on Long Island Railroad Tracks 
KEDLI successfully developed an alternative with input from consultants to incorporate 
automated survey equipment and installing prisms to monitor any movement of the LIRR tracks 
adjacent to the remediation area.  This alternative reduced the need for LIRR flaggers, 
surveyors, and workers on the tracks throughout the duration of the project.  Multiple times a 
day the automated system would survey the prisms on the tracks and site monuments around 
the sites to generate a daily report with the findings.  A cost avoidance of $168,774 was 
recognized between the cost of daily flaggers and surveyors for the project ($191,774) and the 
cost of the automated system for the project ($23,000). 
Bay Shore Former MGP Site 
Requested discharge of dewatering fluids from Site 
KEDLI requested and received approval from NYSDEC to discharge groundwater generated 
during a gate station upgrade project directly to ground surface.  This groundwater was treated 
by downgradient oxygen and ozone systems in lieu of trucking and disposing of at an offsite 
treatment facility.  A cost avoidance of $1.5 million dollars was recognized as the need for 
tankers, hauling the material, and disposal were no longer needed. 
9 N. Clinton Ave. Oxygen System Shut Down 
KEDLI requested and received approval from NYSDEC in August 2021 to shut down the oxygen 
system located at the 9 N. Clinton Ave. property.  An annual cost savings of $17,200 and over 
30-years will be recognized as $3,207,600 for the reduction of system checks, semiannual
maintenances, reporting, and access agreements due to the shutdown.
