2.1. Summary of Conclusions 
2 
Q. 
Please summarize your conclusions regarding the electric EAMs. 
3 
A. 
Our conclusions regarding the electric EAMs are summarized as follows: 
4 
 Niagara Mohawk proposes a large set of EAMs, which will likely increase 
5 
administrative and regulatory complexity, may lead to unintended consequences, 
6 
and could provide excessive incentives in some performance areas.  
7 
 Energy Efficiency Share the Savings (“STS”) EAM: The design of this EAM 
8 
may encourage efficiencies in program administration and delivery, but it may 
9 
also lead to several unintended consequences that can harm ratepayers and 
10 
impede progress toward the state’s ambitious energy and climate goals. Also, the 
11 
proposal for the STS EAM lacks sufficient detail regarding the calculation of the 
12 
STS metric. 
13 
 Beneficial Electrification Building Electrification EAM: Heat pumps will be a 
14 
vital component of any strategy to aggressively reduce greenhouse gas (“GHG”) 
15 
emissions from the building sector to meet the Climate Leadership and 
16 
Community Protection Act (“CLCPA”) goals; however, the Company’s Building 
17 
Electrification EAM targets are well below the CLCPA’s carbon emission 
18 
targets. 
19 
 Beneficial Electrification Transportation Electrification EAM: Niagara Mohawk 
20 
already has a financial incentive to support electric vehicles (“EV”) through its 
21 
ability to earn a return on capital investments made to support increased charging 
22 
and charging infrastructure. Also, there are many EV-related state and local 
23 
policies that contribute to the sales of new EVs, making it difficult to identify the 
24 
Company’s contribution to the EV sales. As a result, this EAM would likely 
25 
provide excessive incentives to the Company.  
26 
 System Efficiency Peak Reduction EAM: This metric overlaps with several other 
27 
EAMs, creating the potential for the Company to earn excessive financial 
28

awards. Also, the development of the baseline against which this metric is 
1 
measured is not transparent. 
2 
 System Efficiency Load Factor EAM: The proposed EAM could provide 
3 
incentives for outcomes that the Company did little or nothing to bring about. 
4 
Further, the Company’s selection of the targeted transformers is not transparent, 
5 
and there is the potential for receiving two different incentives for the same 
6 
action (i.e., double-counting).

 System Efficiency Managed EV Charging EAM: This EAM appears to align

3

4

10

3. BACKGROUND  
20 
Q. 
Please describe New York’s policy framework and goals for electric utilities. 
21 
A. 
In 2014, Governor Andrew Cuomo launched REV, a broad initiative to build an 
22 
integrated energy network able to harness the combined benefits of the central grid with 
23 
clean power. The 2015 State Energy Plan, which serves as a roadmap for REV, includes 
24 
GHG reduction targets of 40 percent from 1990 levels by 2030 and 80 percent by 2050. It 
25 
also established goals to increase energy efficiency savings to 600 trillion British thermal 
26 


--- Page 15 ---
 
Direct Testimony of Alice Napoleon and Kenji Takahashi 
Page 12 
units (“Btu”) statewide, and establishes that 50 percent of electricity will come from 
1 
renewable energy resources.1 
2 
In April 2018, the New York State Energy Research and Development Authority 
3 
(“NYSERDA”) and the Department of Public Service proposed a comprehensive 
4 
approach to achieving the energy efficiency and GHG reduction goals of the State Energy 
5 
Plan. This white paper, entitled New Efficiency: New York, identified 185 trillion Btu 
6 
(“TBtu”) of realistically achievable, cumulative statewide site energy savings by 2025, of 
7 
which 31 TBtu could be achieved through additional utility programs.  
8 
On December 13, 2018, the Commission issued an order titled Order Adopting 
9 
Accelerated Energy Efficiency Targets (“Accelerated Efficiency Order”) in Case 18-M-
10 
0084. The Accelerated Efficiency Order adopted the overall utility goal of 31 TBtu set 
11 
forth by the New Efficiency: New York paper. It set immediate minimum electric and gas 
12 
energy efficiency savings targets for each of the state’s utilities for the period 2019–2020, 
13 
and it laid out utility-specific goals through 2025 to create market certainty and guide 
14 
future implementation decisions.2 As a subset of the electric efficiency target, the 
15 
Accelerated Efficiency Order established a savings target of 5 TBtu for heat pumps as a 
16 
minimum target for all the state’s electric utilities combined. Further, the Accelerated 
17 
Efficiency Order set forth the Commission’s expectations for cost reduction, offerings to 
18 
LMI customers within efficiency portfolios, and regulatory provisions including 
19 
performance incentive mechanisms. In addition, it allowed electric and gas utility 
20

as oil or propane, under certain conditions.3 
2 
In July 2019, Governor Cuomo signed into law the Climate Leadership and Community 
3

4 
targets. It requires all sectors of the state’s economy to collectively achieve 40 percent 
5

6 
2050, as well as achieve net zero GHGs by 2050 (meaning sectors must offset any 
7

8 
and 100 percent carbon-free electricity by 2040.4

On January 16, 2020, the PSC issued its Order Authorizing Utility Energy Efficiency and 
10 
Building Electrification Portfolios Through 2025 (“Implementation Order”) in Case 18-
11 
M-0084. This order puts the state on a path to saving 3 percent of electricity sales by 
12

13 
energy savings targets for the investor owned utilities and NYSERDA amount to 35.8 
14 
TBtu through 2025, including 2.9 TBtu in electric and gas savings by Niagara Mohawk.5

A.  
We believe that an emphasis on shared cost savings should help to promote efficacy and

Q. 
Are there other relevant principles that apply to the design of effective EAMs? 
6 
A.  
Yes. EAMs should include metrics that are clearly tied to policy goals, unambiguously 
7 
defined, easily quantifiable, and easily understood. EAMs should focus on activities and 
8 
outcomes that are within the utility’s control and are largely free from outside influence.10 
9 
Also, incentive formulas that determine how much the utility is compensated at different 
10 
levels of achievement should be consistent with the desired outcome and should be 
11 
calibrated to effectively incent utility performance.11  
12

13

14 
of providing excess incentive to the utility. This basic principle was articulated in the 
15 
Accelerated Efficiency Order, which cautioned that, “[p]rogram-specific EAMs, if any, 
16 
must be reconciled with portfolio-wide EAMs to avoid double-counting.”12 A large 
17 
number of EAMs is difficult to track, and with a large set there can be many potential

The Company proposes to create an EAM tied to the number of low-income customers

Q. 
Please provide more information about the geothermal loop service program. 
10 
A. 
The geothermal loop service program aims to provide up to 2,600 tons of geothermal heat 
11 
pump capacity over a course of four years. This is equivalent to serving 650 customers on 
12 
average, assuming a 4 ton system on average per customer.45 The Company intends to 
13 
solicit a range of customer types, including existing delivered fuels customers who are far 
14 
away from the Company’s gas mains and new construction customers who would 
15 
otherwise install gas heat supplied by the Company’s network. The Company is also 
16 
considering “existing gas heat customers who are served by a segment of leak-prone pipe, 
17 
as a way to avoid replacement of the leak-prone pipe and instead remove that segment 
18 
from service.”46

This program has the potential to offer benefits, especially to the extent that it targets gas 
13 
heat customers who are served by a segment of leak-prone pipe to avoid investments in 
14 
pipe replacement, which could become stranded assets as New York pushes forward with

CLCPA compliance. Also, this service can stabilize energy costs and may prove 
16 
economically attractive relative to expensive bottled gas or other delivered fuels, which 
17 
are common heating sources for customers in remote locations.

Q. 
Please explain your concern about the cost of the proposed geothermal shared loop 
1 
service program. 
2 
A. 
The Company’s proposal lacks information on other costs that participants would incur. 
3 
Costs that are not covered by the Company’s monthly fee could be substantial. According 
4 
to the 2017 NYSERDA study Renewable Heating and Cooling Policy Framework, the 
5 
cost of drilling (including the cost of underground pipes, corresponding to the ground 
6 
loops) accounts for just 32% of the total installed costs of GSHPs. This means that 
7 
participants may need to pay as much as than 3 times as much as the Company’s 
8 
proposed monthly fee, as the fee only covers roughly 30% of the total costs.
