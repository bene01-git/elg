Q. Please state your name and business address.
A. Arif Ullah
Q. What is your position at South Bronx Unite?
A: Executive Director
Q. What is South Bronx Unite?
A. We are a community-led organization that brings together neighborhood residents,
community organizations, academic institutions, and allies to improve and protect the social,
environmental, and economic future of Mott Haven and Port Morris.
Q. Please summarize your background.
A: I graduated from Hunter College and have a Master’s in Public Policy from Duke University.
I am a social and environmental justice advocate, grassroots urban planner, and community
activist with more than 20 years of experience in designing and managing community
development programs, establishing diverse alliances, and co-creating campaign strategies
around local, state, and national issues.
​Prior to joining South Bronx Unite, I was Director of Programs at Citizens Committee for New
York City where we supported hundreds of grassroots groups every year in their self-determined
efforts to improve the quality of life in under-resourced neighborhoods across NYC. Before that,
I worked as an immigrants’ rights advocate with American Friends Service Committee where he
helped establish a local alliance of immigrant advocates, contributed to a national campaign for
immigration reform, and designed and led workshops on community organizing.
I am co-founder of Bangladeshi Americans for Political Progress, a steering committee member
of Queens Climate Project, and a core member of Malcolm X Community Garden. I have been
on the boards of ioby, Farm School NYC, and NY Immigration Coalition.
Q: On whose behalf are you testifying in this proceeding?
A: Sane Energy Project
Q: What is your purpose in submitting this testimony?
A: To review ConEd’s proposed investments in the South Bronx and Northern Queens

A: Impact of Bronx River Tunnel to Bronx Westchester Border Pipeline and Astoria LNG Facility
on Disadvantaged Communities
Q: Please describe the communities in which South Bronx Unite works
A: South Bronx Unite works in the Mott Haven and Port Morris sections of the South Bronx. This
area is NYC’s epicenter for environmental injustice, where generations of people of color have
grown up overburdened with environmental pollution, including a disproportionate number of
waste transfer stations, peak power plants, expressways, heavy manufacturing, and diesel truck
intensive shipping operations. At the same time, the area has among the lowest access to green
space per capita in the entire city. This has resulted in asthma hospitalization rates eight times
higher than the national average and 21 times higher than any other NYC neighborhood. Not
surprisingly, due to the high prevalence of respiratory illnesses, our community has been among
the worst hit by the pandemic. And, of course, poor health and poverty are a vicious and
inextricably linked cycle.
Q: Are these communities located in state-designated Disadvantaged Communities or
Potential Environmental Justice Areas?
A:Yes
(Source: New York State Disadvantaged Communities Map)
Q: Please describe the sources of fossil fuel pollution in the communities in which South
Bronx Unite works.

A: Fossil fuel pollution sources include four peak power plants, as well as vehicle traffic from:
Major Deegan Expressway (I-87); Bruckner Expressway; Third Avenue Bridge; Willis Avenue
Bridge; Waste Management waste transfer station, which processes 100% of The Bronx’s
household waste; Action waste transfer station; Stericycle medical waste disposal facility; FedEx
warehouse; and Fresh Direct warehouse (NYC headquarters for deliveries)
Q: Please describe other sources of pollution in the communities in which South Bronx
Unite works.
A: Several combined sewer overflow points, which spill out into the Harlem River; Emissions
from energy-inefficient buildings
Q: What Consolidated Edison infrastructure is currently located in the communities in
which South Bronx Unite works?
A: The Company has two major transmission lines, one that brings fracked gas into New York
City from the west and one from the North and Iroquois Pipeline, that merge together under the
south Bronx before crossing under the river into Astoria.
(Source: Sane Energy Project You Are Here Map)
Q: Are there any proposed investments that would impact your district? If so, what are
they?

A: Yes. Investments in the Bronx River Tunnel to Bronx Westchester Border Pipeline and
Astoria LNG Facility will lock in decades of fossil fuel transportation and storage, and thus fossil
fuel pollution, in the South Bronx.
Q: Does your organization support these investments?
A: No.
Q: Has Consolidated Edison ever contacted your organization about past or proposed
infrastructure projects?
A: No
Q: Did South Bronx Unite have a role in the passage of the Climate Leadership and
Community Protection Act (CLCPA)? Is the organization active in the implementation of
the CLCPA? If yes, how so?
A: We supported the passage of the CLCPA, though our involvement was limited. We are now
actively supporting its implementation in coalition with other organizations, including NY
Renews. We have urged our legislators to support funding for the implementation of CLCPA and
continue to educate our community about its importance.
Q: Is Consolidated Edison’s proposal consistent with the CLCPA?
A: No.
