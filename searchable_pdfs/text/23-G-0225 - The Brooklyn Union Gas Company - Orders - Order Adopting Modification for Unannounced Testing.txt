BY THE COMMISSION: 
INTRODUCTION 
 
 
On May 20, 2025, the City of New York (the City) filed 
a petition to modify interruptible customer testing requirements 
related to mandatory, system-wide testing of dual-fuel equipment 
customers, specifically, to modify those contained in the 
tariffs of The Brooklyn Union Gas Company d/b/a National Grid NY 
(KEDNY) and KeySpan Gas East Corporation d/b/a National Grid

the requirement, and require National Grid, to allow for a

of dual-fuel equipment customers, including the January

Companies (LDCs)2 to modify their tariffs to rename the 
“unannounced test” to “reliability performance test,” redefining 
the parameters of the test to allow for up to 24 hours’ notice 
ahead of the reliability performance test. 
 
BACKGROUND 
 
 
LDCs design their systems to ensure they can provide 
safe, adequate, and reliable service to their firm core 
customers during periods of peak demand.  This is imperative 
because firm core customers are those that solely rely on 
natural gas for their heating needs and are unable to switch to 
an alternate fuel or cease consuming natural gas.  During times 
without high demand from firm core customers, LDCs have 
additional capacity on their systems and the LDCs also provide 
service to customers on an interruptible basis.  Such

service customers elect between Tier 1 and Tier 2

PETITION 
 
 
The City of New York requests that the Commission 
revise the interruptible gas testing requirements established in 
the Communications Order.  The Petition specifically requests 
the implementation of a minimum 24 hours’ notice before the 
January unannounced test due to safety and operational concerns 
experienced by the City of New York.  The City of New York 
operates approximately 230 interruptible accounts within the 
National Grid service territory.  The City asserts that 
maintaining non-firm service is essential to its gas demand 
management, recognizing downstate supply constraints, while also 
recognizing the overall improved performance of its 
interruptible accounts.5   
 
 
The City of New York relies on its own internal 
procedures to safely execute fuel switching.  These procedures 
require coordination, safety checks, and confirmation of system 
readiness, which the City of New York asserts take a specific 
period of time to implement the protocol.6  The City argues that 
the Order in Case 15-G-0185 did not account for customer-side 
safety risks associated with unannounced testing.  The City of

LEGAL AUTHORITY 
 
 
Pursuant to Public Service Law (PSL) §§65 and 66, the 
Commission has authority to ensure that the LDCs provide safe 
and adequate service and clearly define the terms of service in 
their tariffs. 
 
DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION 
 
 
The City of New York states, ” ... the unannounced 
January test requirement has created troublesome safety concerns 
for the City, as it needs a certain amount of time to follow 
specific protocols to switch its system from gas over to an 
alternative fuel source.”  While the City of New York describes 
potential safety concerns, it has historically demonstrated 
adequate compliance with directives to interrupt its use of 
natural gas with only two-hours’ notice. 
 
 
The fundamental purpose of an interruptible service 
tariff is to support system reliability for firm core customers 
by requiring interruptible customers to interrupt their gas 
consumption when directed, thereby reducing peak demand on the

As required by interruptible service class tariffs, 
those participating in the service class must stand ready to 
stop using natural gas when their LDC calls upon them to do so.  
It would not be appropriate for interruptible customers to pay 
the otherwise applicable firm service rate as they do not 
receive the same level of service as firm customers.  
Additionally, interruptible customers generally incur costs to 
undertake additional actions to prepare to stop their gas usage, 
such as maintaining a supply of alternate fuel.

Similarly, the City asserts that its Petition is 
limited to modifying the testing procedures, redefining 
“unannounced” to include a fixed notice period, and that its 
Petition is not a proposal to modify requirements associated 
with emergency interruptions.  However, the City’s proposed 
modifications alter the ability of the test to replicate 
emergency conditions.  Since the core purpose of a test is to 
ensure customer readiness for real-time interruption, any 
modification to test protocols must be assessed in light of this 
obligation.  The City argues that in most instances, based on 
predicated weather patterns, the LDC provides 24 hours’ notice 
or more when calling an interruption.10  Yet, the insistence that 
most interruptible events provide at least 24 hours’ notice does 
not obviate with the need to test whether interruptible 
customers can switch to alternative fuel sources in the most 
severe or time-sensitive emergencies.   
 
 
While we agree that in most cases LDCs call 
interruption events with at least 24 hours’ notice, this does 
not preclude the possibility that in the most severe 
emergencies, the LDCs may need to require customers to interrupt 
with less notice.  The unannounced test has served to assess 
customer’s readiness to switch in those events.  Yet, we also 
recognize there is ambiguity in the definition of an unannounced 
test and recognize that LDCs have implemented the test while 
providing varying amounts of notice.  Accordingly, we direct all 
gas LDCs, not only National Grid as proposed in the City’s

To ensure consistent implementation, the LDCs are 
required to file tariff amendments on not less than five days’

interruptible customers with direct notification of the tariff 
amendments.
