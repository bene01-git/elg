PETITION 
 
I. 
Introduction 
            Niagara Mohawk Power Corporation d/b/a National Grid (“Niagara Mohawk” or the 
“Company”) hereby respectfully petitions the New York State Public Service Commission (the 
“Commission”) for a limited waiver of the Transmission Revenue Adjustment (“TRA”) provisions 
included in Rule 43.5.1 of the P.S.C. 220 – Electric Service Tariff (“Electric Tariff”) to increase 
the $8 million monthly cap on the amount that can be collected from or credited to customers.  The 
petition seeks to temporarily increase the cap to $16 million through the end of the Company’s 
current rate plan, which is March 31, 2025.  Currently, Niagara Mohawk’s TRA balance is in a 
regulatory liability position of approximately $386 million.  Increasing the cap would enable the 
Company to refund customers and reduce the current TRA deferral balance more expeditiously.  
At the same time, a temporary waiver through the end of the current rate plan would enable parties 
to explore potential revisions to the TRA in Niagara Mohawk’s upcoming rate case, including 
assessing whether changes should be made to the cap on a more permanent basis.  The Company 
respectfully submits that the limited waiver, as described below, is in the public interest and should 
be approved by the Commission.

II. 
Background 
The TRA is based on the monthly comparison of (1) a forecast based transmission revenue 
credit that is reflected in delivery rates, and (2) the actual transmission revenue realized, exclusive 
of revenue taxes imposed thereon.  Any under or over recovery of base transmission revenues 
through the TRA are deferred for refund to or recovery from customers subject to a cap.  
Specifically, Rule 43.5.1 of the Electric Tariff imposes a monthly cap of $8 million on the amount 
that can be collected from or credited to customers.  This $8 million cap remains in place as long 
as the TRA balance exceeds +/- $6 million. The Company currently has a TRA deferral balance 
of $386,193,354 and the requested relief would allow the Company to begin refunding customers 
more quickly than the Electric Tariff allows.   
 
III. 
Requested Relief 
 
Each month, pursuant to Rule 43.5 of the Electric Tariff, the Company calculates the 
over/under reconciliation of the base transmission revenue credit.  If the monthly comparison of 
base transmission revenue to actual transmission revenue exceeds plus or minus $6 million in any 
given month, the amount over the $6 million is deferred to the next cost month, with carrying 
charges applied at the Company’s cost of capital.  If the $6 million cap is reached for an additional 
two consecutive months, the cap is then increased to $8 million. The $8 million cap remains in 
place as long as the TRA exceeds +/- $6 million, including recovery of the deferral and 
corresponding return, and reverts back to the $6 million monthly cap once the TRA, including 
recovery of the deferral and return, falls to less than or equal to +/- $6 million.  Pursuant to Rule 
43.6 of the Electric Tariff, the TRA amount is allocated to applicable service classifications, which

include residential customers and small and large commercial customers, based on the single

The current TRA deferral balance as of December 2023 is $386,193,354, which is 
primarily due to higher than forecast Transmission Congestion Contract (“TCC”) Auction 
revenues.  Due to the magnitude of the current deferral balance owed to customers, the Company 
is requesting a temporary waiver of Rule 43.5.1 of its Electric Tariff through the end of the current 
rate plan, which is March 31, 2025.  The Company believes that temporarily increasing the 
monthly cap to $16 million is reasonable given the current circumstances.  The TRA will continue 
to be allocated pursuant to Rule 43.6 as explained above.  
The limited waiver through the end of the current rate plan will allow the Company to 
provide a refund to customers while at the same time more expeditiously reducing the deferral 
balance.  The chart below provides the average monthly bill reduction to customers based on 
changing the monthly cap from $8 million to $16 million.  As shown, residential customers are 
expected to see a 3.51 percent delivery bill decrease with commercial customers expected to see a 
decrease ranging from 2.83 percent to 7.23 percent on their delivery bills.

Notice
