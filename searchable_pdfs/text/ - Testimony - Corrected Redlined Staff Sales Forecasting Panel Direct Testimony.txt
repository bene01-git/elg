ELECTRIC FORECAST METHODOLOGY 
3 
Q. 
What methodology did the Company use to develop 
4 
its electric delivery volume forecast? 
5 
A. 
According to pages 8-9 of the EFP’s initial 
6 
testimony, the Company based its forecasts of 
7 
delivery volumes for all service classifications 
8 
(SCs), except SCs 5, 6, and 13, on econometric 
9 
models.  An econometric model consists of two 
10 
parts.  The first part is a regression model 
11 
that relates the delivery volume to a set of 
12 
independent variables.  These independent 
13 
variables can include the number of customers, 
14 
real price of electricity, personal income or 
15 
employment, weather variables, dummy variables, 
16 
and mobility variables that serve as a proxy to 
17 
account for the effects of the COVID-19 
18 
pandemic.  These independent variables normally 
19 
cannot explain all variations in delivery volume 
20 
because not all influencing variables are known, 
21 
quantified, or available with measurable data.  
22 
In addition, a time series may possess 
23 
characteristics of non-stationarity or serial 
24 


--- Page 14 ---
Cases 25-E-0072 & 25-G-0073       Staff Sales Forecasting Panel 
13 
 
correlation, which could result in large 
1 
forecasting errors.  Therefore, the second part 
2 
of the econometric model is introduced to 
3 
capture the remaining variations left by the 
4 
included independent variables, as well as to 
5 
address the characteristics that may produce 
6 
forecasting errors.  This part may consist of 
7 
one or more autoregressive integrated moving 
8 
average (ARIMA) terms, depending on data 
9 
patterns, and is statistically determined.  The 
10 
combined model is referred to as an ARIMAX 
11 
model, where the “X” represents the independent 
12 
variables included in the model. 
13 
Q. 
Does the Company use the same methodology to 
14 
forecast its electric customer counts? 
15 
A. 
Yes.  Con Edison developed an ARIMAX model for 
16 
each of the SCs 1, 2, 8, 9, and 12. Similar to 
17 
the electric volume models, the Company included 
18 
the mobility, dummy, and ARMA terms as the 
19 
independent variables, and employment for the 
20 
service class 1 model.   
21 
Q. 
How is an ARIMAX model estimated? 
22 
A. 
An ARIMAX model is estimated using actual 
23 
historical data for both the dependent and 
24 


--- Page 15 ---
Cases 25-E-0072 & 25-G-0073       Staff Sales Forecasting Panel 
14 
 
independent variables.  In its update filing, 
1 
the Company updated the historical data, 
2 
forecasted economic variables, and its estimated 
3 
post-model adjustments through December 2024, 
4 
except for the SC 12 customer count model in 
5 
which the historical data ending January 2025 
6 
was used to account for higher-than-normal 
7 
actual customers observed in the fourth quarter 
8 
of 2024.  The Company then used this updated 
9 
data to produce its updated electric delivery 
10 
volume and customer forecasts.  
11 
Q. 
How is an ARIMAX model used to forecast delivery 
12 
volume? 
13 
A. 
After the ARIMAX model is estimated, the 
14 
relationships between delivery volume and 
15 
independent variables, along with the 
16 
coefficients of the ARIMA terms for each service 
17 
class, are established. The estimated 
18 
coefficients of the forecasting equation are 
19 
applied to the forecasted values of the 
20 
explanatory variables to arrive at the forecast 
21 
of the dependent variable.
