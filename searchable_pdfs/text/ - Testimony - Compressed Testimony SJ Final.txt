II. 
OVERVIEW OF ENVIRONMENTAL JUSTICE 
13 
Q. 
What is environmental justice? 
14 
A. 
The U.S. Department of Energy defines environmental justice as “the fair treatment and 
15 
meaningful involvement of all people, regardless of race, color, national origin, or 
16 
income, with respect to the development, implementation, and enforcement of 
17 
environmental laws, regulations, and policies.”1  
18 
Q. 
What is your understanding of fair treatment and meaningful involvement? 
19 
A. 
Fair treatment means that no population bears a disproportionate share of 
20 
negative environmental consequences resulting from industrial, municipal, 
21 
and commercial operations or from the execution of federal, state, and local 
22 
laws; regulations; and policies. Meaningful involvement requires effective

Q. 
What is the history of the environmental justice movement? 
3 
A.  
The concept of environmental justice was forged by a movement of scholars and activists 
4

5

7

8

9

10 
seminal report “Toxic Waste and Race” in 1987, which revealed that Black and Hispanic 
11 
populations are exposed to more toxic contamination from hazardous waste sites than 
12 
white neighborhoods.9 Principles of the Environmental Justice Movement stating that all 
13
