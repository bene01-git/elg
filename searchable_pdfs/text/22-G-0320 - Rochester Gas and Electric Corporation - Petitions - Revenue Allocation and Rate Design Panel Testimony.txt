IV. BACKGROUND – REVENUE ALLOCATION AND RATE DESIGN 
22 
Q.    
Please provide an overview of the Companies’ revenue allocation and rate design goals. 
23 
A. 
The Companies’ primary revenue allocation and rate design goals are adequacy, fairness, 
24 
and efficiency.  Also important is the goal of rate stability.  Adequacy is necessary to 
25 
ensure that the rates are designed to recover the necessary revenue requirement set forth 
26 
by the Revenue Requirements Panel.  Fairness calls for allocating the total revenue 
27 
requirement among the various customer classes in a way that most closely reflects the 
28 
cost of providing services to each class.  Efficiency means designing rates to recover 
29 
costs from customers in a way that reflects, as closely as possible, the manner in which 
30 
those costs are incurred by NYSEG and RG&E.  Rate stability recognizes the need to 
31 
employ gradualism when the implementation of rates based solely on the other goals 
32 
would cause unexpected changes that significantly impact customer bills.

Q.

1 
ECOS study? 
2 
A. 
As stated in the testimony of Witness Rimal, the service class rate of return is derived by

4 
allocated to each service class.  The rate of return index for each service class is 
5

6 
overall system average rate of return.

Q.    
Please describe how the Companies allocated the proposed revenue increase, net of the 
8 
Energy Efficiency costs, to service classes. 
9 
A. 
The revenue allocation process for the residual revenue requirement increases occurs in 
10 
three steps.  The Companies’ goal is to move each service class rate of return toward the 
11 
system rate of return through the allocation of the revenue requirement increase to each 
12 
service class.  Recognizing that some judgments and approximations are part of any cost 
13 
analysis, the first step is the application of a 15% tolerance band to the results of the 
14 
ECOS studies to account for potential variation in results.  That is, if the index rate of 
15 
return for any of the service classes falls outside of the +/-15% tolerance band, the 
16 
contributions for those classes would change by a percentage other than an overall system 
17 
average revenue increase.  The service classes whose index rate of return is within the 
18 
15% band received the overall system average revenue increase.  The service classes 
19 
whose index rate of return is 1.15 or greater received less than an overall system average 
20 
revenue increase because they are over-contributing to revenue requirement recovery.  
21 
The service classes whose index rate of return is 0.85 or less received more than an
