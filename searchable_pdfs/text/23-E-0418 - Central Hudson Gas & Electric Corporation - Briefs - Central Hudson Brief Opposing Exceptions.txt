4. 
Productivity 
Arguing for its 2% productivity imputation, Staff claims, without citation, that “the ALJs 
specifically recommended initiatives that could lead to additional unintended benefits/savings 
during the Rate Year, including, 31.5 additional FTEs above the 1,270.5 FTEs that Staff 
recommended, and additional cost allowances.”  SBOE, p. 8.  Here, Staff is merely repeating its 
testimonial position that the added FTEs ultimately adopted will produce productivity beyond the 
traditional 1% imputation.  The flaw in Staff’s case is that – beyond merely asserting this to be 
true – Staff produced not a smidgeon of evidence that this would be the case.15  In fact, when the 
final FTE headcount for the Rate Year is established, the 1% productivity imputation will be 
applied to the entirety of the employee workforce, as it should be, thereby capturing the 
productivity associated with the additional FTEs.  
Given the absence of any evidence to support Staff’s doubling of the productivity 
imputation, the standard 1% productivity adjustment should be applied, as it has been for 
 
15  See fn. 9.

capture “nonspecific, unquantified and unidentified productivity gains.”17  To double it, in order

advocates, is wholly without basis in the record.
