II. 
Purpose of Testimony 
16 
Q. 
What is the purpose of the Panel’s testimony? 
17 
A. 
The Company’s outdoor lighting business has changed substantially in the past few 
18 
years.  Lighting and communications technologies have evolved and advanced such 
19 
that customers have significantly more opportunities than ever before to save 
20 
energy and tailor the operations of the lighting systems that serve them.  In addition, 
21 
the potential for customers to purchase their lighting systems from the Company at 
22 
Net Book Value (“NBV”) established in the Company’s 2017 Rate Case, and the 
23 


--- Page 8 ---
Testimony of Outdoor Lighting Panel  
 
 
Page 6 of 38 
 
financial and technical support available from the Power Authority of the State of 
1 
New York (“NYPA”) to help municipalities buy their lighting systems, has shifted 
2 
some of the Company’s focus from how best to operate and maintain the Company-
3 
wide street lighting system, to how to effectively and accurately transfer ownership 
4 
and responsibility for large parts of that system to individual municipalities across 
5 
the state.   
6 
 
7 
 
In addition, although several municipalities have purchased or are pursuing the 
8 
purchase of street light systems, many municipalities continue to rely on the 
9 
Company for street lighting service.  These municipalities, nevertheless, want the 
10 
benefits of emerging advances in lighting and communications technologies.  
11 
Therefore, in addition to working to improve the process for customers interested 
12 
in buying their lighting systems, the Company is also making new technologies and 
13 
capabilities available to customers that are not interested in owning their street 
14 
lighting system.   
15 
 
16 
 
Therefore, the primary purpose of the Panel’s testimony is to address: (i) the 
17 
process for transferring street lighting systems to municipalities; (ii) service 
18 
offerings available for customers that elect to buy their street lighting systems; and 
19 
(iii) service offerings available for customers who do not purchase the lighting 
20 
systems that serve them.  The Panel also describes specific cost allocation and rate 
21 
design proposals that support the proposed outdoor lighting improvements that are 
22 
addressed in the direct testimony of the Company’s Electric Rate Design Panel.

consistent and predictable for customers.

Q. 
Why is the Company proposing to continue to offer municipalities the option 
10 
to buy their street lighting systems at NBV? 
11 
A. 
Offering municipalities the option to buy their complete street lighting systems at 
12 
NBV for the period to be governed by this case provides customers additional time 
13 
to consider whether they wish to buy their lighting systems and take on the 
14 
responsibilities of owning, operating, and maintaining them into the future without 
15 
the pressure of having to rush into a transaction to achieve NBV pricing.  This will 
16 
enable these customers to make more considered decisions.  It also recognizes the 
17 
fact that the large number of customers that have expressed interest in buying their 
18 
lighting systems has resulted in a long queue of potential sales and inquiries that 
19 
require additional time and resources to address. 
20 
Because the NBV can fluctuate (up or down) from the time the Company initially 
21 
provides that amount to the customer until the date of the closing, the NBV sale 
22 
amount to be paid by the customer also may fluctuate over time.  It is the 
23

1

B. 
LED Street Lighting Energy Efficiency Program 
7 
Q.

9

adjusted conversion forecast is anticipated to be approximately 28,200 luminaires, 
17 
which is significantly lower than the originally forecasted 42,667 luminaires.  As 
18 
of July 1, 2020, the Company has completed 10,265 luminaire conversions within 
19 
the calendar year.

utilizing various wireless communication applications and other customer selected 
23

“smart cities” technologies.  The NLC systems offer the City an opportunity to

4

with actionable intelligence to optimize various city services focusing on public 
6

7 
to improve the quality of community life.

Service Classification No. 6 (SC-6) Changes

What change is the Company proposing to make to SC-6? 
7

The Company is proposing to establish a sunset date of June 30, 2025 for the

9 
installations, or installations experiencing material change since February 1, 2011.

Q. 
Why is the Company proposing this change? 
12 
A. 
The proposed change is in conformance with the Company’s strategy to transition 
13 
all Company supported outdoor lighting services to more energy efficient LED 
14 
technology.  During the period of this rate case, the Company anticipates the rapid 
15 
ramp down in HID technology utilization and subsequent reductions in related 
16 
maintenance stock quantities like lamps and standard photocontrols.  As the internal 
17 
availability of these products decreases, service levels to the existing customers will 
18 
be reduced.  The Company believes the period of this rate plan provides adequate 
19 
time to facilitate appropriate transition plans for each customer.
