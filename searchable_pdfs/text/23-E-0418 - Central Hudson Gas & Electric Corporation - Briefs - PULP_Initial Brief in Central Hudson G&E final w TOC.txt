I. 
INTRODUCTION  
A. Overview 
The Public Utility Law Project (“PULP”) hereby submits our Initial Brief in these 
proceedings. From the filing of Central Hudson’s (“the Company”) rate case, PULP has been 
openly concerned about the Company’s failed SAP customer information system (“CIS”) 
upgrade, the billing issues that followed, and whether ultimately, it was appropriate for Central 
Hudson to be seeking a rate increase at this time.1 PULP’s concern has not faded during this rate 
proceeding.  
The Evidentiary Hearing provided an opportunity for PULP to cross examine the 
Company on several topics, which are discussed in greater detail below. Ultimately, we urge the 
Administrative Law Judges (“ALJs”) to review the entire record with great scrutiny as the public 
should not be expected to pay more for their electric and gas service to the extent that such costs 
are due to the Company’s failure to properly implement its billing system. PULP maintains that 
the various investigations and proceedings should be completed prior to the issuance of any rate 
increases.2 As such, PULP encourages the ALJs to deny the Company’s overall rate increase 
request. Moreover, PULP urges the ALJs to consider the positions and proposals discussed 
herein when drafting the recommended decision (“RD”) in these proceedings. Should any 
changes be made to the Company’s rates, charges, and tariff, we believe that actions such as 
increased reporting and communication with the Company’s customers of such changes are 
paramount. PULP thanks you for your consideration of the arguments made below.

PULP POSITION 2: DISCRETIONARY CUSTOMER CREDITS SHOULD NOT 
BE CONSIDERED AS PART OF THE REVENUE REQUIREMENT  
PULP is concerned about Central Hudson’s use of discretionary customer credits, and we 
believe that it’s against the public interest to include program related costs in the Company’s 
revenue requirement. As PULP also discusses under Section X for Customer Service issues, our 
organization has multiple points of contention with Central Hudson’s use of discretionary 
customer credits. The use of such credits provides a very high level of discretion to Company 
staff when resolving issues and the program without reporting related activity to the 
Department.9 As such, PULP is opposed to the Company’s continued use of the program unless 
the Commission orders significant transparency and reporting requirements associated with 
future use.10

The Company has explained that these discretionary customer credits are treated as 
“operating expenses or reductions to other operating revenue that have historically been included 
in revenue requirements and are treated as such in the determination of regulatory ROE and the 
measurement of shared earnings.”11 Central Hudson has also said that they are “unaware of any 
other reconciliation mechanisms that would be impacted by discretionary credits.”12 Moreover, 
these “are not recorded to delivery revenue accounts, and therefore are not included in the 
calculation of the Revenue Decoupling Mechanism (“RDM”).”13 As such, the Company does 
include discretionary customer credits in the Revenue Requirement. 
During PULP’s cross examination of the Company’s Revenue Requirement Panel, PULP 
learned that “approximately ... $50,000 for this program is included in revenue requirements”14 
for the Rate Year. Since the use of this program by the Company is not reported to the 
Department of Public Service, at this time,15 it is not appropriate to include any portion of the 
estimated cost in the Company’s revenue requirement. Without any regular reporting to DPS 
staff, PULP also cannot confirm whether the estimated amount of $50,000 is accurate. For the 
sake of argument, if one can assume this amount is baseline $50,000, then the $50,000 associated 
should not be included in the revenue requirement. In conclusion, PULP urges the ALJs to assess 
the Company’s use of the discretionary credit program with great scrutiny and we ultimately 
urge the exclusion of such costs from inclusion in the revenue requirement.

PULP POSITION 3: PULP OBJECTS TO THE USE OF RATEPAYER FUNDS 
FOR MODERNIZATION OF INTERACTIVE VOICE RECOGNITION SYSTEM 
PULP objects to the $1.7 million in funding stipulated between Staff, the Company, and 
the Utility Intervention Unit (“UIU”), regarding Interactive Voice Recognition (“IVR”) 
modernization as memorialized in Exhibit 528. Any and all IVR modernization work should be 
halted until there is confirmation by the Department that the SAP CIS system is operating 
properly.16 Moreover, PULP opposes the use of ratepayer funding for IVR modernization during 
the rate year, because the company has not conducted a formal process justifying the need for 
these funds.17  
During cross examination, the Customer Experience panel admitted that it was able to 
meet customer service reporting metrics with the current system by utilizing a series of queries.18 
Further, the panel stated that the brief analysis it did conduct—regarding potential IVR 
vendors—revealed a second viable option that it did not pursue.19 Since the Company has not 
conducted a full analysis of the potential options available to it, Central Hudson and Staff are 
essentially speculating that the requested $1.7 million is the most appropriate use of rate payer 
funds, without providing any guarantees that this is indeed the case. Finally, since the Company 
stated it would be open and willing to assess vendor options, no funding of the current IVR 
system should take place until that assessment has taken place.20  
As proposed, the IVR modernization program has not been shown to be an optimal use of 
rate payer funds. Thus, it should not be recommended by the ALJ’s, nor approved by the 
Commission.

LACKED FOUNDATIONAL KNOWLEDGE REGARDING THE

is responsible for ensuring that the final product of a rate making proceeding fully aligns with the 
aforementioned goal of the CLCPA.22 The Company’s answers to cross examination questions
