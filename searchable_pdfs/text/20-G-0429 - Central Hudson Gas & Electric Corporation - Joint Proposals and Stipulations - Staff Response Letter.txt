Dear Secretary Phillips: 
This letter is filed by Department of Public Service Staff (Staff) in response to 
certain statements contained in the Statement in Opposition to the Joint Proposal filed on 
September 22, 2021 by Mr. Bob Wyman, and to reply to the September 22, 2021 comments filed 
by Citizens for Local Power (CLP).  After reviewing all filings concerning the Joint Proposal 
filed August 24, 2021, Staff continues to recommend adoption of the Joint Proposal, in its 
entirety, as reasonable and in the public interest. 
Mr. Wyman opposes two elements in the Joint Proposal.  First, Mr. Wyman 
opposes the reduction in the amount of the credit provided by the Geothermal Rate Impact Credit 
(GRIC) and the change in funding source for the GRIC.  He also opposes the potential 
termination of the GRIC.  The Signatory Parties to the Joint Proposal agreed to the reduction of

the GRIC amount because a subscription to the Company’s Insights+ program is no longer being 
required.  The reduction in the amount of the GRIC is roughly equal to the cost of the previously 
required monthly subscription fees.  Therefore, when the previously required annual subscription 
fee is netted out of the previous GRIC, the result is approximately the GRIC amount agreed to in 
the Joint Proposal.  It should be noted that a Signatory Party, New York Geothermal Energy 
Organization, filed a statement in support of the Joint Proposal and did not oppose the GRIC 
amount.  The agreed upon funding source for the GRIC was also changed from being collected 
through the Revenue Decoupling Mechanism (RDM) to now be through the Company’s heat 
pump program budget authorized by the Commission.  The Signatory Parties agreed that it was 
reasonable for the GRIC, a geothermal credit, to be funded through the heat pump program.   
Finally, the Joint Proposal stipulates that the GRIC will continue to be available 
until a suitable opt-in or standby rate is available for customers using heat pumps, or until 
December 31, 2025 in alignment with the term of the authorized heat pump program budget, 
unless a future Commission Order extends the availability of the GRIC.  It should be understood 
that a purpose of the GRIC was to address the “inverse cost shift” that SC 1 residential customers 
may experience with geothermal heat pump systems.  The “inverse cost shift” is created because 
arguably customers using heat pumps consume more electricity than others in the service class 
and charges assessed to the volumetric portion of their bill may result in an overpayment 
compared to the costs attributed to that service class.  In its 2019 Standby Rates Order,1 the 
Commission directed each utility to develop opt-in “standby rates” for each service 
classification, with the specific terms of such rates to be developed subject to the utilities 
completing an Allocated Cost of Service study.  Thereafter, Staff submitted its Whitepaper on 
Allocated Cost of Service Methodology, which is pending before the Commission.  After this 
determination, it is anticipated that residential “standby rates” will be available for customers.  
These residential “standby rates” will presumably be designed to more accurately align a 
customer’s costs with the charges a customer pays to be on the electric system, thus eliminating 
the “inverse cost shift.”  This would eliminate the need to offer a GRIC.

Second, Mr. Wyman raises concerns with the agreed upon treatment of gas assets 
and infrastructure given the goals of the Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act 
(CLCPA).  Mr. Wyman suggests the use of Unit of Production depreciation methodology in lieu 
of Straight-Line depreciation methodology, and that the service lives of certain gas assets be 
re-examined.  Staff believes that the depreciation issues raised by Mr. Wyman are of statewide 
interest and importance and deserve statewide stakeholder input to produce a reasonable and 
rationally based resolution.  Therefore, Staff believes these issues are more appropriately 
addressed generically, possibly in Case 20-G-0131, otherwise known as the “Gas Planning 
Proceeding,”2 instead of individual utility rate proceedings. 
Like Mr. Wyman’s gas depreciation concerns, CLP’s long-term concerns with the 
agreed upon replacement of Leak Prone Pipe and the likelihood of stranded gas assets, is a 
statewide issue which also may be addressed in the Gas Planning Proceeding.  However, it must 
be noted that in the short-term Central Hudson has an obligation to provide safe and adequate gas 
service, and the Company, pursuant to the Joint Proposal, will seek alternatives to the 
replacement of pipelines by eliminating double runs of pipe, bringing unprotected steel pipe into 
cathodic protection, and pursuing Non-Pipes Alternatives. 
CLP also expressed concern that the Joint Proposal did not include a mechanism 
for debt/arrears relief.  The Joint Proposal recognizes the importance of Pandemic related 
assistance, noting that this assistance can be best provided on a statewide basis through Case 
20-M-0266.  However, the Joint Proposal stipulates that, in the event the Commission does not 
address residential and small business arrears arising from the Pandemic by September 15, 2021 
in Case 20-M-0266 or another generic proceeding, the Company will initiate a collaborative to 
discuss arrears resolution to begin no later than November 1, 2021, or two weeks after the 
approval by the Commission of this Joint Proposal, whichever is later (Joint Proposal, p. 42). 
Staff notes that after the Joint Proposal was filed, the Secretary to the Commission 
issued a Notice on September 8, 2021, in Cases 14-M-0565 and 20-M-0266.  The Notice stated 
that a working group “shall develop a list of priorities and objectives for EAP improvements and

In summary, the Joint Proposal is an agreement among multiple parties that 
provides the Company with an opportunity to invest in new equipment, update aging 
infrastructure and deploy new information and electric grid technology to modernize the 
Company’s systems so it can continue to deliver electricity and natural gas to its customers in a 
reliable and safe manner at just and reasonable rates.  The Joint Proposal also reflects Pandemic 
considerations, is aligned with, and furthers the goals of the CLCPA and other New York State 
goals and Commission policies and produces a result that is rational and well within the range of 
reasonable outcomes had the proceeding been fully litigated.  The adoption of the Joint Proposal 
is in the public interest and it is recommended it be adopted in its entirety.
