BACKGROUND 
Capital Capacity Project Review Process 
 
 
The Rate Order adopted the terms of the Joint Proposal 
filed on May 14, 2021, with modifications and established rate 
plans for KEDLI and its affiliate, The Brooklyn Union Gas 
Company d/b/a National Grid NY (KEDNY) (collectively, National 
Grid or the Companies).  Among other things, the Joint Proposal 
set forth a process for additional review of certain identified 
Long-Term Capital Capacity Projects (Capacity Projects), 
including the Southeast Suffolk Infrastructure Project, Phases 1 
and 2.  In this Order, the term “SE Suffolk Project” is used to 
refer to the Southeast Suffolk Infrastructure Project – Phase 1. 
 
 
The Rate Order provided that the Companies could seek 
recovery of the revenue requirement associated with the Capacity 
Projects, including the SE Suffolk Project, through the Demand 
Capacity Surcharge Mechanism (DCSM).2  The Companies can recover 
the costs of Capacity Projects if the Commission finds that 
reasonably forecast peak customer demand requires the 
implementation of the project to meet that demand and 
alternatives that could sufficiently meet, reduce, or eliminate 
the demand cannot reasonably be expected to be available and 
operational in the timeframes necessary for a lower all-in cost.3

Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act 
 
 
The Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act 
(CLCPA) went into effect on January 1, 2020.  Amongst its 
provisions, §7(2) requires that state agencies shall consider 
whether decisions, including “issuing permits, licenses, and 
other administrative approvals and decisions… are inconsistent 
with or will interfere with the attainment of the statewide 
greenhouse gas emissions limits established in article 75 of the 
environmental conservation law.”  Further, §7(2) requires that 
“[w]here such decisions are deemed to be inconsistent with or 
will interfere with the attainment of statewide greenhouse gas 
emissions limits, each agency … shall provide a detailed 
statement of justification as to why such limits/criteria may 
not be met and identify alternatives or greenhouse gas 
mitigation measures to be required where such project is 
located.”  Additionally, §7(3) requires that, in considering 
administrative approvals and decisions, state agencies “shall 
not disproportionately burden disadvantaged communities as 
identified pursuant to subdivision 5 of section 75-0101 of the 
environmental conservation law.” 
 
Description of the SE Suffolk Project 
 
 
The Southeast Suffolk Project consists of an 
approximately 1.6-mile-long transmission main with a regulator 
station and associated distribution facilities to serve 
customers on eastern Long Island.  The 350 pounds per square 
inch gauge (psig) transmission main runs on County Road 104 from

Report of the Independent Consultant 
 
 
On March 25, 2022, PA Consulting filed its Assessment 
Report (PA Report) of the SE Suffolk Project.  Pursuant to the 
Joint Proposal adopted by the Rate Order, PA Consulting was 
charged with addressing the following: (1) need for the project 
to meet a reasonable forecast of customers’ peak demand, based 
on the most recent forecast available; (2) any safety and/or 
reliability benefits from the project; (3) viable alternatives 
to the project that would ensure reliable service; (4) all-in 
cost of the project and a comparison of the all-in cost of 
viable alternatives; (5) GHG emissions attributable to the 
project and any viable alternatives; and (6) any comments and 
analysis submitted by intervening parties and the public. 
 
 
The PA Report concluded that the SE Suffolk Project is 
a reliability driven project and that, without it, maintaining 
adequate distribution system pressures downstream on a Design 
Day is at risk.  The PA Report continued that, if the SE Suffolk 
Project is not in-service by the winter 2022/2023, widespread 
design day outages in East Hampton and Amagansett are possible.  
The PA Report explains that the SE Suffolk reinforces, and 
results in incremental capacity within, KEDLI's distribution

The PA Report considered other potential

Need for the SE Suffolk Project 
 
 
As noted in the PA Report, the SE Suffolk Project was 
originally conceived based on load forecasts developed for the 
2016 rate proceeding23 to address potential reliability concerns 
of demands placed on the gas system for both existing and future 
customers in the eastern portion of the Long Island service 
area.  In 2018, the Company selected the SE Suffolk Project as 
the best available option to address reliability system 
requirements by winter 2022/2023.  Prior to the final selection 
of SE Suffolk Project, KEDLI considered alternative routes but 
rejected them as they would have been more costly, if even 
feasible, without the additional benefits the SE Suffolk Project 
provided to address operating conditions and maintaining safe 
and reliable gas service for customers.  The PA Report concurred 
with the Company’s rationale for selection of the SE Suffolk 
Project. 
 
 
PA Consulting’s analysis confirmed that the forecast 
of peak design day demand indicates that there is a risk of 
widespread design day outages for the winter of 2022/2023 in the 
areas downstream of the SE Suffolk Project, if SE Suffolk 
Project had not been built.  A core concern here for our 
determination is that the PA Report’s engineering assessment 
highlighted that the gas system downstream of the SE Suffolk 
Project was already experiencing difficulty maintaining minimum
