Q. 
What is a credit rating, and what distinguishes it from 
15 
other measures of the financial condition of a utility? 
16 
A. 
A credit rating summarizes credit risk, which is the ability 
17 
and willingness of an issuer of fixed income securities to 
18 
fulfill its contractual financial obligations in full and on 
19 
time. Ratings address the relative probability that an issuer 
20 
or an issue will experience default, i.e., the failure to pay 
21

either the required periodic payment or the principal when it 
1 
matures under the terms of the security. 
2 
Credit ratings reflect a more extended view of financial 
3 
health than other, more familiar financial measures such as 
4 
quarterly financial results, earnings-per-share, rate of 
5 
return for a particular reporting period, and the market 
6 
prices of a company’s securities. Ratings are also an 
7 
independent opinion offered by firms that have no financial 
8 
stake in the outcome of its analysis. The long-term and 
9 
independent nature of credit ratings make them an ideal 
10 
benchmark to assist utility regulators navigate the many 
11 
decisions they must make as they balance competing interests. 
12 
I think that as disinterested observers with a long-term 
13 
mindset, 
rating 
agencies 
are 
well 
aligned 
with 
the 
14 
sensibilities of regulators.

16 
A. 
The primary role of a credit rating agency is to provide an 
17 
assessment of the creditworthiness of a company or a 
18 
financial instrument to facilitate access to fixed income 
19 
capital markets at the most efficient cost. The agencies 
20 
publish analyses of the issuers and issuances to 
21 
communicate with more detail the ratings and the analysis 
22 
behind them to the capital markets. Ratings are expressed

issue. The ratings scales of the two major rating agencies,

S&P and Moody’s Investor Service (“Moody’s”), appear in

Ratings in the BBB/Baa category and above are considered

“investment-grade” by market participants. Ratings below

Investors use them to assist in investment decisions on

which companies to invest in and the price that they will

A.

12 
political influences on regulation, are analyzed to discern 
13 
the risk surrounding the ultimate factor of covering all 
14 
costs sufficiently to earn a reasonable return on 
15 
investment.

Q.

1 
side of credit analysis? 
2 
A. 
Credit analysis is distinguished by its emphasis on cash 
3 
flow. Recognizing that debt is serviced with cash, not 
4 
earnings, credit analysts strive to understand the cash-
5 
flow dynamics of a company’s financial results as much as 
6 
or more than the accounting-derived earnings. The most 
7 
recent example of this dichotomy is the effect of the Tax 
8 
Cut and Jobs Act of 2017 (“TCJA”) on utilities, which 
9 
placed downward pressure on utility ratings because of its 
10

11

12 
agencies use as a base for most cash-flow metrics is cash 
13 
flow from operations (“CFO”) or some derivation of it.4 The 
14 
other major element of financial risk to a credit analyst 
15 
is the total amount of debt or debt-like obligations on the 
16 
issuer’s balance sheet and from other activities. Items 
17 
that the rating agency regards as debt-like are underfunded 
18 
pension obligations, lease liabilities, long-term power 
19 
purchase obligations, and deferred taxes. 
20
