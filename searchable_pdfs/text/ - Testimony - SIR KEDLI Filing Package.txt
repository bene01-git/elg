2

KEDLI’s SIR program includes activities in connection with the 
3 
management, investigation, and remediation of MGP sites that have been 
4 
contaminated by the past release of substances from property owned or 
5 
formerly owned by KEDLI or its predecessors.  KEDLI is responsible for 
6 
29 MGP sites, one of which, Belle Harbor, involves other potentially 
7 
responsible parties (“PRPs”) that share responsibility for the site with 
8 
KEDLI.  As of the end of the Historic Test Year, 16 sites were actively 
9 
tracked 
with 
the

including 
one

in 
the 
site 
10

11

12

13 
degrees of DEC regulatory closure.  It is important to note that despite 
14

15

16

Q. 
Could actual SIR costs differ from the Company’s forecast? 
1 
A. 
The forecast reflects the Company’s best estimate of SIR costs.  While 
2 
KEDLI believes that its forecast is reasonable, SIR costs are subject to a 
3 
high degree of variability, through no fault of the Company, as projects are 
4 
subject to schedule and scope modifications by the DEC and environmental 
5 
regulators, as well as site access issues with property owners or delays 
6 
resulting from onsite condition constraints.  KEDLI’s forecast reflects costs 
7 
that it expects will be incurred in the Rate Year and Data Years; however, 
8 
there is a risk that actual costs could be more or less.  Accordingly, as 
9 
discussed below, KEDLI is proposing to continue to fully reconcile these 
10 
costs.   
11

APPENDIX 1 – KEDLI SIR COST CONTROL EFFORTS 
1 
 
2 
The Company follows the cost management practices set forth in the Inventory of 
3 
Best Practices for Utility SIR Programs established in Case 11-M-0034 and as 
4 
clarified by the New York State Department of Environmental Conservation 
5 
(“NYSDEC”).  Examples of the Company’s SIR cost control efforts are discussed 
6 
below. 
7 
 
 
8 
Experienced Staff and Contractors 
9 
The Company staffs its SIR program with experienced project managers.  The 
10 
project managers are responsible for, among other things, monitoring project 
11 
activity, ensuring that work is performed in accordance with work plans and project 
12 
specifications, and managing project costs, including reviewing contractor 
13 
invoices.  The Company also utilizes specialized environmental consultants and 
14 
contractors to perform investigation, engineering design, remedial evaluation and 
15 
construction, construction oversight, and operations and maintenance activities in 
16 
accordance with the Company’s policies and accepted industry practices. 
17 
 
18 
Competitive Bidding Process 
19 
Procurement of consultants and contractors differ due to the nature and scope of the 
20 
work.  The scope of work for contractor work is typically well defined in bid 
21 
                                   Appendix 1 
                                   Page 1 of 8

specifications based on prior field studies and approved by the NYSDEC.  The 
1 
scope of work for consultants is defined based on the immediate project scope of 
2 
work, which may expand due to the progressive nature of the work.  For instance, 
3 
the scope could change following the collection of new data and direction set by 
4 
the NYSDEC.  As more data is collected, it is often advantageous to keep the same 
5 
consultant on the project due to reduced transaction costs and the benefit of 
6 
consultant’s site knowledge. 
7

Contractors 
9 
Contractor work relating to environmental response activities are subject to a 
10 
project-specific competitive bid selection process, with the exception of a select 
11 
number of small projects performed under Blanket Construction Contracts.

Site Investigation and Remediation Progress January 2016 through December 2018

OU1; DNAPL monitoring and recovery in OU1; supplemental oxygen injection groundwater treatment 
systems in OU1, OU2 and OU3; and a sitewide groundwater monitoring program. Monthly and/or 
quarterly OM&M activities are ongoing for operation of these systems. As groundwater impacts are 
remediated, as demonstrated by the groundwater monitoring data, oxygen injection systems are shut-
down upon approval from the NYSDEC. The systems are abandoned when additional groundwater 
monitoring data demonstrates no remaining impacts and upon approval from the NYSDEC. The Final CCR 
for the oxygen injection system in OU3 was submitted NYSDEC in April 2016. A draft CCR for an oxygen 
injection system in OU2 was submitted to NYSDEC in December 2016. The draft SMP was submitted to 
NYSDEC in January 2017. An oxygen injection system was removed from OU2 in June 2017. Soil

2018. A work plan for the abandonment of two OU2 oxygen injection systems was submitted to NYSDEC

associated with the OU1 ozone system was submitted to the NYSDEC in June 2018 and approved in July 
2018. Groundwater sampling for emerging contaminants was requested by NYSDEC in May 2018 and 
completed in September 2018.

Glossary of Terms 
The following tenns shall have the following meanings: 
"BPM Director": the Director of the Bureau of Program Management within the Division of

"CERCLA": the Comprehensive Environmental Response, Compensation, and Liability Act of

"Day": a calendar day. In computing any period of time under this Order & Settlement Agreement, 
where the last day would fall on a Saturday, Sunday or State holiday, the period shall run until the 
close of business of the next working day. 
"Department": the New York State Department of Environmental Conservation. 
"Director": the Division Director, Division of Environmental Remediation. 
"ECL": the Environmental Conservation Law, Chapter 43-B of the Consolidated Laws of New 
York, as amended. 
"Feasibility Study" or "FS": a study undertaken to develop and evaluate options for remedial 
action. The feasibility study emphasizes data analysis and is generally perfonned concurrently and 
in an interactive fashion with the remedial investigation, using data gathered during the remedial 
investigation. The tenn also refers to a report that describes the results of the study. (See 6 
NYCRR Part 375) 
"Force Majeure Event": an event which is brought on as a result of fire, lightning, earthquake, 
flood, adverse weather conditions, strike, shortages oflabor and materials, war, riot, obstruction or 
interference by adjoining landowners, or any other fact or circumstance beyond Respondent's 
reasonable control. 
"Inactive Hazardous Waste Disposal Site Remedial Program" or "Remedial Program": activities 
undertaken to eliminate, remove, abate, control, or monitor existing health hazards, existing 
environmental hazards, potential health hazards, and/or potential environmental hazards in 
connection with the Site and all activities to manage wastes and contaminated materials at or 
removed from the Site. (See ECL 27-1301(3) and 6 NYCRR Part 375) 
"Interim Remedial Measure" or "IRM": a discrete set of activities, including removal activities, to 
address both emergency and non-emergency Site conditions, which can be undertaken without 
extensive investigation or evaluation, to prevent, mitigate, or remedy environmental damage or the 
consequences of environmental damage attributable to the Site. (See 6 NYCRR Part 375)

KEDLI Examples of Changes to DEC Remedy Decisions/Cost Savings Following Discussions

Patchogue Former MGP Site 
Negotiated design scope changes in advance of the remedial action 
KeySpan Gas East Corporation d/b/a National Grid (“KEDLI”) negotiated revisions to the 
remedial design approved by the New York State Department of Environmental Conservation 
(“DEC”).  DEC issued a Record of Decision (“ROD”) in 2011 which identified the remedy 
selected for the site and adjacent property referred to as the “Off-Site Property”.  The ROD 
specified that the Off-Site Properties impacts were to be addressed by excavating impacted soil 
and backfilling with clean imported soils.  KEDLI negotiated with the DEC to use an alternative 
to excavation which was In-Situ Solidification (“ISS”) of soil.  ISS was already a large part of 
the remedy selected for the site.  In October 2017 DEC approved an Explanation of Significant 
(“ESD”) change to the ROD.  The change is based upon reducing impacts to public health and 
the environment by solidifying the contamination and preventing impacts to surface water, 
reduction of the volume of material handled, thus reducing construction impacts to the 
surrounding community, reduction of potential vibrations, reduction of truck traffic, and resulted 
in approximate savings of $2,600,000. 
 
Hempstead Former MGP Site 
Negotiated with DEC to reduce the number of oxygen injection systems 
KEDLI successfully negotiated with DEC a reduction in the number of oxygen injection systems 
to be installed at the site in August 2018.  Two of the four systems that were originally proposed 
as part of the remedy did not have to be installed, maintained, or monitored which resulted in a 
reduction in treatment system installation and monitoring costs of $2,000,000. 
Negotiated groundwater sampling reductions  
KEDLI successfully negotiated long-term groundwater sampling reductions and reporting with 
NYSDEC in June 2018.  KEDLI received approval from DEC to reduce groundwater sampling 
frequency from quarterly to biannually resulting in an approximate cost savings of $87,000 per 
year.

Former MGP with a cost reduction of $126,000; and Hempstead Clinton/Glenwood Landing 
with a cost reduction of $50,000.  The total KEDLI cost reduction in the OMM program is 
$1,534,000.

eliminated the third-party property from future long term SMP access, inspections and 
monitoring.  The total cost savings is $10,000 a year for 30 years.
