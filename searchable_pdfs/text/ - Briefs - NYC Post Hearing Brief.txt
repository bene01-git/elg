PRELIMINARY STATEMENT 
 
On February 16, 2023, a Joint Proposal containing recommendations for new electric and 
gas rate plans was filed in the above-referenced proceedings with the New York State Public 
Service Commission (“Commission”).  The City of New York (“City”), Consolidated Edison 
Company of New York, Inc. (“Con Edison” or the “Company”), Department of Public Service 
Staff (“Staff”) and six other parties were full signatories to the Joint Proposal, and five other parties 
supported various/partial aspects of it.  Parties filed statements and reply statements in response to 
the Joint Proposal on March 30, 2023, and April 3, 2023, respectively.  On April 18, 2023, an  
evidentiary hearing (“Hearing”) on the Joint Proposal and the rate filings, generally, was conducted 
by Administrative Law Judges (“ALJs”) Ashley Moreno and James Costello.   
As stated at the Hearing and addressed here, the City respectfully submits that no record 
evidence was developed at the Hearing that justifies any change to the Joint Proposal.  
Accordingly, the City respectfully requests that the Commission adopt the Joint Proposal in its 
entirety as Con Edison’s new electric and gas rate plans for the period from January 1, 2023 to 
December 31, 2025.

DISCUSSION 
At the Hearing, the Public Utility Law Project (“PULP”), AARP New York (“AARP”) and 
Bob Wyman cross-examined Staff, Con Edison and the City on issues related to the proposed 
return on equity, earnings adjustment mechanisms, depreciation, Con Edison’s pursuit of funding 
under the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act and the Inflation Reduction Act, and 
disadvantaged communities.  The City cross-examined AARP and PULP on Energy Affordability 
Program (“EAP”) enrollment. 
No new evidence was developed during the Hearing on any of the issues raised by PULP, 
AARP, or Mr. Wyman, and there is no basis in the record before the Commission to justify any 
changes to the Joint Proposal.  PULP moved two additional exhibits into the record – Exhibits 706 
and 707 – both of which pertained to data already in the public domain and not establishing 
anything new regarding the cost of equity.  The testimony of the Con Edison, DPS Staff, and City 
witnesses testifying in support of the Joint Proposal affirmed that the recommendations for new 
rate plans set forth within the Joint Proposal would satisfy the requirements of Public Service Law 
§ 65(1) and, on the whole, ensure the continuation of the reliable, resilient, adequate, and safe 
electric and gas service that customers expect and want.   
Separately, the evidence adduced through the City’s cross-examination at the Hearing 
demonstrated that claims made by PULP and AARP regarding the EAP lack merit and warrant no 
changes to the EAP.  In their Statements and Reply Statements in Opposition to the Joint Proposal, 
PULP and AARP claimed that there are approximately 440,000 low-income households in Con 
Edison’s service territory that are not enrolled in Con Edison’s EAP but which are eligible to

The analysis PULP Witness Yates relied upon in advancing the conclusion that hundreds 
of thousands of eligible Con Edison low income households were not enrolled in the EAP was 
fatally flawed.  The record demonstrates that Witness Yates failed to apply the correct factors 
approved by the Commission for enrolling customers in a utility’s EAP.  Witness Yates agreed 
that in order to be enrolled in and receive a discount from Con Edison’s EAP, the resident must: 
(1) be a Con Edison customer; and (2) be enrolled in one of the Commission-approved eligibility 
programs.2   
Despite knowing these enrollment eligibility requirements, Witness Yates performed an 
analysis that disregarded them.  Mr. Yates used the American Community Survey (“ACS”) public 
use micro data set from 2020, which he then coded with various filters.3  While Mr. Yates claims 
that there is a filter applied that indicates that each household is a utility customer, the filter failed 
to indicate whether the customer is a Con Edison customer – only that they are a utility customer.4

CONCLUSION 
For the reasons set forth in the City’s Statement in Support and as set forth herein, the Joint 
Proposal meets the requirements of the Commission’s Settlement Guidelines and its terms and 
recommendations are just and reasonable.  The record in these proceedings provides a rational 
basis and substantial evidence for the Commission to find that the provisions of the Joint Proposal 
are in the public interest, produce just and reasonable rates and provide for safe and adequate 
service.  For all these reasons, the City respectfully recommends that the Commission adopt the 
Joint Proposal in its entirety without modification.
