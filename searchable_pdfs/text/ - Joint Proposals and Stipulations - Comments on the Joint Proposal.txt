Introduction 
The Westchester Municipal Consortium (“WMC” or “Consortium”) comprises 
forty (40) Cities, Towns, and Villages located within Westchester County.  A list of the 
municipalities is annexed hereto.  Each community has residents and businesses that fall within 
Con Edison’s service territory.  This is the first time these municipalities have joined together to 
intervene in a Con Edison rate case.  This unprecedented formation recognizes the adverse 
impact of rising utility rates on affordability. There is also a concern that there may be inequity in 
the investment in facilities between New York City and Westchester, and that the rates charged to 
Con Ed customers in Westchester may not properly reflect the cost of service.   
The Joint Proposal (“JP”) sets forth provisions that represent modest initial steps 
toward more fully addressing these concerns.  At all times, the Consortium has emphasized the 
importance of using inflation as an objective benchmark for evaluating the rate increase. This is 
the primary basis for the Consortium's evaluation of the impact of the JP’s proposed negotiated 
rates on affordability.  
The JP negotiations on important issues to the Consortium continued until a few 
hours before the JP was filed. As a result, due to the associated deadline constraints, it was 
impossible to have forty (40) elected bodies convene to determine the position of each 
municipality on the final negotiated results.  A consensus was reached that overall, the terms of 
the JP, including the provisions advocated by WMC, were sufficient not to oppose the JP.  
However, there is no question that Con Edison and DPS Staff heard our concerns and worked 
very professionally to address them. We also want to commend the DPS Staff, Con Edison, the 


--- Page 3 ---
other parties, and the ALJ assigned to assist in administering the negotiation process for all their 
hard work.  
While the WMC has not signed the JP and proposed lower increases than those in 
the JP, we recognize the intensive efforts of all parties to substantially reduce the initial proposed 
increase to the lower level set forth in the JP.  The WMC is cognizant there must be a balance of 
interests, keeping rates as low as possible while maintaining safe and reliable service, and 
meeting the Commission’s other statutory obligations and policies.  
 It is also vital to WMC’s position that the JP includes certain provisions related to 
a review of Con Edison’s capital investment levels in Westchester as well as providing interested 
parties with an opportunity to further investigate the cost and quality of service provided to radial 
and network customers. Con Ed has agreed to cooperate in providing the information needed to 
investigate these issues in a future rate proceeding. For all of these reasons, the WMC is not 
opposing the JP. 
 
The Standard of Review 
 
The JP is to be reviewed pursuant to the guidelines adopted by the Commission for 
review of settlements of rate proceedings.  (Cases 90-M-0255 et al., Opinion, Order and 
Resolution Adopting Settlement Procedures and Guidelines, Opinion No. 92-2 (issued March 24, 
1992).  The guidelines provide the following criteria for review of whether the JP is in the 
public’s interest: 
A. 
Consistency with the law and regulatory economic, social and environmental 
State and Commission policies; 


--- Page 4 ---
B. 
Whether the terms of the JP compare favorably with the likely result of a fully 
litigated case and produce a result within the range of reasonable outcomes; 
C. 
Whether the JP fairly balances the interests of ratepayers, investors and long-term 
soundness of the utility; and 
D. 
Whether the JP provides a rational basis for the Commission’s decision. 
 
The bottom line is that the end result must not be discriminatory, and it must achieve just 
and reasonable rates.  Not just reasonable to Con Edison, or specific intervenors, but to the 
businesses and residents within its entire service territory. 
 
The Percentage Rate Increase 
THE JP’S REDUCTION OF THE RESIDENTIAL ELECTRIC DELIVERY RATE 
INCREASE FROM 19.1% TO 4.4% AND THE OVERALL BILL IMPACT FROM 13.4% 
TO 2.8% RECOGNIZES WMC’S  CONCERNS OVER AFFORDABILITY 
 
While the JP’s proposed electric and gas rate increases do not go as far as WMC’s 
testimony position of limiting any increase to the inflation rate (Ex. 1172), the material 
reductions are a clear and significant response to the concerns raised by WMC, the public and 
other parties. The recent series of rapid rate hikes, combined with other increases in the cost of 
living, has caused an affordability crisis across all Westchester municipalities.  The WMC 
member municipalities have all seen the need for expanded programs for low-income customers, 
sharp increases in customers falling behind on bill payments, and rising levels of uncollectible 
revenues. These objective facts confirm that there is a pressing need to adjust the balance 
between costs and reliability. Rate increases need to be brought closer to the inflation rate. The 
negotiated rates in the JP clearly demonstrate a significant movement toward that benchmark.

As noted earlier, due to time constraints, it was not feasible to receive formal 
determinations from  all of the relevant elected and appointed decision-makers across all 40 
municipalities.  Accordingly, the Consortium is not taking a position on the final negotiated rate 
levels. However, we call the Commission’s attention to Exhibit 1123 (WMC-1, Schedule 1). This 
exhibit shows the annual rate of inflation and the far more rapid rate at which Con Edison’s 
delivery rates have been increasing in recent years. A straightforward comparison of this data 
with the proposed total bill impact of 2.8% per annum rate increase for electric and total bill 
impact of 2.0% for natural gas as set forth on pages 4 and 10 of the JP, respectively, speaks for 
itself.  The JP makes strides towards moving the electric rate increase closer to the inflation rate 
by reducing the proposed increase by approximately seventy-five percent (75%).  
 
Capital Investments 
THE DISPARITY IN THE LEVEL OF INVESTMENT BY CON EDISON IN 
WESTCHESTER MUNICIPALITIES VERSUS NEW YORK CITY 
 
In its filing update in this case, Con Edison sets forth a list of projects to be undertaken 
over 2026-2028. (Ex.461-EIOP-1UPD) .  Only a handful of the planned projects are located in 
Westchester communities.  In fact, in their response to a WMC data request, Con Ed provides 
data that shows that 96% of the investment in facilities over that period will be within NYC and 
only 4% in Westchester.  (Ex. 1174 WMC-3-43,44).  WMC thus has a reason to be concerned 
that the level of investment in our communities will not be sufficient to maintain, let alone 
improve, the quality of service provided to our residents and businesses. There is ample reason to 
be concerned that NYC is sucking up nearly all of the available dollars for investment in Con 
Edison’s service territory. In an effort to address the perception of inequitable treatment, the JP 
provides that Con Edison leadership will meet annually with WMC municipalities and other 


--- Page 6 ---
interested government officials to review the status of Westchester operations, planned projects, 
and the relative investment in Westchester communities compared to New York City.  (JP. p. 74).  
The annual meeting will also be a forum to address other areas of concern, such as double poles, 
AI data centers, and other issues that may arise over the next few years. The WMC commends 
Con Edison for agreeing to include this provision in the JP.  
WMC views the annual meetings as a significant good-faith step towards addressing our 
members’ questions and apprehensions. It also provides an opportunity to strengthen existing 
working relationships at the decision-making level between Con Edison’s personnel responsible 
for maintaining and improving the system in our communities and the corresponding public 
officials.  This effort should pay long-term dividends for Con Edison and our residents by 
highlighting local concerns early in the engineering and planning process. Since our member 
municipalities are in constant contact with their residents, they are uniquely positioned to be 
aware of certain capital needs and emergency response situations in their respective 
municipalities. They are able to anticipate problems that might be perceived as less of a priority 
or of which  Con Edison’s engineering and planning staff are not aware. These meetings will 
facilitate and strengthen the  working relationships and hopefully address the genuine concerns 
of WMC as to the level of investment in Westchester. They will also provide a valuable 
opportunity for our 40 municipalities to ask questions and obtain information annually on other 
issues affecting quality of service and safety.  
 
Aligning Rates with the Quality and Cost of Service 
THE RELATIVE COST OF SERVICE OF UNDERGROUND GRID NETWORKS AND 
OVERHEAD RADIAL (NON-NETWORK) SYSTEMS SHOULD BE INVESTIGATED 
 
 


--- Page 7 ---
Although WMC is not opposing the JP, it strongly supports inclusion of the following 
provision:  
Consistent with its responses to WMC-4, the Company will prepare an 
informational analysis, apart from its next ECOS study, of primary and 
secondary distribution facilities and service connections, categorized by 
overhead and underground.  Additionally, the Company will provide, on a 
sample level, SC 1 residential characteristics including annual kWh sales, KW 
demand, and customer counts, further classified by radial, N-1 network, and N-
2 network connections.  This data will be offered solely for informational 
purposes, as the sample selected to determine ECOS study cost allocations 
aligns with the requirements of the demand analysis, which is structured for 
class-level rather than customer connection-level assessment.  This analysis 
will be included in the Company's next electric base rate filing.  The Company 
agrees to hold a virtual meeting with Westchester Parties’ representatives and 
other interested parties before undertaking the analysis to review the 
parameters. 
(JP p, 52). 
 
Expert testimony in this case provides overwhelming support for this modest first step 
toward better aligning costs and rates across Con Edison’s integrated system. The issue was 
explained in great depth by WMC’s expert witness, Dr. Ben Johnson. See in particular, pages 16-
18, 21-23, 28, 31, 34-36, 39, 41, 52-53, 77, 83, 85, and 86 of his prefiled testimony. (Ex. 1172).  
On the above pages (and others), Dr. Johson provides a detailed, well-documented 
explanation of why it costs more to serve customers on an underground network than on an 
overhead radial system. He also offered unrebutted evidence that the cost differences are large 
enough to merit further investigation. More specifically, he offered his expert opinion, based 
upon more than 40 years of experience investigating the cost of providing electric service,  that 
the cost differential exceeds 5%.  (Ex. 1172, p.97).  It should be noted that this is not an issue 
that only affects the Westchester communities.  Customers are served by radial systems in every 
outer borough.   Staten Island is almost exclusively served by an overhead radial system. 


--- Page 8 ---
Given the actual state of the factual record in this proceeding, the only disputed 
remaining issues are (1) the magnitude of these cost discrepancies, (2) whether that magnitude is 
sufficient to justify the effort required to modify the rate design, (3) how best to reflect the actual 
differences in cost of service, and (4) how slowly these changes should be phased in.  The JP’s 
provision simply enables data to be collected to assist in evaluating the answer to these 
questions.  This will ensure that parties are not hampered in their attempts to find answers to 
these questions.  The data can then be reviewed and analyzed, within the context of accepted rate 
design practices and adopted Commission policies, for consideration in future proceedings,  
Consumer Protections and the Environment 
WMC endorses the JP’s additional protections for consumers and for the environment.  
Those consumer protections include enhanced  weather-related turn-off protections, additional 
disclosures and other information to assist customers to fully understand their options.  
Environmental protections include requirements for further reporting of greenhouse gas 
emissions and incentives for Con Edison to meet clean energy goals. 
Conclusion 
 
As with all negotiated settlements, the end result is not going to fully address everyone’s 
concerns or go as far as each of the parties may have wanted.   That is certainly true for WMC.  
However, on balance, the material reductions in Con Ed’s proposed rate increases  bringing it 
closer to the rate of inflation combined with the JP addressing other issues raised by WMC are 
sufficient for WMC not to oppose the JP.
