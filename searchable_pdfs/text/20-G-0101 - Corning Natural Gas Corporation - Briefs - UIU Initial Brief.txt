I. Introduction 
On February 27, 2020, Corning Natural Gas Corporation (the “Company”) filed a petition 
to the New York State Public Service Commission (the “PSC” or the “Commission”) to seek 
revised rates for gas service in the Company’s service territory over a proposed three-year rate 
plan. The Company proposed significant bill impacts of 23.4%, 2.56%, and 2.01% for each of the 
three years, respectively, and in the filing proposed levelizing increases over three years to impact 
customer total bills at 10.93% each year.1 As an initial matter, UIU requests that the Commission 
adopt a one year rate plan only. Further, UIU objects to one of the Company’s primary rate 
drivers—a proposal for accelerated depreciation—because the Company has not met its burden of 
proof and the resulting increase would be unjust and unreasonable to the Company’s customers. 
Finally, UIU makes recommendations on rate design and revenue allocation issues.

III. 
Overview and General Policy Considerations 
The Utility Intervention Unit (“UIU”) is an office of the Division of Consumer Protection, 
within the New York State Department of State, and has the statutory responsibility to represent 
the interests of customers in utility proceedings, with a particular focus on residential and small 
commercial customers. In this proceeding, UIU was initially alarmed at the significant proposed 
revenue requirement increase in cost to Corning customers, tied closely to the Company’s proposal 
to accelerate depreciation in a drastic and specific response to the recently enacted Climate 
Leadership and Community Protection Act (“CLCPA”).11 UIU also became concerned about the 
potential cost impact to mass market customers that would result from parties’ proposed changes 
to revenue allocation and corresponding rate design.  
While UIU did not submit testimony in this case, UIU participated in settlement efforts as 
well as the evidentiary hearing. The outcome of this case could shift significant cost and 
responsibility to mass market customers of Corning and—with respect to the Company’s proposal 
on accelerated depreciation—could disrupt the delicate balance among customer and shareholder 
interests that is necessary for establishing the just and reasonableness of rates, by shifting too much 
cost and risk onto ratepayers without sufficient evidence to justify doing so.  
As an initial matter, UIU request that the Commission adopt a one-year rate plan only. UIU 
opposes the Company’s proposal on accelerated depreciation because the Company has not met 
its burden of proof and the increase in bill impacts resulting from the proposal would harm its 
customers. In addition, UIU urges caution against adopting one particular Embedded Cost of

IV. 
Accelerated Depreciation 
The Company’s request for accelerated depreciation should be rejected because the 
underlying data is speculative and the Company has, therefore, not met its burden of proof on this 
issue. The Commission is empowered with broad authority to determine the correct balance 
“between the utility’s customers and their stockholders,” in determining that “the result is ‘just and 
reasonable’” and “fair.”12 The Public Service Law and regulations of the Department of Public 
Service are clear that the burden of proof to show that a proposed change is just and reasonable 
belongs to the requesting utility.13 In doing so, the utility must present “testimony to support such 
estimates . . . and all estimates must be explained in detail and the bases definitely established.”14 
More specifically, “[s]peculative or conjectural data are not acceptable”15 in establishing the 
necessary evidence requisite for fulfilling the utility’s burden of proof. Establishing a definite basis 
for estimates is critical and necessary for stakeholders to advocate effectively and for the 
Commission to make objective decisions when “balanc[ing] the interests of shareholders and 
ratepayers, and the interests of different groups of ratepayers, to reach a conclusion that is within 
the reasonable range of outcomes to determine just and reasonable rates.”16  
The Company’s expert, Paul Normand, recommends that the Company accelerate 
depreciation in light of the State’s “aggressive goals to limit or eliminate carbon emissions by the

As for revenue allocation, the Company’s witness Normand recommended an across-the-
board revenue increase for all service classes. (Ev. Hr. Tr. 215). Staff generally agreed with the 
Company’s revenue allocation approach with minor modifications. (Ev. Hr. Tr. 738). MI’s revenue 
allocation proposal places less costs onto SC-6 customers and assigns no costs to SC-7 
Transportation and SC-14 Aggregation Com & Public while increasing costs to other customers

instead of the Company’s uniform system average approach). (Ev. Hr. Tr. 606–07; Hr. Ex. 50).

results of the Company’s ECOS results, the most reasonable approach is to at minimum apply a 
system average increase for each Service Classification. This approach has been adopted by the 
Commission in prior rate cases and will not financially burden one particular service 
classification.24 UIU also sees merit in gradually moving SC-8 Hammondsport Transportation 
closer to SC-6 Commercial Transportation in an effort to bring the two delivery rates closer for 
these service classes as supported by the Staff Rate Panel Testimony. (Ev. Hr. Tr. 738–40).
