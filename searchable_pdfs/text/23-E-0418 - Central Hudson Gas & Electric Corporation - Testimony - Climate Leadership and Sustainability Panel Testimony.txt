VII. 
CLCPA AND CAC SCOPING PLAN 
10 
Q.    
What is the CLCPA?  
11 
A. 
The CLCPA is New York State legislation that was signed into law on July 
12 
18, 2019.  The CLCPA mandates substantial reductions in GHG 
13 
emissions across all sectors of the state’s economy and accelerated 
14 
deployment of clean energy technologies.  There are many critical 
15 
milestones established within the CLCPA, including a 40% reduction in 
16 
statewide GHG emissions by 2030, 70% of electricity generated by 
17 
renewable sources by 2030, 100% of electricity generated by emissions 
18 
free sources by 2040, and an 85% reduction in statewide GHG emissions 
19 
by 2050.  The CLCPA also has provisions to ensure benefits of clean 
20 
energy investments accrue to disadvantaged communities.   
21

Q.    
What is the Scoping Plan? 
1 
A. 
The CLCPA directed the establishment of a Climate Action Council 
2 
(“CAC”), which is a 22-member appointed body charged with developing a 
3 
Scoping Plan to serve as the roadmap to achieve the State’s progressive 
4 
clean energy and climate goals.  In accordance with the CLCPA, the CAC 
5 
developed its final Scoping Plan in December of 2022, after releasing its 
6 
Draft Scoping Plan in 2021 and a subsequent period of public review and 
7 
input.  The Scoping Plan presents three Scenarios which serve as 
8 
proposed roadmaps for how the State might achieve the goals of the 
9 
CLCPA, and recommendations for additional legislation and new 
10 
regulations affecting the electricity and gas sectors plus the transportation, 
11 
buildings, industry, agriculture, forestry, and waste sectors.  Estimated 
12 
costs through 2050 of these recommendations, as calculated from the 
13 
Plan’s technical appendices, are about $300 billion in 2020 in net present 
14 
value terms.  To the extent that these recommendations are adopted, 
15 
Central Hudson along with the other utilities in New York and their 
16 
customers will face rapidly increasing requirements to support new 
17 
renewable generation and electrification initiatives in the transportation 
18 
and buildings sectors.   
19
