INTRODUCTION 
On August 27, 2020, Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corporation (Central 
Hudson or Company) filed tariff amendments to implement new electric and gas rates to become 
effective no later than July 1, 2022.1  These rate proceedings have been challenging and 
presented the parties with unique concerns and opportunities beyond the normal ratemaking and 
revenue requirement issues.  After several months negotiating terms that further the goals stated 
in the Climate Leadership and Climate Protection Act (CLCPA) and addressing the necessary 
and prudent gas and electric rate increases, as well as addressing the effects of the COVID-19 
Pandemic (Pandemic), the parties reached agreement on virtually all issues.  At the time of the 
Joint Proposal (JP) scheduling conference, no party expressed opposition to the Joint Proposal, 
except for possible limited opposition by one individual party.  The filed JP contemplates and 
substantially resolves the vast majority of issues and concerns raised during the settlement 
negotiation process, including consistency with the goals and objectives of the CLCPA, and 
Commission and State policies, while managing through various ratemaking techniques to 
reduce total bill revenue increases to be under 2%.  
The JP electric revenue requirement removed, reduced and deferred many costs 
that might have otherwise been allowed absent the Pandemic, while increasing Energy 
Efficiency and Heat Pump program funding, complying with Electric Vehicle (EV) charging 
initiatives and allowing recovery for Phase 1 transmission and distribution projects.2  The 
revenue requirement for Electric for Rate Year (RY) 1 now stands at a decrease of $1.0 million, 
well below the Department of Public Service (Department) Staff’s (Staff) testimony’s 
recommended revenue increase and a 100% reduction from the Company’ initial request. 
The JP continues all Commission and New York State policy goals, while 
providing a means to further incorporate these and other unknown initiatives during the term of

BACKGROUND 
In the August 27, 2020 rate filings, Central Hudson requested an electric rate 
delivery revenue increase of $32.8 million (8.4 % on base delivery revenues) and a gas rate 
increase of $14.4 million (12.1 % on base delivery revenues).  The Company requested a 9.1% 
return on equity (ROE) with a common equity ratio of 50%.  The 2018 Rate Order allowed the 
Company a 9.0% ROE and an increasing common equity ratio of 48% in Rate Year 1, 49% in 
Rate Year 2, and 50% in Rate Year 3.  The Company requested that new rates become effective 
July 1, 2021. 
A procedural conference was held on October 6, 2020, before Administrative Law 
Judges (ALJs) Michael Clarke and Anthony Belsito in Albany.  The purpose of the procedural 
conference was to identify parties and major issues, establish a schedule for the proceedings and 
address issues related to service of documents, discovery and any other procedural matters 
identified by the parties at the conference.  By ruling dated October 14, 2020, a case schedule 
was adopted calling for Staff and Intervenor direct testimony due December 22, 2020; rebuttal 
testimony due January 22, 2021; and an evidentiary hearing to begin February 16, 2021. 
On December 22, 2021, Staff and other parties filed direct testimony and exhibits 
in response to the Company’s filing.  Staff’s pre-filed testimony and exhibits recommended a 
one-year revenue decrease of $12 million for electric and an increase $5.9 million for gas.  On 
January 22, 2021, the Company filed extensive rebuttal testimony, as did several other parties. 
On January 29, 2021, the Company filed a letter with the Secretary, pursuant to 
16 NYCRR §3.9, to provide notice of impending settlement negotiations in these proceedings.5  
Specifically, the letter noted that the Company, Staff and other parties had agreed to enter into 
settlement negotiations as soon as logistically possible. 
Settlement negotiations commenced virtually on February 4, 2021 and continued 
on a number of occasions thereafter in as all-party and working group meetings.  The Company

OVERVIEW OF THE JOINT PROPOSAL 
As discussed in greater detail below, and in the various sections of this Statement, 
the JP contains a number of provisions designed to protect and benefit ratepayers, maintain and 
improve the Company’s ability to provide safe, adequate and reliable service, take into account 
and incorporate provisions to mitigate the effects of the Pandemic, and, to the extent feasible and 
appropriate, provide a resolution to other issues raised by intervenors, including, but not limited 
to, incorporating provisions to further the goals and mandates of the CLCPA. 
The JP proposes a three-year Rate Plan for electric and gas service commencing        
and continuing through June 30, 2024.  For purposes of the JP, RY1, RY2 and RY3 are defined 
as the 12 months ending June 30, 2022; June 30, 2023; and June 30, 2024; respectively.  The JP 
provides the requisite make-whole revenue adjustments to accommodate the change in the 
effective date of the tariffs. 
Importantly, the JP is broadly supported by parties with diverse interests, 
including customer groups, as well as business, industry, governmental and environmental 
interests.  As stated above, the parties engaged in months of negotiations concerning the effects 
of the Pandemic and support for the CLCPA and Commission goals and initiatives. 
The JP attempts to balance the varied interests of the parties by providing 
customer relief from the effects of the Pandemic, improving system reliability, making needed 
and mandatory infrastructure improvements, enhancing vegetation management to increase 
system reliability during blue sky days and storm events, increasing the Company’s workforce,  
implementing Earnings Adjustment Mechanisms (EAMs) consistent with Commission policies, 
and continuing to institute initiatives that are not only consistent with the CLCPA, but move 
positively move forward to accomplish the CLCPA mandated goals.  The major components of 
the Joint Proposal are summarized below.

requirements, many of which nor available outside of the multi-year Rate Plan.

CLCPA Compliance 
 
The JP, Section XXIII.B  and Appendix BB, establishes a set of commitments that 
are consistent and further the mandated CLCPA goals.  Specifically, the JP recommends that the 
Company develop a comprehensive plan to reduce its GHG emissions.  Additionally, 1) 
anticipating cumulative savings that equate to approximately 2.5% from 2019 gas sales and 6.9%

plug-in hybrid electric vehicles through 2025; 5) phase-in the elimination of the natural gas 
declining block rate which is consistent with the promotion of energy efficiency; 6) facilitate the 
subscription of community distribution generation through the Company’s Clean Energy 
Marketplace; and 7) pro-actively seek non-pipes alternatives locations.

Electric Reliability and Resiliency   
 
The JP maintains the Company’s current Customer Average Interruption Duration 
Index (CAIDI) metrics and proposes increasingly stringent System Average Interruption 
Frequency Index (SAIFI) targets over the course of the Rate Plan.  All revenue adjustments 
related to the Electric Reliability mechanism will come from shareholder funds and will be 
deferred and credited for the benefit of customers through the Rate Adjustment Mechanism 
(RAM).  The JP also reinforces the Company’s commitment to electric reliability by authorizing 
increased funding for vegetation management along with a program to address impacts caused 
by the Emerald Ash Borer and other invasive species. 
Additionally, in compliance with the Commission’s Orders in Case 20-E-0197, 
the Company has identified and received funding for Phase 1 electric transmission/distribution 
projects that will enhance system reliability while unbottling transmission system constraints. 
 
Economic Development 
 
  Economic development programs will be implemented  to focus on the 
expansion of manufacturing in the Hudson Valley: 1) Building and Infrastructure Program which 
will provide (a) monetary awards up to $200,000 per site to owners of undeveloped sites to be 
used for utility infrastructure improvements, or (b) varying monetary awards based on specific 
facts and certain criteria regarding vacant or severely underutilized properties to be used for site 
development needs; 2) Manufacturing Productivity Program which will provide matching grants 
of up to the lesser of $15,000 or 40% of the costs incurred by eligible applicants whose top 
management commits to “lean manufacturing” projects or manufacturing assistance projects that

adopted.

B. Environmental Sustainability Efforts

Company support for subscription community distribution generation projects through its Clean 
Energy Marketplace; continuation of NPA project development; and the continuation of natural 
gas energy efficiency rebates, but discontinuation of rebates for oil-to-natural gas conversions.

policies, the CLCPA, and established Commission objectives.  Accordingly, these provisions of 
the JP are reasonable, in the public interest, and should be adopted.

XXI. SALES TAX REFUND 
On March 16, 2020, Central Hudson filed a notice to the Commission for the 
disposition of a sales tax refund (Refund) totaling approximately $3.4 million from the New 
York State Department of Tax & Finance (DTF), as required under New York State’s 
Compilation of Rules and Regulations, 16 NYCRR §89.3, along with a proposed disposition of 
the refund, as called for under PSL §113(2).30  The Company recorded $1.9 million of the refund 
to capital and deferred regulatory accounts on its books, which provided immediate benefit to 
ratepayers.  The Company paid approximately $0.8 million in costs to achieve to a consultant 
and requested to retain the remaining approximately $0.6 million for its efforts in achieving the 
refund as an incentive to engage in tax refund pursuits. 
In its testimony, Staff recommended addressing the Refund jointly with the rate 
proceedings for efficiency (Staff Accounting Panel, pp. 69-70).  Staff testimony discussed Staff’s 
examination of the Refund and findings of several other sales tax refunds (Other Refunds) dating 
back to 2018 that Central Hudson had not noticed in accordance with 16 NYCRR §89.3 and PSL 
§113(2) and had not fully remitted to its customers (Staff Accounting Panel, pp. 70-72).  Staff 
found that the time and effort invested by Central Hudson to engage a consultant to obtain a 
refund, or to obtain a refund on its own appeared relatively routine and was unpersuaded by 
Central Hudson’s arguments on why it should obtain a portion of the Refund as an incentive 
(Staff Accounting Panel, pp. 72-74).  Staff testimony agreed that any amounts of the Refund or 
Other Refunds credited to capital of deferred regulatory accounts provided a ratepayer benefit 
but recommended that the expense portion of each of the Refund and Other Refunds, totaling 
$1.2 million, be accounted for as a regulatory liability account for ratepayer benefit and added to 
the available rate moderators in this proceeding (Staff Accounting Panel, pp. 74-76).

CONCLUSION 
The JP represents a carefully balanced, collaborative agreement among multiple 
parties that provides the Company with an opportunity to invest in new equipment, update aging 
infrastructure and deploy new information and electric grid technology to modernize the 
Company’s systems so it can continue to deliver electricity and natural gas to its customers in a 
reliable and safe manner at just and reasonable rates.  The JP also reflects Pandemic 
considerations, is aligned with and furthers the goals of the CLCPA and other New York State 
goals and Commission policies and produces a result that is rational and well within the range of 
reasonable outcomes had the proceeding been fully litigated.  The adoption of the JP is in the 
public interest and it is recommended it be adopted in its entirety.
