INFRASTRUCTURE ENHANCEMENT 
19 
Q. 
What is infrastructure enhancement?  
20 
A. 
Infrastructure enhancement concerns the 
21 
Company’s efforts to remove leak prone pipe

(LPP).  
23 
Q. 
What is leak prone pipe?  
24 


--- Page 18 ---
Case 21-G-0394  
Staff Gas Pipeline Safety Panel 
 
 
17 
 
A. 
Generally, leak prone pipe is unprotected steel 
1 
pipe, cast iron pipe, wrought iron, and some 
2 
vintage plastic pipe that may become brittle.  
3 
Q. 
What does unprotected mean in this context?  
4 
A. 
For purposes of this measure, unprotected means 
5 
that the pipe lacks adequate cathodic protection 
6 
rendering it susceptible to corrosion.  It is 
7 
also commonly referred to as bare steel pipe. 
8 
Cathodic protection is a method by which steel 
9 
pipelines are protected from corrosion by making 
10 
the surface a cathode, thereby inhibiting the 
11 
formation of iron oxides which can result in 
12 
reduced wall thickness of the pipe.  Unprotected 
13 
pipe often has no surface coating or inadequate 
14 
coating, rendering efforts to cathodically 
15 
protect the pipe ineffective and uneconomical.   
16 
Q. 
How does the leak prone pipe removal program 
17 
address the safety of the Company’s gas system?  
18 
A. 
Leaks on underground piping can create safety 
19 
risks to the public and can potentially lead to 
20 
gas-related incidents, such as fires or more 
21 
serious events that could impact health and 
22 
safety.  Leak prone pipe generally leaks at a 
23 
higher rate than coated and cathodically 
24 


--- Page 19 ---
Case 21-G-0394  
Staff Gas Pipeline Safety Panel 
 
 
18 
 
protected steel or plastic pipes.  The removal 
1 
of leak prone pipe reduces these safety risks.  
2 
Q. 
Explain the importance of removing unprotected 
3 
or bare steel and cast-iron pipe.  
4 
A. 
Unprotected or bare steel pipes are more prone 
5 
to leakage because they are more susceptible to 
6 
corrosion.  Removal of these pipes reduces risks 
7 
associated with leakage.  Currently, Corning 
8 
does not have any cast iron pipe in its system.  
9 
Q. 
What are other benefits associated with removing 
10 
leak prone pipe?  
11 
A. 
The removal of leak prone pipe should also 
12 
reduce the number of active leaks, lead to a 
13 
decline in leakage rates on the distribution 
14 
system and reduce overtime and operating and 
15 
maintenance (O&M) costs associated with 
16 
responding to leak calls and monitoring leaks.  
17
