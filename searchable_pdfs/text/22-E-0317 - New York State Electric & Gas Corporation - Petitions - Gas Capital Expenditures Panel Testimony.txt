1 
Q. 
Please provide an overview of the Panel’s testimony. 
2 
A. 
The Companies are committed to providing a safe and reliable gas system for their 
3 
customers, the public, and the employees and contractors of the Companies while at the 
4 
same time taking steps to mitigate potential adverse impacts to the environment.  As such 
5 
the Companies’ gas capital expenditure plan includes necessary investments to meet the 
6 
following key objectives:  1) continue to maintain safe, reliable, and secure gas systems; 
7 
2) reduce greenhouse gas (“GHG”) emissions and sustain the environment consistent 
8

10 
needs of the municipalities which the Companies serve; 4) enhance service reliability,

and facilities based on condition and obsolescence, after consideration of Non-Pipes 
13 
Alternatives (“NPAs”); and 6) improve the gas system’s effectiveness and efficiency.

Companies’ gas capital investment plans; 2) introduce and define the project and program

and 3) highlight major projects and programs included in the Companies’ gas capital 
18 
plans under each category.  The Panel also supports the need for 11 full time equivalents

(“FTEs”) to perform engineering designs operations currently performed by an outside

V.    GAS CAPITAL PLAN 
6 
Q. 
What is the Companies’ outlook regarding long-term gas investment needs? 
7 
A. 
The Companies continue to focus on system needs and the Companies’ commitment to 
8 
continue to provide safe, secure, reliable, and affordable service to customers.  
9 
Additionally, the Companies are committed to reducing GHG emissions and working 
10 
towards helping New York State achieve its climate goals.  The Companies will continue 
11 
to proactively replace aged infrastructure through the Leak Prone Main (“LPM”) 
12 
Replacement Program, which strategically identifies and ranks LPM segments though a 
13 
risk-based assessment in accordance with the Companies’ Distribution Integrity 
14 
Management Program.  By targeting the pipe segments that are the most likely to leak, 
15 
the Companies are proactively addressing potential future methane emissions.  Through 
16 
their decades-long strategy, the Companies have replaced nearly all of their cast 
17 
iron/wrought iron pipe and a significant portion of bare steel main.  Due to its material 
18 
properties, cast iron pipe is the most likely to experience fractures and cracking, causing 
19 
significant breaks and leaks when it fails.  The Companies have long been proactively 
20 
replacing cast iron pipe and thus curbing methane emissions long before the passage of 
21 
the CLCPA.  The Companies are proposing modest reductions in annual LPM 
22 
replacement targets to mitigate cost impacts on customers.  As discussed further in this 
23

1 
cast iron/wrought iron and bare steel main by 2030.  The Companies are also assessing 
2 
the condition of gate and regulator stations and have proposed replacements which will 
3 
modernize these critical pieces of infrastructure, thereby increasing resilience and helping 
4 
to mitigate climate impacts by reducing methane emissions.
