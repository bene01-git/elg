Q. 
HAVE YOU IDENTIFIED ANY ISSUES WITH THIS APPROACH? 
1 
A. 
Yes.  There is a tension that exists between rate structures that support the economics of 
2 
energy efficiency and rate structures that support electrification objectives.  Energy 
3 
efficiency reduces loads.  To support energy efficiency investments, it is desirable to have 
4 
relatively higher usage-based charges and relatively lower fixed customer charges.  On the 
5 
other hand, electrification increases loads.  To support investments in electrification 
6 
measures, it is desirable to have relatively higher monthly fixed charges and relatively 
7 
lower usage-based charges.  If customer charge under-collections are collected on a usage 
8 
basis and revenue neutrality is maintained, customers adopting electrification measures, 
9 
who are likely to have above-average consumption, will pay more of the under-collection 
10 
and will subsidize remaining customers.  In summary, the customer charge strategy for a 
11 
rate structure promoting energy efficiency is the opposite of the customer charge strategy 
12 
needed for a rate structure promoting electrification. 
13 
Q.  
CAN YOU PROVIDE AN EXAMPLE OF A RATE DESIGN THAT IS 
14 
SUPPORTIVE OF ELECTRIFICATION? 
15 
A. 
Yes.  The Company’s SC1 Rate Schedule IV, currently applicable to geothermal heat 
16 
pumps, is an example of such a rate.  On page 30 of Con Edison’s Electric Rate Panel’s 
17 
pre-filed direct testimony the panel states that the customer charge for SC1 Rate IV is set 
18 
based on the embedded customer cost level excluding billing and payment processing.  
19 
This reduces the revenue that must be collected from demand charges which improves heat 
20 
pump economics.

SEASONAL RATE STUDY  
5 
Q. 
WHAT POSITION DOES UIU TAKE WITH RESPECT TO THE COMPANY’S 
6 
SEASONAL RATE STUDY?  
7 
A. 
The UIU Policy and Rate Panel recommends that the Company update and refine its 
8 
seasonal rate study to help the Commission and other interested parties better understand 
9 
what rate design changes, if any, might be appropriate to accurately reflect evolving 
10 
seasonal peak load patterns to help evaluate what improvements can be made in the price 
11 
signals embedded in the Company’s tariffs with respect to the cost of using alternative 
12 
energy sources for winter heating.  UIU states that ideally, the updated study would provide 
13 
better insight into the impact of heating electrification on customers (if any) who are served 
14 
by substations where winter peak usage is already approaching the summer peak. 
15 
Q. 
WOULD YOU MAKE ANY CHANGES TO THIS RECOMMENDATION?  
16 
A. 
While I agree that that the seasonal rates study should be updated to provide better insight 
17 
into the impact of heating electrification on customers, I recommend that proposed focus 
18 
should not be limited to customers who are served by substations where winter peak usage 
19 
is already approaching the summer peak.
