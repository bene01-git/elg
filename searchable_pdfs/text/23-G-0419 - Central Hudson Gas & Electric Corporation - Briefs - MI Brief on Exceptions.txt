IV. 
REVENUES 
The Recommended Decision proposes to increase the Company’s electric and gas delivery 
revenue requirements by approximately $75.45 million (16.7 percent) and $29.56 million (21.8 
percent), respectively.3  Notwithstanding that these recommendations are less than the amounts 
that Central Hudson requested, they remain excessive, contrary to the public interest, and would 
harm customers of all types as well as ongoing economic attraction and retention efforts in the 
Company’s service territory. 
Multiple Intervenors explained in its Initial Brief that every request for rate relief 
necessarily requires careful distinction between “wants” and “needs.”  Central Hudson’s rate filing 
exemplifies the growing challenge before the Commission as to how it can balance energy 
affordability with investments in system reliability and decarbonization.  This challenge is 
daunting, given that traditional annual investments for system reliability can be substantial, utility 
bills already reflect material costs to pursue the Climate Leadership and Community Protection 
Act (“CLCPA”) objectives, and that the potential magnitude of the as-yet-unknown – but 
undoubtedly enormous – costs to achieve the CLCPA targets continuing to accumulate in rates.   
There is an urgent and compelling need to support energy affordability for all customers 
by moderating the electric and gas delivery rate increases recommended in this proceeding.  
Multiple Intervenors proposed several opportunities to do so in its Initial Brief, and some of those 
recommendations are reflected in the Recommended Decision whereas others are not.  Whether or

and,29 apparently, continued in successive Central Hudson rate proceedings.
