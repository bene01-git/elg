I. 
Introduction 
Niagara Mohawk Power Corporation d/b/a National Grid (“Niagara Mohawk” or the 
“Company”) petitions the New York Public Service Commission (the “Commission”) for a 
modification/adjustment to the billing loading factor requirements established in Appendix 16, 
Section 2.5 of the Joint Proposal approved by the Commission in Cases 20-E-0380 and 20-G-0381 
to allow for quarterly, as opposed to semi-annual, adjustments (the “Proposed Modification”).1  
The Proposed Modification is characterized as a “modifications/adjustment” because it involves 
limited modification to an appendix to the Joint Proposal adopted by the 2022 Rate Order and is 
required to implement the process improvement-based change to the burden clearing process in 
the SAP financial system.

II. 
Background 
The miscellaneous corporate provision set forth in Appendix 16, Section 2.5 Adjustments 
to Billing Loading Factors of the Joint Proposal limits the number of times Niagara Mohawk may 
adjust its third-party job order billing loading factors to no more than twice a year (in April and 
September).  The provision further requires Niagara Mohawk to include any adjustment necessary 
to reconcile the loading factors used during the prior period to actual amounts in the April 
adjustment.  Lastly, Niagara Mohawk must follow the same method for capitalizing fringe benefits 
into its construction costs, subject to Section 1 of Appendix 9, Electric and Gas Pension and OPEB 
Expense Provisions. 
In December 2021, the Company implemented a process improvement-based change to the 
burden clearing process in the SAP financial system. The new automated process allows the 
Company to clear the burden loading rate differences between forecasted amounts applied by the 
loading factors and actual incurred costs.  The Company ran this automated process again in March 
2022, as this is a semi-annual process performed for both calendar and fiscal year-end.  The long-
term intent is to run the process and make the resultant adjustments on a quarterly basis.   
The new automated process, however, occurs more frequently than the semi-annual process 
proscribed in Section 2.5 of the miscellaneous corporate provisions described in Appendix 16 of 
the Joint Proposal.  As a result, the loading factors being applied to third-party job billings are 
updated more than twice per year.  Because the new automated process provides significant 
benefits in the form of improved accounting, timing, and accuracy and the elimination of a labor-
intensive manual process, the Company requests a modification of the semi-annual adjustment 
provided in Appendix 16, Section 2.5 to allow the automated burden clearing process to be 
conducted on a quarterly basis.

III. 
Discussion 
The new automated burden clearing process provides several benefits and advantages over 
the replaced manual process and the restrictions imposed by the provisions contained in Section 
2.5 including timelier recognition of changes resulting from differences between forecasted 
loading rates and the actual costs incurred. The increased frequency of the adjustments improves 
capital/project forecasting and budgeting.  Likewise, the quarterly updates are anticipated to save 
an estimated 160 hours per annum for Service Company Accounting department personnel and 80 
hours per annum in system requirements.  In addition, the automated process obviates the need for 
manually addressing approximately 40 semi-annual and 30 annual journal entries.  Lastly, the new 
process provides the ability to better match the cleared burden amounts with the actual work 
performed as the automated process can clear the burden residual amounts at the specific work 
order level rather than the higher-level department cost center level inherent in the manual process. 
As such, the Company believes the requested modification and adjustment to an automated 
quarterly burden clearing process will provide overall benefits for the Company and its customers. 
IV. 
Conclusion 
For the reasons set forth above, Niagara Mohawk respectfully requests that the 
Commission approve the petition described herein.
