II. 
SIR Program Overview 
1 
Q. 
Please provide a brief overview of the Company’s SIR program. 
2 
A. 
Niagara Mohawk’s SIR program includes activities in connection with the 
3 
management and remediation of environmentally contaminated sites.  
4 
Utilities’ obligations to remediate SIR sites arise from federal and state law, 
5 
including the federal US Comprehensive Environmental Response, 
6 
Compensation, and Liability Act, State Superfund laws, and various 
7 
environmental statutes, regulations, and orders (e.g., DEC Orders on 
8 
Consent or US Environmental Protection Agency (“EPA”) Administrative 
9 
Orders).  In its SIR Generic Order, the Commission noted that utilities are 
10 
required by law to incur SIR expenses and these costs should be treated as 
11 
normal costs of doing business:  
12 
The utilities are required by law to incur these expenses, and 
13 
they should therefore be treated as normal costs of doing 
14 
business in today’s society.  If the balance of equities creates 
15 
unfairness, it can best be resolved by other branches of State 
16 
government, using avenues such as enlarging the State 
17 
Superfund.  (pp. 13-14) 
18 
 
19 
In total, Niagara Mohawk has responsibility for 191 sites, including many 
20 
closed (or inactive) sites.  The open sites include 45 active former 
21 
Manufactured Gas Plant (“MGP”) sites, six active operating sites, and ten 
22 
active sites owned by third parties where the Company shares some level of 
23 
responsibility or potentially responsible party exposure under applicable 
24

law.  The Company’s SIR program includes activities in connection with 
1 
the management, investigation, and remediation of MGP and Superfund 
2 
sites that have been contaminated by the release of substances from property 
3 
owned or formerly owned by the Company or its predecessors.  Of the 45 
4 
active MGP sites, 25 have been remediated but are subject to future 
5

Q. 
What types of costs does the Company incur under its SIR program? 
14 
A. 
Costs under the SIR program include associated consultant and contractor 
15 
costs, remediation activities aimed at reducing the volume, toxicity, or

mobility of pre-existing contamination, and incremental external costs, 
17 
including insurance and legal costs incurred to seek recovery from third 
18 
parties or otherwise to mitigate the Company’s costs or liabilities associated

with the SIR program.  Niagara Mohawk’s internal labor costs are not 
20 
included in SIR program costs.

Q. 
What roles do the DEC and other regulatory agencies play with regard 
1 
to the scope and timing of investigation and remediation work 
2 
conducted at MGP and Superfund sites? 
3 
A. 
The DEC and EPA control both the scope and timing of work at MGP and 
4 
Superfund sites.  The scope of Niagara Mohawk’s site investigations, work 
5 
plans, clean-up, and field work decisions are reviewed, approved, and/or 
6 
expanded by the DEC and the EPA pursuant to various orders.  An example 
7 
Order on Consent for MGP sites is provided in Exhibit __ (CFW-2).  
8 
Although Niagara Mohawk has limited control over the scope and timing 
9 
of SIR activities, the Company challenges the DEC and EPA when a more 
10 
cost-effective and equally protective remedy is available and, as described 
11 
later in my testimony, manages costs in the areas it can control, such as 
12 
contracting procedures.  Exhibit __ (CFW-3) provides examples of changes 
13 
to DEC remedy decisions following discussions with the Company that 
14 
resulted in significant cost savings to customers.   
15

Examples of Changes to the New York State Department of Environmental Conservation 
(“DEC”) Remedy Decisions/Cost Savings Following Discussions with the DEC 
 
Harbor Point MGP OU-1 (Utica, NY)  
Negotiated change to implementation of remedy that resulted in cost savings 
Because of the size and complexity of the remedy at this site, remedial construction has been 
completed in phases. One phase, known as the Polycyclic Aromatic Hydrocarbon Impacted Soil 
Removal project, contemplated the removal and disposal of over 100,000 tons of manufactured 
gas plant (“MGP”)-impacted soil for treatment and disposal.  A majority of the work was to be 
conducted in a wetland with a significant amount of water to treat and dispose at a cost of 
approximately $17.5 million.  The contractor, however, also presented an alternate approach to 
mitigate the significant amount of impacted water and the complex water treatment system.  The 
alternate considered utilizing in-situ soil stabilization (“ISS”) followed by excavation and 
disposal and removing the impacted groundwater treatment system (allowing a far simpler water 
treatment system).  The Company presented the alternate approach to the DEC for approval prior 
to proceeding to ensure the approach met the intent of the Record of Decision required remedy.  
The DEC approved the modified approach.  This phase was completed for $15.7 million. 
 
North Albany MGP (Albany, NY)  
Negotiated remedy change with the DEC, which resulted in cost savings 
During development of the feasibility study report (“FS Report”), the DEC initially 
recommended a remedy involving significant effort to address impacted soil in and around an 
active electrical substation (essentially decommissioning the substation at a cost of 
approximately $10 million) versus the remedy selected by the Company to address this issue.  
Following a series of meetings, the DEC agreed to the remedy presented by the Company in the 
FS Report (with minor modifications).  The cost of initial remedy recommended by the DEC was 
$31.8 million compared to $24.7 million for the remedy proposed by the Company. 
 
Oswego (West Utica St.) Non-Owned MGP (Oswego, NY) 
Negotiated Decision Document remedy change, which resulted in cost savings 
The March 2013 Decision Document submitted by the DEC required a soil excavation and 
offsite disposal remedy.  Following pre-design investigation activities, the Company identified a 
series of complications that impacted the feasibility of achieving the cleanup goals as well as

 negatively affected surrounding properties (causing concern for the structural stability of these 
third party properties).  The Company discussed an alternate remedial approach with the DEC 
and also completed a pilot test program utilizing ISS instead of soil excavation and disposal.  
The pilot test proved successful and the DEC amended the Decision Document in September 
2016.  The original remedy (soil excavation) was $7.2 million and the amended remedy is $5.4 
million.  
 
Syracuse (Erie Blvd.) MGP (Syracuse, NY) 
Negotiated remedy change with the DEC, which resulted in cost savings 
During development of the FS Report, the DEC initially recommended a remedy involving 
significant effort to address impacted soil, which would have also created significant disturbance 
to the use of this site as an office complex for Niagara Mohawk.  Following a series of meetings 
presenting the data collected and demonstration of the controls in place that protect human health 
and the environment, the DEC agreed with the remedy proposed by the Company in its FS 
Report.  The remedial technologies initially considered by the DEC indicated a range from $5.5 
million to $19.8 million in costs whereas the cost of the Company’s proposed remedy was $1.7 
million. 
 
Schenectady (Clinton St.) Non-Owned MGP (Schenectady, NY) 
Negotiated remedy change with the DEC, which resulted in cost savings 
The July 2013 Decision Document submitted by the DEC required a soil excavation and offsite 
disposal remedy.  Following pre-design investigation activities, the Company (in conjunction 
with the property owner) identified safety and logistical concerns for the implementation of the 
remedy.  The Company discussed an alternative remedial approach with the DEC and completed 
a pilot test program utilizing ISS as an alternative.  The pilot test proved successful and the DEC 
amended the Decision Document in 2019.  The original remedy (soil excavation) was $11.4 
million and the amended remedy was $10.4 million.  In addition, the ISS remedy treats more 
material thereby dramatically reducing future operation and maintenance obligations.

Site Investigation and Remediation (“SIR”) Cost Control Efforts 
Niagara Mohawk Power Corporation (the “Company”) follows the cost management practices 
set forth in the Inventory of Best Practices for Utility SIR Programs established in Case 11-M-
0034 and as clarified by the New York State Department of Environmental Conservation 
(“DEC”).  Examples of the Company’s SIR cost control efforts are discussed below. 
 
 
Experienced Staff and Contractors 
The Company staffs its SIR program with experienced project managers.  The project 
managers are responsible for, among other things, monitoring project activity, ensuring 
that work is performed in accordance with work plans and project specifications, and 
reviewing contractor invoices.  The Company also utilizes specialized environmental 
consultants and contractors to perform investigation, engineering design, remedial 
evaluation and construction, construction oversight, and operations and maintenance 
activities in accordance with the Company’s policies and accepted industry practices. 
 
Competitive Bidding Process 
SIR consulting work is initiated by a competitive bid process that starts with a project-
specific request for proposals (“RFPs”).  For smaller construction projects, routine site 
support work, and laboratory analyses at SIR sites, the Company uses Blanket Purchase 
Orders (“BPOs”).  BPOs are established through competitive bids.  Generally, RFPs are 
issued to three or more qualified vendors.   Proposals must meet specific conditions and

requirements, including a narrative description of each task to be completed, such as the 
installation of sampling wells or the production of reports or engineering drawings.  The 
narrative description must include the expected duration of each task, as well as its cost.  
Final vendor selection is based on specific criteria such as cost, technical merit, and 
personnel qualifications. 
 
Exceptions to these standard procedures are made in situations where it is necessary to 
maintain vendor consistency through multi-phased assessment/remediation projects or to 
implement an immediate or a short-term response to imminent hazards that leave no time 
for a competitive bid process.  When work is not competitively bid or performed under a 
BPO, the reasons for not competitively bidding are documented.  In these situations, 
whenever possible, the Company does not reveal to the contractor that it is preparing the 
only proposal for the work. 
 
When the size of a non-competitively bid project warrants, the Company may obtain an 
independent analysis of the proposal.  Any inconsistencies between the proposal and the 
independent analysis are then addressed with the proposal vendor.  If acceptable 
justification for these inconsistencies cannot be documented, the Company will evaluate a 
competitive bid process for the work if time permits or evaluate other alternatives. 
 
As an attachment to its proposal, the consultant or contractor must submit a spreadsheet 
that breaks down labor hours, subcontractor costs, and other direct costs for each task.

For lengthy projects, the SIR Project Manager and the vendor establish a project schedule 
or other progress tracking mechanism for project activities. 
 
For specialized environmental consulting services, the Company entered Master Services 
Agreements with five environmental consulting firms after a lengthy competitive 
sourcing effort.  Consultants were initially evaluated based on their qualifications.  The 
Company requested the consultants best qualified to perform the work to submit 
competitive rates.  The Company then negotiated Master Services Agreements with the 
five consulting firms that offered the best value for the services needed. 
 
Invoice Review 
Vendors must prepare detailed monthly invoices that include the following information: 
• A project description that meets applicable regulatory requirements and 
identifies the purchase order number and work authorization number. 
 
• The budget for each task, the amount invoiced for each task on the current 
invoice, the total amount invoiced to date, and the amount remaining in the 
purchase order or work authorization for completion of the task. 
 
• Backup to the invoice that contains a detailed breakdown of costs for each 
task must be attached to each monthly invoice. Required details include labor 
charges broken down by employee name, labor category, billable rate, current 
and cumulative hours worked on the task, current and cumulative dollars 
billed, and a breakdown of other direct charges. 
 
• For lengthy projects, the vendor must submit a monthly progress report that 
identifies, by task, the activities completed this period, the task budget, the 
amount expended during the current billing period, and the total amount 
expended to date. The vendor must also estimate the percent completion of 
each task.

appropriate, acceptance of all or a portion of the proposed increase, the contractor 
updates the spreadsheet with the approved change. 
 
Cost Effective Alternatives 
Although the Company has very limited control over the scope and timing of its SIR 
activities, it does manage costs in the areas it can control, such as contracting procedures, 
and challenges the DEC and the United States Environmental Protection Agency 
(“EPA”) when a more cost-effective and equally protective remedy is available.  The 
Company manages its SIR programs to eliminate risk to human health and the 
environment in a cost-effective manner, and works closely with the environmental 
regulatory agencies to accomplish this goal.  Examples of instances where the Company 
was successful in developing and receiving approval for more cost-effective alternatives 
than the DEC’s initial remedy decisions is provided in Exhibit __ (CFW-3). 
 
In addition, to ensure appropriate disposal of materials from all MGP sites, the Company 
uses only facilities approved by the National Grid Vendor Advisory Group, a cross-
functional group comprised of National Grid employees from the SIR, Environmental

Outreach Efforts 
The Company provides MGP site progress reports to the DEC on a quarterly basis 
(originally by-monthly but changed in 2019 pursuant to DEC request) and also provides a 
report to EPA on a monthly basis for the one MGP under the management of EPA.  The 
Company also participates in outreach to local communities and officials, citizen groups, 
and property owners, and established public websites for major MGP sites that contain 
information about the sites and the respective investigation and remediation activities.  
The Company strongly believes that purposeful community outreach efforts are critical to 
a successful remediation effort. 
 
Insurance Cost Recovery 
The Company attempts to reduce its SIR cost responsibility by seeking recoveries from 
insurers and other responsible third parties.  Efforts to recover outstanding insurance 
claims have involved a combination of litigation and settlement activities associated with 
the Company’s SIR sites.  In the early 1990s, the Company undertook a comprehensive 
effort to identify and review insurance policies to seek recovery of SIR costs from its 
insurers.  The Company provided notice to its insurance carriers regarding liabilities 
associated with its SIR sites.  The insurance carriers contested their obligations to

Identification of Other Potentially Responsible Parties 
The Company seeks aggressively to contribute only its equitable share of remedial costs.  
For example, the Company pursued and received a contribution by the New York State 
Electric and Gas Corporation (“NYSEG”) for the remediation costs of the former Albion 
MGP site consisting of two adjoining parcels, a 0.3-acre western parcel owned by the 
Company and a 0.2-acre parcel owned by NYSEG.  The Company uses a similar strategy 
to seek other responsible parties at a number of its former MGP sites.  The Company also 
works collaboratively, where possible, with property owners to conduct the construction 
phases of SIR projects during property redevelopment or construction activities to 
minimize the overall contribution of costs.  This approach provides opportunities for 
synergies that could yield cost savings for all parties.
