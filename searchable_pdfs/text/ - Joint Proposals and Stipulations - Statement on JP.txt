STATEMENT OF NATURAL RESOURCES DEFENSE COUNCIL 
 
The Natural Resources Defense Council (NRDC) respectfully submits this statement 
regarding the Joint Proposal (JP) filed with the New York Public Service Commission 
(Commission) on November 5, 2025, in the above-referenced proceedings. NRDC filed testimony 
in these proceedings and participated in the negotiations but is not a signatory to the JP. 
NRDC does not support the Joint Proposal. We are particularly concerned that the gas rate 
plan continues to reflect a business-as-usual approach to gas infrastructure. Maintaining this 
trajectory risks perpetuating unnecessary capital investment, inflating customer bills, embedding 
long-lived costs, and slowing the transition required under New York’s climate mandates. 
In particular, the continued emphasis on traditional pipe replacement exposes ratepayers to 
immediate upward rate pressures and significant stranded asset risk at a moment when lower‑cost, 
lower‑risk alternatives are both available and increasingly necessary. The record in this proceeding 
makes clear that targeted non‑pipeline alternatives, pipe‑integrity remediation strategies such as 
internal lining or in‑place reinforcement, network simplification, and strategic load reduction can 
be better utilized to address safety and reliability needs at considerably lower cost than 
conventional leak‑prone pipe replacement.  Failing to meaningfully integrate these approaches 
forgoes an important opportunity to strengthen affordability, improve system efficiency, and

advance a more orderly and data‑driven transition aligned with the State’s long‑term climate and 
energy goals. 
Despite these concerns, NRDC does not request that the Commission reject the Joint 
Proposal. We respectfully encourage the Commission and Department Staff to take up the 
shortcomings identified here—especially those related to gas affordability, the effective 
implementation of NPAs, and the improvement of NPA data transparency—in its proceedings, 
where sustained attention and corrective action can deliver meaningful and timely progress.
