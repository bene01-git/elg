2.2. Renewable Natural Gas 
1 
Q. 
Please describe Staff’s position on RNG as it relates to this case. 
2 
A.  
Despite acknowledging that RNG is “limited in the amount of energy that is available per 
3 
unit of time,” the Staff Gas Reliability Panel implies that it is “useful for Con Edison to 
4 
implement the interconnection facility for RNG sources within its service territory” 
5 
(Reliability Panel, p. 16). This is, in part, because Staff finds that RNG has “the potential 
6 
to reduce methane emissions” (Id. at p. 16-17).  
7 
Q.  
What does Staff recommend regarding RNG? 
8 
A. 
Staff recommends that, “[in] light of the emissions reductions required by the CLCPA 
9 
and the uncertainty associated with siting new pipeline projects,” the Company should 
10 
continue pursuing NPAs in areas of capacity constraints (Id. at p. 17).  
11 
Q.  
Do you agree with Staff about RNG’s potential to reduce methane emissions? 
12 
A.  
No. While at a small scale, certain types of RNG could provide emission reduction 
13 
benefits, no emissions benefits can be claimed if the environmental attributes are not 
14 
retained. As Con Edison is not proposing to retain or purchase the attributes associated 
15 
with the Mount Vernon RNG facility, this investment must be viewed as traditional and 
16 
should be subject to the recent Commission Gas Planning Order, requiring the Company 
17 
to evaluate NPAs alongside this investment.  
18 
Even if Con Edison had planned on retaining the environmental attributes, the concerns 
19 
we raised in our Direct Testimony regarding leakage, supply, air pollution, and cost 
20 
persist. While some types of RNG may offer methane emissions reductions compared to 
21

5 
Interconnection project as an NPA?

Q.  
In your opinion, is it appropriate to consider this project as an NPA? 
8 
A.  
No. According to the Order Instituting Proceeding in Case 20-G-0131, the purpose of 
9 
NPAs is to reduce or eliminate the need for gas infrastructure and investments. The 
10 
Mount Vernon RNG Interconnection project does not eliminate the need for gas 
11 
infrastructure—it is an investment in traditional gas infrastructure.  
12 
Q. 
What do you recommend? 
13 
A.  
We recommend that the Mount Vernon RNG Interconnection project not be treated as an 
14 
NPA. Also, as stated in our initial testimony, we recommend that the NPA screening 
15 
process should be reviewed and brought into line with the requirements set forth in the 
16 
CLCPA Implementation Order and the Gas Planning Order.

2.3. Certified Natural Gas 
1 
Q. 
Please describe Staff’s recommendations on Certified NG as it relates to this case. 
2 
A. 
The Staff Gas Reliability Panel makes several recommendations regarding Con Edison’s 
3 
Certified NG pilot program. First, Staff recommends that this program be subject to 
4 
similar conditions and reporting requirements as adopted in the 2022 order in the Orange 
5 
and Rockland (O&R) rate case (Reliability Panel, p. 18). Staff also makes 
6 
recommendations regarding the commodity cost to customers and administrative 
7 
procedures.  
8 
Q.  
Does Staff consider whether the Certified NG Pilot Program is necessary? 
9 
A. 
No. Staff seemingly assumes that this program has already been approved and does not 
10 
appear to consider if this pilot program is necessary or a prudent investment.  
11 
Q. 
Do you find that this program would represent an effective use of funds? 
12 
A.  
No. This pilot program should not be approved by the Commission. As described in our 
13 
Direct Testimony (p. 49-52), investing time and money in a program that is unlikely to 
14 
yield emissions reduction benefits could divert attention from the more important efforts 
15 
that Con Edison must take. In the context of Con Edison’s proposed increase in rates, it is 
16 
all the more important to avoid ineffective and wasteful spending. 
17 
Q. 
Does this conclude your direct testimony? 
18 
A.
