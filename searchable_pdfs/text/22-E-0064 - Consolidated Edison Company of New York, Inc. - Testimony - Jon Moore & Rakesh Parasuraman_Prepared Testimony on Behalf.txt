4

Q. 
DO RECENTLY-ADOPTED STATE AND CITY DECARBONIZATION ENERGY 
8 
POLICIES PLACE A BURDEN ON CPA MEMBERS? 
9 
A. 
CPA and its members support reasonable decarbonization efforts and recognize the health 
10 
benefits of reducing greenhouse (GHG) emissions. That said, decarbonization presents 
11 
unique challenges for CPA members, especially for hospital systems, the majority of which 
12 
are required to maintain reliable energy through a combination of grid-supplied electricity 
13 
supplemented with on-site thermal generation that relies on natural gas and fuel oil. The 
14 
challenge of electrification is outside of the scope of this testimony; however, one issue is 
15 
very clear, electrification of hospitals in New York City will be very challenging and, in 
16 
some places, impossible. This is driven, in part, by the high levels of redundancy needed 
17 
when operating lifesaving care equipment in a hospital setting.

INSTITUTIONS TO REMAIN IN NEW YORK CITY? 
1 
A. 
It is a misconception to believe that because hospitals do not relocate; their Biomedical 
2 
Research affiliates will not relocate or otherwise lose funding to institutions in other 
3 
regions. Biomedical Research is energy-intensive. Reduced energy rates have a two-
4 
pronged effect on this industry. First, the Biomedical Research set-aside in the BIR 
5 
program lowers operating costs that, in turn, increase the capital available to attract and 
6 
pay world-class research talent. World-class research talent attracts grant money that 
7 
generates economic growth. Second, lower energy costs specifically earmarked for 
8 
Biomedical Research incentivize these facilities to stay and expand, in New York City. 
9 
Without the benefit of reduced energy costs, these institutions may be less inclined to site 
10 
Biomedical Research facilities in the City and may decide to locate new research where 
11 
operating costs are lower.
