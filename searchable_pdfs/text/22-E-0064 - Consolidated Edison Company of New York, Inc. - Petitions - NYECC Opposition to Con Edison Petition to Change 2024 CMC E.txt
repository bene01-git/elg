Dear Secretary Phillips: 
 
Please consider this letter as the New York Energy Consumers Council’s (“NYECC”) letter in 
opposition to Consolidated Edison Company of New York, Inc.’s (“Con Edison” or “the 
Company”) Petition entitled as a “Petition to change the 2024 EAM baseline period for 
Commercial Managed Charging Program,” dated March 13, 2024, in the above referenced 
proceeding.1 
 
The NYECC and its predecessor organizations2 have represented the energy interests of 
commercial property owners and managers in New York City before State agencies for more 
than 70 years. NYECC also continues to represent hospitals, colleges, governmental agencies, 
cultural and financial institutions, housing cooperatives, and real estate organizations. NYECC's 
membership includes landmark member properties such as 7 World Trade Center and 
Rockefeller Center.

NYECC is a signatory to the Joint Proposal (“JP”) in this proceeding, which covers the three-
year Con Edison electric rate plan (as well as the gas rate plan in case number 22-G-0065), in 
effect from January 1, 2023 through December 31, 2025, inclusive. The Commission adopted the 
Joint Proposal in this proceeding with additional requirements.3  
 
Initially, it is worth noting that despite the name given by the Company for its Petition, namely, 
that it is a Petition to change the 2024 EAM baseline period for the Commercial Managed 
Charging Program, the Company is not only proposing to change the 2024 EAM baseline period. 
 
Significantly, the Company is also proposing to truncate the 9 month Enrollment Baseline 
Growth period previously agreed to in the JP (from January to September) to a 4 month period 
(from June to September). The elimination of 5 months from the agreed to 9 month period is 
elimination of more than 50% of the Enrollment Baseline Growth period. This is not an 
insignificant change because the reduction makes it easier for the Company to attain an Earnings 
Adjustment Mechanism (“EAM”). The Company essentially admits this in its Petition, but seeks 
to minimize this fact by claiming that “the shorter baseline period is roughly cancelled out by . . 
. a higher enrollment baseline starting point in absolute kW terms.” (Emphasis supplied). 
However, there is no assurance of any kind that this will actually and factually be the case either 
in substance or in degree. In fact, the Company’s Petition is replete with the usual caveats and 
estimates devoid of any guarantees that the EAM will be as much of a challenge for the 
Company to attain as what was previously agreed to among the rate case parties.4 In other words, 
Con Edison’s electric ratepayers and party signatories to the JP and to the Collaborative Report, 
filed on May 5, 2023, adopted by the Commission in this matter are entitled to know that they 
will receive a verifiable and equivalent benefit of the bargain for any changes in any replacement 
2024 EAM that the Company may earn and which will be paid for by ratepayers. The 
Company’s Petition falls short of such a demonstration and therefore NYECC respectfully 
requests that the Commission deny the Company’s Petition request to effect the requested 
changes to the 2024 Commercial Managed Charging EAM and to forego this EAM for 2024, but 
to have the agreed to EAM resume in 2025 on the timeline consistent with the timeline 
previously agreed to by the parties.
