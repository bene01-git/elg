Q. 
What is an ogive analysis? 
1 
A. 
An ogive analysis uses historic bill frequencies 
2 
to develop a cumulative percentage distribution 
3 
for usage levels within a rate code.  This 
4 
cumulative distribution is then used to allocate 
5 
the forecasted deliveries into the usage blocks 
6 
for each rate code, after which the deliveries 
7 
can be priced out at the applicable rate for the 
8 
particular block.  
9 
Q. 
Please explain how an ogive analysis is 
10 
performed. 
11 
A. 
The following calculations are required for an 
12 
ogive analysis: (1) scaling, (2) accumulation, 
13 
(3) interpolation, and (4) step volume 
14 
calculation.  The Company’s block reports, which 
15 
were provided in response to IRs DPS-541 and 
16 
DPS-1021, provide the data required for an ogive 
17 
analysis.  Block reports have three components: 
18 
steps, use per customer; customers; and volumes, 
19 
which shows how many customers were at a 
20 
specific step and how much gas they used at that 
21 
step. 
22 
Q. 
Please describe the scaling calculation. 
23 
A. 
The total volume from the sales forecast is 
24 


--- Page 19 ---
CASES 20-E-0380 & 20-G-0381 
 SEGRP  
 
 
18 
 
divided by the total volume from the block 
1 
report of historic data.  Then, the volume ratio 
2 
is multiplied by the volumes in each step of the 
3 
block report to scale the block report volumes 
4 
to the sales forecasted volumes.  The two 
5 
previous calculations are completed for both 
6 
steps and customers. 
7 
Q. 
Please describe the accumulation calculation. 
8 
A. 
For each step, the volumes and customer counts 
9 
from all steps prior to that step are summed. 
10 
Q. 
Please describe the interpolation calculation. 
11 
A. 
Since each step was scaled to the sales 
12 
forecast, interpolation is required to calculate 
13 
the volumes and customer counts associated with 
14 
each block.  Linear interpolation is a process 
15 
of estimating the value of an unknown data point 
16 
between two other known points.  The unknown 
17
