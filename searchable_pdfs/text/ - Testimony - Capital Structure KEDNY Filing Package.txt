II. 
Ratemaking Capital Structure 
1 
Q. 
What capital structure does KEDNY propose to use for ratemaking purposes in this 
2 
proceeding? 
3 
A. 
For a utility that is part of a holding company structure, New York State Public Service 
4 
Commission (“Commission”) precedent supports setting rates using the utility’s stand-
5 
alone capital structure where the utility can demonstrate that it is financially insulated from 
6 
its parent and its capital structure is reasonable and in line with its own financial and 
7 
business risks.  KEDNY is proposing for ratemaking purposes to use a capital structure 
8 
that contains 48 percent common equity for the Rate Year.  However, as shown in the table 
9 
above, should this proceeding result in a settlement where rates are established for multiple 
10 
years, KEDNY proposes that the capital structure should reflect 49 percent common equity 
11 
in Data Year 1 and 50 percent common equity in Data Years 2 and 3.   
12 
 
13 
KEDNY believes an increase in common equity in the Data Years is appropriate given the 
14 
interest of maintaining its credit ratings and achieving continued favorable financing for 
15 
the benefit of customers.  KEDNY’s cash flow metrics have been declining over the last 
16 
few years and it is important that the rates established in this proceeding ensure that those 
17 
metrics do not continue to decline further.  As discussed more fully below, the credit rating 
18 
agency Moody’s Investor Services, Inc. (“Moody’s”) has reviewed the Company’s 
19 
business and financial forecasts and does not envision cash flow generation to improve in 
20 
the medium term, citing the impact of the passage of the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017 
21 
(“Tax Reform Act”) and environmental remediation spending as two main contributing 
22 
factors.  Higher common equity will improve cash generation during a period when 
23 


--- Page 7 ---
 
Page 5 of 17 
 
Moody’s sees limited prospects for improvement, reducing risks of credit rating 
1 
downgrades.   
2 
Moody’s has recognized that changes in utility capital structures to reflect more equity 
3 
could act as an offset to negative cash flow impacts.  It is important that the rates established 
4 
in this proceeding ensure that KEDNY’s cash flow metrics do not decline to a level that 
5 
would not support its current A3 rating.

13 
in the “Generic Financing Proceeding” that is often referred to by the Commission in 
14 
determining the cost of capital in utility rate proceedings? 
15 
A. 
What is referred to as the “Generic Financing Proceeding” – Case 91-M-0509 – resulted in 
16 
a 1994 Recommended Decision that, although never formally adopted by the Commission, 
17 
has often been referenced by the Commission when determining the cost of capital to be 
18 
used in setting rates.  The Recommended Decision in the Generic Financing Proceeding 
19 
proposed that the “A” rating should continue to be the long-term target for utilities and that 
20 
the Commission should continue to offer utilities ratemaking support for an “A” rating.  
21 
The Commission recently reaffirmed the importance of strong credit ratings for utilities in 
22 


--- Page 10 ---
 
Page 8 of 17 
 
Case 17-M-0815, which considered the impact of the Tax Reform Act on utilities, finding 
1 
that “strong investment grade credit ratings benefit both utilities and their customers.”  
2 
KEDNY’s current ratings are in the “A” rating range, but any further downgrades would 
3 
move KEDNY into the “Baa/BBB” rating range.  KEDNY has limited headroom to 
4 
maintain an A3 rating under cashflow metric guidance released by Moody’s in April 2019, 
5 
highlighting the importance of credit enhancing provisions like a higher equity percentage 
6 
in the Company’s ratemaking capital structure.

9

10 
customers by lowering the overall rate of return charged to them.  The ability of KEDNY 
11 
to access the debt markets and/or obtain credit on favorable terms may also be affected if 
12 
KEDNY’s rating falls below the “A” range as certain debt investors do not invest in the 
13 
debt of non-A entities.
