1. Heating Electrification Make-Ready Program 
1 
Q.   
Please describe the Heating Electrification Make-Ready program proposal. 
2 
A.  
The Heating Electrification Make-Ready program would provide incentives to reduce the 
3 
costs of customer-side electrical upgrades required to (1) electrify space and/or water 
4 
heating in buildings, and (2) prepare these buildings’ electrical systems for full 
5 
electrification.20 The program is intended to complement, and to be fully integrated with, 
6 
its existing Clean Heat program, which provides incentives to increase adoption of electric 
7 
heat pump technologies. Eligible upgrades for the Heating Electrification Make-Ready 
8 
program would include new electric panels or sub-panels, wiring upgrades and other 
9 
behind the meter electrical upgrades. 
10 
Q.  
What is the cost of the program? 
11 
A.  
Con Edison is proposing to invest $76.6M over three years in the program: $14.2M in RY2, 
12 
$24.7M in RY2, and $37.7M in RY3. These costs are mostly for customer incentives. The 
13 
Company proposes to amortize the cost over 15 years so that the clean energy benefits 
14 
resulting from the program will accrue over the useful life of the electrical upgrades and 
15 
the operation of the supported heat pumps.   
16 
Q.   
Would the Heating Electrification Make-Ready program address an identified need? 
17 
A.  
Yes, the Heating Electrification Make-Ready program would address the cost of customer-
18 
side electrical upgrades that are not covered by the Clean Heat program.  Con Edison has 
19 
identified that customer-side electrical upgrades are a material cost barrier that is making 
20 
electrification infeasible for a significant percentage of the residential homes (defined as 
21 
1-4 family) and the multifamily and commercial buildings in its territory. 
22

Q. 
Is this cost barrier already addressed by its other energy efficiency programs? 
1 
A. 
No, the Clean Heat program incentives are applicable for electric heat pump technologies, 
2 
but not the customer-side electric upgrades necessary for the installation of those 
3 
technologies.  
4 
Q.  
Please explain how the program would address the cost barrier for residential homes. 
5 
A. 
Con Edison estimates that 10%-30% of existing 1-4 family homes lack capacity on their 
6 
existing electrical panel to connect electrical heating and other electric appliances replacing 
7 
fuel-using ones. The cost of these electrical upgrades can be cost-prohibitive or drive 
8 
customers to pursue only limited upgrades that would not support their anticipated future 
9 
electrification of additional appliances or EV chargers. The Company proposes to provide 
10 
these customers $700-$2,500 to replace their electrical circuit breaker panel or add an 
11 
entirely new sub-panel.  The actual amount of an incentive will depend on the level of the 
12 
clean heat upgrade with a maximum of $2,000 for a market-rate customer or $2,500 for an 
13 
LMI customer. Con Edison expects to support 8,000 homes between 2023 and 2025. 
 
14 
Q.  
Please explain how the program would address the cost barrier for multifamily and 
15 
commercial buildings. 
16 
A. 
In addition to similar cost barriers faced by residential homes, multifamily and commercial 
17 
buildings face additional challenges associated with building layout, heat pump design 
18 
system choices, and existing building conditions. These buildings also often need to 
19 
electrify in phases for a variety of reasons like equipment lifecycles and tenant lease terms.  
20 
Con Edison proposes to make these buildings eligible for incentives covering up to 70% 
21 
of total project costs. LMI-qualifying multifamily buildings would be eligible for
