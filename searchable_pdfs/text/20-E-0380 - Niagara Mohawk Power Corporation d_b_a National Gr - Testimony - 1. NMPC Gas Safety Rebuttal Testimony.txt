II. 
Gas Safety Measures Performance Targets and Incentives 
14 
A. Goals of Gas Safety Performance Targets and Incentives 
15 
Q. 
What is the purpose of gas safety metrics and performance targets? 
16 
A. 
The Company’s current rate plan establishes certain metrics and performance 
17 
targets to ensure that the Company meets its regulatory requirements to provide 
18 
safe and reliable service to customers.  The metrics measure the Company’s 
19 
performance across various activities, ranging from LPP removal to emergency 
20 
response, and are designed to ensure accountability to customers.  Metrics may 
21 


--- Page 5 ---
Rebuttal Testimony of Gas Safety Panel 
Page 3 of 32 
 
include positive revenue adjustments (“PRAs”) or negative revenue 
1 
adjustments (“NRAs”) if the Company’s performance varies from established 
2 
levels.  NRAs penalize the Company for underperformance, while PRAs incent 
3 
the Company to overachieve base expectations and deliver incremental 
4 
improvements for the benefit of customers.  Inclusion of corresponding 
5 
adjustments is important to drive performance and support public policy 
6 
objectives. 
7 
 
8 
Q. 
Please explain why inclusion of PRAs and incentives are appropriate. 
9 
A. 
PRAs do not simply reward the Company for basic operations or business as 
10 
usual activities.  PRAs incentivize the Company to perform additional work and 
11 
provide enhanced services, whether it be incremental leak repair or LPP 
12 
removal, that ultimately benefit customers and the community and further align 
13 
the Company’s performance with public policy objectives.  Appropriately 
14 
challenging PRAs support continuous improvement and superior performance 
15 
with the goal of exceeding customer expectations and industry standards. 
16 
 
17 
Q. 
Are metrics appropriate for a one-year rate plan? 
18 
A. 
No.  The gas safety performance metrics are designed to drive performance 
19 
improvements over time.  To that end, the Company’s proposals for performance 
20 
metrics contemplate a multi-year rate plan.  The Company does not agree with 
21

the inclusion of performance metrics in a one-year litigated case.  Indeed, the 
1 
Company submits that its consistently high level of performance should provide 
2 
the Commission with sufficient comfort that service will not be diminished in a 
3 
one-year rate plan.

how other utilities across New York State, including Consolidated Edison,

KEDNY, and KEDLI, have used such deferral balances.  In the 2017 Rate Case,

procurement functions.  In-progress engineering includes costs associated with responses to 
inquiries, site investigation efforts, other data gathering and analytical work, and the preparation

On page 38 of the Staff Pipeline Safety Panel direct testimony, the Panel indicated that it did
