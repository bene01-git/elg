What is the basis for the Companies’ city gate requirements?  
12 
A.

13 
Companies’ gas supply portfolio.  The primary firm demand is the demand from 
14 
the Companies’ firm customers, regardless of whether they purchase gas 
15

16

17

18

19

20 
do not incorporate any reserve margin assumptions when developing their design 
21 
weather forecasts and capacity requirement determinations.

Q. 
What is the role of underground storage in satisfying customer requirements? 
2 
A. 
Approximately 32 percent of the Companies’ normal winter supply obligation and 
3 
28 percent of their design day demand requirement are met by deliveries of gas 
4 
withdrawn from storage.  Under the Companies’ storage contracts, storage 
5 
deliverability typically declines as inventory decreases (known as “withdrawal 
6 
ratchets”).  Once reached, these ratchets cannot be reversed until the following year.  
7 
Therefore, the Companies establish a storage withdrawal plan prior to the winter 
8 
season to maintain inventories at levels that allow sufficient storage deliverability 
9 
to meet forecast winter peak conditions (storage rule curve).    
10 
 
11 
 
Market area storage provides the Companies with services that cannot be easily 
12 
duplicated with other assets.  The most important attribute of storage assets is 
13 
flexibility, which is vital in serving changing customer requirements.  The 
14 
Companies’ Transco storage service contracts provide end-of-day balancing that 
15 
minimize the risk of over/under run penalties.  Whereas typical supply purchases 
16 
must be dispatched ratably (i.e. at the same volume for each day of a weekend or 
17 


--- Page 15 ---
Testimony of Gas Supply Panel 
Page 13 of 45 
 
holiday), storage allows enhanced flexibility by allowing daily and intraday 
1 
adjustments to meet changing conditions.  In addition, storage improves the load 
2 
factor of flowing pipeline assets and is critical in meeting design weather 
3 
conditions.  This value cannot be duplicated by replacing storage with Marcellus 
4 
production.  The Companies utilize a least-cost dispatch to fill storage.  Where 
5 
Marcellus shale supplies present an opportunity to displace long-haul supplies for 
6 
storage refill, the Companies take advantage of the economically priced supply. 
7 
 
8 
The Companies also have approximately 20 billion cubic feet (“Bcf”) of Gulf Coast 
9 
storage capacity.  These storage fields are made available to ESCOs as part of the 
10 
optional Gulf Coast storage release that occurs every April.  Assuming no ESCOs 
11 
opt-in for the Gulf Coast storage release, approximately 4.5 Bcf of the total capacity 
12 
is reserved for force majeure purposes.  The remaining 15 Bcf of capacity, usually 
13 
the entire KEDNY Washington Storage Service (“WSS”) field, is utilized for off-
14 
system sales (“OSS”).  If all ESCOs opt-in, the capacity utilized for OSS would be 
15 
reduced accordingly.  Transco has received approval from FERC to proceed with 
16 
implementing market-based rates for these Gulf Coast storage fields which may 
17 
impact the Companies’ use of Gulf Coast storage capacity beginning in 2024.

What is the role of liquefied natural gas (“LNG”) in the Companies’ portfolio? 
20 
A. 
The Companies maintain two on-system LNG facilities in Greenpoint, Brooklyn 
21 
and Holtsville, Long Island.  The Greenpoint LNG facility allows KEDNY to store 
22 
approximately 1.6 Bcf of gas and currently has peak day vaporization capability of

approximately 291,000 dekatherms (Dth) per day.  The Holtsville LNG facility 
1 
allows KEDLI to store approximately 0.6 Bcf of gas and has vaporization capability 
2 
of 103,000 Dth/day.  Collectively, the Greenpoint and Holtsville LNG facilities 
3 
provide the Companies with approximately 13 percent of their peak day gas 
4 
supplies.   
5 
 
6 
LNG provides the Companies with on-system services that cannot be easily 
7 
duplicated with other assets.  Because these resources can be brought online 
8 
quickly, the LNG plants can be used to meet hourly fluctuations in demand, 
9 
maintain deliveries to customers, and balance pressures across portions of the 
10 
distribution system during periods of high demand.  Most importantly, these 
11 
resources are vital in preserving delivery pressures in the event that an off-system 
12 
resource becomes unavailable.

Q. 
Please describe the Companies’ gas purchasing process. 
4 
A. 
The Companies contract for quantities of gas to ensure sufficient supply to reliably 
5 
meet design conditions, as well as to account for daily and seasonal load variations.  
6 
Supply contract durations are generally limited to one year or less.  A combination 
7 
of term and spot contracts provides the necessary flexibility with respect to volume, 
8 
which allows the Companies to respond to fluctuations in demand. Term contracts 
9 
have durations of more than one month and, in general, have a specified fixed daily 
10 
base load quantity.  Most of these term contracts have base load quantities 
11 
necessary to satisfy requirements under a “warm winter” scenario and may include 
12 
some summer storage refill quantities.  Other term contracts provide call options to 
13 
buy bundled gas supplies delivered either to the Companies' city gates or to other 
14 
upstream supply points.   
15 
 
16 
The Companies also utilize no-notice storage to manage variations in load, which 
17 
provide the ability to automatically withdraw or inject gas at the end of the day to 
18 
balance system load.  
19 
 
20 
Spot purchases of gas supplies for a term of one month or less are made throughout 
21 
the year to supplement term supplies to meet system demand, manage demand 
22 
variations, and maintain planned storage inventory targets.  North American Energy 
23

Page 22 of 45 
 
Standards Board (“NAESB”) form standard contracts have been established with 
1 
several qualified and reliable gas suppliers, which spreads acquisition and 
2 
performance risk among many counterparties, thus improving reliability.  Soliciting 
3 
bids from multiple counterparties also affords more competitive pricing. 
4 
 
5 
The Companies plan to flow, within their available firm pipeline transportation 
6 
capacity, gas supplies purchased under firm contractual arrangements with varying 
7

VII. 
Gas Cost Volatility Management 
1 
Q. 
What steps do the Companies take to mitigate the impact of gas cost volatility 
2 
on their customers? 
3 
A. 
The Companies mitigate volatility in the gas commodity markets in several ways.  
4 
First, they maintain a balanced portfolio that includes contract storage.  This allows 
5 
the Companies to inject gas during the summer for withdrawal during the winter, 
6 
which enables the Companies to mitigate price volatility during the winter when 
7 
demand is greatest.  Second, the Companies maintain a geographically diverse gas 
8 
supply portfolio that helps to reduce exposure to volatility in any single supply 
9 
region.  Third, the portfolio incorporates pricing diversity that minimizes exposure 
10 
to volatility at a single pricing point or market index.  Finally, the Companies 
11 
mitigate price volatility with a formal hedging program. 
12 
 
13 
Q. 
Please describe the Companies’ hedging program.  
14 
A. 
The Companies’ overall pricing diversity and volatility mitigation plan is to protect 
15 
prices for approximately 50 percent, but no more than 60 percent, of forecast winter 
16 
firm sales and to allow prices to float with the market for the remaining forecast 
17 
winter sales.  For this purpose, the “winter period” is defined as November through 
18 
March.  Prices are fixed through a combination of planned storage withdrawals, 
19 
which provide a natural hedge at the average price of summer period injections, 
20 
and financial hedging using either NYMEX gas futures contracts or NYMEX Over 
21 
the Counter (“OTC”) financial settled swaps and options using bilateral master 
22 
agreements.   
23 


--- Page 40 ---
Testimony of Gas Supply Panel 
Page 38 of 45 
 
To determine the quantity to be hedged, the Companies forecast firm sales for each 
1 
month November through March, assuming normal winter weather conditions, and 
2 
calculate the storage withdrawals as a percent of firm sales (Step 1); the result is 
3 
deducted from 50 percent and that difference, when multiplied by firm sales (Step 
4 
2)  results in the quantity of gas to be financially hedged to achieve the 50 percent 
5 
target in each month.   
6 
 
7 
Once the volume of gas to be financially hedged each month is known, a monthly 
8 
hedging plan is created.  The purchases are spread evenly over 16 months starting 
9 
18 months prior to the start of each November.  Each financial transaction is settled 
10 
against the expiring month’s last day settlement price and the applicable Inside 
11 
FERC “First of the Month” index price.  The gross settlement payout or gain is 
12 
included in the gas cost for that month.
