The Brooklyn Union Gas Company d/b/a National Grid NY (“KEDNY”) and KeySpan Gas 
East Corporation d/b/a National Grid (“KEDLI”) (collectively, “National Grid” or “Companies”) 
hereby petition the New York Public Service Commission (“Commission”) for approval to establish 
alternative recovery mechanisms as described herein, which will align the Commission-authorized 
regulatory accounting mechanisms in the Companies’ current rate plans with Generally Accepted 
Accounting Principles (“GAAP”). 
As detailed below, the Commission approved the KEDNY and KEDLI current rate plans in 
its Order Adopting Terms of Joint Proposal and Establishing Electric and Gas Rate Plans issued 
August 12, 2021 (“Rate Order”) in Cases 19-G-0309 and 19-G-0310.1  The Rate Order allows the 
Companies to defer certain exogenous costs, which are defined to include, inter alia, all of the 
incremental effects on the Companies’ costs, revenues, or revenue requirements associated with or

In response to the PAUSE Order and the amendments to the PSL discussed above, in March 
2020, the Companies suspended field collections activities and stopped collecting the late payment 
fees and other fees associated with customer non-payment including, but not limited to, reconnection 
and no access fees (the “Unbilled Fees” or “Lost Revenues”). The Companies did not collect these 
fees through December 2021.  Following the expiration of protections under the Parker-Richardson 
Act, the Companies resumed assessing late payment charges in January 2022.  Other fees related to 
field collections (e.g., reconnect fees) are expected to remain suspended pending resumption of 
associated field collection activities. 
As described in the Companies’ December 16, 2021, Unbilled Fees/Lost Revenues Deferral 
Letters, KEDNY and KEDLI have met the minimum thresholds for deferral under the respective rate 
plan provisions for Rate Year One and have deferred, for regulatory purposes, the Unbilled Fees for 
the period April 1, 2020 through March 31, 2021.  
The deferral recognizes that the Companies’ rates assume an anticipated level of late payment 
and other fees to offset the cost of providing service to customers, and that the Companies have 
recovered less than the forecasted amounts.  As such, a fee deferral is essentially rate neutral to 
customers.  The Unbilled Fees/Lost Revenues Deferral Letters also explained that KEDNY and 
KEDLI’s deferrals of $12.840 million and $5.925 million, respectively, include offsets for related 
savings of $1.692 million and $0.089 million for KEDNY and KEDLI, respectively, consistent with 
DPS Staff guidance regarding COVID-19 deferrals.  Since the filing of the Unbilled Fees/Lost 
Revenues Deferral Letters, the Companies have made minor adjustments to the amount of deferrals 
and offsets for related savings, as set forth in Exhibit One attached hereto.

II. 
The Commission Should Approve KEDNY and KEDLI’s Proposed Alternative 
Recovery Mechanisms 
 
Although the Companies met the threshold to defer Unbilled Fees for Rate Year One and 
have recognized the deferral for regulatory accounting purposes, they are unable to do so under 
GAAP. Under GAAP, the Companies may only record the deferral on their income statements if the 
Companies will be recovering it within 24 months from the end of the annual period in which it was 
recognized.10  The Companies have recognized the Rate Year 1 deferrals for ratemaking purposes.  
In order to record the deferral on their GAAP financial statements, the Companies would need to 
recover the deferral by the end of March 2024.  However, the Companies current rate plans 
contemplate the Companies not filing for new rates prior to April 1, 2024, and thus, the Companies 
would not begin recovery until that time.  As such, the Companies’ current GAAP financial 
statements must omit the deferrals even though that is an unreasonable financial outcome and results 
in an unintended discrepancy with the Companies’ respective regulatory accounting.11 
To prevent GAAP from effectively undercutting the Commission-approved regulatory 
approach, KEDNY and KEDLI propose to establish a surcharge/surcredit recovery mechanism that 
permits full recovery/refund of any applicable deferred fees within GAAP’s 24-month recovery 
timeframe.  The proposed recovery mechanism will not only permit the Companies to recognize 
deferred fees in their GAAP financial statements, it will also modestly improve the Companies’ cash
