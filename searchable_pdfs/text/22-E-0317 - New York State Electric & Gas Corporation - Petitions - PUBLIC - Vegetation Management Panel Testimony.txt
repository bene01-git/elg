12

13

The Commission’s November 19, 2020, Order in the 2019 Rate Case (the “2020 Rate 
14

17

20

21 
reclamation programs, NYSEG performs whole circuit maintenance to include all line 
22 
spans within each circuit, which provides for cost-effective contracting.

Q. 
What are the reliability benefits of a five-year full-cycle program? 
7 
A. 
The benefits of our existing program were calculated by comparing the SAIFI 
8 
performance of each circuit before and after the year of trim, with the degradation 
9 
calculated based on the performance of circuits that were not trimmed in a similar period. 
10 
On average, the NYSEG system saw an 11.4% degradation on untrimmed circuits during 
11 
2018 – 2020.  Maintenance trimming during this period saw an improvement in 
12 
customers affected by trees of 6.5%, with a benefit adjusted for degradation of 16.2%.  
13 
The five-year cycle trim in Brewster during this period saw an improvement in customers 
14 
affected by trees of 24.3%, with a benefit adjusted for degradation of 31.4%.  The circuits 
15 
that were reclaimed saw an improvement of 47.0%, with a benefit adjusted for 
16 
degradation of 52.6%.  This analysis shows a clear and significant benefit of reclaiming 
17 
circuits and the effectiveness of both the maintenance and cycle trims to prevent 
18 
degradation and improve performance as it pertains to reliability.

VI. 
NYSEG AND RG&E TRANSMISSION VEGETATION MANAGEMENT 
1 
Q. 
Please provide an overview of the Companies’ current transmission line clearing 
2 
program. 
3 
A. 
The transmission line clearing program is set forth in the Companies’ Long-Range Right-
4 
of-Way Management Plan (also referred to as the Transmission Vegetation Management 
5 
Plan or “TVMP”).  The TVMP is designed to meet mandatory North American Electric 
6 
Reliability Corporation standards (i.e., FAC-003-4) and the Commission’s requirements 
7 
established in Case 04-E-0822.  Vegetation management is performed on all designated 
8 
transmission voltage lines 34.5kV and greater on a six-year cycle.  The year prior to the 
9 
plan year, NYSEG and RG&E transmission vegetation management foresters conduct a 
10 
span-by-span field assessment of the transmission cycle line work that will take place in 
11 
the plan year.  Transmission lines are annually aerial patrolled for priority 1, priority 2, 
12 
and hazard trees as defined in the Companies’ TVMP.  A ground patrol of all 345kV, 
13 
230kV and critical 115kV lines is done annually as well. 
14 
Reports that come in from the aerial patrols are field checked for the degree of 
15 
hazard and are prioritized based on that field assessment.  Priority 1 conditions are 
16 
scheduled as soon as possible for reliability and safety.  Conditions that warrant 
17 
immediate attention (i.e., hazard trees) that are discovered by the foresters in their field 
18 
assessments are scheduled quickly and the remainder are taken care of during routine 
19 
maintenance.
