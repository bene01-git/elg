Economic and Functional Obsolescence 
1 
Q. 
Please describe Economic Obsolescence (EO). 
2 
A. 
EO is “the loss of value of property caused by 
3 
an impairment in desirability or useful life 
4 
resulting from factors external to the 
5 
property,” as defined in title 20 of the New 
6 
York Code of Rules and Regulations, Part 8197-
7 
2.8(b). 
8 
Q. 
Provide an overview of the EO process and how it 
9 
effects property tax expense. 
10 
A. 
Utilities apply for an EO allowance by 
11 
submitting a standard calculation to the Office 
12 
of Real Property and Tax Services (ORPTS).  The 
13 
calculation compares the five-year average of 
14 
actual return on rate base to the allowed return 
15 
on rate base.  ORPTS then reviews the 
16 
calculation and makes a formal determination on 
17 
the EO awarded to utilities.  The EO allowance 
18 
is applied as a percentage reduction to a 
19 
utility’s assessed special franchise property, 
20 
which effectively reduces property tax expense. 
21 
Q. 
Please describe Functional Obsolescence (FO). 
22 
A. 
Functional Obsolescence is “the impairment of 
23 
operating capacity or efficiency resulting in a 
24 


--- Page 8 ---
Cases 16-G-0058 and 16-G-0059 
 
Staff Tax Panel 
 
 
7 
 
loss in value brought about by the failure of 
1 
the tangible property to meet present or 
2 
projected needs or where the capacity of the 
3 
tangible property exceeds reasonable anticipated 
4 
demands,” as defined in title 20 of the New York 
5 
Code of Rules and Regulations, Part 8197-2.8(c).  
6 
An FO allowance also applies a percentage that 
7 
reduces assessable values for special franchise 
8 
property, thus reducing property tax expense. 
9 
Q. 
Is there a standard for how FO is calculated? 
10 
A. 
No.  There is no specific standard for how to 
11 
calculate an FO allowance.  The utility would 
12 
have to make a filing to ORPTS to demonstrate 
13 
that a loss in value occurred.
