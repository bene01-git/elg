III. Introduction to New York State Battery Storage Policy 
1 
Q. Why is battery storage policy important in the context of this rate case? 
2 
A. As discussed below, New York State has a stated policy of encouraging battery storage in 
3 
support of its renewable energy obligations under state law. The rates paid by battery storage for 
4 
its charging load are a substantial driver of project economics and constructability. As a result, 
5 
understanding state policy drivers is important context for understanding rate implications.

11

15

rate and therefore their construction relies upon the same revenue requirements and billing 
17

VII. Bulk Storage Should be Exempt from Paying Rider and Adjustment Factors 
11 
Q. Earlier in the testimony there is a reference to riders and adjustment factors. Please describe 
12 
the purpose of rider and adjustment factors. 
13 
A. Riders and adjustment factors are retail charges that recover the costs of specific programs 
14 
and items designated for cost recovery outside of base rates. With the exception of the RDM and 
15 
Rate Adjustment Mechanism (“RAM”) charges, they are independent of base distribution rates.51 
16 
 
17 
Q. What makes bulk storage different from other “customers” on the grid with respect to riders 
18 
and adjustment factors? 
19 
A. A bulk storage project that participates in NYISO markets is not the final point of sale for the 
20 
energy used in charging. With the exception of losses, the energy used to charge a bulk storage 
21 
 
51 RAM Adjustment charges are to reconcile regulatory assets, deferrals, and variances in certain charges relative to 
the provisions in base rates. See New York PSC, Order Adopting Terms of Joint Proposal & Establishing Electric & 
Gas Rate Plan, Case No. 20-E-0428 at p. 22-23 (Nov. 18, 2021). 


--- Page 29 ---
 
 
29 
 
project will be subsequently discharged during a higher value time period for ultimate final 
1 
consumption by other customers connected to the grid. In essence, it is a purchase for resale to 
2 
an eventual retail customer, who will be responsible for paying the rider charges. To charge a 
3 
bulk storage project for the rider costs while it is participating in wholesale markets and then to 
4 
charge the retail customer again is akin to charging twice on the same electrons. This electricity 
5 
in bulk storage systems is not being consumed for retail use, but rather stored by the technology 
6 
waiting to be dispatched back to the system at an economic period. Application of full retail 
7 
riders and other adjustment factor charges are not appropriate to this technology.  
8 
 
9 
Q. Is it appropriate for a WDS tariff to include riders and adjustment factors?
