Embedded Cost of Service Study (ECOS) 
10 
Q. 
Please describe the purpose of an ECOS study. 
11 
A. 
The purpose of an ECOS study is to assign costs 
12 
to different SCs based on how customers within 
13 
those SCs cause costs to be incurred.  The 
14 
results of an ECOS study are used as a guide in 
15 
allocating revenue responsibility between the 
16 
different SCs. 
17 
Q. 
What information does an ECOS study provide? 
18 
A. 
The ECOS study provides a total system rate of 
19 
return, the rate of return for each SC, and the 
20 
relative rate of return for each SC.  The rate 
21 
of return for an individual SC is an effective 
22 
way to measure how well the SC revenues align to 
23 
the costs to provide service to that particular 
24 


--- Page 17 ---
Case 22-E-0317 et al. 
Staff Gas Rates Panel 
 
 
16 
 
SC.  An SC’s relative rate of return is the SC’s 
1 
rate of return divided by the system rate of 
2 
return.   
3 
Q. 
Please explain the three major steps of 
4 
performing and ECOS study.  
5 
A. 
The three major steps of an ECOS study are: 
6 
functionalization, classification, and 
7 
allocation.  Functionalization is the process of 
8 
assigning the Companies’ rate base and expense 
9 
items to specific utility operating functions.  
10 
For gas, the functions are: Supply 
11 
Procurement/Merchant Function Charge, or MFC; 
12 
Transmission & Distribution, or Delivery; and, 
13 
Bill Issuance and Payment Processing, or BIPP.  
14 
Classification is used to further define the 
15 
functionalized rate base and expense items into 
16 
demand, energy, and customer components.  The 
17 
final step – allocation - allocates the 
18 
components to SCs reflective of the cost each SC 
19 
imposes on the utility.  
20

20 
value.  However, if the standard deviation 
21 
produces a lower dead band that is less than 
22 
zero, then the lower dead band is set at zero 
23
