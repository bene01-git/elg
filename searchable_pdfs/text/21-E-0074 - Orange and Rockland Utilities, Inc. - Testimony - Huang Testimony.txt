Depreciation Terms and Concepts 
20 
Q.  Please explain the terms “net salvage” and 
21 
“depreciation” as used in ratemaking. 
22 
A.  Net salvage is the value received for the asset, 
23 
less any expenses incurred to remove it once the 
24

-5- 
 
plant is no longer in service.  Depreciation is 
1 
a method of recovering capital costs, less net 
2 
salvage, related to plant-in-service over the 
3 
plant’s expected life.   
4 
Q. 
Please explain the term “ASL.” 
5 
A. 
ASL is the arithmetic average of the lives of 
6 
the units of property included in a given plant 
7 
account and is the area under a survivor curve 
8 
from age zero to the maximum age divided by 100 
9 
percent. 
10 
Q. 
Please explain the term “survivor curve.” 
11 
A. 
A survivor curve is a graphical representation 
12 
of the percentage of original plant that is 
13 
still in service as a function of age.  Known 
14 
curves, such as Iowa or h-curves, are fit to the 
15 
observed historical survivor data to help 
16 
determine an appropriate ASL.  These known 
17 
curves include a mode that describes the 
18 
relative height of the curve.  Low mode curves 
19 
have wide retirement patterns where assets 
20 
retire over a wide range of lives and few 
21 
retirements occur near the average, while high 
22 
mode curves are the opposite, with narrower 
23 
retirement patterns near the ASL.  The area 
24 


--- Page 7 ---
Cases 21-G-0073, et al. 
    
HUANG 
 
 
-6- 
 
under the survivor curve is the ASL.  The 
1 
observed historical survivor data is generated 
2 
from a mortality study that examines the actual 
3 
retirement patterns over multiple years of 
4 
plant-in-service by account.   
5 
Q. 
Please explain the term “depreciation expense.” 
6 
A. 
Depreciation expense is the amount collected, 
7 
typically annually, to recover the cost of an 
8 
investment, less net salvage, over the expected 
9 
useful life of an asset. 
10 
Q. 
Please explain the term “depreciation rate.” 
11 
A. 
A depreciation rate is a percentage rate that is 
12 
applied to the value of gross plant-in-service, 
13 
by account, to determine the annual depreciation 
14 
expense.   
15 
Q. 
How is the depreciation rate determined for a 
16 
given plant account? 
17 
A. 
The depreciation rate is a function of the ASL 
18 
and net salvage factor, and is calculated such 
19 
that the amount of depreciation expense 
20 
collected over the plant account’s service life 
21 
will recover the original cost of the plant, 
22 
including net salvage. 
23 
Q. 
Please explain the term “net salvage factor.” 
24 


--- Page 8 ---
Cases 21-G-0073, et al. 
    
HUANG 
 
 
-7- 
 
A. 
The net salvage factor refers to the component 
1 
of the depreciation rate that allows recovery of 
2 
estimated net salvage.  It is expressed as a 
3 
percentage based on the original cost of plant.  
4 
The net salvage factor can be positive or 
5 
negative, depending on the cost of removal and 
6 
the value that can be recovered from a retired 
7 
asset.  If the retired asset can be sold for 
8 
more than its cost of removal, then it can 
9 
reduce the original cost that needs to be 
10 
recovered through depreciation expense.  
11 
However, if the cost of removal is more than the 
12 
sale price of the retired asset, then it 
13 
increases the amount that needs to be collected 
14 
through depreciation expense.  For example, let 
15 
us assume that a unit of property has a total 
16 
installed cost of $1,000, $0 estimated future 
17 
salvage, and estimated cost of removal of $500.  
18 
The net salvage factor is calculated as follows: 
19 
Rate = ($0 + ($-500)) / $1000.  This equates to 
20 
a -0.50 or a negative 50 percent net salvage 
21 
factor. 
22 
Q. 
Please explain the term “book reserve.” 
23 
A. 
The book reserve is the accumulated depreciation 
24

-8- 
 
expense of the plant account, less any plant 
1 
retirements and cost of removal, plus any gross 
2 
salvage received.  The book reserve represents 
3 
the amount of the original plant cost that has 
4 
already been recovered through depreciation.  
5 
The net plant is the remaining plant balance 
6 
that has yet to be depreciated and is calculated 
7 
by subtracting the book reserve from the 
8 
original cost of the plant. 
9 
Q. 
Please explain the term “theoretical reserve.” 
10 
A. 
The theoretical reserve is the amount of 
11 
depreciation expense that should have been 
12 
collected at a particular date, if the proposed 
13 
survivor curves, ASLs and net salvage factors 
14 
were used throughout the entire life of the 
15 
plant account.  The difference between the book 
16 
reserve and the theoretical reserve indicates 
17 
whether the Company is recovering its gross 
18 
plant costs properly with the associated rates.  
19 
If the book reserve is smaller or greater than 
20 
the theoretical reserve, that represents an 
21 
under-collection or over-collection, 
22 
respectively.  Usually, if the absolute 
23 
difference between the book and theoretical

reserve, an amortization to recover or refund a 
2

3 
recommended to reduce the possibility of under-
4 
recovered/over-recovered costs once the plant 
5 
has been retired.

Q. 
Please briefly explain the straight-line method. 
17 
A. 
In the straight-line method, the original cost 
18 
of the plant is divided and recovered equally 
19 
over each year of service life.  
20 
Q. 
Please briefly explain the broad group ASL 
21 
procedure. 
22 
A. 
In the broad group ASL procedure, all assets 
23 
belonging to a given plant account are 
24

considered as a single group regardless of 
1 
installation date, and a single ASL is used for 
2 
all assets collectively.   
3 
Q.  Please explain how the depreciation rate is 
4 
calculated using the whole life technique. 
5 
A.  After determining each account’s proposed ASLs 
6 
and net salvage factors, the Company used the 
7 
following equation to calculate depreciation 
8 
rates for each account:  Rate = (100 percent - 
9 
net salvage factor) / (ASL).  For example, if 
10 
the net salvage factor is negative 50 percent 
11 
and the ASL is 50 years, then the depreciation 
12 
rate would be: Rate = (100% – (-50%)) / 50 years 
13 
= 3%/year.  The depreciation rate would equal 
14 
three percent for this example plant account.
