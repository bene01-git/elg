PRELIMINARY STATEMENT 
 
On April 9, 2024, a Joint Proposal containing recommendations for new gas rate plans was 
filed in the above-referenced proceedings with the New York State Public Service Commission 
(“Commission”).  On May 20, 2024, an evidentiary hearing (“Hearing”) on the Joint Proposal was 
conducted by Administrative Law Judges (“ALJs”) Maureen Leary and James Costello.  Pursuant 
to the ALJs’ ruling at the Hearing, DPS Staff and WE ACT for Environmental Justice (“WE ACT”) 
filed post-hearing briefs on June 3, 2024.  WE ACT’s brief, as well as its general opposition to the 
Joint Proposal, focused in part on the proposed biomethane interconnection projects addressed in 
Section 7.8 of the Joint Proposal and the operation of the biomethane interconnection project at 
the Newtown Creek Wastewater Resource Recovery Facility (“Newtown Creek WRRF”).   
Contrary to WE ACT’s assertions at the Hearing and in its brief, the biomethane 
interconnection project at the Newtown Creek WRRF and a proposed biomethane interconnection 
project at the Jamaica Wastewater Resource Recovery Facility promote the goals of the Climate 
Leadership Community Protection Act (“CLCPA”) and the Climate Action Council’s Final 
Scoping Plan (“Final Scoping Plan”) by reducing net emissions of necessary operations at New

POINT II 
 
THE BIOMETHANE PROJECTS UNDERLINE THE NEED 
FOR AN IN-STATE ATTRIBUTE MARKET 
 
WE ACT argues that the biomethane interconnection projects cannot be considered in 
compliance with the CLCPA because National Grid will not be buying the associated 
environmental attribute credits.12  The City recognizes the concern with the credits being sold out 
of state.  This, in itself, should not prevent these projects from moving forward.  To the contrary, 
this only underscores the need to develop an in-state market for these environmental attributes so 
the environmental benefits of the projects can be recognized locally.   
Further to this effort, the Joint Proposal provides a strategy to keep the environmental 
benefits local.  Specifically, the Joint Proposal requires KEDNY senior staff to meet with senior 
staff at Con Edison and other hard-to-electrify customers in New York City to discuss the potential 
purchase and sale of biomethane and associated environmental attributes.13  The City strongly 
recommends that the Commission approve the Joint Proposal so that the State can take this first 
step to developing an in-state market for the environmental attributes from biomethane projects.

POINT III 
 
WE ACT’S CONCERNS ABOUT LEAKAGE FROM 
BIOMETHANE PRODUCTION ARE OVERSTATED 
 
WE ACT is concerned that National Grid has not provided an estimate of the leakage 
resulting from transporting the biomethane from the production facility to the distribution system 
at each interconnection site.14  National Grid provided the leakage rates associated with the 
facilities that are currently installed and would be used to transport renewable natural gas from the 
production facility to National Grid’s distribution systems.  The rates per mile for both 2022 and 
2023 were minimal.15  As such, there is no material impact from leakage and the leakage rates help 
confirm that biomethane production will result in a net emissions reduction. 
Moreover, for the biomethane projects co-located at existing wastewater resource recovery 
facilities, WE ACT overlooks the nature of the highly dense urban environment of New York City.  
The point of production for the biomethane will, in all likelihood, be close to the point of 
consumption.  As the biomethane is not transported far, the concern of leakage during transport is 
significantly lower than concerns with gas leaks in pipelines that transport upstream supply over 
long distances.

ACHIEVED THROUGH THE JOINT PROPOSAL

that “[a] full accounting of the interconnections’ net greenhouse gas emissions is therefore

The City recognizes WE ACT’s concern and agrees that a full accounting of net GHG 
emissions from biomethane production is needed.  Contrary to WE ACT’s assertion, however, this 
accounting can be achieved, and the Joint Proposal provides a mechanism for National Grid to do 
so.  In particular, the Joint Proposal requires National Grid to file a report with the Commission at 
least 90 days prior to commencing construction of a biomethane interconnection project.  This 
report must include “a detailed accounting of the upstream GHG emissions avoided by the 
biomethane that the Companies will procure as a result of the interconnection.”17 
This reporting requirement provides an opportunity for National Grid to examine the full 
benefits from these projects.  To further improve upon the proposed framework, to the extent that 
it deems it necessary, the Commission could require National Grid to include, as part of the 
upstream GHG avoidance calculations, the emissions associated with producing the biomethane.18
