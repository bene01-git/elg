IN SUPPORT OF JOINT PROPOSAL 
The Brooklyn Union Gas Company d/b/a National Grid NY (“KEDNY”) and 
KeySpan Gas East Corporation d/b/a National Grid (“KEDLI”) (KEDNY and KEDLI 
collectively referred to as the “Companies”) hereby submit this Statement in Support of 
the Joint Proposal dated May 14, 2021 (“JP”) in the above captioned proceedings.   The JP 
is the product of more than two years of rate case proceedings and negotiations, and the 
parties supporting the JP represent a broad range of customer, environmental, and energy 
policy interests.  The JP provides funding to KEDNY and KEDLI to operate their gas 
distribution systems safely and reliably over the three-year term of the rate plans.   
However, the settlement package outlined in the JP is far from business as usual.  The JP

I. 
Introduction and Overview 
The JP sets forth a three-year rate plan for each of the Companies commencing 
April 1, 2020, and continuing through March 31, 2023.1  The JP also provides certain 
provisions that will apply during a Stayout Period2 if the Companies do not file revised 
base rates to be effective April 1, 2023.  While the JP establishes a rate plan for the period 
beginning April 1, 2020, the Companies anticipate an August 1, 2021 effective date of new 
rates.3 
The proposal is supported or not opposed by a significant number of parties with 
diverse interests.  Supporting parties include the Companies, the New York State

Balancing Stakeholder Interests  
While the Companies appreciate the JP does not fully satisfy the positions of all 
stakeholders on every issue, the Signatory Parties have undertaken to produce a settlement 
that reflects significant compromise and addresses the parties’ feedback and interests to the 
greatest extent possible.  Consider the following: 
• For the stakeholders that think the Companies should be doing more to encourage 
the transition to clean energy, the JP provides that the Companies will manage their 
business with the goal of reducing billed gas usage, not engage in gas marketing 
activities, terminate any gas conversion and other incentive programs, and work with 
various partners (e.g., electric utilities and trade organizations) to promote the adoption 
of geothermal and other alternate energy options.   
• For stakeholders questioning the need for on-system infrastructure projects to 
expand capacity, the JP provides the Companies will submit certain future capacity 
investments to an independent review and needs assessment.  The assessment will also 
include a public process that will provide ample opportunity for stakeholder input and 
comments.  Moreover, the Companies have agreed to condition recovery of these 
projects on their ability to deliver various non-infrastructure solutions, including energy 
efficiency, demand response, and electrification targets.  The Companies are also

Guidelines.27

results that were within the range of reasonable results that would likely have 
arisen from a Commission decision in a litigated proceeding. 
• In judging the settlement, the Commission shall give weight to the fact that a 
settlement reflects agreement by normally adversarial parties.28

Commission-established targets for activities that ultimately will reduce demand for gas.

State’s environmental goals and would likely be impossible to obtain through litigation.

Term Capital Capacity Projects provides a compelling basis for the Commission to approve
