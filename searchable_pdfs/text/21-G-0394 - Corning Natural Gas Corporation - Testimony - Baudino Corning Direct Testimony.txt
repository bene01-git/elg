A. 
An ECOSS illustrates the costs a utility incurs to serve each customer class.  It is 
1 
widely accepted that costs should be allocated among customer classes on the basis of 
2 
cost causation.  That principle is generally applied to costs that cannot be directly 
3 
assigned to a particular customer class.  As such, costs should be allocated to those 
4 
classes on the basis of how or why those costs are incurred by the utility.  The results 
5 
of such studies are normally relied upon in assigning cost and revenue responsibilities 
6 
to various customer classes. 
7 
Q. 
DO YOU SUPPORT THE PREMISE THAT COST CAUSATION PRINCIPLES 
8 
SHOULD GUIDE THE ALLOCATION OF COSTS TO THE VARIOUS 
9 
CUSTOMER CLASSES? 
10 
A. 
Yes.  Rates that are based on consistently applied cost causation principles are fair and 
11 
reasonable and further the causes of stability, conservation and economic efficiency.  
12 
Other factors such as simplicity, gradualism, economic development and ease of 
13 
administration may also be taken into consideration when determining the final 
14 
allocation of the revenue requirement among classes.  In addition, changes in ECOSS 
15 
methodology yielding disparate results, such as here, may cause parties to reconsider  
16 
overreliance on particular ECOSS results. 
17 
Q. 
PLEASE EXPLAIN THE PURPOSE AND DEVELOPMENT OF A CLASS 
18 
COST OF SERVICE STUDY. 
19 
A 
A class cost of service study allocates and assigns the total cost of providing utility 
20 
service to the classes of customers receiving that service.  The development of a class 
21 
cost of service study consists of three steps: functionalization, classification, and 
22 
allocation.  Functionalization refers to the process by which the Company’s 
23 
investments and expenses are identified and segregated into different cost categories.  
24 


--- Page 5 ---
Case No. 21-G-0394 
 
Richard Baudino 
 
 
 
Page 4 
 
 
 
For CNG, these functional categories include transmission, distribution, metering and 
1 
billing, and various customer accounts and service expenses.     
2 
 
 
Once functionalization is complete, the utility’s costs are classified into 
3 
categories of demand-related, energy related and customer-related costs in order to 
4 
facilitate the allocation of costs by applying cost causation principles.  In general, all 
5 
distribution costs are either responsive to increases in natural gas demand or to the 
6 
number of customers.  Demand-related costs are those that are needed to serve the 
7 
winter peak demands of distribution customers.  Demand-related costs are fixed and 
8 
do not vary with the monthly and annual commodity consumption of the utility’s 
9 
customers.  Customer-related costs are associated with the number of customers and 
10 
are costs that are incurred to connect customers to the system independent of the 
11 
customer’s demand and energy requirements.  Primary examples of customer-related 
12 
costs are investments in meters, services, and a portion of main investment incurred to 
13 
extend the distribution system to the customer’s premises and conform to local and 
14 
national codes and standards.  In addition, such accounting functions as meter reading, 
15 
bill preparation and revenue accounting are generally considered customer-related 
16 
costs. 
17 
 
 
Lastly, the functionalized and classified costs are allocated to the various 
18 
customer classes based on each class’ contribution to the respective cost 
19 
classifications.  In general, demand-related costs are allocated based on each class’ 
20 
contribution to the maximum demand placed on the system by the classes or by the 
21 
customers within the classes.  Customer-related costs are allocated based upon the 
22 
number of customers in each class, weighted to account for the complexity of servicing 
23 
the needs of the different classes of customers.

5 
CNG’s customer class relative rates of return.  The relative rate of return (“RROR”) 
6

7 
example, if a customer class rate of return is 5.5% and the system average rate of return 
8

9 
RROR is below 1.0, the class is earning less than the system average rate of return.  A 
10 
class RROR of 1.0 indicates that the class return is the same as the system average rate 
11 
of return.  A class RROR greater than 1.0 indicates that the class RROR is greater than 
12 
the system average rate of return.  The “2020 CNG ECOSS” columns present class 
13 
returns and RRORs from Case No. 20-G-0101.  The “2021 CNG ECOSS” columns 
14 
present class returns and RRORs from the current case.
