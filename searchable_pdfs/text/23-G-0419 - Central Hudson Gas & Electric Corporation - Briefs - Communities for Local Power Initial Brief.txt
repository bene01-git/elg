I.
INTRODUCTION
A.
Overview
Through its testimony (direct, rebuttal, and cross examination) and responses to information
requests (IRs), Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corporation (the Company) has demonstrated
itself to be experiencing a longstanding organizational structure failure. Central Hudson’s (still
alive and well) billing fiasco is not a standalone issue – it is just the proverbial straw that broke
the camel’s back. Aside from billing failures, the foundation of the Company is built on buck
passing, urging ratepayers to “switch” to gas, and profiting from its proposed build out of gas
infrastructure. The Company’s IR responses (or lack thereof in some instances) and the cross
examination of its panels paint a frightening picture. This situation has catastrophic implications
for both ratepayers and New York State alike that will become more acute if these weaknesses,
and oftentimes outright failures, are not addressed properly.
When one area of a business is dysfunctional, the ability of all the other areas of a
business to function successfully is compromised. Departments do not live in a vacuum – one
dysfunctional area of an organization damages the functionality of the entire organization, and
the end product suffers. Central Hudson ratepayers are suffering – at the hands of Central
Hudson. This pervasive dysfunction is well-documented, the causes for it have not been

programs or capital investments as – again invisible – increases in the “Miscellaneous charges”

XV.
CONCLUSION
At best, Central Hudson’s current company structure relies on a system of jack of all trades,
master of none approach, with no accountability or expectation of accuracy or competency. This
was illustrated each day of the Evidentiary Hearing, when panels (that were seemingly meant to
host experts) would punt basic questions to other panels, and it was a rare moment when an
expert gave substantial responses. The Evidentiary Hearing was Central Hudson’s chance to
showcase what the company has achieved – it was leadership’s time to shine. Instead, every day
in court was a game of witness hot potato. Either the “experts” aren’t experts, or they are
avoiding answering questions because what they can say will harm the Company. Both scenarios
are indicative of a company that is unfit to serve Hudson Valley residents with essential services
such as electric and gas. Additionally, Central Hudson will be unable to fulfill the role that is
required of it under New York State’s climate law. The Company has failed to meet its
obligations under the current rate plan (which are much less complicated as compared to what
lies ahead regarding CLCPA compliance). This, along with its ongoing inability to bill ratepayers
correctly, demonstrate the need for a rate decrease and a pause on the possibility of another rate
case being initiated before investigations of the Company are concluded.
In summary, the Company does not deserve an increase in its revenue – neither for its electric
capital and operations expenditures nor for its gas capital and operations, and certainly not for its
customer service or efficacy in responding to New York’s climate goals. It is striking, in
reviewing the testimony of Company representatives, how often its Panels and expert witnesses
argue that it should, in essence, be rewarded for these failures. The Company and its witnesses
repeatedly argue that any diminution of earnings; risk of diminished earnings; or financial
consequences for managerial failures such as inability to meet goals and targets for the correction
of its billing and other errors, would result in lower ratings and an increased cost of capital. This
circular argument makes a mockery of the regulated utility model, which is designed to ensure
fair and reliable service at affordable rates. Central Hudson’s requests for increases in the Basic
Service Charge, introduction of a hidden “Climate Resilience Surcharge,” and other increased
costs such as those enumerated above should be rejected outright, and the Company’s
long-suffering ratepayers should receive a reduction in the rates they are charged. Based on the
company’s performance during the last three years and its unmistakable inability to reliably

the billing errors of the past two years, and the resulting customer anger, it would be fair,
