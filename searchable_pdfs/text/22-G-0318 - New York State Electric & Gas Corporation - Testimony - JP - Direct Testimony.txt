7 
Electric ECOS Studies 
8 
• 
The Companies’ Electric ECOS studies have been significantly improved since the 
9 
last rate proceedings (Case No. 19-E-0378 et al.).  Specifically, the Companies have 
10 
provided a current analysis of the customer-related portion of the distribution network, 
11 
which properly results in classifying 63.3% (NYSEG) and 51.1% (RG&E) of distribution 
12 
plant and related expenses as a customer-related cost. 
13 
• 
However, the Companies continue to allocate transmission costs to service classes 
14 
using the average of the twelve coincident peak (“12CP”) method.  The 12CP method 
15 
fails to recognize cost causation because both NYSEG and RG&E are predominately 
16 
summer-peaking utilities.  It is also contrary to how the Companies are allocating 
17 
production demand-related costs, which recognizes the importance of the summer 
18 
peaks.  Specifically, for production demand-related costs, RG&E is proposing to use 
19 
the contribution to the annual system peak (“1CP”) method, while NYSEG is proposing 
20 
to average the contributions to the summer and winter annual peak.   
21 
• 
NYSEG’s annual winter peak demand is only 87% of the annual system peak.  
22 
Accordingly, it is inappropriate to average annual summer and winter peak demands 
23 
to allocate demand-related costs.  The better approach is to average the coincident 
24 
demands in July and August (“2CP”) because it would better reflect the cost drivers 
25 
for both production and transmission capacity than averaging either the annual 
26 
summer/winter or 12CPs.   
27 
• 
Accordingly, MI recommends the 2CP (1CP) method to allocate production demand 
28 
and transmission plant and related expenses for NYSEG (RG&E).

2. ELECTRIC ECOS STUDIES 
Q 
WHAT IS AN EMBEDDED COST-OF-SERVICE STUDY? 
1 
A 
An ECOS study is an analysis used to determine each class’s responsibility for a 
2 
utility’s costs.  Thus, it determines whether the revenues a class generates cover the 
3 
class’s cost of service.  An ECOS study separates the utility's total costs into portions 
4 
incurred on behalf of the various customer groups.  Most of a utility's costs are incurred 
5 
to jointly serve many customers.  For purposes of revenue allocation and rate design, 
6 
customers are grouped into homogeneous classes according to their usage patterns 
7 
and service characteristics.  The procedures typically used in an ECOS study are 
8 
described in more detail in Exhibit ___ (JP-3).   
9

20

21

17 
A 
RDM is a mechanism that reconciles actual billed delivery service revenue to the 
18 
allowed delivery service revenue on a rate class specific basis.  As described in 
19 
Appendix FF of the Joint Proposal filed in the last rate proceedings:

For each service class or subclass subject to the RDM, each Company will, on 
1 
a monthly basis, compare actual billed delivery service revenue exclusive of 
2 
the COVID-19 Customer Bill Credit to a delivery service revenue target. If the 
3 
monthly actual billed delivery service revenue exceeds the delivery service 
4 
revenue target, the delivery service revenue excess will be accrued for refund 
5 
to customers at the end of the Rate Year. Likewise, if the monthly actual billed 
6 
delivery service revenue is less than the delivery service revenue target, the 
7 
delivery revenue shortfall will be accrued for recovery from customers at the 
8 
end of the Rate Year. 
9 
At the end of the Rate Year, total delivery service revenues will be compared 
10 
to cumulative monthly target revenues for each service class or subclass. Any 
11 
variance from cumulative target revenues will be either refunded / surcharged 
12 
to customers over the 12-month period beginning August 1 following the Rate 
13 
Year. Any surcharge or credit (RDM Adjustment) amount will reflect interest at 
14 
the then-effective other customer deposit rate and will be either recovered or 
15 
returned on a service class or subclass specific basis. For reconciliation 
16 
purposes the residential service classes will be combined as an RDM class at 
17 
each Company, NYSEG service classes 1, 8 and 12 will be combined and 
18 
RG&E service classes 1 and 4 will be combined. The surcharge or credit for 
19 
each applicable service class or subclass shall be determined by dividing the 
20 
amount to be refunded / surcharged to customers in that service class or 
21 
subclass by estimated kWh or kW deliveries to customers in that service class 
22 
or subclass over a 12-month period or RDM Adjustment Period. A per kW 
23 
surcharge or credit will be applied for those classes that do not have a kWh 
24 
delivery charge and a per kWh surcharge or credit will apply for all other 
25 
classes.15 
26 
 
Thus, if the Companies overestimate the estimated annual delivery revenues for a 
27 
particular class or sub-class, it can implement a surcharge on the customers in the 
28 
class or sub-class to compensate for the revenue shortfall.

Q 
IS THE REVENUE DECOUPLING MECHANISM ACTUALLY DESIGNED TO 
1 
SEVER THE CONNECTION BETWEEN SALES AND REVENUES? 
2 
A 
No.  Instead of decoupling sales and revenues, RDM has morphed into a full-fledged 
3 
sales true-up adjustment that completely insulates the Companies from any 
4 
forecasting errors. 
5 
Q 
SHOULD THE REVENUE DECOUPLING MECHANISM APPLY TO STANDBY 
6 
SERVICE? 
7 
A 
No.  First, customers purchase standby service when they need to replace the power 
8 
that is normally generated from their behind-the-meter generating facilities and to 
9 
serve any load in excess of their generation.  Thus, the extent to which customers 
10 
require standby service can vary based on either planned or forced outages of the 
11 
generators.  By their very nature, forced outages are random events.  Further, planned 
12 
outages will depend on the wear and tear on the generators, coupled with necessary 
13 
inspections and occasional equipment overhauls.  Thus, standby service is clearly 
14 
different than full requirements service.   
15 
 
 
In either case, the use of standby service, and the corresponding operating 
16 
revenues, will vary from month to month and from year to year.  Thus, standby service 
17 
is non-reoccurring.  Therefore, standby service is not conducive to an RDM, which 
18 
requires accurate sales and revenue forecasting.  It is even less conducive when the 
19 
corresponding parent service class is also prone to significant forecasting errors.
