12 
emphasis on the rights and needs of low-income utility consumers.  I have been employed 
13 
by PULP in various capacities since July of 1990.  I am currently Director of Research for

PULP.

16

17 
exceeding 10.5% were designated to be deferred for the benefit of customers.7

9 
Q. 
What is “Excess Unprotected Accumulated Deferred Federal Income Tax”? 
10 
A. 
Accumulated Deferred Federal Income Tax, or “ADFIT”, is a balance sheet item 
11 
representing the historic differences between federal income tax expense based on 
12 
generally accepted accounting principles (“GAAP” or “Book” Basis) and federal income 
13 
tax liability as calculated when preparing a federal income tax return (Tax Basis). GAAP 
14 
Basis deferred taxes are usually net liabilities on the balance sheet; that is, they represent 
15 
taxes to be paid in the future as timing differences between GAAP and Tax Basis 
16 
accounting resolve to a zero balance. 
17 
Significant amounts of the Companies’ accumulated deferred federal income tax (ADFIT) 
18 
liabilities are the result of decades of capital investments in property for which accelerated 
19 
depreciation has been available to be taken when determining Tax Basis income tax 
20 
liability. Until 2017, GAAP Basis deferred corporate federal income taxes were accrued at 
21 
the then applicable corporate rate of thirty five percent (35%). As part of the 2017 Tax Cut 
22 
and Jobs Act (TCJA), however, the federal corporate tax rate was lowered to twenty-one 
23 
percent (21%). For GAAP purposes, this reduction in the tax rate had the effect of requiring 
24 
a substantial reduction of the Companies’ ADFIT balances sheet since approximately forty 
25 
percent (40%) of these liabilities would no longer need to be paid in the future.32  
26

For most corporations, the effect of reducing ADFIT balances (creating “Excess ADFIT”) 
1 
was a windfall that could be returned to owners. The situation for “cost of service” 
2 
regulated utilities was different however, in that utility rates have historically included an 
3 
allowance for income tax expense on a GAAP Basis. (In effect, ratepayers have “prepaid” 
4 
utilities’ income taxes through the rates they have paid over time in their monthly utility 
5 
bills.) In light of this “prepayment” aspect of setting utility rates in New York State, the 
6 
Public Service Commission ruled in Case 17-M-0815, that reductions of utility ADFIT 
7 
balances should be preserved for the net benefit to ratepayers, rather than allowing a

12

16

Q.  
What is a financial statement form and why is it important? 
5 
A.   
A financial statement form (FSF) is a form that a utility may require an eligible residential 
6 
customer to complete who is requesting more lenient terms than the Company’s “standard 
7 
offer”, which typically requires a 15% down payment of past due arrears, with the 
8 
remaining balance to be paid over a 10-month period. FSF disclosures include a customer’s 
9 
assets, income and expenses. The utility may require reasonable substantiation of the 
10 
information on that form, provided that all such information shall be treated as confidential. 
11 
(16 NYCRR § 11.10(a)(1)(ii)). 
12 
FSFs are important because they provide an objective view of a customer’s individual 
13 
financial circumstances: in particular, whether they have “positive cash flow” and therefore 
14 
can afford to make payments toward his or her utility arrears; or have “negative cash flow” 
15 
and therefore already spend more than he or she receives and may only be able to make 
16 
nominal repayments.  Basing the terms of a DPA on the results of a completed FSF is one 
17 
way that a utility can ensure that it has negotiated DPA terms tailored to the customer’s 
18 
financial circumstances, as required by NYCRR § 11.10. 
19
