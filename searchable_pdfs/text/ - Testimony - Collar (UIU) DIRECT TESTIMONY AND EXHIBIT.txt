II. 
CUSTOMER SERVICE QUALITY PROGRAM 
11 
Q. 
Please describe the Companies’ current CSQP.  
12 
A. 
KEDNY’s CSQP comprises four service quality performance metrics: PSC 
13 
Complaint Rate, Residential Customer Transaction Satisfaction, Percent of Calls 
14 
Answered in Thirty Seconds, and Adjusted Customer Bills. KEDLI’s CSQP 
15 
comprises three service quality performance metrics: PSC Complaint Rate, 
16 
Residential Customer Transaction Satisfaction, and Adjusted Customer Bills. 
17 
 
18 
Q. 
What is the function of CSQPs? 
19 
A. 
CSQPs provide utilities with powerful incentives to provide acceptable levels of 
20 
customer service. Quality service is important to customers and is in the public 
21 
interest. Due to the monopolistic nature of utility service, customers cannot select 
22 
another delivery service provider if they are dissatisfied with the quality of service 
23

they receive from KEDNY or KEDLI. CSQPs establish standards by which each 
1 
utility’s performance will be measured and monitored, and help to ensure they 
2 
provide adequate service quality to their customers.  
3 
 
4 
Q. 
How have the Companies performed under the CSQP since 2008?  
5 
A. 
The Panel’s testimony and Exhibit _ (SSP-8) indicate that KEDNY has met all of 
6 
its performance metrics during this time period. However, the same cannot be 
7 
said for KEDLI, which failed to meet its performance target for customer 
8 
satisfaction in 2013 and 2014, and failed to meet its PSC Complaint Rate target 
9 
in 2014. The Panel attributed the failure to meet the customer satisfaction target 
10 
because of challenges related to Superstorm Sandy in 2013, billing issues 
11 
caused by the Customer Service System conversion, and higher than normal 
12 
customer bills in 2014 as a result of the extreme cold weather.  
13

Q. 
Do you agree with this penalty offset mechanism? 
4 
A. 
No. To adopt this mechanism would send the wrong message. Offsetting missed

opposes. The PSC has repeatedly refused to adopt positive incentives in other

utility rate case proceedings.
