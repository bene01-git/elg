PRELIMINARY STATEMENT 
 
 
In a series of orders regarding the proposed acquisition of KeySpan Corporation 
and its affiliates by National Grid plc, the New York State Public Service Commission 
(“Commission”) approved a five-year gas delivery rate plan (the “Rate Plan”) for The Brooklyn 
Union Gas Company d/b/a National Grid NY (“KEDNY” or the “Company”) that spanned the 
period January 1, 2008 through December 31, 2012.1   
On December 5, 2012, less than one month before the Rate Plan was scheduled to 
expire, Department of Public Service Staff (“Staff”) and KEDNY commenced negotiations to 
discuss modifying certain elements of the Rate Plan.2  There was no pre-filed testimony or any 
other filing that preceded the start of negotiations.  On January 15, 2013, KEDNY reported to the 
presiding Administrative Law Judge (“ALJ”) Michelle L. Phillips that the Company and Staff 
had reached an agreement-in-principle,3 and filed with the Commission a Term Sheet

If approved as proposed, the Joint Proposal would extend current rates for an

three years (i.e., eight years after the Commission approved the Rate Plan). 
In accordance with the Procedural Ruling, the City of New York (“City”) hereby 
submits its Statement on the Joint Proposal (“Statement”).  It is the City’s position that the 
process underlying the Joint Proposal was flawed and should not be repeated.  Despite these 
flaws, the City recognizes that the Joint Proposal captures benefits for KEDNY gas customers, 
although the process followed to reach that agreement makes it difficult to compare those 
benefits with potential litigated outcomes.  In addition, the City is concerned that the Joint 
Proposal allows KEDNY’s costs and rates to go unexamined for too long.  Accordingly, the City

ARGUMENT 
IF THE JOINT PROPOSAL IS APPROVED, IT SHOULD BE 
CONDITIONED ON KEDNY FILING A FULL RATE CASE 
FOR RATES EFFECTIVE NO LATER THAN  
JANUARY 1, 2015 
 
 
The process leading up to the filing of the Joint Proposal was flawed and should 
not be repeated.  Settlement negotiations commenced on December 5, 2012, without any 
testimony or other substantive information having been provided to the negotiating parties.  Prior 
to the commencement of settlement negotiations, the only public information pertaining to the 
subject of this proceeding was set forth in a brief summary paragraph in the Settlement Notice.   
Nevertheless, a little more than a month after settlement negotiations commenced, 
KEDNY and Staff reported that they had reached an agreement-in-principle to extend the Rate 
Plan for up to three years without adjusting base delivery rates.8  This rushed process did not 
allow for the level of review that typically would precede a multi-year rate plan extension, nor 
did it develop the evidentiary record that is necessary to demonstrate that the proposed 
agreement is superior to potential litigated outcomes that might result from an alternate process.9

Importantly, if the Joint Proposal is approved as proposed, eight years may pass before 
KEDNY’s costs and rate structure are given a complete review by the Commission.10   
Staff contends that there is no need in this proceeding for a well-developed record 
because there was no proposal to increase base delivery rates.11  Staff argues that “[t]his is 
important because the level of detail required for Commission consideration of the Joint Proposal 
is significantly less than would otherwise be required to support a major rate filing because the 
Commission has already evaluated that level of detail supporting the existing rates when it 
established the 2007 Rate Plan.”12  Accordingly, Staff only “examined the level of detail 
necessary to establish that KEDNY’s rates will continue to be just and reasonable.”13 
When the Commission determined in 2007 that the delivery rates contained in the 
Rate Plan were just and reasonable, that conclusion was a prospective judgment based on the 
facts and circumstances available at that time.  Since that determination, the Company reported 
earned returns on common equity of: (a) 11.02 percent in 2008; (b) 10.49 percent in 2009; (c) 
13.98 percent in 2010; and (d) 14.10 percent in 2011.14  It cannot be assumed that the 
Commission would continue to view the Rate Plan delivery rates as just and reasonable in light 
of earnings that consistently exceed the Company’s allowed ROE of 9.8 percent.

The Commission could have exercised its authority under Sections 65 and 66 of 
the Public Service Law to issue an Order to Show Cause why delivery rates should not be 
adjusted.  Such proceeding would have afforded a full and complete review and understanding of 
the Company’s costs and revenues.  The parties would have been able to examine KEDNY’s 
rates through discovery, testimony and, potentially, an evidentiary hearing.  This alternate 
process also could have been initiated with sufficient time to complete a full examination of 
KEDNY’s costs and revenues before the Rate Plan expired.  
The process that was followed instead excluded the most important issue – 
whether inflated delivery rates have been driving the excessive earnings that KEDNY 
consistently realized under the Rate Plan.15  Staff contends that “the potential range of litigated 
outcomes” may be “viewed as somewhere between what exists” under the Rate Plan and “the 
bounds set by recent Commission policy and rate decisions” because “there is no underlying 
litigated case.”16  This argument is inapt.  Under the Commission’s Settlement Guidelines,17 the 
Signatory Parties are obligated to demonstrate that the Joint Proposal produces results that are 
superior to potential litigated outcomes.  Hampered by a scant record, Staff and KEDNY struggle 
to satisfy this standard, and largely limit themselves to describing how the Joint Proposal is in 
their view consistent with recent Commission precedent.18

Despite the infirmity of the settlement process, the City recognizes that the 
settlement does capture some real benefits for customers, particularly when compared to the 
Company’s recent history of overearning.  As proposed, the Joint Proposal would reduce 
KEDNY’s authorized return on common equity by 40 basis points, from 9.8 percent to 9.4 
percent.19  Pursuant to the Joint Proposal, KEDNY also would increase annual spending on its 
low-income discount program from $7.4 million to $10.4 million for Rate Year 6 and Rate Year 
7.20  KEDNY also would provide eligible low-income customers with a one-time bill credit of 
$15 per eligible residential heating customer in Rate Year 6 only, as well as enhanced low-
income discounts for Rate Year 6 and Rate Year 7.21  The City supports the change to KEDNY’s 
low-income discount program, which will bring such spending closer to the statewide average 
for gas low-income programs, although total spending would remain below the statewide 
average.22  The expanded low-income program would provide needed support for qualifying 
customers, and represents an important benefit of the Joint Proposal. 
KEDNY also accepted material modifications of its Gas Safety Performance 
Metrics.  Pursuant to the Joint Proposal, the Company would expand its Leak Prone Pipe 
(“LPP”) Removal program by removing from service 85 miles of LPP by the end of Rate Year 7,

As noted earlier, it is not clear from the sparse record developed in this 
proceeding that the benefits described above are superior to what may have been achieved if an 
alternate process had been followed (i.e., a comprehensive investigation of KEDNY’s rates in 
response to an Order to Show Cause).  Moreover, the extension of the Rate Plan prevents the 
Commission from examining existing rate inequities.  For example, although the Joint Proposal 
would benefit a few discrete customers by modifying the annual price cap applicable to S.C. No. 
6-M,28 this limited change does not address the broader issue of whether interruptible rates 
generally need to be updated.  A more comprehensive review of KEDNY’s interruptible pricing 
is needed, and should be undertaken.  The Joint Proposal delays that and other examinations of 
the Company’s rates. 
The City is reluctant to disrupt the benefits captured by the Joint Proposal, but is 
concerned that the process that yielded the settlement should not serve as a precedent in other 
proceedings.  In addition, the extension of the Rate Plan must have a defined termination date – 
no utility should be allowed to avoid a full examination of its costs and rates for more than seven 
years, particularly when it is overearning in the manner that KEDNY was.  In light of the 
unusual circumstances attending this proceeding, if the Commission approves the Joint Proposal 
it should be conditioned upon KEDNY agreeing to file a new rate case for rates to be effective

Although the City has significant procedural concerns relating to the abbreviated 
manner in which this case was developed, its principal reservation concerning the agreement 
embodied in the Joint Proposal is actually one with substantive implications.  The Joint Proposal 
would delay the implementation of a new rate plan for up to three years, thereby forestalling an 
opportunity for KEDNY to implement measures that go materially beyond those last reviewed 
and directed by the Commission in 2007.  
This is important because the extension of prior criteria based on a five-year old 
record will necessarily fail to incorporate recent regulatory and environmental developments 
with clear potential implications for the KEDNY distribution system.  At least two such material 
developments have manifested themselves only recently.   
First, the Commission instituted a generic proceeding to examine the expansion of 
natural gas service in New York State.30  That proceeding may result in new policies and 
measures that would require tariff modifications and capital budget adjustments that may be 
addressed adequately only in the context of a full rate case.  Were that to be the case, the
