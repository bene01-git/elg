 STATEMENT ON THE NATIONAL GRID UPSTATE JOINT PROPOSAL 
 BY ALLIANCE FOR A GREEN ECONOMY 
 1. Introduction 
 Alliance for a Green Economy (“AGREE”) neither supports nor opposes the Joint Proposal filed 
 on September 27, 2021 in the above referenced proceeding. 
 AGREE works for safe, affordable energy and the development of a just, green economy in New 
 York State. We educate the public and our policymakers about renewable alternatives that can 
 both revitalize the economy and safeguard human health and the environment. AGREE is the 
 only environmental nonprofit to participate in every major rate case across the state in order to 
 promote a collaborative transition to a renewable energy future. 
 AGREE participated thoroughly in the process of this rate case, including submitting expert 
 testimony, conducting discovery, and attending settlement negotiations. 
 The agreement developed through settlement is an improvement over the terms initially filed by 
 National Grid (“the Company”). 
 Positive aspects of the Joint Proposal include, briefly: 
 ●  Rate increases that stay below 2% per year; 
 ●  Commitment by National Grid to withdraw the permit application for the controversial 
 Albany Loop pipeline and limiting the circumstances under which the Company may 
 resubmit its application during the course of the three year agreement; 
 ●  Commitment by National Grid to endeavor to achieve “net zero” increase in billed gas 
 usage; 
 ●  Additional $1 million for heat pumps from shareholders specifically for gas-to-heat-pump 
 conversions; 
 ●  The end of marketing for new gas connections and conversions, including any customer 
 rebates for heating oil-to-gas conversions or new gas customers, for the term of this rate

 plan. The Company has further agreed that future direct energy efficiency marketing will 
 not include language referring to gas as having environmental benefits; 
 ●  Marketing heat pumps along-side gas efficiency measures; 
 ●  Avoiding new gas main extensions and leak prone pipe replacements by pursuing 
 non-pipe alternatives like putting new customers on heat pumps; 
 ●  Guardrails around the Company’s proposed investment in and use of local biomethane 
 (referred to by National Grid as “renewable natural gas”) and hydrogen; and 
 ●  Cancellation of the Company’s proposed Carbon Capture pilot. 
 That said, the proposal lacks provisions that convincingly put National Grid on a path to 
 contribute to meeting the mandates of the Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act 
 (“CLCPA”). The CLCPA requires a 40% reduction in greenhouse gas emissions by 2030 from 
 1990 levels and 85% reduction by 2050. Though the Company’s rate proposal was filed well 
 after the CLCPA went into effect, the Company did not file any emissions analysis along with its 
 rate plan. Testimony filed by Thomas G. (Jerry) Acton on behalf of AGREE and Pace in this 
 case demonstrated what a robust analysis can look like and provided recommendations for the 
 amount of greenhouse gas reductions that must be achieved in order to comply with the CLCPA 
 by 2030.  1 
 The Joint Proposal does not incorporate many of the recommendations provided by Jerry Acton. 
 In particular, the Company will  not  be reducing gas sales, and instead will keep gas sales flat in 
 years two and three of this agreement. This is a concession on the part of both the Company 
 and AGREE. The net zero growth provision will avoid the Company’s projected 2% growth for 
 that time period, but staying flat now instead of beginning reductions will mean that the future 
 ramp up of renewable thermal energy conversions needed for the company to meet its portion 
 of the climate goals may be more difficult, and probably more costly, than if deeper reductions 
 are begun sooner. For this reason, while we take no position on whether this Joint Proposal 
 should be approved or rejected, we do urge the New York Public Service Commision 
 (“Commission”) to act in other proceedings to ensure that the CLCPA mandates are met in a 
 cost effective and timely manner.

 2. Further Action is Necessary 
 As a state agency, the Commission is required to enforce the CLCPA and to ensure that its 
 decisions are consistent with the greenhouse gas emissions reductions mandated by the law. In 
 August, 2021, the Commission expressly acknowledged this requirement extends to rate case 
 decisions.  2  T o truly enforce the CLCPA and to understand whether rate case proposals are 
 CLCPA-consistent, actual emissions analyses and annual emissions reduction targets are 
 needed. Without such an analysis and a standard by which to compare a utility’s actions against 
 what is required by the law, it is not possible to determine how far forward or backward any 
 particular set of actions by a utility company will take us toward compliance with the CLCPA. 
 Going forward, the Commission should require utilities to file emissions analyses with their rate 
 case filings and to demonstrate how their plans are consistent with the CLCPA mandates. The 
 Commission must also create standards for CLCPA compliance, such as annual emissions 
 limits and requirements for strategic asset retirement in line with the CLCPA. We also urge the 
 Commission to reform its pipeline permitting processes to ensure that electrification is the 
 default rather than a niche alternative pursued if and when utilities choose. This should 
 especially apply to hotly contested pipelines like the Albany Loop, which without proper 
 guardrails, could be built in the future.

 of increased heating needs or emergencies have been called for by dozens of organizations  3 
 and almost 200 elected officials  4  in the Commission's Gas Planning Proceeding (Case

 Without clarity from the Commission, rate cases are serving as the arena for both policy setting 
 and implementation, resulting in inconsistent goals and outcomes across utility territories, with 
 no utility moving quickly or boldly enough to meet the 2030 mandates. Recent analysis 
 presented to the Climate Action Council by the integration analysis team demonstrates that 
 “energy efficiency and end-use electrification will be essential parts of any Pathway that hits 
 NYS Emissions Limits.”  6  In particular: 
 ●  In all scenarios modeled, zero emission vehicles and heat pumps become the majority of 
 new purchases by the late 2020s, and fossil-emitting cars and appliances are no longer 
 sold after 2035 
 ●  1 -2 million efficient homes electrified with heat pumps by 2030 
 ●  Consumer decision-making plays a large role, especially important for the purchase of 
 new passenger vehicles and heating systems,  for homes and businesses through the 
 next decade 
 Though the final outcome of the Climate Action Council process is not yet known, much is 
 already clear about how the heating sector must change, including the rapid timeframe of 1-2 
 million electrified homes by 2030. 
 Mr. Acton’s testimony estimated that to meet the 2030 mandates, 154,333 homes would need to 
 move off of gas, and 88,377 homes would need to move off oil in National Grid’s territory by 
 2030. These are the kinds of actionable targets that must be set by the Commission so that rate 
 cases can contend with implementation and programmatic questions rather than overarching 
 goals.

 3. Rising Costs and Debt 
 Since the filing of the Joint Proposal, projections about the cost of gas for the upcoming winter 
 have been publicized. National Grid projects its customers will face a 31% increase in 
 commodity costs this winter.  7  Though commodity costs are not the subject of the rate case, the 
 bill impacts of these rising costs of gas will be devastating for many families, especially those 
 already deeply indebted to the utility and suffering from the pandemic recesion. Meanwhile, it 
 was reported in the  Albany Times Union  yesterday that National Grid is sending debt collectors 
 to residents who are in arrears, despite the fact that shutoffs are still prohibited until mid-January 
 of 2022.  8 
 We urge the Commission to take immediate action in the Proceeding on Motion of the 
 Commission Regarding the Effects of COVID-19 on Utility Service (Case 20-M-0266). It has 
 now been well over a year since the Commission issued the initial order directing Department of 
 Public Service Staff (“Staff”) to study the effects of the pandemic on ratepayers, and over 1.2 
 million residential ratepayers are now mired in debt.  9  The average household debt exceeds 
 $1,300, and we are dismayed to see a lack of recognition from the Commission of this level of 
 crisis.

 4. Conclusion 
 Taken as a whole, the Joint Proposal is an improvement over the Company’s original proposal 
 and includes many concessions worth supporting. The Joint Proposal also builds on Joint 
 Proposals developed by multiple parties in the NYSEG/RG&E and Central Hudson rate cases. 
 These utilities, respectively, were the first to agree to plan for no net increase of gas sales and 
 the first to agree to reduce their gas sales. However, this proposal does not go as far as Central 
 Hudson, which is why in the midst of some of the worst climate disasters in recent history, we 
 cannot fully support the Joint Proposal. 
 More importantly, we cannot continue to just tinker with business as usual in utility rate cases. 
 What would have been seen as major concessions in previous years now fall short of what is 
 needed to meet the current crisis and to catch up with New York’s law. We urge the Commission 
 to create a process for ending gas expansion in all parts of the state, followed by an aggressive 
 annual schedule of gas reductions, supported by a participatory planning process and binding 
 targets.
