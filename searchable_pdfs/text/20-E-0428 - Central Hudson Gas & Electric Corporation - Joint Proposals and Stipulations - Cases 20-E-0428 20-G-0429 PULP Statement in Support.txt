I. Introduction 
 
This Statement of Support (“Statement”) is submitted by the Public Utility Law Project 
of New York (“PULP”) in support of a Joint Proposal for settlement issued and signed by several 
of the parties to Cases 20-E-0428 and 20-G-0429.1 The Joint Proposal (“JP”) was filed by its 
signatories on August 24, 2021 with the Secretary to the Commission in accord with the Public 
Service Commission’s (“PSC’s” or “Commission’s”) regulations and settlement guidelines,2 and 
the orders of Administrative Law Judges Anthony Belsito and Michael Clarke.

II. Procedural Background 
 
Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corporation (“Central Hudson” or “Company”) initially 
filed their tariff leaves and testimony on August 27, 2020, setting forth a one-year rate plan 
ending June 30, 2022. Prior to moderation, the increase in electric delivery revenue would have 
resulted in an average residential monthly delivery bill increase of $7.76 per month which 
represents a 9.39% increase on the delivery bill or a 6.22% on the total bill for an average 630 
kWh per month customer.3 Additionally, prior to moderation, the increase in gas delivery 
revenue would have resulted in an average residential monthly gas delivery bill increase of $9.45 
per month which represents an 10.11% increase on the delivery bill or a 7.96% increase on the 
total bill for an average residential gas customer utilizing 70 Ccf per month.  
The PSC issued a notice of suspension on September 8, 2020, after which discovery was 
begun by Department of Public Service Staff (“DPS Staff”) and interested parties. 
Administrative Law Judges Anthony Belsito and Michael Clarke were appointed to conduct the 
proceeding and convened a procedural and technical conference on October 6, 2020, to propose a 
litigation schedule.  
Public statement hearings were held telephonically and virtually on November 25, 2020. 
PULP provided statements at such hearings. PULP provided expert testimony on December 22, 
2020, and on January 19, 2021 opposed a motion by Central Hudson to exclude testimony of the 
Town of Saugerties. 
On January 29, 2021, the Company filed a Notice of Impending Settlement Negotiations 
pursuant to 16 NYCRR 3.9. Negotiations took place between February 44 and August 24, 2021.

III. Standard of Review 
 
The standard of review used by the Commission in reviewing settlement agreements is 
found in the Settlement Guidelines.5 The Settlement Guidelines require that all decisions must be 
just and reasonable and in the public interest. Determining whether the terms of the Joint 
Proposal are in the public interest involves substantive consideration of the following:  
 
1. consistency with the law and regulatory, economic, social and environmental State and 
Commission policies;  
2. whether the terms of the Joint Proposal compare favorably with the likely result of a fully 
litigated case and produces a result within the range of reasonable outcomes;  
3. whether the Joint Proposal fairly balances the interests of ratepayers, investors and the 
long-term soundness of the utility; and,  
4. whether the Joint Proposal provides a rational basis for the Commission’s decision.6  
 
The Commission must also give weight to the fact of whether or not a settlement reflects  
an agreement between normally adversarial parties.7

IV. Terms of the Joint Proposal 
 
PULP supports the settlement arrived at in the JP. There are several areas of the JP that PULP 
wishes to draw attention to in its statement of support. 
 
a. Rates 
The Company made significant strides to lower rates for consumers in the upcoming rate 
term. The electric rates in the first rate year decrease $3.0 million; however, the JP reflects a 
decrease of only $1.0 million in order to moderate rates by $2 million in the second and third rate 
years. Additionally, the Company plans to apply gas bill credits of $0.8 million in rate year one, 
$3.2 million in rate year two, and $5.6 million in rate year three. The average monthly bill 
impacts for electric customers will be: a decrease of $0.33 in rate year one, an increase of $1.72 
in rate year two, and an increase of $1.82 in rate year three. The average monthly bill impacts for 
gas customers will be: an increase of $1.64 in rate year one, an increase of $2.17 in rate year two, 
and an increase of $1.50 in rate year three. These increases reflect an accommodation to the 
economic impact of the COVID-19 pandemic and the impact it has had on the customers of 
Central Hudson. 
 
b. Low-Income Affordability 
The JP implements the Public Service Commission’s most recent Energy Affordability 
Policy Order8. In accordance with the Order, the Company’s low-income program discounts 
have been increased, and customer eligibility for the low-income program has been expanded.

c. COVID-19 Provisions 
The JP considers both the short- and long-term impacts of COVID-19 on the economy 
and the financial hardship it has placed on Central Hudson’s customers. In addition to the 
expanded low-income program eligibility, the Company has also made a commitment to work on 
equitable arrears resolution for residential and small business customers if no action is taken by 
the Public Service Commission prior to September 15, with a goal of arriving at arrears 
resolution programs to benefit its customers. To help moderate bills during this time of economic 
hardship, a $4.55 million COVID-19 Adjustment - Customer Bill Moderation Credit has been 
applied. The Company has also paused their Residential Service Terminations/Uncollectible 
Target customer service performance metric for 2021 and 2022. All of these actions will help 
mitigate the bill increases that will occur and show that the Company has considered their 
customers’ financial changes during COVID-19. 
 
d. Consumer Protections 
In addition to the COVID-19 and low-income provisions, the JP takes additional actions 
to protect consumers. The Company plans expand Spanish language customer support, including 
translations of vital documents such as termination notices and deferred payment agreements, 
and its web site. Central Hudson has also agreed to implement additional cold weather 
protections, including accepting all Home Energy Assistance Program payments and providing 
customers with continued service regardless of the amount due, and refraining from terminating 
customers who are coded as Elderly, Blind, or Disabled. The Company will also implement hot 
weather provisions, suspending electric residential terminations during periods of extreme heat. 
 
V. Conclusion 
  
As stated above, PULP is a signatory party of the Joint Proposal. PULP believes the JP’s 
mitigation of rate increases, low-income provisions, COVID-19 considerations, and consumer 
protections, as well as efforts to promote the goals of the Climate Leadership and Community 
Protection Act (“CLCPA”) are in the public interest. The JP reflects the collaborative effort of 
many parties and should be approved by the Public Service Commission. PULP therefore
