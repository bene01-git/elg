Q. 
Please explain why the July 1, 2016 management increase has been 
13 
revised from 3.5% to 3.2%. 
14 
A. 
In my direct testimony, I projected that a 3.0% average increase in base 
15 
pay would be provided to National Grid’s entire management workforce.  
16 
This average increase has been reduced to 2.5% for the twelve months 
17 
beginning July 1, 2016.  While market survey sources continue to support 
18 
a 3.0% increase, National Grid believes that consideration of its overall 
19 
financial circumstances supports a reduction in the average percentage

Percent Total bills 
Adjusted/Billing Accuracy
“Adjusted Customer Bills” is a measure of the proportion of customer bills that require later 
adjustment due to errors by KEDNY / KEDLI, expressed as a percentage of total customer bills.  
Adjusted Customer Bills will be measured monthly and the performance incentive mechanism 
will be based on the average of the twelve monthly scores.  Following recommendations of the 
NY PSC Customer Service Data Audit (Case 13-M-0314), a June 25, 2015 Adjusted Bills Work 
Group summary defined the following situations which will not constitute an adjustment due to 
company error (and are thus excluded):
 • an estimated bill replaced by a bill based on an actual reading
 • a customer reading replaced with an actual or estimated reading
 • a change in a customer bill due to switching to or from budget billing
 • changes to correct for the responsible billing party (e.g., where bills have been rendered to a 
prior resident, and bills are re-issued to the current resident)
The company has calculated new targets based upon the definitions of the Adjusted Bills Work 
Group summary and recent actual performance benchmarks.
Threshold (target) = 3 year avg, floor = .2 less 
than threshold, offset = .2 above highest of CY 
12,13, 14 or 15 YTD.  KEDLI targets further 
reduced by .11% to normalize for future inclusion 
of estimate->actual conditioner (average 
difference Jan-Jun 2015).  KEDNY targets further 
increased by .15% to normalize for historical 
change from Overland audit findings (average 
difference July-Dec 2014).
Payment Processing
Payment processing is a new metric that measures exception payments that result from 
processing or procedural exceptions due to company error.  The metric is a measure of the 
number of exceptions due to company error, per 100,000 payments processed. 
Offset = Top 6 months averaged                               
Threshold = Bottom 6 months averaged                   
Floor = Bottom 3 months averaged   
Percent Appointments kept
The appointments kept metric is currently a performance indicator with the following revised 
definition:  An appointment will now be defined as any scheduled meeting between the Company 
and the customer (or customer’s representative) at the customer’s premises in which a date and 
time or timeframe is agreed upon by both parties.  The metric will apply to appointments made 
and performed by the Company’s Customer Meter Services (“CMS”) organization, which 
constitute more than 95 percent of appointments performed by KEDNY and KEDLI employees.  
Examples of appointments included in the metric are meter reads, turn-ons or offs, and meter 
changes.  Under the proposed metric, an appointment is considered kept if the Company 
satisfies the terms of the appointment (date, time, and location).  In addition, an appointment is 
considered kept (i) if the customer is unavailable at the premises when the Company arrives 
within the scheduled appointment window, or (ii) the customer is available at the premises 
before the scheduled window with prior communication of the adjusted time made to the 
customer.  The proposed definition takes into account discussions of the ongoing working group 
in the Focused Operations Audit of Utilities’ Reported Data (Case 13-M-0314).
Offset = 1σ above mean, threshold = 2σ below 
mean, and floor = 3σ below mean.  Levels are 
based on historic appointment metrics and 
performance would need to be re-baselined if 
appointment definitions/classifications change
IVR self-service rate
This is a new metric measuring the percent of customers using the IVR that are able to complete 
their task through the automated system
Offset = 1σ above mean, penalty threshold = 2σ 
below mean, and penalty floor = 3σ below mean
Contact Center Service 
Level
No change to the current performance metric is proposed for KEDNY.  This is a new metric for 
KEDLI.  A new target was determined for KEDNY based on historical data, and the service level 
for KEDNY was used to establish the threshold for KEDLI.  Service level is measured as percent 
of calls answered by a live customer service agent in less than 30 seconds. Calls handled and 
abandoned in the IVR are not part of the service level calculation.
KEDNY offset = 1σ above mean, penalty threshold 
=  historic mean, and penalty floor = 1σ below 
mean.  To align performance metrics, KEDLI 
threshold, floor and offset are set to the more 
stringent KEDNY levels. 
Satisfaction with customer 
service provided
No change is proposed to the current definitions and targets for customer satisfaction for 
KEDNY and KEDLI.  The Companies are proposing a penalty floor at 2σ below the existing 
penalty threshold levels and potential offset at 1σ above the existing threshold. The Companies 
are investigating moving to a new metric consistent with the methodology used by Niagara 
Mohawk.  The Niagara Mohawk methodology rates satisfaction, via % answering 8, 9, or 10, to 
the following question: “28) Overall, on a scale from 1 to 10, where 1 means Dissatisfied and 10 
means satisfied, how satisfied are you with the services provided by National Grid?" 
Offset = 1σ above current metric level, threshold = 
existing metric levels, and floor = 2σ below 
existing metric level
Escalated Complaints
This is an existing metric – with no change to definition.  This metric measures the number of 
escalated complaints per 100K Customers.   For KEDLI, the approach of using historical 
performance less two standard deviations would result a reduced standard (1.35 complaints per 
100,000 customers) vs the current target of 1.1 complaints.  Thus, KEDLI proposes to retain the 
existing more stringent standard of 1.1 complaints per 100,000 customers.  KEDNY's historical 
data, less two standard deviations results in a new, more rigorous standard of 1.05 complaints 
per 100,000 customers.  
KEDLI: Offset =  1σ above mean, threshold = 
existing target of 1.1, and penalty floor = 3σ below 
mean.  KEDNY: Offset = 1σ above existing metric, 
penalty threshold = 2σ below existing metric, and 
penalty floor = 3σ below existing metric.   
Performance targets are contingent upon adding 2 
FTEs to handle escalated complaints, one for each 
company.

B.   Service Line Proceeding 
6 
Q. 
Please summarize the Service Line Proceeding. 
7 
A. 
In 2015, the Commission adopted a new definition of service line in New York that 
8 
extends the service line “to the outlet of the customer meter or at the connection to a 
9 
customer’s piping, whichever is further downstream where a meter is located within 
10 
the building.”  This new definition significantly expands the scope of facilities 
11 
subject to leak survey and corrosion inspection requirements to include inside, 
12 
customer owned piping for buildings with inside meters.  While the Commission 
13 
adopted the new service line definition, in recognition of the significant operational 
14 
implications for local distribution companies, it stayed implementation to allow 
15 
time to develop a survey/inspection framework and schedule for addressing the 
16 
expanded definition.

On March 1, 2016, Staff released its “Straw Proposal for the Adoption of Gas 
1 
Service Line Leakage Survey and Corrosion Inspection Requirements” (“Straw

3

4

5

6

7 
sampling and related work is currently underway to develop these risk based

assessments, with an expected completion date of early 2017.  Upon completion of 
9 
this work, Staff recommends that LDCs develop individual survey and inspection 
10 
plans, and that each LDC present the plans to the Commission no later than 
11 
September 2017.
