STATEMENT OF NEUTRALITY OF SIERRA CLUB AND NATURAL RESOURCES 
DEFENSE COUNCIL  
 
The Sierra Club and Natural Resources Defense Council respectfully submit this 
statement of neutrality regarding the Joint Proposal (Proposal). As set forth below, while our 
organizations appreciate the progress that the Joint Proposal represents over Niagara Mohawk’s 
initial rate case filing concerning compliance with New York’s Climate Leadership and 
Protection Act (CLCPA), and do not oppose the Proposal’s approval, we believe that in 
subsequent rate cases a considerably more robust CLCPA analysis should be required in both the 
utility’s initial filing and in support of any proposed settlement. Further, given that investments 
in the gas distribution system being made today and approved in current rate cases have 
significant implications for utilities’ ability to achieve the deep greenhouse gas emission 
reductions mandated by the CLCPA, additional guidance from the Commission about how to 
decarbonize the building sector is urgently needed. We recommend that the Commission develop 
a statewide Gas System Transition Plan (Transition Plan) to identify the lowest-cost path for 
decarbonizing the state’s gas distribution system as a whole and each fossil gas utility’s system.

I. 
Action on Climate to Ensure Compliance with the CLCPA Is Critical 
 
New York—like the planet more broadly—is facing a climate crisis. The recently-
released 2021 report from the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change indicates that without 
deep reductions in greenhouse gas emissions in the immediate future, global warming is likely to 
exceed 3.8 degrees Fahrenheit (2 degrees Celsius)—a point at which environmental impacts of 
climate change are anticipated to become much more severe.1 New York is already experiencing 
the effects of climate change in the form of increased temperatures (up 2.4 degrees Fahrenheit 
since 1970), higher sea levels (up one foot since 1900), and devastating storms.2 Rising 
temperatures are putting New York residents’ health in danger—especially in dense urban 
communities subject to the heat island effect.3 Climate change will endanger many native New 
York species4 and will also affect agriculture.5 Further, the sea level may rise between 18 and 50 
inches by 2100,6 which could lead to 100-year floods occurring 19 times more often by 2100 
than they do today.7 This puts at risk New York residents’ homes, the state’s transportation 
systems, and the state’s energy and telecommunications systems.8

The present-day effects of extreme, climate change-induced weather hit home in New 
York this fall. In September, parts of New York and New Jersey were inundated by rainfall from 
Hurricane Ida, a climate-strengthened storm that initially made landfall a thousand miles away in 
Louisiana. The flooding killed at least 18 people in New York City and caused millions of 
dollars in damage.9 
The window for averting the worst impacts of climate change is rapidly closing.10 While 
the Climate Action Council’s Scoping Plan will likely provide important guidance for utility 
planning, it will not be finalized for several years and as the PSC has previously determined, 
CLCPA Sections 7(2) and (3) apply to rate cases decided today. Achieving an equitable 
transition of New York’s gas and electric service requires early action to manage costs and 
protect vulnerable customers from undue energy burdens that will grow over time. Delayed 
action will result in shortened amortization periods for gas assets, which increases the cost and 
equity challenges presented by transitioning off fossil gas use. The span of this rate plan covers 
more than one tenth of the remaining time for achieving New York’s target of net zero 
greenhouse gas emissions. It is therefore important for rate case resolutions to appropriately 
address the requirements of the CLCPA by laying a prudent path for a just transition of their 
electric and gas systems off climate-warming fossil fuels.

The Company’s initial proposals regarding so-called “renewable” natural gas

III. 
Despite the Important Progress Reflected in the Joint Proposal, Much More Should 
Be Required in Future Rate Cases to Ensure Compliance with the CLCPA 
 
While we appreciate the progress made in this rate case and do not oppose approval of 
the Joint Proposal, to address the requirements of Section 7 of the CLCPA going forward, all 
future rate case filings should be required to include a robust CLCPA analysis that quantifies the 
aggregate impact of all of the utility’s programs and proposals in the case and impacts to 
disadvantaged communities. And while halting growth of gas use is a critical step, there is no 
time to lose in utilities taking additional steps to begin reducing gas use and planning for 
contraction of the gas system. 
In its August 12, 2021 order approving the settlement proposal in National Grid’s 
Downstate affiliates’ rate cases, the Commission affirmed that CLCPA Section 7(2) and (3) are 
“applicable to rate cases.” Aug. 12 Order at 70. CLCPA Section 7(2) requires state agencies to 
“consider whether [their] decisions are inconsistent with or will interfere with the attainment of

10 
 
the statewide greenhouse gas emissions limits established” by the CLCPA. In a parallel manner, 
CLCPA Sections 7(3) requires that decisions by state agencies “shall not disproportionately 
burden disadvantaged communities.” 
While we are mindful that the present rate case was filed in the absence of guidance from 
the Commission about how CLCPA Sections 7(2) and (3) were intended to be applied to rate 
cases—and before the Commission’s order affirming their application to rate cases—and we are 
sensitive to the timing of the current Joint Proposal in this case and the potential for any further 
delays to exacerbate issues of rate compression, in future rate cases more analysis is needed. 
Specifically, we urge the Commission to require detailed CLCPA Section 7(2) and (3) analyses 
by the utility to support its initial rate case filing and separate analyses to support any subsequent 
settlement proposal that differs materially from the initial proposal. 
Moreover, the CLCPA Section 7(2) analysis should incorporate the most current 
guidance from State agencies in accordance with CLCPA definitions about how to quantify the 
impacts of non-carbon greenhouse gases. The CLCPA requires accounting for the 20 year global 
warming potential (GWP), as well as the emissions associated with  “the extraction and 
transmission of fossil fuels imported into the state.” Env. Conserv. L. 75-0101(13). As a result, 
the GWP of natural gas has increased from 117 lbs/mmbtuCO2e, to 182-235 lbs/mmbtuCO2e. 
NYS Climate Action Council July 22, 2021 meeting presentation, at 26.  The analysis must also 
incorporate the interim criteria identified for a disadvantaged community until the Climate 
Justice Working Group establishes criteria for defining disadvantaged communities. 
In addition, the Commission should expeditiously move forward with a generic gas future 
proceeding consistent with the recommendations laid out by NRDC, Sierra Club and other public 
interest organizations in their May 3, 2021 comments in Case 20-G-0131. There is a fundamental

IV. 
Commission Action Is Needed to Advance Electrification of Medium- and Heavy-
Duty Vehicles 
 
Electrifying the transportation sector--which is the largest source of greenhouse gas 
emissions in New York--is critical to achieving the State’s CLCPA mandates and climate goals. 
Governor Hochul has also signed into law a goal for 100% EVs by 2035, and 100% electric 
trucks and buses by 2045. To support these vehicles and their necessary charging infrastructure, 
utilities will play a vital role in helping to build out a robust charging station network and 
optimize EVs usage of the electric grid to put downward pressure on rates for all customers.   
In addition to the climate benefits of EVs, electrifying medium- and heavy-duty vehicles 
(such as delivery vehicles, long-haul trailers, school buses, etc) will help to reduce toxic air 
pollution and improve health in the communities that these vehicles traverse and serve. 
Therefore, it is critical that utilities--in addition to supporting light-duty vehicles--also consider 
support for electrifying trucks and buses.   
While the Joint Proposal will help to advance electrification of light-duty vehicles, it 
misses a vital opportunity to support medium-and heavy-duty vehicle electrification. The 
electrification of the light-duty sector was discussed extensively by the Commission’s July 2020 
Make-Ready Order in the generic electric vehicle (“EV”) docket (18-E-0138). However, the 
medium-and heavy-duty vehicle sector is left largely unaddressed in the Order, accounting for

V. 
Conclusion 
 
As discussed above, the Joint Proposal represents an important improvement over the 
Company’s initial rate case filing. Because we are sensitive to the need to resolve this case to 
avoid further rate compression but believe that a significantly more robust CLCPA Section 7(2) 
and (3) compliance analysis will be required to demonstrate consistency with the CLCPA in rate 
cases going forward, we are remaining neutral on this proposal. 
We also note that the real world value of many of the Company’s commitments in the 
Joint Proposal will depend on how seriously and rigorously the Company implements them. In

14 
 
particular, the value of the Company’s commitment to “endeavor to achieve” the identified billed 
gas usage reductions will depend greatly on how whole-heartedly the Company implements the 
five strategies it has identified for achieving those reductions. This is also true for the Company’s 
CLCPA study both in terms of the robustness of the scenarios and strategies that the Company 
analyzes and also in how meaningfully the Company engages stakeholders and incorporates their 
feedback. A similar study in Massachusetts being conducted on behalf of the state’s three 
distribution utilities (including National Grid), has benefited greatly from ongoing stakeholder 
engagement and input.19 Finally, as noted above, the value of the Company’s NPA commitments 
will hinge on how rigorous the Company is in its identification of opportunities for NPAs and 
how zealously the Company promotes non-gas alternatives to existing and prospective 
customers.
