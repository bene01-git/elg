Q. 
How does the Company’s capital structure affect its financial health? 
7 
A. 
The Company’s financial health is dependent on having the cash flows necessary to meet 
8 
financial obligations and support business operations.  Increasing the proportion of debt in 
9 
the capital structure (i.e., increasing financial leverage) can undermine the utility’s credit 
10 
quality by: (i) increasing the firm’s fixed interest payment obligations; and (ii) lowering 
11 
the amount of equity in the capital structure.  This leads to lower earnings and cash flow to 
12 
support rate base and operations, and results in less of an equity cushion in the balance 
13 
sheet to buffer debt holders from fluctuations in cash flows.  Taken together, higher 
14 
payment obligations, lower earnings and cash flows, and less equity cushion to absorb 
15 
fluctuations in cash flows increases the risk of financial default and therefore weakens 
16 
credit ratings.  
17 
 
18

19 
terms.  The more debt on the balance sheet, the less capacity the Company has to issue 
20 
additional debt.  This is a particular concern for capital intensive businesses that provide 
21 
essential services, like KEDNY, which require uninterrupted access to external funds to 
22 
meet the needs of business-as-usual operations as well as to potentially finance large,

B. KEDNY’s Credit Ratings History 
4 
Q. 
What are credit ratings and how are they determined by rating agencies? 
5 
A. 
Credit ratings are a measure of a borrower’s ability to meet its financial obligations and 
6 
reflect the risk that a borrower will not make its contractual debt service payments and the 
7 
potential severity of loss in the event of a default.  Credit rating agencies consider both 
8 
quantitative factors (e.g., credit metrics) and qualitative factors (e.g., the quality of the 
9 
business and its operating environment) when assessing credit quality.  While the specific 
10 
metrics used vary, all three of the principal rating agencies focus on cash flow-to-debt 
11 
based credit metrics (or debt-to-cash flow in the case of Fitch) as the main driver of their 
12 
quantitative rating assessments.  For regulated utilities, the agencies focus on factors such 
13 
as the legislative and regulatory environment and a utility’s ability to recover costs on a 
14 
timely basis when determining their qualitative rating assessments. 
15 
 
16 
Borrowers with higher default risk have lower credit ratings and must pay higher rates as 
17 
compensation for that increased risk.  They also face a more limited pool of investors which 
18 
can make it difficult to attract capital on suitable terms, particularly during periods of 
19 
market disruptions.  Credit ratings are, therefore, important to a borrower’s ability to attract 
20 
capital and heavily influence the pricing and contractual terms at which securities may be 
21 
issued.

7 
A. 
The Company proposes to set base rates based on the interest rates presented on Exhibit __

9

14 
  
15 
Q.  
Why is a long-term debt true-up mechanism appropriate?  
16 
A. 
There has been significant volatility in long-term interest rates over the past few years. For 
17 
example, the 30-year Treasury yield fell by nearly 120 basis points in 2019, and then 
18 
increased more than four-fold from a low of 0.99 percent in March of 2020 to a high of 
19 
4.40 percent in October 2022.  The volatility in interest rates increases the likelihood that 
20 
actual cost of debt rates for future debt issuances will be either lower or higher than 
21 
assumed.  The Company always strives to issue debt at the most opportune times and in 
22 
the most efficient manner.  Interest rates, however, are set by the market, with the influence 
23

2

3 
higher than forecast.  In this way neither customers nor the Company are disadvantaged.
