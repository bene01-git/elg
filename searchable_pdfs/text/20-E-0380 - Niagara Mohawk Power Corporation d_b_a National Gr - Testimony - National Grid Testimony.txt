Q. 
PLEASE DESCRIBE THE ISSUE WITH NATIONAL GRID’S USE OF 
16 
LOAD MODIFIERS IN CONNECTION WITH ANCILLARY SERVICES 
17 
CHARGES. 
18 
 A. 
National Grid uses Load Modifiers to avoid NYISO Ancillary Services.  The 
19 
NYISO assigns Ancillary Service costs to all Load Serving Entities (“LSEs”) 
20 
proportionately based on load share.  However, National Grid uses behind-the-
21 
meter generation in its service territory to offset some of its load that is reported to 
22 
the NYISO.  As a result, National Grid is assigned a smaller portion of the total

Q. 
HOW ARE NATIONAL GRID LOAD MODIFIERS EXPECTED TO 
5 
CHANGE GOING FORWARD? 
6 
A. 
With new load modifying generation from new distributed energy resources 
7

10 
complete.1  These and other new load modifiers will continue to increase the 
11 
difference between National Grid’s Ancillary Services costs and the costs other 
12 
LSEs expend in the market for Ancillary Services.       
13 
Q. 
HOW DOES THIS AFFECT OGS? 
14 
A. 
OGS pays for Ancillary Services for all of the kWh of commodity service that 
15 
OGS supplies.  In total, OGS supplied almost 700 million kWh and paid over $2 
16 
million in Ancillary Services for 2020.  National Grid, on the other hand, only 
17 
paid for Ancillary Services for a portion of its total LSE load due to its use of load 
18 
modifiers.  This has the dual effect of decreasing costs for National Grid 
19 
commodity customers, while simultaneously increasing costs for all the other 
20 
market participants.  The total amount of Ancillary Service costs that the NYISO
