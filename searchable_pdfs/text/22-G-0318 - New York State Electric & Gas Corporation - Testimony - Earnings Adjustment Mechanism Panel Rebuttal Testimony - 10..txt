III. 
DER UTILIZATION EAMS 
1 
Q. 
Does the Panel agree with the Staff Panel’s proposal to strike the “Turn Around Time for 
2 
CESIR Studies” and “Percent of Developers who pass the checkout on the first try” 
3 
EAMs?  
4 
A. 
No.  The Staff Panel proposed cutting these EAMs on the basis that they are similar to 
5 
those eliminated in the Order Eliminating Interconnection EAMs.  The Companies’ 
6 
proposed DER Utilization EAMs are distinguishable from the interconnection EAMs that 
7 
the Commission ordered to be eliminated in that proceeding.  Unlike the EAM at issue in 
8 
that case – the scoring for which involved the weighing of developer responses to 
9 
customer satisfaction surveys – here, the Companies’ proposed EAMs incentivize 
10 
measurable improvements in the expedited completion of two discrete tasks required to 
11 
bring a new DER onto the system: completion time of a CESIR study and successful 
12 
checkout rate for DER developers.  These two EAMs more closely resemble a customer 
13 
service-focused EAM, rather than a true “Interconnection EAM” in that they support 
14 
developers’ ability to bring new projects online more quickly.  In addition, it should be 
15 
noted that the CLCPA was passed by the New York State Legislature two months after 
16 
issuance of the Order Eliminating Interconnection EAMs.  The CLCPA dramatically 
17 
altered the outlook and importance of utility investments in incorporating new generation 
18 
resources (particularly renewables) onto New York’s electric grid.  In light of the shift in 
19 
priorities signaled and required by this landmark legislation, it is reasonable to revisit and 
20 
reconsider certain pre-CLCPA positions and Orders.  Accordingly, for the reasons 
21 
discussed below, these two DER Utilization EAMs, which the Staff Panel recommends 
22 
be eliminated, are worthy programs and should be approved because they further the
