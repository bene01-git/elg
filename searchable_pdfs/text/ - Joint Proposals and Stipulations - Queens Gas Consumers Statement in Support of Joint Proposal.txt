A.
TC Annual Cap
The Queens Gas Consumers recoimnend that the Joint Proposal be adopted by the
Commission as being in the public interest. In particular, the Queens Gas Consumers support the
revision of the annual price cap for customers served under Service Classification (“S.C.”) 6M —
TC Service Multi-Family. As set forth below, the Joint Proposal revises the annual price cap for
this service classification to reflect the appropriate equivalent firm service for these customers.
(See Section 2.4)
Under current rates,
the annual price cap for S.C. 6M service was set at the annual
revenue, including surcharges, that would be derived if such customers received service under
S.C. 2-2. S.C. 2-2, provides general firm service to non-residential customers. However, S.C. 3
— Firm Heating Service Multi-Family, is the appropriate equivalent service for S.C. 6-M
customers.
In general, the rate under S.C. 2-2 is higher than S.C. 3.
Accordingly, the
unfortunate result of linking the annual price cap to S.C. 2-2 rather than the equivalent firm rate,
is that in some months, S.C. 6M customers could pay more for TC service than the equivalent
firm service (i.e., S.C. 3 — Firm Heating Service — Multi-Family).
The Commission has traditionally set the price cap for interruptible or TC service “based
upon rates paid by comparable firm service customers.”2
As the Commission has explained,
“[tjhe cap exists because firm service is superior to TC and interruptible services and customers

B.
Individually-Negotiated Transportation Only Agreements
In order to encourage economic development in the Company’s service territory and
provide greater unbundled service options to customers, the Joint Proposal provides that the
Company “will file a new rate schedule for TC transportation (SC- 1 8-6n) that will allow for
negotiated TC transportation rates” for qualified S.C.
6 customers. (Section 2.3.1.)
The
provision of negotiated transportation rate option for S.C. 6 customers is consistent with both the
Commission’s vision for the natural gas industry in New York and prior precedent.
Since the mid-1980’s, the Commission has required local gas distribution companies
(“LDCs”) to unbundle their tariffs.4
As part of its vision for the natural gas industry in New
York, the Commission sought to, among other things, increase customer choice in both gas
supply and delivery service options.5 The provision of an individually-negotiated transportation
rate option meets both goals.
The availability of an attractive negotiated transportation rate
would further unbundle costs, potentially allowing eligible customers to stabilize transportation

Conclusion
The provisions of the Joint Proposal
— individually and collectively
— are reasonable and
their adoption is in the public interest. Furthermore, the provisions were the subject of extensive
settlement discussions and reflect numerous, interrelated compromises made by the signatories to
the Joint Proposal, including the Queens Gas Consumers. In fact, the incorporation and totality
of such compromises are essential to the Queens Gas Consumers’ support. Where the terms of a
Joint Proposal are reasonable and in the public interest, as is the case here, the Commission
should be extremely reluctant to upset the delicate balance of interests achieved by the settling
parties.
For all the foregoing reasons, the Queens Gas Consumers urges the Commission to adopt,
expeditiously and without modification,
the Joint Proposal
negotiated
and filed in this
proceeding.
