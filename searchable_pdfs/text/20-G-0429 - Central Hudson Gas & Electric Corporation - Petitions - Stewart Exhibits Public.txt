John D. Stewart 
Vice President 
Mr. Stewart has over 35 years of experience on utility finance, energy and regulatory policy issues at 
Concentric and the New York State Public Service Commission (the “Commission"). Since joining 
Concentric in 2011 Mr. Stewart has worked on a wide variety of regulatory matters in rate cases and issue 
specific proceedings. Over the last three and a half years, Mr. Stewart has been the leader of a Concentric 
team working for New York’s investor owned electric utilities in the Reforming the Energy Vision 
proceeding. He is also an integral member of Concentric’s Future of Energy team. 
Prior to working at Concentric Mr. Stewart spent three decades at the New York Public Service Commission 
where he testified as an expert witness and presented analyses directly to the Commission on a range of 
topics including prudence matters, generation restructuring and divestiture, rate of return, capital structure, 
financial integrity/cash flow adequacy, mergers and acquisitions, revenue decoupling, demand side 
management, and renewable resources. His final positions at the Commission were first as the Director of 
the Office of Accounting, Finance, and Audits and then as the Managing Director of the Commission’s 
Offices of Accounting & Finance, Regulatory Economics, Energy Efficiency and the Environment, and 
Electric Gas & Water. In his final position Mr. Stewart was a senior technical and policy advisor to the 
Commission with direct reporting responsibilities to the Chairman. 
REPRESENTATIVE EXPERIENCE 
Rate Case Work 
Experience at Concentric addressing capital structure, return on equity, and general cost of service estimates in 
rate cases, reliability must run proceedings, and general rate inquiries from regulators. Drafted legislation  defining 
the regulatory regime for a governmental body in the Caribbean. Extensive rate case experience as a member of 
Commission rate case teams, a supervisor of Commission rate case teams and then an advisor to the Commission 
on rate case matters. Prior to assuming the Director’s position at the Commission, full responsibility for all 
accounting, finance and ratemaking issues related to Con Edison. 
Revenue Decoupling, Energy Efficiency and Renewable Resources 
Provided analyses to the Commission in 1989 demonstrating why the adoption of the first Electric Revenue 
Adjustment Mechanism (ERAM) in NY (Orange and Rockland Electric) was in the interest of shareholders and 
the public.  Provided input and direction to staff in other cases supporting Commission adoption of ERAMs  and 
GRAMs for most of the State’s other energy utilities in the early 1990s. Provided recommendations to Commission 
concerning instituting a proceeding (2007) to explore the desirability of re-establishing revenue decoupling 
mechanisms (RDMs) for electric and gas utilities. Assisted in the development of the mechanics of per class and 
per customer RDMs now used for electric and gas customers, respectively.

Utility Mergers, Acquisitions and Corporate Structure Changes 
Provided financial analyses to the Commission related to the restructuring of the electric utility industry in NY in 
the late 1990s. Worked as part of a Staff team on the National Grid-KeySpan merger and advised the Commission 
on financial and ratemaking matters related to the Iberdrola acquisition of Energy East 
. 
Prudence 
In his role as a senior technical and policy advisor at the Commission, Mr. Stewart was routinely involved in internal 
discussions regarding prudence matters both within and outside of rate cases. Mr. Stewart was also directly involved 
in the prudence review regarding the cost of constructing the Shoreham nuclear plant by the Long Island Lighting 
Company (“LILCO”). In that role Mr. Stewart was part of a 5-person team that was asked by the Chairman to 
resolve a Commission prudence Order that the Courts had remanded back to the Commission. This team 
eventually resolved this matter with LILCO as part of a larger Federal Court RICO settlement. 
Performance Based Regulation 
Responsible for analysis of financial, accounting, ratemaking, and general policy implications raised by all multi- 
year rate plans coming before Commission since 2006. Responsible for financial issues in the rate case negotiations 
which produced NY’s first multi-year rate plan (Con Edison 1987) and first price cap rate plan (Rochester 
Telephone 1988).
