A. Goals of Gas Safety Performance Targets and Incentives 
12 
Q. 
What is the purpose of gas safety metrics and performance targets? 
13 
A. 
The Companies’ current rate plans establish performance metrics and targets to 
14 
ensure the Companies meet their regulatory requirements to provide safe and 
15 
reliable service to customers.  The metrics measure the Companies’ 
16 
performance across various activities, ranging from leak-prone pipe (“LPP”) 
17 
retirement to emergency response.  Metrics may include negative revenue 
18 
adjustments (“NRAs”) or positive revenue adjustments (“PRAs”) if the 
19 
Companies’ performance varies from established levels.  NRAs serve to

Q. 
Please explain why inclusion of PRAs and incentives are appropriate. 
1 
A. 
PRAs do not simply reward the Companies for adequate performance or 
2 
business-as-usual activities.  PRAs incentivize the Companies to perform 
3 
additional work and provide enhanced services, such as incremental leak repair, 
4 
that benefit customers and the community and further aligns the Companies’ 
5 
performance with public policy objectives.  Appropriately challenging PRAs 
6 
support continuous improvement and superior performance with the goal of 
7 
exceeding customer expectations and industry standards.  The Commission 
8 
commonly uses them for this purpose, most recently when approving 
9 
Consolidated Edison of New York’s (“Con Edison”) joint proposal (Case 22-
10 
G-0065). 
11 
 
12 
Q. 
Are metrics appropriate for a one-year rate plan? 
13 
A. 
No.  The gas safety performance metrics are designed to drive performance 
14 
improvements over time.  To that end, the Companies’ proposals for 
15 
performance metrics contemplate a multi-year rate plan.  The Companies do not 
16 
agree with the inclusion of performance metrics in a one-year litigated case.  The 
17 
Companies submit that their performance history in this area (having met or 
18 
exceeded all gas safety metrics over the term of the current rate plan) should 
19 
provide the Commission with sufficient comfort that safety performance will not 
20 
be diminished in a one-year rate plan. 
21

1 
Q.

2

As discussed above, under the terms of the current rate plan, the Companies’ 
3

6 
 
7

8 
A. 
The Companies have historically used NRA balances to fund selected safety 
9 
programs for the benefit of customers.  Such treatment is consistent with how 
10 
other utilities across New York State, including Con Edison and Niagara 
11 
Mohawk Power Corporation (“Niagara Mohawk”), have used similar deferral 
12 
balances.  The Joint Proposal from the 2019 KEDNY and KEDLI Rate Cases 
13 
(at 115) specifically provides that “negative revenue adjustment[s] incurred will 
14 
be deferred for future use to fund gas safety and compliance improvement 
15 
programs.”  The 2019 Joint Proposal also provided for the use of NRAs to fund 
16 
the distribution of residential methane detectors (“RMDs”) and implementation 
17 
of additional surveys and system mapping.
