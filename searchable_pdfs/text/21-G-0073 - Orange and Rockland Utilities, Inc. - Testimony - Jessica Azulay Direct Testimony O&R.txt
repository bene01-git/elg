4. POLICY CONTEXT 
3 
Q.  
What is the policy context for your testimony on the Company’s gas investments?   
4 
A.  
In 2019, the New York State Legislature passed and Governor Cuomo signed the Climate 
5 
Leadership and Community Protection Act (“CLCPA”). The CLCPA requires economy-
6 
wide greenhouse gas emissions reductions in the amount of 40% from 1990 levels by 
7 
2030 and 85% by 2050.  The requirements apply not just to the electricity sector, but to 
8 
all sectors, including from on-site combustion in buildings and transportation. 
9 
Q.  
What is on-site combustion? That’s not a term most people are familiar with. 
10 
 
On-site combustion refers to the combustion (or burning) of fuels inside buildings to 
11 
make energy. For example, the combustion of fuel oil or fossil gas in a furnace to make 
12 
heat for a home is on-site combustion. Other examples include the burning of fossil gas 
13 
or propane in the stove in someone’s kitchen to cook food, or a fireplace that burns gas or 
14 
wood. Basically, when there is a fuel that is being burned inside a building to make 
15 
energy, that is on-site combustion.  
16 
Q. 
You used the term “fossil gas” above. What is that? 
17 
 
I use the term “fossil gas” to refer to what the utilities and the gas industry call “natural 
18 
gas.” Specifically, fossil gas is a fossil fuel consisting mostly of methane that is extracted 
19 
from the Earth. I use the term “fossil gas” to differentiate it from other gasses.

24

25

26 
pumps), cold-climate air source heat pumps, and induction cooktops and ranges.   
27 
Heat pumps use a relatively small amount of energy, supplied by electricity, to capture 
28 
and move thermal energy stored in the ground or the air to provide indoor heating and

7. RECOMMENDATIONS 
7 
Q.  
What are your recommendations? 
8 
A.  
It’s time to consider an entirely different approach to the utility gas business. This needs 
9 
to start with a recognition that New York’s greenhouse gas goals will necessitate the 
10 
eventual elimination of most if not all of the fossil gas used for heating. Once we 
11 
understand that the era of the utility gas distribution system is coming to an end, we can 
12 
consider how best to enable the transition to renewable heating. Rather than seeing 
13 
themselves as a gas utility, Orange & Rockland prepare for either for a managed phase 
14 
out or a transition to an “energy” or “thermal” utility, with the purpose of serving 
15 
customers with electricity and thermal (heat or cooling) energy.
