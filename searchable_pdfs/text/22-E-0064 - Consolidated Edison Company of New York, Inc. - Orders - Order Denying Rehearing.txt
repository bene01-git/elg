STANDARD OF REVIEW 
 
 
Under Public Service Law §22 and the Commission’s 
regulations at 16 NYCRR §3.7(b), a request for rehearing must 
establish that a Commission action committed errors of law or 
fact sufficient to grant rehearing, or that new circumstances 
warrant a different determination.  Additionally, under those 
provisions, a petition for rehearing must separately identify 
and specifically explain and support each alleged error or new 
circumstance said to warrant rehearing.   
 
PETITION FOR REHEARING AND RESPONSES 
AARP alleges that the Commission committed an error of 
fact in concluding that the Company’s energy affordability 
program for low-income customers (EAP) is “not under-enrolled by 
a significant number of customers”.2  AARP also argues that the 
Commission improperly rejected or ignored evidence concerning 
the number of individuals/households that are eligible but 
unenrolled in the Company’s EAP, including the negative effect 
upon Medicaid recipient customers of the City of New York’s 
Human Resources Agency’s (HRA) discontinuance of including 
Medicaid in the group of public benefit programs that had 
resulted in automatic enrollment of eligible customers in the

In response to AARP’s rehearing petition, the Company 
and Staff argue that AARP has not identified any actual error of 
fact in the Rate Order and, accordingly, the petition for 
rehearing should be denied.  Both responders maintain that, at 
most, AARP only establishes that the Commission, in evaluating 
the evidentiary record, drew different conclusions than those 
advanced by AARP.  Both responding parties identify 
countervailing facts in the record that support the conclusions 
made by the Commission that adopting the JP, as whole and 
including the EAP provisions, was in the public interest.  The 
responders identify gaps, speculation, or concessions in AARP’s 
and Public Utility Law Project’s (PULP’s) testimony in the 
record.  Con Edison and Staff, citing their respective pre and 
post hearing submissions, also discuss issues with the relevance 
and reliability of the data AARP claims the Commission ignored.  
Both responders note such factual analysis and evaluation is not 
the same as making a factual error, but that it is a Commission 
obligation attendant to rendering a decision on the case 
presented.

specifically explain and support each alleged error.  AARP’s

determined that they fail to establish any error sufficient to 
modify the Commission’s Rate Order.  As AARP has failed to 
demonstrate the core predicate of its contention that the
