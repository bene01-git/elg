1 
A. Beneficial Transportation Electrification Programs 
2 
Q. 
What are the new Beneficial Transportation Electrification Programs being proposed by 
3 
the Companies? 
4 
A. 
The Companies are proposing a Medium and Heavy-Duty Vehicle (“MHD”) Make-
5 
Ready Program, a Municipal Curbside Charging Pilot, and an EV Charging Hub Pilot.   
6 
Q. 
How do these proposed programs align with State climate goals? 
7 
A. 
The proposed programs directly support the State’s goal of 100% of light-duty vehicle 
8 
sales being zero-emissions by 2035 and 100% of MHD vehicle sales being zero-
9 
emissions by 2045.1F2  The MHD Make-Ready Program would enable as many as 2,000 
10 
charging plugs that could support over 2,300 MHD vehicles, which will help create 
11 
momentum towards decarbonizing the MHD sector.  The EV Charging Hub Pilot will 
12 
support up to 80 direct current fast chargers (“DCFC”) in a single, strategically placed 
13 
location built to accommodate a variety of vehicle and charging types and showcase a 
14 
next-generation model for DCFC stations and infrastructure.  The Municipal Curbside 
15 
Charging Pilot will deploy up to 100 low-cost chargers in underserved communities with 
16 
the goal of driving down the cost of charging to a level that is comparable to the cost of 
17 
at-home charging.  These programs will accelerate the adoption of EVs in the 
18 
Companies’ service territory and support the greenhouse gas emissions reduction goals of 
19 
the CLCPA.    
20 
 
2  
See Environmental Conservation Law § 19-0306-b (1).

4

The Companies currently implement the following programs ordered by the Commission 
5 
in Case 18-E-0138: 1) DCFC Per Plug Incentive Program; 2) Light-Duty Make-Ready 
6 
Program; 3) Transit Bus Make-Ready; 4) MHD Make-Ready Pilot; and 5) Fleet 
7

8 
Q. 
Please describe the DCFC Per Plug Incentive Program. 
9 
A. 
The DCFC Per Plug Incentive Program is an annual declining per-plug incentive to 
10 
qualifying public DCFC operators intended to offset electric delivery costs incurred by 
11 
DCFC stations while charging utilization is low.  As of March 2022, NYSEG has 
12 
enrolled 19 DCFC plugs across three participants at five sites, and RG&E has enrolled 0 
13 
plugs.  These account for most of the new DCFC plugs installed in the Companies’ 
14 
service territory.  
15 
Q. 
Please describe the Light-Duty Make-Ready Program. 
16 
A. 
The program provides incentives for the installation of light-duty EV infrastructure for 
17 
both Level 2 (“L2”) and DCFC stations.  The program covers up to 100% of the eligible 
18 
costs needed to prepare a site for EV charging if all eligibility criteria are met, or 50% of 
19 
the costs if the station does not meet the public accessibility or standardized plug 
20 
eligibility requirements.  As of March 2022, the program has supported installation of
