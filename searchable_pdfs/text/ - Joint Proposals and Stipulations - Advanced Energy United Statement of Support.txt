Statement of Support of Advanced Energy United for the Earnings Adjustment 
Mechanism (EAM) portion of the Joint Proposal 
 
Advanced Energy United engaged in the settlement discussions related to the Earnings 
Adjustment Mechanisms (EAMs) only, and this statement of support is only for the sections of 
the Joint proposal related to EAMs. Advanced Energy United takes no position on any other 
parts of the Joint Proposal.  
 
Advanced Energy United has long supported the use of performance incentive mechanisms as 
a way to better align utility financial incentives with outcomes that drive customer benefits and 
help to achieve state policy goals. In New York, Earnings Adjustment Mechanisms (EAMs) were 
established by the Commission early on in the Reforming the Energy Vision proceeding and 
were appropriately intended by the Commission to provide meaningful financial incentives for 
utilities to pursue innovative solutions to meeting state policy objectives and provide benefits 
to customers. EAMs have continued to evolve over the last several years, and Advanced Energy 
United believes that the package of EAMs in the Joint Proposal is consistent with these 
overarching objectives. The EAMs in the Joint Proposal create incentives for Consolidated 
Edison (ConEd or the Company) to achieve higher levels of performance, above established 
baseline levels of performance, across a range of outcomes that support achievement of the 
Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act (CLCPA) and reduce customer costs.

The EAMs that directly support CLCPA achievement include the Smart Building Electrification 
EAM, the Light-Duty Vehicle Emissions EAM and the Transportation Interconnection Timeline 
EAM. The Demand Response and EV Managed Charging EAMs are focused on the broad 
outcome of reducing peak demand and should therefore reduce customer costs, given the 
relatively high cost of incremental capacity in ConEd service territory. The SBE EAM will also 
support long-term energy savings for customers and reduce the grid impacts of electrification. 
The two Distributed Energy Resource (DER) Utilization metrics for solar and storage are 
designed to bring the benefits of DERs to more customers, while simultaneously contributing 
to CLCPA goals. 
 
Lastly, Advanced Energy United supports the use of actual dollar amounts for setting reward 
levels, rather than basis points. Although basis points are used to initially determine reward 
levels, it is more appropriate to use dollars for the final targets. This avoids creating the 
situation where the Company may try to increase the size of its rate base as a means of 
increasing the size of the potential reward from achieving an EAM.
