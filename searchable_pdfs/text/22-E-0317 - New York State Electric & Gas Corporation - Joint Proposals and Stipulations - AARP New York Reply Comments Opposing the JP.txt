Dear Secretary Phillips and Judges Bergen, Lecakes and Overton: 
 
AARP New York appreciates this opportunity to provide a limited response to comments 
filed in support of and in opposition to all or parts of the Joint Proposal.  We agree with 
most of the arguments advanced by Alliance for a Green Economy, Campaign for 
Renewable Energy, Cedar Valley Townhomes, Climate Solutions Accelerator Fossil Free 
Thompkins, Multiple Intervenors, Public Utility Law Project, and Ratepayers and 
Community Intervenors, demonstrating the ways in which myriad provisions of the JP are 
not in the public interest.  The massive rate hikes recommended by the Companies,

Convergent Energy and Power, DPS Staff, International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers,

The main focus of AARP New York in these limited comments is on one element of the 
Commission’s standard of review, namely, whether “typically adverse” or “often adverse” 
parties support the agreement.  The Companies explained this perspective in their 
comments (at 1-2): 
This Proposal is supported (in total or in part) by the Companies and eight 
other diverse parties, including: the New York State Department of Public 
Service Staff (“Staff”); Convergent Energy and Power LP; International 
Brotherhood of Electrical Workers, Local Union 10 (“IBEW”); Multiple 
Intervenors (“MI”); New York Power Authority (“NYPA”); Nucor Steel Auburn, 
Inc. (“Nucor”); Utility Intervention Unit of the Division of Consumer Protection 
at the Department of State (“UIU”); and Walmart, Inc. (collectively, the 
“Signatory Parties”). [footnote omitted] The resulting broad base of support 
(in total or in part) for the Joint Proposal among these often adverse parties 
strongly supports adoption of the Joint Proposal by the Commission.1 
First, the Signatory Parties seem to believe that it is an unusual accomplishment for DPS 
Staff and the Companies to support the JP.  Putting aside the issue of whether DPS Staff and 
utilities are really adverse parties as that term is commonly understood, AARP New York 
observes that DPS Staff and the Companies are necessary parties supporting this JP.  That is, 
without the support of both of those parties, there would not be a JP.  Accordingly, to avoid 
relying on a tautology, when reviewing the claims that the JP enjoys support by a wide 
range of diverse parties with adverse interests, one must remove the support of DPS Staff 
and the Companies from consideration.2

Second, the New York Power Authority and the Utility Intervention Unit are sister state 
government entities of DPS and advance limited issues that are typically consistent with 
PSC policies and rate case orders.  Third, Convergent Energy will benefit financially from 
approval of the energy storage part of the JP.  Fourth, Nucor Steel Auburn and Walmart are 
large established businesses that can absorb delivery cost increases or pass them onto 
their customers. Fifth, IBEW did not file any comments. 
Sixth, although the UIU, which described itself in its initial comments (at 1) as representing 
the interests of consumers in utility proceedings with a particular focus on residential and 
small commercial customers, did not object to any of the JP’s provisions, the non-
government parties that are or represent the interests of customers (MI, PULP, and AARP 
New York along with Alliance for a Green Economy, Campaign for Renewable Energy, Cedar 
Valley Townhomes, Climate Solutions Accelerator Fossil Free Thompkins, and Ratepayers 
and Community Intervenors) urge rejection of the JP .  These are parties that often take 
positions adverse to utilities and DPS Staff. 
Seventh, some of Signatory Parties present confusing degrees of support for the JP, 
devoting much of their respective statements to their unique interests, but nevertheless 
directly or indirectly expressing support for the JP.  For example, Walmart stated at (2):  
Although Walmart did not address, and therefore does not take a position on, 
every substantive issue raised by the Companies or other parties in the course 
of these proceedings or resolved by the Joint Proposal and the settlement 
among the various parties, Walmart is a signatory to the Joint Proposal in full 
and supports the Joint Proposal and settlement in full as a just, reasonable, and 
fair resolution of the issues in the electric and natural gas cases. Walmart 
believes the Joint Proposal is in the best interest of the Companies, its 
customers, and the New York public. Accordingly, and for the following 
reasons specific to the issues raised by Walmart in the course of these 
proceedings, Walmart respectfully requests that the Joint Proposal be 
approved without modification.
