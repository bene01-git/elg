RE: CPA Statement in Support of the Joint Proposal 
 
Dear Secretary Phillips: 
 
Pursuant to your Ruling on Process and Schedule in the above referenced proceedings, please consider 
this letter as a Statement in Support of the Joint Proposal ("JP") filed on behalf of Consumer Power 
Advocates ("CPA"). CPA is an association of large, non-profit institutions whose primary goal is to 
decrease the cost of energy in an environmentally sustainable way by focusing on regulatory decisions 
and programs that impact energy consumers in New York City. 
 
CPA is a signatory to the JP in these proceedings, which covers a three year Con Edison electric and gas 
rate plan from January 1, 2026 through December 31, 2029. By this letter statement in support, CPA 
respectfully requests that your Honors recommend to the Commission adoption of the JP in the above 
referenced proceeding as agreed to by the party signatories (the “Parties”and filed with the Commission's 
Secretary.  
 
CPA believes that the provisions of the JP are in the best interest of the public in general and of the 
signatories in particular because of the significant negotiated reductions by the Parties from the amounts 
originally sought by the Company, as well as other provisions in the JP which reflect a negotiated 
compromise. In addition, the JP recognizes and endorses the commitment to billing transparency 
advocated in our testimony in this rate case. We are particularly pleased to see a commitment to publicize 
on individual electric and gas bills the impact of property taxes on such bills, starting no later than the 
beginning of Rate Year 2. 
 
Based upon the extensive period of negotiations and discussions among the Parties and Con Edison, held 
over a period of more than seven months in these cases, and after weighing the probable costs of further 
litigation, CPA believes that the JP confers upon the public and the Parties greater benefits than costs and 
falls within the expected range of results likely to realized in a fully litigated case. Accordingly, the JP 
should be approved by the Commission.
