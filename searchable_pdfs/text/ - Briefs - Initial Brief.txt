I.
Overview 
 
As we enter 2020, we enter the last decade the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate 
Change (IPPC) says that we have left to meaningful address climate change. If we do not 
dramatically reduce greenhouse gas emissions in the next ten years we will lock ourselves into 
irreversible, catastrophic global warming. We must also recognize that the dramatic growth in 
production from the Marcellus Shale through high-volume hydraulic fracturing means that the 
gas our New York State utilities are delivering has a different environmental impact than the gas 
they were delivering a decade ago. While keeping the IPCC's dire climate warning in mind and 
taking into account the scientific realities of the environmental impacts of fracked gas, it is time 
for the Public Service Commission (the Commission)  to reassess what “safe and reliable” 
service means, not only in the short-term but by looking at the long-term impacts the decisions 
made in this case will have on the viability of downstate New York as a safe place to live.  
 
It’s not just the world’s leading climate scientists telling us we must pivot immediately from 
business as usual and our dependence on fossil fuels. Laws passed by New York City and New 
York State last year also mandate significant reductions in greenhouse gas emissions in the 
next 10 years. With only 10 years left to make these drastic changes, spending even one more 
year and hundreds of millions of dollars headed in the wrong direction is iniquitous and will cost 
lives. 
 
National Grid’s (the Companies) proposals to continue to invest ratepayer funds to build and 
expand fracked gas infrastructure goes against climate science as well as city and state climate 
law. As discussed herein, and as demonstrated by the evidence in this proceeding, the 
Commission should reject the Companies’ proposed funding request for the following projects: 
Leak Prone Pipe (LPP) replacement, Metropolitan Reliability Infrastructure (MRI) project, new 
Compressed Natural Gas (CNG) facilities, expanding and extending the life of CNG and 
Liquefied Natural Gas (LNG) facilities, Clove Lakes Transmission Loop Project and Riverhead 
Transmission and Southeast Suffolk Projects.

LPP Summary 
  
The Companies have been presenting an incomplete picture of the climate impacts their system 
has and in doing so presenting a false solution in their LPP investments capabilities to reach 
greenhouse gas emission targets set by the IPCC and CLCPA. When the short-term potency of 
methane as a greenhouse gas is taken into account along with the full lifecycle of the fossil fuel, 
including upstream methane emissions and the emissions produced during combustion, which 
is now required by the CLCPA, it is not enough to reduce methane leakage within the system. 
To reach the level of emissions reduction outlined by the IPCC and CLCPA the Companies 
must begin to reduce the amount of gas delivered and consumed and begin retiring gas 
infrastructure. 
 
By relying on LPP replacement for a majority of emission reduction the Companies are investing 
hundreds of millions of dollars every year to maintain a delivery system that needs to be retired. 
Instead of looking at how to responsibly retire the thousands of miles of LPP, the Companies 
are betting on unproven technologies like hydrogen blending and renewable gas to prevent their 
investment in LPP from becoming a stranded asset.

IV.
CNG & LNG
 
Based on the evidence presented throughout this case the Commission should also reject the 
Company’s requested funding to expand and extend the life of their LNG and CNG facilities. 
 
During the evidentiary hearing on ​February 25, 2020  the Company testified that they have not 
23
done a greenhouse gas emissions assessment or evaluation of disadvantaged communities for 
the following projects: 1. Adding two new vaporizers to the Company’s Greenpoint LNG facility 
2. Adding portable LNG trucking capabilities at the Company's Greenpoint facility, 3.Double 
capacity at the Company’s Glenwood CNG facility 4. Adding two new CNG facilities within the 
Company’s service territory 
 
Greenhouse gas emissions evaluation is not only required by law under the CLCPA, but is 
critically important for LNG & CNG because as Sane Energy Project testified to in both Direct 
and Supplemental Testimony, and the Company recognizes in their recent report on Long-term 
solutions, LNG and CNG has larger greenhouse gas impact than other gas infrastructure.  
 
Expanding the Greenpoint and Glenwood facilities would increase greenhouse gas emissions, 
instead of reducing them in a disadvantaged community that has been burdened by 
environmental pollutants and is extremely vulnerable to climate change flooding  as the 
Company note in its Direct Testimony to justify investments in the Greenpoint facility LNG “The 
bulkhead is situated along Newton Creek and prevents land erosion from rising water level. The 
bulkhead may have hidden damage from past storms. This project was identified as a critical 
component during the Greenpoint Flood Study that resulted from the Company’s Storm 
Hardening Collaborative, because it provides structural integrity along Newtown Creek.”
