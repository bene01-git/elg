1. Introduction and Executive Summary 
New York State Electric & Gas Corporation (“NYSEG”) and Rochester Gas and Electric Corporation 
(“RG&E” and together with NYSEG, the “Companies”), submit this quarterly Non-Wires Alternative 
(“NWA”) progress report for the fourth quarter of 2021 in accordance with the November 19, 2020 
Electric and Gas Rate Plan Orders1.  The Companies’ quarterly NWA report includes project costs 
and expenditures, project activities, anticipated in-service dates, project cost and incentive 
recoveries and operational savings and other benefits. 
This quarterly report provides programmatic and financial details from October 1, 2021 through 
December 31, 2021 NWA activities and projects currently under consideration, which include 
NYSEG’s Stillwater and Java microgrid NWA projects. NYSEG is continuing to evaluate the potential 
for an NWA solution in the future to defer a full upgrade and rebuild of the Hilldale substation. The 
Java peak shaving project continues to be on hold, and except for the information provided in the 
Conclusion of this report and in Exhibit B, will not be included in this report. 
 
2. NWA Projects and Activities 
2.1 NYSEG NWA PROJECT: STILLWATER 
NYSEG plans to meet the Stillwater NWA project needs (i.e., projected overload and low voltage 
power quality issues) through a developer installed and operated 1 MW/2.9 MWh battery energy 
storage solution located roughly 1.8 miles from the Stillwater substation on the 4.8 kV distribution 
circuit. More details regarding the traditional solution and previously reported NWA activities can be 
found in Exhibit A. 
2.1.1 Description of NWA Activities 
NYSEG continues to work with the selected developer to implement the NWA project. In September 
2021, the developer sought and obtained approval from the Village of Stillwater to extend the 
required special use permit for an additional year through September 2022 with the goal of starting 
project construction prior to that date. NYSEG held several meetings with the developer in the third 
and fourth quarters to review interconnection details and the NWA contract. As of the date of this 
report, the NWA contract has been drafted by NYSEG and the developer and is pending execution by 
both parties. 
2.1.2 Project Costs and Expenditures 
The total Stillwater NWA project costs will be tracked and reported following NWA contract 
execution. 
2.1.3 Anticipated Project In-Service Date 
NYSEG anticipates that the Stillwater NWA battery storage project will be in service by December 
31, 2022, however this date may change given the recent project delays.

Java substation customers’ electric demand for up to approximately 12 hours (until the contingency 
condition is repaired or a mobile substation is rolled out).

The primary benefit of the Java microgrid NWA project will be the back-up supply provided to the

microgrid solution is more conducive to utility ownership than developer ownership, due to the 
complex nature of the technical solution required and the multiple grid benefits being provided.

support the development of advanced technologies and distributed energy resources (“DER”) such 
as battery storage, effectively across NYSEG’s service territory. Connecting battery storage at 
distribution substations can accelerate project development, reduce overall implementation costs, 
and provide benefits to a larger number of customers.

EXHIBIT A – NYSEG STILLWATER NWA PROJECT DETAILS 
NYSEG’s Stillwater substation (located in NYSEG’s Mechanicville Division, Town of Stillwater), 
serves approximately 1,270 residential and small commercial customers via (1) 2.8 MVA transformer 
bank and one (1) 4.8 kV distribution circuit where overload conditions and power quality (i.e., low 
voltage) issues are identified system needs. The proposed traditional wires solution alternative was 
to upgrade and replace the existing transformer bank with a new 14 MVA LTC 34.5-12.5 kV 
transformer and convert the distribution circuit to 12.47 kV, with an estimated cost of $15.15 
Million. With the Stillwater NWA project, NYSEG plans to defer for 10 years the implementation of 
this traditional wires solution. 
NYSEG issued a RFP in July 2017 for NWA resources to defer the costs of planned Stillwater 
substation upgrades, and chose a bidder project which consisted of a 1 MW/2.9 MWh battery 
storage solution to address the issue of the peak loading on the Stillwater substation transformer 
bank. The project point of interconnection to the existing 4.8 kV NYSEG distribution system is 
approximately 1.8 miles from the Stillwater substation, which is expected to minimize any power 
quality issues. 
In the 2021 First Quarter Report, NYSEG reported that the following steps and milestones were 
completed: relocation of preferred site; revisions to interconnection application consistent with 
relocation; local permitting; public outreach; and interpretation/application of the current tariffs to 
battery storage charging. In the 2021 Second Quarter Report, it was reported that NYSEG 
collaborated with DPS Staff on the BCA calculation to validate the methodology, assumptions, and 
results.  In the 2021 Third Quarter Report, NYSEG reported Developer’s receipt of approval of their 
request for extension of single use permit for the site, and progress with the NWA agreement.

EXHIBIT B – NYSEG JAVA NWA PROJECT DETAILS 
NYSEG’s Java electric substation in NYSEG’s Lancaster Division, town of Java, Wyoming County, is 
comprised of (1) 34.5-4.8 kV 5 MVA transformer serving approximately 1,700 residential and small 
commercial customers via two 4.8 kV distribution circuits; circuit #280 and circuit #281. The needs 
identified for either a traditional wires solution or an NWA solution (in total) were to reduce the peak 
loading on the individual transformer bank to below its nameplate rating of 5 MVA, address reliability 
and power quality issues that exist on the Java circuit #280 and address the potential risk of failure 
of the existing transformer. 
The proposed traditional wires solution alternative was to replace the existing 5 MVA transformer 
bank with two (2) new 14 MVA LTC 34.5-12.47 kV units, install 12.5 kV Gas Insulated Switchgear, 
and a new control house. The proposed wires alternative also includes a 12.5 kV conversion of Java 
circuit #280 and #281 and a new second 34.5 kV transmission line to create an in-and-out 
configuration to provide a redundant sub-transmission supply (“N-1”). The estimated costs for the 
proposed traditional solution alternative as of April 2019 were $35.8 million. 
NYSEG’s 2016 RFP sought to defer the costs of the traditional Java substation upgrade alternative. 
The selected bidder’s proposal utilized a battery storage solution to address the peak loading on the 
Java substation transformer bank and a separate battery storage solution to address the potential 
risk of failure of the existing transformer. 
After discussions with the selected bidder, NYSEG separated the NWA project into two independent 
NWA projects to: address liability concerns in the event of unforeseen events impacting customers; 
address technical and operational complexities which would occur when operating a battery as an 
electrical “island” or microgrid; and to address potential issues associated with the battery system 
acting as the sole temporary electrical supply to all Java customers following the N-1 event until 
normal supply is restored. The resulting two projects are the Java peak shaving NWA project 
designed to address the peak shaving need which would be owned by a selected developer, and the 
Java microgrid NWA project to address the potential risk of failure of the existing transformer which 
will be owned by NYSEG. 
The Java peak shaving NWA project would interconnect a 1 MW/5 MWh lithium ion battery energy 
storage system to the existing 4.8 kV NYSEG distribution circuit #280 at 401 Holland Road, Arcade, 
NY, which is approximately 0.3 miles from the Java substation, and would defer the traditional wires 
solution associated with upgrading the existing substation transformer only, for a period of seven 
years. 
The Java microgrid NWA project is a battery storage system designed to establish the redundancy 
necessary to address the potential risk of loss of the existing single incoming sub transmission line 
and/or failure of the existing transformer bank at the Java substation, and thus will defer portions of 
the traditional wires solution alternative. The solution is being designed to meet all Java substation 
customers’ demand for up to 12 hours (until the contingency condition is repaired or a mobile 
substation is rolled out). A Request for Proposals (“RFP”) for the preliminary engineering design of 
the Java microgrid project was issued in September 2020. Four bids were received and reviewed by 
an internal NYSEG cross-functional team, and the preferred vendor was selected in December 2020. 
Negotiations with the selected vendor continued during the first quarter of 2021, and a project kick-
off meeting was held during the third week of March. 
Substation transformer load readings obtained in 2020 at the Java substation have shown that the 
subsequent peak loading is 3.8 MVA, which is less than the substation transformer bank’s 5 MVA 
rating. As the loading at this substation is no longer a concern, the Java peak shaving NWA project 
has been placed on hold. NYSEG will continue to monitor the load at this substation and will re-
evaluate the need for the peak shaving project based on existing and future forecasted load levels.

EXHIBIT C – NYSEG HILLDALE NWA PROJECT DETAILS 
Hilldale substation, located in NYSEG’s Liberty division, consists of (3) single-phase 3.5 MVA 
transformers, for a total substation bank capacity of 10.5 MVA, feeding (1) 12.47 kV distribution

this substation. The substation transformer bank has exceeded its capacity nameplate rating three of 
the past four years. In addition, the loading on the circuit at this substation is approaching its 
maximum capacity (> 90%). The traditional wires solution alternative planned to address the peak 
loading need at NYSEG’s Hilldale substation consists of upgrading the existing transformer bank by 
installing two (2) new 34.5/12.47 kV three-phase 22.4 MVA transformers (for N-1 considerations),

to establish a second circuit. After a comprehensive reanalysis, it was determined that NYSEG will 
address the immediate thermal overload need by implementing in-field tie switches to other adjacent 
circuit sources to reduce the peak loading on the Hilldale substation. NYSEG will continue to 
evaluate the potential for an NWA solution in the future to defer a full upgrade and rebuild of the 
Hilldale substation.

NYSEG’s Ithaca Division has experienced significant growth on its natural gas distribution system 
which has led to lower than acceptable delivery pressures during peak conditions in the Lansing, New 
York area. NYSEG’s gas distribution system is designed to maintain a portion of the Maximum 
Allowable Operating Pressure (“MAOP”) during very cold days. NYSEG’s MAOP for the Lansing area 
is 60 pounds per square inch-gauge (“psig”). NYSEG Gas Engineering standards for reliability 
recommend system pressures no less than 50% of MAOP to ensure safe operation of its system on 
peak days. NYSEG reports that its modeling currently results in pressures during a design day of 
between 14 psig (23% of MAOP) and 6.8 psig (11% of MAOP). Due to these reliability concerns, 
NYSEG has operated under a moratorium on new or expanded gas service in the Lansing area since 
February of 2015.2 
For much of the last decade, NYSEG has unsuccessfully sought remedies to the low-pressure 
situation in Lansing, including: the traditional pipeline solution provided by the Lansing/Freeville 
Reinforcement Pipeline Project, and the non-traditional approach of the Natural Gas Compressor 
Pilot program (“Compressor Pilot”). NYSEG reinforced the system with the East Shore Drive gas 
main installation project and anticipates additional system reliability gains with an NPA solution.3 
Prior to 2021, in its efforts to create an NPA solution, NYSEG issued a Request for Proposals (“RFP”), 
a Request for Information (“RFI”) and a second RFP; all seeking cost-effective, market-based 
solutions to the low-pressure situation, which would have a high potential for acceptance in the 
Lansing community. In support of those activities, NYSEG held several technical conferences, 
conducted appropriate community outreach and maintained a dialogue with DPS Staff, taking 
guidance from, and adjusting the developing NPA plan to best meet the needs of all stakeholders. 
On September 1, 2020, NYSEG filed a petition seeking authorization to implement a portfolio of 
NPA solutions4 which would eliminate the need for natural gas compressor stations, and which was 
also expected to improve the low-pressure situation on the Lansing area distribution system. On 
June 21, 2021, the New York State Public Service Commission (“Commission”) issued the Lansing 
NPA Order (the “Order”) authorizing NYSEG to proceed with the procurement of a modified 
portfolio of seven NPA projects for a total cost of $9.7 million. The Lansing NPA Portfolio is expected 
to provide a total reduction in hourly natural gas demand in the Lansing area of approximately 56 
MCFH, which is expected to improve the low-pressure situation in the Lansing area.

Solution Alternatives:  
 
Alternative geothermal district energy pilot site options proposed were in Rochester, NY in South 
Wedge neighborhood and in Norwich, NY.

The rationale and feasibility parameters used to select the final pilot project site are listed below with 
an overview of each metric along with its associated weight.

The Ithaca demonstration site scored an 8.6 out of 10. These results coupled with NYSEG Gas System 
Planning’s review of the proposed sites rendered Ithaca the top choice for a pilot project. There is a 9.4 
MCFH total Design Day Gas model NTM load potential for reduction within the project site boundary.
