Embedded Cost of Service Study (ECOS) 
3 
Q. 
Please describe the purpose of an ECOS study. 
4 
A. 
The purpose of an ECOS study is to assign the 
5 
costs of providing utility service to the 
6 
different service classes based on the costs 
7 
incurred to provide service to customers within 
8 
those classes.  The results of an ECOS study are 
9 
used as a guide in allocating revenue 
10 
responsibility between the different classes of 
11 
customers. 
12 
Q. 
What information does an ECOS study provide? 
13 
A. 
The ECOS study provides a total system rate of 
14 
return, the rate of return for each SC, and the 
15 
relative rate of return for each SC.  The rate 
16 
of return for an individual SC is an effective 
17 
way to measure how well the SC revenues align to 
18 
the costs to provide service to that particular 
19 
SC.  A SC’s relative rate of return is the SC’s 
20 
rate of return divided by the system rate of 
21 
return.   
22 
Q.

Q. 
Please explain the three major steps of 
2 
performing and ECOS study.  
3 
A. 
The three major steps of an ECOS study are: 
4 
functionalization, classification, and 
5 
allocation.  Functionalization is the process of 
6 
assigning the Companies’ rate base and expense

items to specific utility operating functions.

9 
Procurement/MFC; Transmission & Distribution, or

11 
Processing, or BIPP.  Classification is used to

further define the functionalized rate base and 
13 
expense items into demand, energy, and customer 
14 
components.  The final step – allocation - 
15 
allocates the components to SCs reflective of 
16 
the cost each SC imposes on the utility.
