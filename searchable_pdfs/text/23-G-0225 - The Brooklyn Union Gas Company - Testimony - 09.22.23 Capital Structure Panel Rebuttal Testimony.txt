Q. 
Does he agree with the Companies’ projected cost of long-term debt? 
1 
A. 
No.  While he accepts the Panel’s proposed credit spreads, Staff Witness Duah does not 
2 
agree with the Panel’s use of forward Treasury yields to determine the interest rate of the 
3 
planned Rate Year debt issuance.  Instead, he recommends (at 54) the use of current U.S. 
4 
Treasury yields plus the Companies’ calculated credit spreads to estimate interest rates for 
5 
the Companies’ projected new debt issuances.  Mr. Duah claims that “current interest rates 
6 
are the best predictors of future interest rates.”  He further argues that “this approach is 
7 
more reliable for predicting future interest rates, particularly for a one-year rate plan in 
8 
these rate proceedings.” 
9 
 
10 
Q. 
Do you agree? 
11 
A. 
No.  The Federal Reserve Economic Data on recent 10-year U.S. Treasury bond yields in 
12 
Figure 1, below, illustrates that current yields at any given point in time are not the best 
13 
predictors of future interest rates.  We recognize the Federal Reserve has been raising the 
14 
federal funds rate (i.e., the inter-bank lending rate) and normalizing its balance sheet to 
15 
address inflation.  However, the specific path of the Federal Reserve’s monetary policy is 
16 
uncertain and will respond to changing market conditions. We do not believe setting future 
17 
rates based on a point in time during the Federal Reserve’s monetary tightening cycle 
18 
would be reasonable.  Figure 1 demonstrates that a point-in-time estimate using recent 
19 
market conditions is not the appropriate estimate of Treasury yields over the period that 
20 
the rates determined in this proceeding will be in effect.

V. 
Financial Integrity 
4 
Q. 
Do you agree with Staff Witness Duah’s assertion (at 36) that his recommended 
5 
common equity ratio and ROE are generally consistent with the performance 
6 
required by Moody’s and S&P to maintain the Companies’ current credit ratings? 
7 
A. 
Not entirely.  Beginning with the analysis of KEDNY’s S&P rating (at 44) and in Exhibit 
8 
__ (KXD-12), Staff Witness Duah indicates that his analysis shows an implied S&P rating 
9 
of “BBB-”.  This is not consistent with KEDNY’s existing BBB+ issuer credit rating.  S&P 
10 
currently assigns KEDNY a stand-alone credit profile (“SACP”) of ‘bbb’ and an actual 
11 
issuer credit rating of BBB+.  The SACP is S&P’s opinion of creditworthiness in the 
12 
absence of extraordinary intervention or support from a parent, affiliate, or related 
13 
government or third-party entity.  The actual rating assigned is one notch higher at BBB+ 
14 
due to KEDNY’s status as a core subsidiary of National Grid North America (“NGNA”), 
15 
which, in turn, is a core subsidiary of National Grid plc (“NG”), both of which have a 
16 
“BBB+” issuer credit rating.  Mr. Duah’s implied rating of “BBB-” would only result in a 
17 
BBB+ assigned rating if NGNA and NG continue to maintain BBB+ ratings and if S&P 
18 
does not alter its approach under its group rating methodology.  We believe that it is most 
19 
appropriate for KEDNY to maintain a stand-alone rating profile in line with its actual 
20 
BBB+ rating, as otherwise a change in S&P’s approach could lead to a downgrade, which 
21 
would increase future debt costs. The credit ratings of NGNA and NG are also a function 
22 
of the credit ratings of their constituent parts and therefore it is important for individual 
23

1

3

Companies.  In its latest published reports on KEDNY and KEDLI (Exhibits ___ (KXD-
4 
5) and ___ (KXD-6), respectively), Moody’s sets out factors that could lead to a rating 
5 
upgrade or downgrade.  Among other qualitative factors Moody’s states that KEDNY’s 
6 
rating could be upgraded “if its ratio of CFO pre-WC/debt were to be at least 15% on a 
7 
sustainable basis,” and it could be downgraded if the ratio was “persistently below 13%” 
8 
and there was “a deterioration of the consolidated credit quality of the National Grid 
9 
group.”  Similarly for KEDLI, alongside other qualitative factors, Moody’s indicates the 
10 
utility’s rating could be upgraded if the ratio “were to be at least 19% on a sustainable basis 
11

12 
expected to fall persistently below 15%, or if state energy policy resulted in a deterioration 
13 
in either cash flow predictability; or ability or timeliness of cost recovery.”  Therefore, 
14 
KEDNY and KEDLI ought to maintain Moody’s CFO pre-WC/debt ratios at least above 
15 
13 percent and 15 percent, respectively, to avoid the risk of a rating downgrade.
