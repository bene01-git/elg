INTRODUCTION 
The Recommended Decision makes substantial errors of law and fact, sending a clear message to 
battery storage developers not to develop projects in Central Hudson’s service territory. It further 
abdicates the State’s policy and rate-making authority, unnecessarily ceding policy-setting 
jurisdiction to the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (“FERC”) and fails to consider an 
unjust and unreasonable rate structure that lumps bulk storage facilities in with commercial & 
industrial load.  The Recommended Decision’s reference to these issues as “academic” fails to 
grasp the urgency with which these issues must be addressed given development timelines, the 
state’s upcoming battery storage procurements, and the state’s policy goals.1 The approach that 
the Recommended Decision takes with respect to bulk storage2 rates sends a clear message that 
the State is not serious about inviting investment for more than 1700 megawatts of storage in 
Central Hudson’s service territory, contrary both to state policy and ratemaking standards.3 
Unless some other resolution is found, battery storage in Central Hudson’s service territory will 
likely remain “academic” so long as these rates are applied to bulk storage. 
The Recommended Decision implies that the rate applied in Wholesale Distribution Service 
tariffs is outside the purview of the instant rate case. However, as KCE noted in its Reply Brief4, 
Central Hudson’s WDS tariff filing at FERC5 requests that FERC “grant deference to the NY 
Commission in its WDS tariff approval request.”6 Further, KCE’s expert testimony raises serious 
concerns about how those rates are designed and recovered.7 While it is true that the Federal 
Energy Regulatory Commission has authority over WDS tariffs, FERC defers to the New York 
Commission on state policy and factfinding – the drivers of the underlying WDS rate.8 As a 
result, the Commission should take a closer look at Central Hudson’s bulk storage analysis and 
rates; the Company’s assumption that all bulk storage facilities interconnected at 69, 115, and 
345 kV should pay distribution rates based on the rates paid by an extremely small number of 
commercial and industrial customers; and the unintended consequences of the rates at issue 
herein.

The Recommended Decision fails to consider whether Central Hudson’s bulk battery 
storage rates are just and reasonable and allows for no state remedy to address a barrier to 
achieving state policy issues.   
The Commission’s decision on these rates is anything but “academic,” as claimed in the 
Recommended Decision.9 Battery storage development takes years.10 During that period, 
developers spend millions of dollars preparing projects to be completed. Lenders, financiers, and 
investment committees will not approve expenditures without a line of sight to addressing these 
unjust and unreasonable rates. Without action on these rate issues, bulk storage rates in Central 
Hudson’s service territory will be “academic,” because developers will move their investment 
dollars to other parts of New York State, and to other states entirely, in contravention of state 
policy and the State’s needs.  
1. The Recommended Decision introduces additional uncertainty regarding applicable rates.  
The Recommended Decision introduces additional uncertainty regarding the applicable rate for 
bulk storage. According to the Order, “We agree with Staff’s claim that, absent a FERC-approved 
WDS rate, and given that a Commission-approved rate cannot be applied, there currently is no 
identifiable rate applicable to any bulk storage projects within the Company’s service territory.”11 
This conclusion contradicts Central Hudson’s sworn testimony in the proceeding.12. A battery 
developer cannot plan based on an enunciation of “no identifiable rate,” as this makes it 
impossible to estimate the impact of rates on project investment decisions in lieu of a WDS rate.  
KCE asks the Commission to clarify what it means by “no identifiable rate,” given these 
issues.13 
2. The Recommended Decision improperly defers to FERC on matters that are squarely in 
the State’s purview. 
With respect to development and application of the eventual WDS rate, the Recommended 
Decision does not reflect FERC practice associated with WDS rates. Typically, the state sets its 
own policies with respect to battery storage, and then FERC applies those policies in the context 
of wholesale distribution rates.14  None of the parties dispute that FERC’s conclusions regarding 
a just and reasonable distribution rate are most often based on state policy. KCE’s testimony and

The order of magnitude change in the SC 13 Transmission Contract Demand as a result of a 
seemingly modest calculation error illustrates the inherent volatility in a very small rate class 
discussed by KCE Witness Bride, and the issues with designing a bulk storage rate based on a 
very small C&I customer class.21  The Recommended Decision defers to FERC with respect to 
rate design and recovery, however, FERC is likely to defer to the State with respect to rate-
setting. If the New York Commission does not address these issues, KCE is concerned that FERC 
will defer to the Commission, resulting in battery storage rates that are unjust and unreasonable 
and limit the ability to develop storage in Central Hudson’s service territory.  
Requested Remedy 
In the first instance, the Commission should reject Central Hudson’s proposed SC 13 rate in this 
proceeding to the extent it applies to bulk storage and should require Central Hudson to: (a) 
apply Order 841 by analogy to bulk storage that falls under SC 13 and (b) resubmit its WDS 
tariff so that it exempts bulk storage from paying retail distribution rates, or derivations thereof, 
when providing a service to the wholesale electricity market, consistent with the rate treatment 
that Order 841 accords federally-jurisdictional transmission-interconnected bulk storage.  
If in the alternative, the PSC opts to approve Central Hudson's proposed SC 13 rate 
(notwithstanding the errors described above and the attendant policy and cost implications for 
bulk battery storage) such that it will inform FERC’s WDS consideration, we recommend that 
the PSC immediately open a proceeding to establish a new rate that applies to bulk storage in 
Central Hudson’s service territory, with a deadline for Commission action by December 31, 2024 
to align with anticipated bulk storage solicitations as part of the pending Updated Energy Storage 
Roadmap Order.22 In particular, there is ample justification for specific consideration of Central 
Hudson’s bulk storage rates as a result of the information uncovered in this proceeding and in the 
Commission-directed WDS proceeding at FERC. The proceeding requested above must include 
an ACOS study conducted at a FERC Account level basis which can inform a new rate to be 
applied to bulk storage, without the contravening elements associated with the applicable SC 13 
C&I rate. Finally, in the interim, given that applying Central Hudson’s current rate structures 
(either via SC 13 or via FERC’s application of state policy in its consideration of WDS) results 
in an unjust and unreasonable rate for bulk storage, the Commission should find that no charging 
rates should apply for bulk storage until the new bulk storage-specific rate is established.
