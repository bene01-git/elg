Q.    
How does the Company forecast its day-ahead requirements? 
4 
A. 
Central Hudson has implemented a two-model system for the daily 
5 
forecasting of natural gas requirements.  The Company utilizes a 
6 
combination of the results from an internally developed Excel spreadsheet 
7 
model and a commercially developed model, GasDay, for forecasting daily 
8 
rolling 24-hour send-out forecasts and supply requirements.  The resultant 
9 
forecast is comprised of a base load component (usage that is not 
10 
weather sensitive) and a heating usage component (weather sensitive).   
11 
Heating usage is calculated as the product of the daily forecast of effective 
12 
degree days (“EDD”) and usage per EDD.  EDD is a measurement 
13 
designed to reflect the demand for energy needed to heat a home after 
14 
taking wind and cloud cover into consideration.  A gas day (10am – 10am) 
15 
weather forecast is received two times per day from the weather 
16 
forecasting service vendor WSI, Inc.  Depending on the specific month in 
17 
the heating season, unusual weather patterns, or a particular day of the 
18 
week or weekend, minor manual adjustments to the forecasted send-out 
19 
may be necessary.  The Company has the ability to make intra-day 
20 
changes to the send-out forecast and requirements with gas suppliers or 
21 
pipelines or both, as necessary, due to changes in the weather.

Q.    
What does Central Hudson do with the available upstream capacity during 
1 
off-peak periods? 
2 
A. 
Central Hudson releases available upstream transportation and storage 
3 
capacity to an Asset Manager (“Manager”) under an Asset Management 
4 
Agreement (“Agreement”).  The Agreement provides the Manager with 
5 
Central Hudson’s entire portfolio of storage and transportation capacity 
6 
assets.  The Manager can utilize the Company’s excess capacity as it 
7 
sees fit to extract value after slices of the system are released to 
8 
Aggregators and the Company’s daily delivery requirements are met.  
9 
Operationally, the Company retains the responsibility to forecast system 
10 
send-out and submits supply requirements daily to the Manager, who will 
11 
transport daily base and storage requirements to the Central Hudson city 
12 
gates as necessary to assure reliable gas supplies to the Company’s full-
13 
service and delivery customers.  Further, the Agreement provides storage 
14 
refill services during the summer and, at any time necessary, the ability to 
15 
purchase or sell daily volumes of spot gas.  The Company has retained 
16 
the ability to purchase base and peaking services through a competitive 
17 
RFP process to maintain customer price transparency.  The Agreement 
18 
allows Central Hudson to optimize the value of the entire portfolio for the 
19 
entire year.  The system-wide Asset Management approach, in turn, 
20 
maximizes the Company’s ability to reduce commodity bill pressure.
