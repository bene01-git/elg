C. Proposed Modification to Geothermal and Heat Pump Pilot Program 
1 
Q. 
What is the Geothermal and Heat Pump Pilot Program? 
 
2 
A. 
This program provides one-time supplemental grant assistance to new or existing non-
3 
residential customers to purchase heat pump systems in gas-constrained areas or for 
4 
business expansion/attraction projects.   
5 
Q. 
Do the Companies propose to modify this program? 
6 
A. 
Yes.  The Companies propose to modify the following two aspects: 1) the one-year 
7 
payback requirement under certain situations; and 2) program eligibility. 
8 
Q. 
What is the one-year payback requirement? 
9 
A. 
When the Geothermal and Heat Pump Pilot Program was initially developed, the 
10 
Companies assumed that all heat pump projects would result in a positive annual savings 
11 
relative to counterfactual HVAC systems.  Therefore, the pilot program was designed to 
12 
provide supplemental grant assistance to an applicant (after accounting for: 1) any 
13 
assistance the Companies have provided to the applicant under the New York State Clean 
14 
Heat Program (“NYCHP”); and 2) any program incentives that the applicant may have 
15 
received from other available sources), such that the simple payback period for the heat 
16 
pump system (i.e., the period required for the customer to recoup the system’s 
17 
incremental cost through annual bill savings) is no more than one year or $200,000, 
18 
whichever is less.  
19 
Q. 
Why do the Companies propose to change the one-year payback requirement?  
20 
A. 
An analysis performed by the implementation contractor administering this program for 
21 
the Companies demonstrated that, with the current price of natural gas relative to the 
22 
price of electricity, converting a gas heating system to heat pump technology oftentimes 
23 


--- Page 12 ---

10 
 
will not achieve a positive bill savings, and, therefore, will not have a positive payback 
1 
within a year.  Accordingly, in situations where projects result in a negative annual cash 
2 
flow, the Companies propose to provide up to 100% of the eligible heat pump system’s 
3 
remaining incremental first cost, after factoring in the NYCHP assistance and any 
4 
program incentives from available sources, or $200,000, whichever is less.     
5 
Q.    
How do the Companies propose to modify the program’s eligibility criteria? 
6 
A. 
Currently, eligibility for the Geothermal and Heat Pump Pilot Program is restricted to 
7 
commercial, industrial, and municipal customers.  During the pilot program, educational 
8 
institutions expressed interest in heat pump technology.  Accordingly, the Companies 
9 
propose to include educational institutions, such as colleges/universities and schools, to 
10 
the list of eligible recipients.  Expanding program eligibility is aligned with the State’s 
11 
ambitious renewable technology and electrification goals.
