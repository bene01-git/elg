Gas Safety Measures Performance Targets and Incentives 
1 
A. Goals of Gas Safety Performance Targets and Incentives 
2 
Q. 
What is the purpose of gas safety metrics and performance targets? 
3 
A. 
The Companies’ current rate plans establish certain metrics and performance 
4 
targets to ensure that the Companies meet their regulatory requirements to 
5 
provide safe and reliable service to customers.  The metrics measure the 
6 
Companies’ performance across various activities, ranging from LPP removal 
7 
to emergency response, and are designed to ensure accountability to customers.  
8 
Metrics may include positive revenue adjustments (“PRAs”) or NRAs if the 
9 
Companies’ performance varies from established levels.  NRAs serve to 
10 
penalize the Companies for underperformance, while PRAs incent the 
11 
Companies to overachieve base expectations and deliver incremental 
12 
improvements.  Inclusion of corresponding adjustments is important to drive 
13 
performance and support public policy objectives. 
14 
 
15 
Q. 
Please explain why inclusion of PRAs and incentives are appropriate. 
16 
A. 
PRAs do not simply reward the Companies for basic operations or business as 
17 
usual activities.  PRAs incentivize the Companies to perform additional work 
18 
and provide enhanced services, whether it be incremental leak repair or LPP 
19 
removal, that ultimately benefit customers and the community and further align 
20 
the Companies’ performance with public policy objectives.  Appropriately 
21 


--- Page 16 ---
Rebuttal Testimony of Gas Safety Panel 
Page 14 of 64 
 
challenging PRAs support continuous improvement and superior performance 
1 
with the goal of exceeding customer expectations and industry standards. 
2 
 
3

What is the Companies’ position on the SPP’s proposal for an overall 
4 
safety and reliability capital investment productivity metric in lieu of the 
5 
LPP unit cost productivity incentive?

The Companies believe the LPP unit cost productivity incentive is an

12 
be removed; an overall capital program productivity metric could be designed 
13 
to exclude the LPP retirement program productivity to avoid any overlap.   
14

Summary of First Responder Training Plans for 2019 
The Brooklyn Union Gas Company d/b/a National Grid NY and KeySpan Gas East Corporation 
d/b/a National Grid (collectively, “National Grid” or “Company”) have provided training to first 
responders (fire, police, and ambulance) on gas safety for years.  The first responder training 
program provides emergency response personnel with a solid understanding of utility hazards 
and includes practical guidance to aid first responders in safely identifying and responding to 
incidents involving natural gas pipelines and facilities.  National Grid’s first responder program 
includes a combination of online and in-person gas safety training and education, as well as 
integrated field response drills as summarized below.   
E-Learning Modules 
National Grid has developed a comprehensive, interactive set of e-learning training modules on 
gas safety issues, including gas distribution basics, the properties and characteristics of natural 
gas, carbon monoxide poisoning, gas incident management, and response tactics for incidents 
involving liquefied natural gas.  The modules are available to fire and police departments and 
municipalities, and include free safety materials such as workbooks, training presentations, and 
training guides.  The training modules are self-paced and include knowledge checks for each 
chapter to gauge the user’s basic understanding.  They provide natural gas case studies and 
scenarios, as well as access to National Grid’s transmission pipeline system and the Pipeline and 
Hazardous Materials Safety Administration’s (“PHMSA”) National Pipeline Mapping System 
training.  External agencies may also submit a request for additional first responder training 
opportunities.  
In 2018, the Company launched two new modules: “Responding to Solar Emergencies” and 
downloadable “Group Training Exercises.”  The Responding to Solar Emergencies helps first 
responders build a knowledge base on photovoltaic solar system basics, understand structural 
risks, roof and ground access limitations, shock hazards, toxic byproduct of combustion and 
other risks associated with solar systems, and addresses incident response precautions that should 
be taken when responding to emergencies involving solar systems. National Grid also identified 
the need to supplement its e-learning program to enable first responders to experience elements 
of the current training in a group setting to promote consistency among the entire team.  Group 
Training Exercises will cover the top four most frequent natural gas-related fire service calls with 
real-world scenarios and Q&A learning practices. The training exercises are downloadable from 
National Grid’s training website and can be printed and used by training officers on their own, 
when conducting their own training seminars, and as a supplement to National Grid’s e-learning 
certification program. Natural gas group training exercises include: 
• 
Gas main leak with no ignition 
• 
Gas service leak with ignition

In-Person Training and Presentations

Integrated Tabletop Exercises and Field Response Drills 
National Grid is planning field response drills to facilitate corporate preparedness by enabling

capability gaps and areas for improvement to prevent, protect against, mitigate, respond to, and 
recover from any variety of hazards.  The exercises further promote:



 
evaluation of the established emergency management system by facilitators and through 
feedback from participants; and 
 
continual improvement of the emergency management program. 
The Company integrates external partners and first responders into its drills and exercises to 
build knowledge of activities implemented by external partners, establish working relationships 
and improve communication and coordination that can be leveraged during real incidents.

Response: 
 
National Grid disagrees with Gas Safety Staff’s position that “relocates” for KEDNY and 
KEDLI and “reissues/recalls” for NMPC should be excluded from the one-call ticket count for 
purposes of gas safety performance reporting.  In that regard, gas safety performance reporting 
must be distinguished between the Quarterly Performance Reports required by the 2016 Gas 
Safety Performance Measure Guidance and Instructions (dated December 11, 2015) (“Staff 
Guidance Document”) and the Annual Gas Safety Performance Metrics report submitted as part 
of the Company’s rate plans.  The calculation and reporting of the damage prevention metric in 
these reports has some differences.

First, Recommendation 2-5 required the Company to describe in its Implementation Plan the 
one-call tickets included and excluded in the damage prevention metric.  In accordance with the 
recommendation, the Company’s Implementation Plan, which was filed on May 19, 2016 in 
Case 13-M-0314, explained that “relocates” for KEDNY and KEDLI and “reissues/recalls” for 
NMPC are properly included in the metric.  Specifically, while these tickets have the same 
requesting party and work location, they are different than “retransmit” or “refresh”1 tickets that 
the Staff’s Guidance Document indicates should be excluded from the metric.  “Relocate” tickets 
for KEDNY and KEDLI and “reissues/recall” tickets for NMPC are issued when the original 
ticket has expired (e.g., construction was delayed, which could range from weeks to months) or 
circumstances have caused the original mark-out to become unreliable (e.g., weather or 
construction activities have caused the mark-out to fade or disappear).  In these circumstances, 
the one-call ticket center issues “relocate” or “reissues/recall” tickets, which require the 
Company to physically respond to the location and re-mark its facilities as if the first ticket was 
never issued.  In contrast, a “retransmit” ticket is typically issued by the one-call center if the 
Company did not respond to the first ticket.  KEDNY, KEDLI, and NMPC agree that a 
“retransmit” ticket should be excluded from the damage prevention metric.  However, the 
Company believes that because it is responding a second time and re-marking its facilities, 
“relocates” for KEDNY and KEDLI and “reissues/recalls” for NMPC should be included in the 
metric.   
 
Second, the Commission approved the Company’s Implementation Plan and directed National 
Grid to implement the plan in its Order Approving Implementation Plans in Case 13-M-0314 
(issued and effective March 10, 2017) (“March 2017 Order”) (at 3).2  The Company does not 
believe that Gas Safety Staff’s subsequent rejection of the plan is in keeping with the March 
2017 Order.  As stated at page 5 of the March 2017 Order, Public Service Law Section 66(19) 
authorizes the Commission to approve a utility’s implementation plan, at which time the 
implementation plan becomes enforceable.  The Commission, in approving the Company’s 
Implementation Plan, did not require any change to the Company’s filed plan relative to one-call 
tickets.

contained in the Company’s rate plans.  In the 2016 KEDLI and KEDNY Rate Cases (Cases 16-
G-0058 and 16-G-0059), KEDLI and KEDNY carefully explained the changes it made to the 
damage prevention metric to address the audit recommendations.  These changes included 
excluding “retransmit” tickets and including coating damage in the calculation of the metric,

Company will continue to report the damage prevention metric contained in its rate plans
