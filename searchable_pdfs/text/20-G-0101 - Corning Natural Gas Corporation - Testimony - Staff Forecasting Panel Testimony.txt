Q. 
Please describe the basic functioning of 
9 
econometric modelling methodology. 
10 
A. 
Econometric models relate gas sales or number of 
11 
customers to a set of explanatory or independent 
12 
variables, economic and demographic, weather, 
13 
and other seasonal variables. Economic and 
14 
demographic variables include those such as 
15 
population, price of electricity, personal 
16 
income, or employment. Weather variables are 
17 
represented by heating degree days.  Seasonal 
18 
variables may adjust for calendar month or other 
19 
billing cycle related variations. The 
20 
relationship between these explanatory variables 
21 
and gas deliveries over time constitutes the 
22 
structural component of the econometric 
23 
forecasting models.  Also, because gas customer 
24 


--- Page 12 ---

Staff Forecasting Panel 
 
 
11 
 
and usage data are time-series data, econometric 
1 
models may also include a non-structural 
2 
component. 
3 
Q. 
Please explain the non-structural component of 
4 
an econometric forecasting model. 
5 
A. 
The non-structural component is represented by a 
6 
procedure that relates the contemporaneous 
7 
values and estimation errors of a time-series to 
8 
its past values and estimation errors in the 
9 
model. This is done via the addition of moving 
10 
average and autoregressive variables to the 
11 
model structure. This component is included to 
12 
capture the remaining variations in gas sales or 
13 
number of customers, stemming from time trend, 
14 
seasonality, as well as serial correlations in 
15 
residual errors.  
16 
Q. 
How is an econometric model estimated? 
17 
A. 
An econometric model is estimated using 
18 
regression techniques on historical data. 
19 
Econometric estimation techniques attempt to 
20 
minimize the amount of variation in the gas 
21 
delivery and customer data that is not explained 
22 
by the structural and non-structural explanatory 
23 
variables in the model. An econometric forecast

uses the estimated historical relationship 
1 
between the variables and applies it to 
2 
forecasts of the explanatory variables in order 
3 
to produce an estimate of future energy 
4 
deliveries or customers. 
5 
Q. 
Why is an econometric modeling approach better 
6 
than the Company’s subjective method? 
7 
A. 
Under an econometric modeling approach, the use 
8 
of natural gas in a historical period is not 
9 
treated as a standalone phenomenon.  Rather, the 
10 
use of gas is presumed to be related to factors 
11 
such as consumers’ and business’ economic 
12 
behavior and activities using their gas 
13 
appliance and equipment.  Economic theory posits 
14 
that use of natural gas in a historical period 
15 
is driven by factors which include the cost of 
16 
gas as well as consumers’ and businesses’ 
17 
wellbeing.  Econometric modelling uses 
18 
statistical techniques to estimate the relative 
19 
impact of such explanatory drivers on gas usage.  
20 
Our econometric models are estimated by 
21 
multivariate regression techniques on historical 
22 
data over a five-year period to simultaneously 
23 
determine the effect of weather and economic 
24

Staff Forecasting Panel 
 
 
13 
 
variables on the variation in gas usage.  In 
1 
addition, as indicated by the lagged 
2 
relationships and non-structural components in 
3 
our econometric models, the estimated lagged 
4 
relationships and forecasting errors of the past 
5 
can be fed back into the models and refined 
6 
parameter estimates can be generated to improve 
7 
future forecasting results.  The forecasting 
8 
confidence levels can also be generated.  
9 
Therefore, our method is dynamic, probabilistic, 
10 
and objective, rather than the static, 
11 
deterministic, and subjective approach used by

the Company.
