I. 
Introduction 
 
Niagara Mohawk Power Corporation d/b/a National Grid (the “Company”) respectfully 
requests Commission approval to utilize $2,843,183 in prior deferral credit balances that are 
necessary to fund the Company’s existing portfolio of electric Economic Development grant 
programs during the remaining term of the current rate case. Since 2003, the Company has 
administered a portfolio of Economic Development grant programs. During that time, the 
programs provided over $160 million in assistance, helped create or retain almost 70,000 jobs, 
and leveraged nearly $12 billion in new capital investment across the Company’s upstate New 
York service territory.  The level of program activity has been very high during the term of the 
current rate plan, and the Company anticipates it will fully utilize – and potentially exceed – 
program budgets over the term of the rate agreement.  As of April 1, 2023, when the Company 
filed its Economic Development Programs Annual Report (“Annual Report”), there are prior

deferral credit balances owed to customers, totaling approximately $2.84 million.1 This balance 
of prior deferral credit balances is a result of under-expenditures during the Company’s two 
previous rate plans in Cases 17-E-0238 and 12-E-0201.2  The Company is requesting the 
authority to utilize those deferral balances as necessary to remain within the current rate

II. 
Discussion 
 
As provided in the Joint Proposal in Case 20-E-0380, the rate allowance for the 
Company’s electric Economic Development grant programs is $11 million per year3, subject to a 
downward-only reconciliation.4 Any difference between the respective rate allowance and actual 
program costs in a given Rate Year will be carried forward and reconciled at the end of Rate 
Year Three, with any under-expenditure to be deferred for future use in the respective Economic 
Development Grant Programs.5 The Company has almost fully utilized this rate allowance 
during the current rate plan and, as of September 30, 2023, the electric Economic Development 
grant deferral had a cumulative deferral credit balance owed to customers of $61,274.

As discussed in the Company’s Annual Report filed with the Commission on April 1, 
2023, electric program activity is expected to remain very high6 – and will most likely increase – 
due to:  
1) The direct and indirect (supply chain) Economic Development activity associated with 
Micron Technology’s announced $100 billion investment in New York. 
2) A surge in interest and investment in New York from the clean energy industries, 
including electric-intensive manufacturers of solar/photovoltaic (“PV”) components, 
battery storage technology, green hydrogen, and the offshore wind supply chain.  The 
Company’s Economic Development staff, in collaboration with state and local Economic 
Development organizations, is currently working with many prospective customers in 
these industries.  The 12 largest of these prospective customers represent a combined 
total of over 3,200 new jobs and $3.3 billion in new capital investment in the Company’s 
service territory.  The successful attraction of those businesses also will require 
substantial electric infrastructure upgrades that could qualify the customers for assistance 
through the Company’s Economic Development grant programs, most notably the 
Electric Capital Investment Incentive program.    
3) Increasing electrification of customer systems and processes that are currently fueled by 
natural gas and other alternatives.  
4) New York State Empire State Development’s recent launch of “Fast NY,” a statewide 
site development program that is also expected to generate new applications for National 
Grid’s Economic Development grant programs, particularly the Shovel Ready Incentive 
program.

The Joint Proposals in Cases 20-E-0380, 17-E-0238, and 12-E-0201 all provide that, in 
the event of any anticipated over-expenditures, the Company may petition the Commission for 
deferral of such over-expenditures and shall have no obligation to make any such additional 
expenditure unless and until the Commission authorizes the Company to defer amounts in excess 
of the rate allowance for future recovery.7   Those three Joint Proposals also provide that under-
expenditures during the term of the rate plan will be deferred for future use in the Company’s 
Economic Development grant programs.8   
 
There were under-expenditures of $2,782,105 and $61,078 during the terms of the rate 
agreements in Cases 12-E-0201 and 17-E-0238, respectively, for a total balance (credits owed to 
customers) of $2,843,183.  Given the high level of current and anticipated grant program activity 
described above, and the potential for program expenditures to exceed the rate allowance during 
the remainder of the current rate agreement, the Company is seeking approval to utilize the prior 
deferral credit balances as necessary to remain within the rate allowance.  In doing so, the 
Company will likely avoid the need to petition the Commission for future deferral treatment of 
any over-expenditures during the remaining term of the current rate plan.

Conclusion
