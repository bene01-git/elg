PRELIMINARY STATEMENT 
 
The City of New York (“City”) submits this Statement in Support of the Joint Proposal 
filed with the New York State Public Service Commission (“Commission”) on February 16, 2023 
in the above-captioned Consolidated Edison Company of New York, Inc. (“Con Edison”) electric 
and gas rate cases.  Although the City is very concerned about the magnitude and impact on 
customers of the rate increases, the City believes that the rate plans set forth within the Joint 
Proposal satisfy the requirements of Public Service Law § 65(1) and should be adopted by the 
Commission. 
The City is very concerned with the size of the rate increases, particularly for low- and 
moderate-income Con Edison customers.  However, based on the record developed in these 
proceedings and the likely outcome had the cases been fully litigated, the City believes that the 
Joint Proposal represents a better outcome for customers.  On the whole, it ensures continuation 
of the reliable, resilient, and safe electric and gas systems that customers expect and want, and it 
includes costs of decarbonizing our existing infrastructure and building the necessary clean energy 
infrastructure of the future.  As to the rate levels, it is notable that the recommendations in the Joint 


--- Page 3 ---
2 
 
Proposal reflect a 58% reduction in the electric revenue requirement and a 45% reduction in the 
gas revenue requirement from the amounts sought in the Initial Filing. 
Key to the City’s support of the Joint Proposal is its assessment that, as compared to the 
Company’s initial rate requests, the increases reflected in the Joint Proposal have been 
significantly reduced from Con Edison’s initial requests.  Had the cases been litigated, the 
increases could have been much higher and the benefits included in the Joint Proposal, such as 
increased customer protections from terminations during extreme hot weather, aggressive targets 
for shareholder incentives, performance metrics with negative revenue adjustments for billing 
problems, greater transparency regarding the Company’s use of conservation voltage optimization, 
and a heightened emphasis on gas safety, may not have been achieved.  Further, the reliability, 
resiliency, adequacy, and safety of the electric system in New York City are of paramount 
importance, and the proposed rate plans provide sufficient funding for, and ensures that the 
Company focuses on, maintaining and enhancing all of these system attributes. 
For the reasons set forth herein, the City respectfully recommends that the Commission 
adopt, without modifications, the Joint Proposal as the Company’s electric and gas rate plans for 
the next three years. 
PROCEDURAL HISTORY 
 
Con Edison sought new rates on January 28, 2022 (“Initial Filing”).  The Initial Filing 
requested a $1.2 billion electric rate increase and a $503 million gas rate increase, to take effect 
on January 1, 2023.  Those increases were estimated to result in a 11.2% overall bill increase for 
electric customers and a 18.2% bill increase for gas customers.  On April 8, 2022, the Company 
filed updates to the Initial Filing.  On May 20 and 23, 2022, the City and 21 other parties submitted 


--- Page 4 ---
3 
 
direct testimony in response to the Company’s Initial Filing and updates.  Con Edison, the City, 
and four other parties submitted rebuttal testimony on June 17, 2022. 
On June 17, 2022, the Company filed a Notice of Impending Settlement Negotiations with 
the Secretary to the Commission.  On July 6, 2022, the active parties to these proceedings 
commenced discussions to determine if the differences in their respective positions could be 
overcome and if compromise rate plans could be negotiated.  After numerous negotiating sessions, 
many parties, including the City, were able to reach agreement on compromise positions, as 
reflected in the Joint Proposal.  On February 16, 2023, the Joint Proposal was filed with the 
Commission. 
Parties received appropriate notice of all negotiation sessions.  The City’s understanding is 
that all interested parties were given an opportunity to participate in all negotiating sessions.  
Unfortunately, the issues advanced by some parties could not be resolved.  While the City 
understands the concerns advanced by those parties, neither those concerns nor the inability to 
resolve them undermines the reasonableness of the Joint Proposal or provide a basis for the 
Commission to reject or modify the provisions in the Joint Proposal.

A. The Size Of The Rate Increases Is A Concern 
In its Initial Filing, Con Edison proposed to increase its electric and gas revenue 
requirements by over $1 billion and $500 million, respectively.  Those increases would have raised 
electric and gas rates by approximately 11% and 18%, respectively.  The Joint Proposal 
recommends lower revenue requirement increases and reduces the bill impacts, but they are still 
significant – 5.00% in Rate Year (“RY”) 1, 4.20% in RY2, and 3.30% in RY3 for electric and 
8.80% in RY1, 8.10% in RY2, and 6.60% in RY3 for gas.  However, those increases do not reflect 
the total bill impacts Con Edison customers will experience over the next three years. 
They do not affect surcharges and other rate recoveries for infrastructure improvements 
undertaken in furtherance of the Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act (“CLCPA”) 
and Accelerated Renewable Energy Growth and Community Benefit Act, compliance costs arising 
from the Clean Energy Standard, costs associated with the New Efficiency: New York programs, 
or costs associated with the provision of relief to customers adversely impacted by the COVID 
pandemic.  While all of these programs are important and meritorious and have been supported by 
the City, they nevertheless have substantial price tags.

other utilities’ requests for rate increases in their respective rate cases, it is important for the

for the next three years, whereas a litigated case would have set rates only for one year.  
Additionally, and as discussed above and below, the Joint Proposal secures benefits that will

8. Voltage Optimization and Reduction 
The City is aware that there can be substantial benefits from deploying conservation 
voltage optimization, and it is not opposed to Con Edison’s use of it.  However, the provision of 
benefits to customers, generally, should not come at the detriment to certain customers, as has 
occurred.  Indeed, Section 65(1) of the Public Service Law applies equally to all customers, and 
all customers are entitled to safe and adequate service at just and reasonable rates.  The manner in 
which Con Edison has deployed conservation voltage optimization has been very problematic in 
that the Company has not properly analyzed its impacts on customers, has not notified customers, 
particularly high tension customers, of its plans, and has unreasonably burdened some customers, 
including the City, with unanticipated costs.42  The City detailed the inadequacies of Con Edison’s 
approach in its pre-filed testimony.43  Through the course of these proceedings, the City learned 
that its experiences are not unique, and other high-tension customers have been similarly adversely 
impacted by Con Edison’s actions.

9. Earnings Adjustment Mechanisms 
In the last two sets of Con Edison rate cases, the City was very critical of the massive 
shareholder incentives included for achievement of very modest energy efficiency and gas safety 
targets.  While the City strongly supports energy efficiency measures, shareholder incentives 
should be available only for superior performance.  However, the incentives established in those 
cases arguably applied to minimum levels of performance.  Because Con Edison has a statutory 
obligation to provide safe gas service and can be penalized for failing to do so, the City believed 
that providing positive incentives related to gas service was unnecessary and inappropriate.  
The Joint Proposal contains shareholder incentives, or earnings adjustment mechanisms, 
for both energy efficiency and gas safety.46  However, there is a significant difference in these 
provisions.  To earn the incentives, Con Edison must achieve levels that well exceed its historical 
performance.  In other words, the incentives are for superior performance.  This is appropriate.  
Additionally, the level of incentives is much lower than in prior rate cases.  This also is appropriate, 
and the City supports this change.

11. Other Provisions 
A few items are notable because of their exclusion from the Joint Proposal.  These include 
Con Edison’s proposals to (i) construct a large-scale solar farm in New York (but not in its service 
territory), (ii) construct a series of energy storage facilities; and (iii) spreading the costs of its 
energy efficiency programs to New York Power Authority (“NYPA”) customers, including the 
City. 
There was extensive opposition to the first two items, as evidenced by the pre-filed direct 
testimony submitted in these proceedings.  There is robust competitive development for large-
scale renewable projects and no reason for Con Edison to enter that arena.  Similarly, there is 
extensive interest among merchant companies in developing energy storage systems, so no need 
exists for Con Edison customers to directly bear the costs of such projects through their rates.   
As to the third item, the City and other NYPA customers already heavily invest in energy 
efficiency measures, for which they bear all costs, themselves.  During the discovery phase of 
these proceedings, the City inquired about the opportunities and funding that would be available
