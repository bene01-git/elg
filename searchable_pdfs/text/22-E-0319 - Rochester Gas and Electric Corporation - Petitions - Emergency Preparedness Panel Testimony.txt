Q. 
Have the Companies experienced any success in the area of emergency preparedness and 
10 
response? 
11 
A. 
Yes.  While weather continues to have an impact within the Companies’ service area, the 
12 
Companies have seen the benefit of “leaning in” – actively staging and acquiring 
13 
resources to prepare for potential impacts of significant storm events.  This practice 
14 
benefits our customers, because the Companies are prepared in advance of significant 
15 
storm events and pre-staged resources can respond faster to storm events than resources 
16 
mobilized after the event impact.  In addition, the Companies have complemented this 
17 
“leaning in” with: 1) improvements in storm response; 2) teams establishing rotational 
18 
shifts early in the event lifecycle to plan and execute necessary repair work promptly; and 
19 
3) active coordination with municipal partners and communication to stakeholders to set 
20 
restoration expectations.  These efforts have played an important role in decreasing the 
21 
number of outage events lasting more than 72 hours.

A. 
Base Camp Retainer Agreement 
2 
Q. 
Have the Companies identified resourcing issues that can be improved? 
3 
A. 
Yes.  There have been challenges with housing workers near the event impact areas due 
4 
to hotel congestion.  Beyond securing available hotel or other available housing space, a 
5 
best practice of other utilities includes having a base camp solution that would provide 
6 
housing and/or food services.   
7 
Q. 
Are the Companies making a proposal to address hotel congestion? 
8 
A. 
Yes, the Companies propose entering into an agreement to have a base camp solution 
9 
available on or near the Companies’ service areas.  
10 
Q. 
Please describe the base camp solution. 
11 
A. 
Base camps are temporary facilities that provide housing and/or food services during an 
12 
emergency event.  The Companies propose to enter into a retainer agreement with a 
13 
vendor that would provide storage and set up of these facilities in or near the service 
14 
territory and in proximity to the location of the emergency event.  Vendors generally 
15 
require the utility to utilize the camp for a minimum number of days, usually three, due to 
16 
the complexity of activating, mobilizing and running these camps.  This option would be 
17 
for major events that are forecast to cause significant damage to the Companies’ 
18 
infrastructure.   
19 
Q. 
Why are the Companies proposing a base camp retainer agreement? 
20 
A. 
The Companies have contacted vendors to determine if they can offer the base camp 
21 
service on an as-requested basis (without a retainer agreement).  Vendors, however, are 
22 
not willing to commit to being able to respond without a retainer.  They will provide a 
23 


--- Page 16 ---

13 
base camp only if their current clients with retainer options do not need the vendor’s base 
1 
camp services.  Unfortunately, due to the nature of severe weather in New York and New 
2 
England, it is unlikely that NYSEG and RG&E would be the only company needing a 
3 
base camp, which negates this option. 
4 
Q. 
Did the Companies consider utilizing a base camp vendor further away from the 
5 
northeast? 
6 
A. 
Using a vendor outside of the northeast, such as a vendor from the southeast United 
7 
States, is not feasible.  Those vendors are days away from the Companies’ service areas, 
8 
and the decision to utilize a base camp would therefore require acquisition well ahead of 
9 
the known storm impacts, leading to considerable higher expense.  Additionally, base 
10 
camps require notice and a number of set up days and vendors from another geographical 
11 
area could arrive later than needed. 
12 
Q. 
Do the Companies have an estimate of the cost to enter into a base camp retainer 
13 
agreement?

A. 
Based on preliminary discussions with vendors, the Companies received estimates for 
15 
available options that are approximately $1.33 million for NYSEG and $0.57 million for 
16 
RG&E annually, which the Companies propose to charge against the major storm 
17 
reserve.
