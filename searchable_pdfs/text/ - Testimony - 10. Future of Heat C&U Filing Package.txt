Response: 
 
1. 
a. 
The tiers that are represented in the proposal were initially based on the structure 
of a similar program that was successfully implemented at Vermont Gas Systems.  
In that structure, customers can elect to purchase a certain percentage of their fuel 
as RNG.  The levels used were 5%, 20%, 25%, and 50% for residential customers 
and 5%, 10%, 20%, 40%, and 100% for non-residential customers.  Upon further 
consideration, the Company will be revising its proposal in its corrections and 
updates filing.  The Company will be updating the residential numbers to more 
closely align the “high option” with customers seeking to offset 100% of their use 
with RNG.  Conversations with SoCalGas, who developed a similar program, 
suggested that residential customers preferred a structure that provides a fixed bill 
impact rather than one that covers a fixed percentage of gas consumption. The 
Companies used an estimated average cost of $15/dekatherm of RNG to calculate 
the bill impacts at the various percentage rates.  The Companies expect the pricing 
may change in the future based on the supply cost for RNG as reflected in the 
associated contracts.  The tier names were designed to communicate relative 
levels of participation for customers who selected a fixed-cost rate.  Please see the 
workpapers included as Attachment 1 for the fixed-pricing calculations.
b. 
For non-residential customers, the monthly consumption percentages refer to the 
amount of monthly consumption that will be served with RNG.  All of the RNG 
costs will be an additional charge that will be added on top of existing rates. For 
customers electing the percentage of RNG option, the Companies will need to 
true-up the costs at the end of the year as consumption varies.
2. 
a. 
In an effort to contract for the lowest cost RNG, the Companies would go through 
a competitive bidding process to select an RNG supplier.  The process would 
include an RFP and would be consistent with the Companies’ existing long-term 
supply contracting process.
b. 
Under the contract between the Companies and Element Markets, the firm 
selected to monetize RNG from the Newtown Creek Facility, the Companies have 
retained flexibility to use RNG produced at Newtown Creek as part of the Green 
 Appendix A

Gas Tariff offering.  The Companies plan to do so if it is found to be the most 
cost-effective source of RNG.
3. 
No, the Companies do not intend to cap the number of participants or the volume of RNG 
sold in the Green Gas Tariff program.  There may be a limit to the amount of RNG that 
can be contracted based on the available supply in the market, but that level is currently 
unknown.  A 2010 study by National Grid found that 11% of natural gas demand in the 
states served by National Grid could realistically be met with RNG.  The Companies are 
participating in a study to update this estimate and expect that the potential for RNG has 
grown due to technology developments. 
4.  
a. 
As discussed in the direct testimony of the Future of Heat Panel, the Companies 
determined they will need two additional FTEs to implement the Green Gas Tariff 
offering.  The additional resources are needed to perform new job functions 
related to procurement and implementing the Green Gas Tariff.  The 
responsibilities will include traditional procurement functions (e.g., tracking 
costs/revenues for the green gas, ensuring proper entries into associated 
transaction systems, and performing relevant reporting requirements), as well as 
new tasks such as the purchase and sale of environmental attributes.  Based on the 
Companies’ experience performing similar functions, they determined that two 
incremental FTEs are necessary to adequately fulfill the tasks.  Attachment 2 
includes the supporting FTE calculations. 
b. 
Customers electing to participate in the Green Gas Tariff will be listed in the 
billing system with a unique identifier that allows the Companies to identify, 
track, and report RNG consumption and associated revenue.  The costs associated 
with the purchase of RNG will be booked monthly using a unique general ledger 
account.
5. 
As set forth in Schedule 1, the Companies considered two alternatives that would allow 
them to support RNG production without purchasing or selling the RNG:
1.
RNG marketplace to match suppliers with large consumers, limiting consumption 
to those large enough to be anchor customers for RNG development projects; and  
2.
Working with energy service companies to support transportation customers. 
For option 1, the Companies could serve as a facilitator to connect RNG project developers and 
large customers who could purchase the majority, if not all, of the RNG produced. This sort of 
anchor customer approach can work well but it requires finding compatible counterparties. This 
approach would also restrict participation, and thus access to RNG, to large customers.
For option 2, the Companies could continue to educate transportation customers regarding the 
environmental opportunity to reduce their emissions through RNG and the financial opportunity
