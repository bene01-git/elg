II. 
BACKGROUND 
1 
Q.  
Why are municipalities opting to purchase their street light assets from utility 
2 
companies? 
3 
A.  
In November 2015, Governor Andrew Cuomo signed Chapter 495 of the 2015 Laws of 
4 
New York into law, which added Public Service Law Section 70-a and established 
5 
procedures for the transfer of ownership of street lighting systems to municipalities or 
6 
other government entities. In an October 2016 order in Cases 15-E-0745, et al., which we 
7 
will refer to as the “2016 Street Light Transfer Order,” the Public Service Commission 
8 
(PSC) approved tariff changes for all of New York State’s investor-owned utilities, 
9 
including the Company, that implemented the new street lighting ownership transfer 
10 
procedures. This regulatory change opened new opportunities for municipalities to 
11 
achieve both energy and cost reductions that did not exist previously. Municipalities 
12 
mainly choose to buy the street light systems in their jurisdictions because they can 
13 
achieve savings from both the reduced energy usage associated with conversion to LED 
14 
luminaires and reduced facilities charges associated with the street light system.  
15 
Q. 
Are there other factors driving street light asset sales?  
16 
A.  
Yes. Dissatisfaction with utility maintenance and operations of street light facilities are 
17 
another factor driving municipalities to purchase their street light assets. A common 
18 
complaint that NYPA hears from municipalities throughout the state is that many of their 
19 
street lights do not function properly and have not functioned for an extended period, yet 
20 
the utility company continues to assess facilities and usage charges. To address this 
21 
problem, municipalities want to purchase their lights and make them functional again.  
22

Purchasing their street lights allows municipalities to have control over the 
1 
maintenance necessary to keep them in working order and ensure more streamlined 
2 
communications with constituents regarding street light maintenance issues. Furthermore, 
3 
the municipality can competitively bid the maintenance and realize maintenance cost 
4 
savings compared to what otherwise would be paid to the utility. Some municipalities 
5 
also are interested in maintaining a uniform or unique appearance, and purchasing the 
6 
street lights gives them the flexibility to install various styles of street lights that would 
7 
not be offered by the utility. 
8 
Q.  
What role does NYPA play in the conversion of customer-owned street light assets 
9 
to LED? 
10 
A.  
Governor Cuomo directed NYPA to lead the Smart Street Lighting NY Program in his 
11 
2018 State of the State Address with a goal to reduce energy consumption from street 
12 
lights across the state. NYPA implements the Smart Street Lighting NY Program as a 
13 
one-stop-shop model for customers to replace inefficient streetlights with LED 
14 
technology. Municipalities rely on NYPA’s technical expertise in design, procurement, 
15 
and construction to purchase and own their streetlight facilities and retrofit them with 
16 
LED fixtures as well as dimming nodes and other smart solutions, thereby maximizing 
17 
energy efficiency and the resulting bill savings. NYPA also offers upfront financing for 
18 
street light projects, where financing costs are offset by the cost savings created by the 
19 
reduced energy use of LED streetlights.

Q.  
Are there additional benefits to the Smart Street Lighting NY Program?  
1 
A.  
Yes. As the Commission recognized in the 2016 Street Light Transfer Order, the 
2 
conversion of street lights from high-intensity discharge (HID) luminaires to LED 
3

4

5 
the Smart Street Lighting NY Program goals also will directly support the 40 percent 
6 
reduction of GHG emissions by 2030, based on 1990 levels, that is mandated in the 
7 
Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act.

16 
A.  
NLC nodes are modules that take the place of photo sensors. They allow the street 
17 
lighting customer to directly control lighting levels—including dimming capabilities—

and reduce energy consumption.

IV. 
STREET LIGHT DIMMING 
3 
Q. 
What benefits are associated with the adaptive dimming control capabilities of 
4 
smart street lighting devices?  
5 
A.  
The Seattle LED Adaptive Lighting Study, prepared by Clanton & Associates in 2014 
6 
(Seattle Study), included as Exhibit__(SLP-2), estimated that street light dimming 
7 
strategies could reduce energy usage by up to an additional 25 percent as compared to 
8 
pre-LED energy usage levels, depending on the dimming strategy. Seattle Study at p. i. 
9 
The adaptive dimming control capabilities provide a mechanism to implement such 
10 
dimming strategies. Without network lighting controls, street lights can be operated only 
11 
at full brightness or be turned off. The Seattle Study demonstrated that, based on field 
12 
tests with new lamps, dimming does not significantly impact object detection by the 
13 
naked eye, and even dimming by 50 percent did not significantly reduce object detection 
14 
in dry pavement conditions. Seatle Study at p. ii. Installation of NLC nodes—which 
15 
facilitate the dimming of street lights—will allow municipalities to reduce energy 
16 
consumption and extend the useful life of the lamp without sacrificing lighting quality or 
17 
public safety, ultimately reducing costs for the municipal tax payers. 
18 
Customers can benefit from larger-scale dimming strategies to reduce street light 
19 
energy use. For example, lighting can be reduced to a greater extent during low traffic 
20 
early morning hours for further energy savings. Strategic use of dimming high wattage 
21 
luminaires also may improve public safety. For example, the higher wattage luminaires 
22

normally could be operated at a lower wattage but brightened during police or first 
1 
responder activities to enhance public safety. Another strategic use is blinking or flashing 
2 
lights that could warn the public of natural disasters and/or direct residents away from 
3 
danger.  
4

13

14 
nodes can be used as a cost-effective way to accurately measure energy savings from 
15 
dimming.

Q.  
Does the Panel have any recommendations on a street light dimming offering?  
8 
A.  
Utilizing the NLC node to meter street lights, with associated metered rates in the tariff, 
9 
is the most accurate way to reflect the energy savings associated with street light 
10 
dimming strategies. However, because no NLC nodes have been approved for metering 
11 
purposes in New York State, we recommend that the Company establish adaptive 
12 
operating schedules that reflect street light dimming capabilities and enable customers to 
13 
achieve energy cost savings. These schedules function by identifying equivalent hours of 
14 
operation for certain levels of street light dimming (e.g., 30 percent, 50 percent). The 
15 
equivalent hours of operation can then be used with the total device wattage to estimate 
16 
overall energy use for billing purposes.  
17 
A schedule-based approach is an appropriate interim step that would allow 
18 
customers to realize savings associated with street light dimming while the necessary 
19 
metering testing and certification takes place. National Grid utilized this approach in 
20 
Massachusetts and Rhode Island and proposed in its most recent rate case filing to follow 
21 
the same approach in New York State. Such an offering is in line with outdoor lighting’s

residential areas.

We also recommend that once Company-approved schedules are established, that 
12 
customers opting into the dimming schedules submit a proposed weekly operating 
13 
schedules identifying their planned dimming schedule. The Company then could identify 
14
