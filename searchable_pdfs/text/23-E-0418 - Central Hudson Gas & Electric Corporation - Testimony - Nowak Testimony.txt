III. REGULATORY GUIDELINES AND FINANCIAL 
CONSIDERATIONS 
Q. 
Please describe the guiding principles to be used in establishing the cost 
14 
of capital for a regulated utility. 
15 
A. 
The foundations of public utility regulation require that utilities receive a 
16 
fair rate of return sufficient to attract needed capital to maintain service to 
17 
customers at reasonable rates.  The basic tenets of this regulatory doctrine 
18 
originate from two decisions by the United States Supreme Court, notably 
19 
Bluefield Waterworks and Improvement Company v. Public Service 
20 
Commission of West Virginia, 262 U.S. 679 (1923) (Bluefield), and Federal 
21 
Power Commission v. Hope Natural Gas Company, 320 U.S. 591 (1944) 
22 
(Hope).  In Bluefield, the Court stated:   
23 
A public utility is entitled to such rates as will permit it to 
24 
earn a return on the value of the property which it employs 
25

Page 7 of 72 
for the convenience of the public equal to that generally 
1 
being made at the same time and in the same general part of 
2 
the country on investments in other business undertakings 
3 
which 
are 
attended 
by 
corresponding 
risks 
and 
4 
uncertainties… 
5 
The return should be reasonably sufficient to assure investor 
6 
confidence in the financial soundness of the utility and 
7 
should be adequate, under efficient and economical 
8 
management, to maintain and support its credit and enable it 
9 
to raise the money necessary for the proper discharge of its 
10 
public duties. 
11 
Later, in Hope, the Court established a standard for the ROE that remains 
12 
the guiding principle for ratemaking regulatory proceedings to this day: 
13 
[T]he return to the equity owner should be commensurate 
14 
with returns on investments in other enterprises having 
15 
corresponding risks.  That return, moreover, should be 
16 
sufficient to assure confidence in the financial integrity of 
17 
the enterprise, so as to maintain its credit and to attract 
18 
capital. 
19 
Q. 
Please explain how these principles apply in the context of the regulated 
20 
rate of return.  
21 
A. 
Regulated utilities rely primarily on common stock and long-term debt to 
22 
finance permanent property, plant, and equipment.  The allowed rate of 
23 
return for a regulated utility is based on its weighted average cost of capital, 
24 
where the costs of the individual sources of capital (i.e., debt and equity) 
25 
are weighted by their respective book values.  The ROE represents the cost 
26 
of raising and retaining equity capital and is estimated with one or more 
27 
analytical techniques that use market data to quantify investor requirements 
28 
for equity returns.  However, the ROE cannot be derived mechanically 
29 
through quantitative metrics and models alone.  To properly estimate the 
30

ROE, the financial, regulatory, and economic context must also be 
1 
considered. 
2 
Based on the standards established in Hope and Bluefield, the 
3 
authorized ROE in this proceeding should provide the Company with the 
4 
opportunity to earn a fair and reasonable return that is: 
5 
 Adequate to allow the Company to attract the capital that is 
6 
necessary to provide safe and reliable service (the “capital attraction 
7 
standard”); 
8 
 Sufficient to ensure the Company’s ability to maintain its financial 
9 
integrity (the “financial integrity standard”); and 
10 
 At a level that is comparable to returns required on investments of 
11 
similar risk (the “comparability standard”).  
12 
Importantly, a fair return must satisfy all three of these standards.  The 
13 
allowed ROE should enable the Company to finance capital expenditures 
14 
on reasonable terms and provide the Company with the ability to raise 
15 
capital under a full range of capital market circumstances to serve its 
16 
customers.  The DCF and CAPM approaches, while fundamental to the 
17 
ROE determination, are still only models.  The results of these models 
18 
cannot be mechanically applied without also using informed judgment to
