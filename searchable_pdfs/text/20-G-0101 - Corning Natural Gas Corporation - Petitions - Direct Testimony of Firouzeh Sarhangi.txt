Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act
4 
Q. 
Given that the CLCPA is a new policy issue not addressed in previous cases, please 
5 
comment on its relevance. 
6 
A. 
The CLCPA, in conjunction with additional proposed legislation in the Governor’s 
7 
budget, is intended to reduce and then eliminate greenhouse gas emissions in New 
8 
York State.  Over time, this legislation will reduce gas use, revenue and access to 
9 
external capital.  It is critical that new mandated capital expenditures be financed with 
10 
internally generated funds (principally depreciation) and that depreciable lives match 
11 
the expected economic lives of utility assets.  The Company believes that either 
12 
systematic replacement capital expenditures have to be curtailed or the depreciable 
13 
lives of assets have to be reduced.  It is imperative that depreciable lives are reconciled 
14 
with the expectations of the CLCPA in this case, rather than delaying such action.  
15 
First, nominal gas commodity prices are low, so consumer impacts will be mitigated.  
16 
Second, it is important to stabilize/reduce rate base while the Company’s customer 
17 
count remains high.  Third, it is confiscatory to mandate large capital expenditures and 
18 
not provide depreciable lives consistent with CLCPA. 
19 
As the CLCPA takes effect, volumes will be reduced and rate base should decline in 
20 
parallel.  Addressing the CLCPA and depreciation in this case is consistent with how 
21 
the Commission addressed the federal Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017 (the "Federal 
22 
Tax Act") in Case 17-M-0815 (i.e., generic proceedings for companies not before the 
23 
PSC in a rate case; individual rate cases for companies already before the Commission 
24

Q. 
Have you reflected the impact of the CLCPA in the Company’s revenue 
3 
requirement? 
4 
A. 
Yes.  New York State passed the CLCPA that mandates reduced greenhouse gas 
5 
emissions to 60% of 1990 levels by 2030, and 15% of 1990 levels by 2050, with the 
6 
remaining emission reduction achieved by controlled offsets.  The CLCPA also 
7 
requires electric generators to meet 70% of demand with renewable energy by 2030.  
8 
These climate change and greenhouse gas initiatives will impact the Company's 
9 
customer base over time.  The initiatives will also increase the Company’s cost of 
10 
environmental compliance by increasing reporting requirements and requiring the 
11 
Company to repair all leaks and replace all leak prone pipe.  This will adversely 
12 
affect the Company’s ability to raise funds.  The CLCPA has a very direct and 
13 
undeniable impact on Corning's facilities and construction expenditures.  In addition 
14 
to requiring greater expenditures to address the CLCPA's mandates, the new law 
15 
effectively shortens the effective life of the Company's existing and future 
16 
investment in infrastructure.  As a consequence of these impacts of the CLCPA, we 
17 
are proposing that the service lives of the Company's fixed assets be reduced to 30 
18 
years for purposes of calculating depreciation expense. 
19 
Adequate depreciation will allow the Company to continue it systematic 
20 
replacement program with more internally generated funds.  Given the CLCPA 
21 
external funds will become increasingly difficult to generate at competitive rates, 
22 
even though the Commission continues to require significant investment in pipe 
23 
replacement and other system upgrades.  It is confiscatory to base rates on
