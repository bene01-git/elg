The Brooklyn Union Gas Company d/b/a National Grid NY (“KEDNY” or “Company”) 
hereby submits this verified petition to modify the “Capital Expenditures and Net Utility Plant 
and Depreciation Expense Reconciliation Mechanism” (“Capital Reconciliation Mechanism”) 
that was included in the Joint Proposal adopted by the New York State Public Service 
Commission (“Commission”) by order dated June 13, 20131 in the above proceeding.  As more 
fully discussed below, the proposed modification to the Capital Reconciliation Mechanism 
would permit a cumulative four-year reconciliation of the revenue requirement associated with 
KEDNY’s actual capital expenditures to the established target revenue requirement over the 
period January 1, 2013 through December 31, 2016.  As is, the Capital Reconciliation 
Mechanism permits a cumulative, two-year reconciliation for the two years ended December 31, 
2014 and annual reconciliations thereafter.

KEDNY’s Current Rate Plan and the Capital Reconciliation Mechanism

ending December 31, 2012.  In its June 13 Order, the Commission modified and extended

Under its proactive leak prone pipe replacement program, the Company uses a risk-based

incident probabilities and consequences.  This algorithm has enabled KEDNY to develop a 
comprehensive replacement program to improve the overall risk profile of its gas distribution 
network and to optimize resources available for the program.

3. 
Pipeline Integrity Expenditures 
 
KEDNY’s transmission pipeline Integrity Management Plan (“IMP”) is a federally 
mandated safety program established in response to the Pipeline Safety Improvement Act of 
2002.  The purpose of the Company’s IMP is to identify and address defects affecting the 
physical soundness of the Company’s facilities before they become safety/performance 
problems. 
 
The IMP requires the Company to conduct baseline and periodic assessments of its 
transmission facilities to identify and evaluate threats to “covered segments” of pipelines, such as 
transmission facilities that could affect “High Consequence Areas”10 in the event of a pipeline 
failure, as well as remediation of significant defects discovered during such assessments.  
Although the costs of tests and inspections under the IMP are operating expenses, capital work is 
required to support in-line inspections, such as for the installation of pig launchers and receivers, 
for pipe reconfiguration/removal, and to resolve issues discovered during physical inspections.

7. 
Installation of AMRs 
 
KEDNY is proceeding with the installation of AMRs throughout its service territory.  To 
date, KEDNY has deployed more than 770,000 AMR devices (approximately 60 percent of 
accounts).  This program will complete KEDNY’s AMR deployment by installing an additional 
528,000 devices on the remaining meters.  The installation of AMRs will decrease the incidence 
of both estimated bills and erroneous bills that require cancellation and rebilling.  Additionally, 
the use of AMRs will enhance storm resiliency and improve customer service.  In the aftermath 
of major storms, meters are not read because of the need to use the meter-reading workforce to 
assist in storm recovery.  AMRs will allow the Company to continuing gathering gas usage data 
following major weather events, and that data can be used to identify customer outages and

data, which assists in accurate billing following an extended outage.  While the deployment of 
AMRs will ultimately reduce operating and maintenance expense, the program must be 
completed before savings are realized.

requests that the Commission modify its Capital Reconciliation Mechanism to provide for a 
cumulative four-year reconciliation of the revenue requirement associated with its actual capital 
expenditures to the established target revenue requirement15 over the period January 1, 2013 
through December 31, 2016.

reconciliation to a cumulative four-year reconciliation.  Under this proposal, KEDNY would be

PURSUANT TO THE PROVISIONS of the State Administrative Procedure Act, NOTICE is

Proposed Action: The New York Public Service Commission is considering whether to approve 
or reject, in whole or in part, a petition filed by The Brooklyn Union Gas Company d/b/a 
National Grid NY (“KEDNY” or the “Company”) requesting modification of its existing Capital 
Expenditures and Net Utility Plant and Depreciation Expense Reconciliation Mechanism 
(“Capital Reconciliation Mechanism”). 
 
Statutory authority: Public Service Law, Section 66 
 
Subject: Uniform System of Accounts – Deferral of a cost item. 
 
Purpose: Modify KEDNY’s Capital Reconciliation Mechanism. 
 
Substance of the proposed rule: The Public Service Commission is considering a request by 
KEDNY to modify its existing Capital Reconciliation Mechanism to provide for a cumulative 
reconciliation of the difference between KEDNY’s actual annual average net utility plant and 
depreciation expense revenue requirement and KEDNY’s target average net utility expense 
revenue requirement over a four year period beginning January 1, 2013 and ending December 
31, 2016.  The Commission may approve, reject or modify, in whole or in part, KEDNY’s 
request.
