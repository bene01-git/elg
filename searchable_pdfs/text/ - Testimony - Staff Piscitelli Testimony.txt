Fair Rate of Return 
5 
Q. 
Please discuss the estimation of a utility’s 
6 
overall rate of return. 
7 
A. 
A utility's overall rate of return represents 
8 
the cost of capital used to finance the assets 
9 
providing regulated utility service to 
10 
customers.  The estimation of a utility’s rate 
11 
of return requires an estimate of the company’s 
12 
capital structure and the cost rates of the 
13 
various forms of capital financing its assets.  
14 
The ratemaking process should provide a utility 
15 
with a reasonable opportunity to earn a return 
16 
on utility investments equivalent to the return 
17 
available on investments of similar risk.  It is 
18 
management’s ultimate responsibility to operate 
19 
the company in a manner that allows it to 
20 
actually earn the allowed return for its 
21 
investors. 
22 
Q. 
How is the fair rate of return calculated? 
23 
A. 
In New York State, the fair rate of return for a 
24 


--- Page 8 ---
Cases 16-G-0058 and 16-G-0059 
 
Piscitelli 
 
 
7 
 
utility company is calculated through a weighted 
1 
average cost of capital (WACC), which is 
2 
comprised of common equity, customer deposits, 
3 
preferred stock and debt.  Calculating the 
4 
proper cost components involves forecasting and 
5 
reconciling a company's sources of capital with 
6 
their individual requirements.  Long-term debt 
7 
and common equity are the two primary sources of 
8 
capital, while customer deposits typically 
9 
account for a very small portion of the total 
10 
capital.  Preferred stock is not commonly used, 
11 
but can be a source of capital as well. 
12 
Q. 
How are the cost rates determined for each of 
13 
the individual components? 
14 
A. 
The method for estimating the current cost of 
15 
long-term debt is generally established in the 
16 
financing agreements between a company and its 
17 
lending institutions.  In return for lending 
18 
money to a company, debt holders receive 
19 
earnings in the form of contractual payments of 
20 
interest and principal.  The analyst must, then, 
21 
forecast the amounts, timing and terms of any 
22 
contemplated future debt issuances through the 
23 
rate year to estimate the weighted average rate 
24 


--- Page 9 ---
Cases 16-G-0058 and 16-G-0059 
 
Piscitelli 
 
 
8 
 
year cost of debt.  Customer deposits have a 
1 
prescribed cost that is set by the Commission on 
2 
an annual basis.  The customer deposit rate is 
3 
currently established by the Commission at .85%.  
4 
The Companies have not issued preferred stock, 
5 
and have no plans to do so in the near future, 
6 
thus it is a non-factor in this calculation.  
7 
Finally, common equity returns are neither 
8 
contractual nor prescribed by the Commission 
9 
and, therefore, must be estimated through 
10 
analysis.
