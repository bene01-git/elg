INTRODUCTION 
 
 
On December 22, 2023, Niagara Mohawk Power Corporation 
d/b/a National Grid (Niagara Mohawk or the Company) filed a 
petition (the Petition) for a limited waiver of Rule 43.5.1 of 
P.S.C. 220 – Electric Service Tariff (Electric Tariff).  Rule 
43.5.1 institutes a monthly cap in the amount of $8 million that 
can be collected from or credited to customers through the 
Transmission Revenue Adjustment (TRA).  The Petition proposes to 
temporarily increase the monthly cap amount from $8 million to 
$16 million as of May 1, 2024, through the end of the Company’s

BACKGROUND 
 
 
The Company’s Electric Tariff Rule 43 contains 
provisions governing operation of the TRA.  The TRA reconciles 
the forecast transmission revenue credits included in base 
delivery rates to the actual transmission revenues realized by 
the Company, exclusive of revenue taxes.  Specifically, each 
month, the Company compares the actual transmission revenues to 
those included in base delivery rates and either collects or 
credits any variances up to $8 million.2  Any remaining 
difference, in excess of $8 million, is deferred to the next 
month and is subject to carrying charges at the Company’s pre-
tax weighted average cost of capital.  The monthly 
credit/collection is allocated to the applicable service

As of December 2023, the Company had a deferred TRA 
balance of $386.2 million.  The deferred balance is primarily

contract auction revenues in excess of the amounts the Rate

the Rate Plan.  In the Petition, the Company claims that 
increasing the monthly TRA cap from $8 million to $16 million 
through the end of the Rate Plan would provide immediate 
benefits to customers while balancing future rate impacts.  
Specifically, the Company indicates that, excluding the TRA 
balance, it has a deferred regulatory asset balance of

claims that the increase in the monthly TRA cap would not impact

PUBLIC COMMENTS 
 
 
Multiple Intervenors filed comments in favor of 
granting the limited waiver of Rule 43.5.1 on February 13, 2024.  
Multiple Intervenors states that since the TRA balance exceeded 
the $6 million cap for multiple months, and also exceeded the $8 
million cap, it would be appropriate to increase that cap to $16 
million as the Company proposes.  According to Multiple 
Intervenors, this would allow customers to take advantage of the 
additional transmission revenues over the remainder of the Rate 
Plan.  In the remaining 11 months of the Rate Plan that the 
Company proposes to increase the cap, Multiple Intervenors 
estimates there would be an additional $88 million dollars 
credited to customers of the total $386.2 million excess 
transmission revenues in the TRA balance as of December 22, 
2023.  Multiple Intervenors argues that during a particularly 
tough economic climate, these excess funds should be returned to 
customers.  Multiple Intervenors posits that delivery rates 
would have been lower under the Rate Order adopting the terms of 
the parties’ joint proposal if the parties had the benefit of 
more accurate transmission revenue forecast.

DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION 
 
 
While base rates reflect an estimate of transmission 
revenues, the actual transmission revenues received by the 
Company are variable and difficult to predict.  Accordingly, to 
protect Niagara Mohawk customers from bill volatility and to 
ensure they receive the benefit of the transmission revenues 
received by the Company, the Commission approved the existing 
TRA mechanism caps in 2005.3  At that time, the caps were 
established based on a lower level of transmission revenue, as 
well as lower delivery bills.  Over time, delivery rates and 
bills have increased, as too has imputed transmission revenue, 
but these caps have remained unchanged.  In the years since the 
Commission approved the Company’s current Rate Plan, the TRA 
balance has grown to a level that cannot be returned to 
customers in a timely manner under the cap constraints of the 
existing TRA mechanism.  Therefore, we agree with Multiple 
Intervenors that it is reasonable to grant a limited waiver of 
the TRA provisions under the Electric Tariff to increase the 
monthly TRA cap from $8 million to $16 million, thereby 
decreasing electric bills for customers.  Granting the temporary 
waiver will allow the Company to use the TRA mechanism to refund 
transmission revenues to customers in a more timely manner than 
would otherwise be possible, while not materially impacting the 
Company’s cash flow metrics.  Therefore, the Commission grants 
the Company’s request for a limited waiver of its Electric 
Tariff Rule 43.5.1 and will allow it to increase the monthly TRA 
cap from $8 million to $16 million beginning May 1, 2024, 
through March 31, 2025.
