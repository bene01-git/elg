LEAK PRONE PIPE REMOVAL 
8 
Q. 
What type of pipe is considered leak prone? 
9 
A. 
LPP generally includes unprotected steel, cast 
10 
iron, wrought iron, and some early vintages of 
11 
plastic pipe.  The Company’s response to IR DPS-
12 
316, shows that NMPC’s LPP consists of 
13 
unprotected steel and cast iron. 
14 
Q. 
What is meant by the term unprotected? 
15 
A. 
Unprotected means that the pipe lacks adequate 
16 
cathodic protection, thus rendering it 
17 
susceptible to corrosion.  Unprotected steel 
18 
often has inadequate or no coating, which makes 
19 
efforts to cathodically protect such pipe 
20 
ineffective and uneconomical. 
21 
Q. 
How do LPP removal programs improve the safety 
22 
of natural gas systems? 
23 
A. 
Leaks on underground piping can create safety 
24 


--- Page 11 ---
Cases 20-E-0380 and 20-G-0381 
SPSP 
 
10 
risks to the public and potentially lead to 
1 
serious incidents, resulting in injuries to 
2 
persons or property damage.  Therefore, removing 
3 
LPP reduces these safety risks. 
4 
Q. 
Please explain the importance of removing 
5 
unprotected steel pipe. 
6 
A. 
Data collected by the United States Department 
7 
of Transportation, Pipeline and Hazardous 
8 
Materials Safety Administration (PHMSA) shows 
9 
that corrosion is a leading cause of pipeline 
10 
failures, and that unprotected steel is the more 
11 
susceptible material to experience corrosion. 
12 
Q. 
Please explain the importance of removing cast 
13 
iron pipe. 
14 
A. 
In general, cast iron pipe is brittle, 
15 
susceptible to graphitization, which is a form 
16 
of corrosion, and has low beam strength.  Beam 
17 
strength refers to the amount of load a 
18 
structure can withstand before it fails.  Low 
19 
beam strength means that cast iron pipe can fail 
20 
if it is subjected to increased loading from a 
21 
force, or from a loss of ground support.  This 
22 
characteristic makes the material particularly 
23 
susceptible to stresses from underground forces 
24 


--- Page 12 ---
Cases 20-E-0380 and 20-G-0381 
SPSP 
 
11 
or disturbances.  Such disturbances include, but 
1 
are not limited to, ground settlement, freeze-
2 
thaw cycles, soil erosion or nearby excavation 
3 
activities.  The physical characteristics of 
4 
cast iron make it more prone to catastrophic 
5 
failure than cathodically protected steel or 
6 
plastic.  In addition, segments of cast iron are 
7 
joined by hub joints, also known as bell and 
8 
spigot joints.  Packing material, such as jute, 
9 
was used in the joints to prevent leakage when 
10 
cast iron pipelines were first installed in the 
11 
19th century.  Unlike steel welded joints, cast 
12 
iron joints can develop leaks over time. 
13 
Q. 
How does the removal of cast iron contribute to 
14 
the safety of the natural gas system? 
15 
A. 
Cast iron tends to be located in densely 
16 
populated areas where there are many enclosed 
17 
structures, subsurface structures, and 
18 
continuously paved areas.  In the event of a 
19 
major leak or pipeline failure, these 
20 
environmental circumstances may lead to greater 
21 
volumes of below-ground gas migration, 
22 
particularly under paved areas and through 
23 
connected subsurface structures, which creates 
24 


--- Page 13 ---
Cases 20-E-0380 and 20-G-0381 
SPSP 
 
12 
an increased risk for fires or explosions.  
1 
Removing cast iron pipe reduces the potential 
2 
for incidents resulting from major leaks and 
3 
failures, thereby improving public safety. 
4 
Q. 
Are there other benefits associated with 
5 
removing LPP? 
6 
A. 
Yes, removing LPP should reduce the number of 
7 
active leaks, lead to a decline in leakage rates 
8 
on the distribution system, reduce methane 
9 
emissions, and reduce the operating, 
10 
maintenance, and overtime costs associated with 
11 
surveying leaks and responding to emergency 
12 
reports.

Q. 
How does NMPC prioritize the removal of LPP? 
2 
A. 
The pipe to be removed from service is 
3 
identified and ranked using a risk assessment 
4 
model-based approach. 
5 
Q. 
What is a risk assessment model? 
6 
A. 
A risk assessment model prioritizes all segments 
7 
of LPP according to attributes that pose the 
8 
highest associated risk.  The model uses several 
9 
weighted factors, including, but not limited to 
10 
material type, diameter, pressure, date of 
11 
installation, and leak history, to determine a 
12 
ranking.  This allows the Company to provide a 
13 
higher level of public safety by focusing its 
14 
efforts on pipeline segments with higher risk.  
15 
Although there are commonalities between the 
16 
State’s distribution companies, it is important 
17 
to note that the model has been tailored to the 
18 
Company’s unique mix of assets and may not be 
19 
appropriate for other LDCs.  A uniform approach 
20 
cannot accommodate the characteristics that must 
21 
be considered for each system. 
22

5 
A.

6

7

does not require access to the premises in order 
13 
to perform the required inside inspections.
