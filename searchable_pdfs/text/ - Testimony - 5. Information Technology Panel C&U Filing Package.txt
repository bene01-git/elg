1 
 
CIS Project 
2 
Q. 
Please describe the corrections and updates associated with the proposed CIS 
3 
project. 
4 
 A. 
There are two changes related to the CIS project: (i) inclusion of inadvertently 
5 
omitted AFUDC; and (ii) an update to the in-service dates initially proposed. 
6 
 
7 
Q. 
Please describe the correction to include AFUDC in the determination of the 
8 
cost of the CIS project. 
 
9 
A. 
Large, longer-term capital projects, such as the planned CIS project, require 
10 
utilities to finance the cost of development prior to the asset going in-service and 
11 
becoming used and useful.  Utilities in New York are compensated for their costs 
12 
to finance such projects through the accrual of AFUDC for recovery in rates.  In 
13 
their initial filings in these proceedings, the Companies inadvertently failed to 
14 
include any AFUDC amount associated with the CIS project in the model used to 
15 
determine the revenue requirements for KEDNY and KEDLI.  The Companies are 
16 
correcting that error by including the appropriate AFUDC amount in their 
17 
corrected and updated revenue requirements.  
18 
 
19

Q. 
What is the amount of AFUDC included for the CIS project? 
1 
A. 
The total forecast AFUDC amount attributable to the CIS project is approximately 
2 
$41 million.  Of that amount, $26.7 million is associated with projects that go into 
3 
service during the Rate Years and Data Years, and it is therefore appropriate to 
4 
include KEDNY and KEDLI’s allocable share of this amount in the determination 
5 
of the Companies’ revenue requirements in these proceedings.

10 
change results in a corresponding change of the in-service date for the CIS project 
11 
from April 1, 2022 to October 1, 2022 for both KEDNY and KEDLI.  See Exhibit 
12 
__ (ITP-4CU), page 1 of 5, which reflects the new planned in-service dates.     
13

Changes To The Mandated Projects Budget    
6 
Q. 
Please describe the updates to the capital and operation expense budgets for 
7 
mandated IT investments anticipated to occur during the Rate Year and 
8 
Data Years. 
9 
A. 
Each year, National Grid is required to invest significant amounts in IT projects 
10 
needed to implement requirements imposed by governmental authorities.  Such 
11 
authorities include state and federal utility regulators, state or federal lawmakers, 
12 
taxing authorities, and similar bodies.  Based on the experience of being subject to 
13 
annual emergent mandatory IT obligations, National Grid proactively budgets for 
14 
such expenditures and includes an amount in its annual budget for anticipated 
15 
mandatory projects, the details of which are generally not known at the start of the 
16 
budget year.  In their April 30, 2019 filings, the Companies included $20 million 
17 
per year of capital investment for mandated IT projects across National Grid’s US 
18 
operations for the Rate Year and each Data Year as shown in Exhibit __ (ITP-4), 
19 
page 1 of 5, and annual operating expenses of between $1.5 million and $2.5 
20 
million for such projects as shown in Exhibit __ (ITP-7), page 1 of 2.  Subsequent 
21 


--- Page 8 ---
 
Corrections and Updates Testimony of the Information Technology Panel 
Page 6 of 10 
to the April 30, 2019 filing, National Grid reexamined the inputs and historical 
1 
information used to estimate the annual mandatory projects capital and operating 
2 
expense budgets and determined that the capital investment amount should be 
3 
reduced to $14 million per year for each of the Rate Year and the Data Years, and 
4 
the operating expense budget should be increased to $6 million per year.   These 
5 
changes are reflected in Exhibit __ (ITP-4CU), page 1 of 5, and Exhibit __ (ITP-
6 
7CU), page 1 of 2.

18 
during the “Discovery Roadmap” phase of the project, which began after the

Updated RTB Costs  
6 
Q. 
Please describe the correction to address omitted RTB costs for projects that 
7 
went or will go into service prior to the end of the Rate Year.   
8 
A. 
RTB costs are the costs of operating and maintaining an IT project once it is in 
9 
service.  Such costs include costs associated with software licensing and 
10 
maintenance agreements, hardware maintenance agreements, and application 
11 
support contracts, as well as consultant/contractor costs, and internal labor.   
12 
 
13 
 
National Grid continually puts new IT projects into service and incurs RTB costs 
14 
associated with such projects.  In developing its operating expense forecast for the 
15 
April 30, 2019 filing, the Companies failed to identify the RTB costs for the 
16 
projects that went in-service, or are planned to go in-service, in the time period 
17 
between the Historic Test Year and the Rate Year.  Additionally, the Companies 
18 
inadvertently omitted RTB costs in Data Years 1, 2, and 3 for certain projects that 
19 
were properly reflected in the forecast for the Rate Year.  The updated RTB costs 
20 
are reflected on Exhibits __ (ITP-7CU) and (ITP-8CU) and total $28.5 million in 
21

Page 10 of 10

1 
KEDLI, respectively, above the level included in the Companies’ April 30 filings.
