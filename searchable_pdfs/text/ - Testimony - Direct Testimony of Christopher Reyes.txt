A. 
My testimony addresses the Gas Demand Response programs implemented by KeySpan 
1 
Energy Delivery of New York, Inc. (KEDNY) and KeySpan Energy Delivery Long 
2 
Island (KEDLI, and collectively with KEDNY, National Grid). 
3 
Q. 
PLEASE DESCRIBE THOSE PROGRAMS. 
4 
A. 
As National Grid’s Customer Panel explains in their pre-filed testimony, National Grid’s 
5 
Gas Demand Response programs provide a monetary incentive for commercial, 
6 
industrial, and multifamily firm service gas customers either to reduce or shift their gas 
7 
consumption for a pre-determined amount of time when requested.  These programs 
8 
benefit both National Grid and its customers by reducing the need for costly 
9 
infrastructure upgrades and increasing gas system reliability. 
10 
Q. 
WHAT CONCERNS DOES NRG HAVE WITH THIS PROGRAM? 
11 
A. 
Unfortunately, when National Grid enrolls an eligible customer that is receiving gas 
12 
service from NRG or another ESCO in its Demand Response program, National Grid 
13 
does not inform the customer’s ESCO of this fact.  Similarly, when National Grid asks 
14 
customers served by NRG or another ESCO to reduce or shift their demands, the 
15 
ESCO(s) serving those customers similarly do not receive notice from National Grid. 
16 
Q. 
WHY IS THIS A CONCERN FOR NRG? 
17 
A. 
As an ESCO supplying natural gas to customers on National Grid’s distribution systems, 
18 
NRG formulates customer pricing based on estimated customer usage.  When a customer 
19 
enrolls in a National Grid Gas Demand Response program, it is likely that the customer 
20 
will reduce its usage below the levels anticipated by NRG.  The reduction in usage causes 
21 
financial harm to NRG because NRG includes certain charges (such as mandatory asset 
22 
related demand charges) in customer pricing on a per dekatherm basis.  If a customer’s 
23

usage is reduced below anticipated levels by a Utility Initiated Demand Response Event,

Q. 
IS THIS THE ONLY RISK THAT NRG FACES AS A RESULT OF NATIONAL 
6 
GRID’S GAS DEMAND RESPONSE PROGRAMS? 
7 
A. 
No.  NRG is also at risk for higher than anticipated cash out volumes and dollars as the 
8 
amount of natural gas that is delivered by NRG will no longer be consistent with the 
9 
customer’s anticipated usage due to the demand reduction.  This adds additional 
10 
monetary risk as the amount of the cash out will not match the estimated costs used to 
11 
price out the customer.

We do try to do this, but often the person we deal with is not the person enrolling the

17

18

19 
guide NRG’s gas procurement process.

Q. 
WHAT EXACTLY ARE YOU PROPOSING THAT NATIONAL GRID SHOULD 
21

A. 
National Grid’s tariffs should be amended to include a separate rate class for customers 
1 
participating in its Demand Response programs and to require such customers to specify 
2 
the volumetric level at which they have selected to participate.  A new rate class would 
3 
enable NRG and other ESCOs to immediately identify existing customers who are 
4 
participating in the program and to determine the volumetric level at which they have 
5 
selected to participate.  ESCOs would also be able to tell when additional customers 
6 
switch to the program, as their rate class would change.  That would enable ESCOs to 
7 
better estimate and discuss appropriate changes with the customer. 
8 
9 
In addition, National Grid’s tariffs should also be revised to require National Grid to 
10 
provide ESCOs with specific details about the Demand Response Events, specifically -
11 
what date and time period the event is called and the volume reduction at a customer 
12 
level, as soon as practicable after the customer agrees to participate in such demand 
13 
response events. This information will allow ESCOs to understand the usage impacts to 
14 
our operating pools and give us a better view into our anticipated pool imbalances.
