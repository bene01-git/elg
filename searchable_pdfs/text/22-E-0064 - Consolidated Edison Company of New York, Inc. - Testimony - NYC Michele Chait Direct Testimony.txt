A. 
The purpose of MOCEJ’s analysis was to better understand potential utility energy bills 
1 
(natural gas and electricity) for the Company’s residential customers before and after 
2 
adopting heat pumps.  The analysis required two steps.  The first step involved developing 
3 
electric and natural gas usage scenarios for hypothetical buildings under different 
4 
scenarios.  Usage scenarios differed based on the building type (1-4 Family or Multi-
5 
Family); the building’s level of energy efficiency (“EE”) (base or high); and whether 
6 
natural gas or heat pumps were assumed to serve heating loads.  In the second step, these 
7 
usage scenarios were applied to current Con Edison rates to estimate total annual energy 
8 
bills for each scenario. 
9 
Q. 
WHAT ASSUMPTIONS WERE MADE IN THE ANALYSIS? 
10 
A. 
The following assumptions were made.  Customers were assumed to reside in either a 3-
11 
family building or a 14-unit multifamily building.  Prior to adopting a heat pump, 
12 
customers in all scenarios were assumed to use natural gas for heating needs.  Customers 
13 
residing in the 3-unit building were assumed to be individually metered for natural gas and 
14 
electricity.  Customers residing in the 14-unit multifamily building were assumed to be 
15 
individually metered for electricity but natural gas costs were assumed to be included in 
16 
rent.  2019 data for New York City from the U.S. Census Bureau’s American Housing 
17 
Survey indicates that approximately 30% of units of a similar size to those in the MOCEJ’s 
18 
analysis have natural gas supply included in rent.
