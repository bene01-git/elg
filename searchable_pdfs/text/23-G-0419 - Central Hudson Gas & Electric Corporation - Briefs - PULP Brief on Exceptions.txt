I. 
INTRODUCTION 
Pursuant to the Notice of Schedule for Filing Exceptions issued May 1, 2024, in the 
above-referenced proceeding, the Public Utility Law Project of New York, Inc. ("PULP") hereby 
submits its Brief on Exceptions to the Recommended Decision (“RD”) by Administrative Law 
Judges (“ALJs”) in this proceeding. The Brief on Exceptions does not repeat the arguments 
presented by PULP in its Initial and Reply Briefs, except to the extent necessary to respond to 
the RD.1  
II. 
EXCEPTIONS 
a. Labor Expense 
i. Exception 1: The RD mistakes PULP’s proposal on full time equivalents 
(“FTEs”) in relation to prudence proceedings, while also overlooking 
PULP’s concerns with FTEs as developed in litigation.   
PULP takes exception with the RD’s position on FTEs.2 To begin, the RD misunderstood 
PULP’s proposal from initial testimony, filed on November 21, 2023, where we recommended 
that costs associated with incremental FTEs be deferred pending the outcome of a prudence 
review.3 PULP believes that by deferring these costs, the utility would not be able to recognize 
the income from the recovery of costs associated with these hires, unless a prudence review 
occurred and resulted in a favorable outcome for the Company. However, a prudence review 
finding against the Company where any costs associated with the FTEs had been deferred 
previously would have resulted in a surcredit to customers to the extent of any consequential 
expenses deemed to be imprudently incurred. This outcome is essentially the same as a “refund,” 
except that PULP specified that a surcredit should be provided to customers in the amount of 
such refund.  
Additionally, PULP does not believe that we contradicted our testimonial position.4

i.  Exception 2: IVR funding should not be approved until all SAP CIS issues 
have been fully resolved. 
PULP takes exception to the RD’s recommendation that the $1.7 million included in the 
Billing Reporting Stipulation for IVR modernization be included in the final rate plan.12 PULP

queries and analytics.14 As a result, we do not believe that Staff nor the Company have shown a 
sufficient need for the enhanced reporting.15  
Additionally, the Company has had well known and enduring issues related to the SAP 
CIS transition.16 The issues arising from that system upgrade resulted in “unprecedented levels of 
customer dissatisfaction.”17 While PULP acknowledges that IVR modernization would result in

c.  Disadvantaged Communities 
i.  Exception 4: Rate cases are an appropriate venue to address bill impacts for 
Disadvantaged Communities.  
PULP respectfully takes exception to the statement in the RD that a rate proceeding is not 
the appropriate venue to address bill impacts for disadvantaged communities (“DACs”), and 
rather it would be more appropriate for Commission consideration in a generic proceeding.25 
PULP still has the same concerns we have reiterated throughout this rate proceeding as it 
pertains to DACs. Each utility across the State has a different number of DACs and it is 
imperative that each utility, including Central Hudson, begin specific outreach and direct benefits 
to DACs. Enrollment in the Energy Affordability Program (“EAP”) is not enough. While it is 
accurate to state that the EAP "helps offset the impacts of rate increases by providing bill 
discounts to low-income customers, including those located in DACs,"26 the Company's own 
testimony stated that “based on its projected participation in the Rate Year, applying the current 
discount levels, it expects the program budget to be exceeded and, consequently, proposed to 
reduce gas heating discounts, to ensure it could serve all eligible customers and stay on 
budget.”27  
There are other proposals in this rate proceeding related to DACs that will be decided by 
the Commission.28 Additionally, the RD states “we find that the rate plans we recommend 
appropriately balance the interests in reliability, public safety, and reasonable rates with GHG 
emissions reduction and clean energy objectives, without disproportionately burdening DACs.”29 
Respectfully, this position seems contradictory, as the RD suggests that considerations for DACs

d.  Customer Service 
i.  Exception 5: Central Hudson has not proven that the status quo provides an 
adequate incentive mechanism. 
PULP takes exception to the recommendation in the RD that any performance incentives, 
such as Negative Revenue Adjustments (“NRAs”) should be maintained at the status quo. When 
considering Earnings Adjustment Mechanisms (“EAMs”), the ALJs believed the status quo was 
inappropriate, but deviated from that logic with regards to NRAs.30 The RD accurately states that 
the authority to determine specific and targeted utility performance programs based on a 
combination of the individual characteristics of a utility’s infrastructure and customer base, 
together with a utility’s historic performance, is necessarily incidental to the power expressly 
granted in PSL §65(1).31 However, the application of this principle in the RD was found wanting 
as the individual characteristics and historic performance of Central Hudson suggests that more 
stringent measures are required to ensure that the Company is able to meet its baseline 
performance. For example, the RD, in citing Staff’s brief, states that the Company failed to meet 
its customer service metrics in both 2022 and 2023.32 Additionally, Central Hudson is one of a 
few major utilities with a widespread, ongoing investigation into customer services issues in the 
state.33 The above indicates that Central Hudson’s unique circumstances and historical 
performance warrant a departure from the status quo.

iv.  Exception 8: The RD erred in its characterization of PULP’s testimony 
regarding Extreme Heat Protections. 
PULP takes exception to the statements in the RD that characterizes PULP’s extreme 
heat proposals as “unwieldy and administratively difficult to track.”52 PULP’s testimony on this 
issue has remained consistent in rate cases across the state, and, in many instances, contributed to 
the enhanced extreme heat protections customers now enjoy. The Commission adopted Joint 
Proposals in New York State Electric and Gas Corporation (“NYSEG”), Rochester Gas and 
Electric Corporation ("RG&E), and Consolidated Edison Company of New York, Inc.  (“Con 
Edison”) that contained the core of PULPs extreme heat proposals.53 As the Commission has 
acknowledged in the past, the extreme weather protections arising from PULP’s efforts 
“appropriately protect customers from the loss of utility service when cooling may be necessary 
for customers health and safety during periods of extreme heat.”54 With average seasonal 
temperatures rising yearly, it is becoming more important than ever to protect customers during 
the cooling season.55 The Commission has wisely adopted enhanced protections to ensure the 
health and safety of utility customers across the state.56 Central Hudson should not be an 
exception to that practice.
