2. SUMMARY OF CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS 
1 
Q. 
Please summarize your conclusions. 
2 
A. 
My conclusions are summarized as follows: 
3 
• The Companies’ gas investments align with National Grid’s Clean Energy 
4 
Vision (CEV) and CLCPA study, however both of these documents depict 
5 
a pathway that is not compatible with the CLCPA. Further, the CEV 
6 
ignores very large costs that could fall on ratepayers. Given these 
7 
deficiencies and the lack of detail on its modeling and assumptions, it is not 
8 
appropriate to use the CEV for resource decisions in the context of this rate 
9 
case, or in any other proceeding. 
10 
• The Companies’ proposed investments in hydrogen, RNG, and pipe 
11 
replacement in this proceeding have negative impacts on affordability, do 
12 
not represent least-cost approaches to CLCPA compliance, and have 
13 
serious air quality and health implications from a continued reliance on 
14 
combustion of fuels. These investments appear to be a step in a larger 
15 
strategy that uses hydrogen, RNG, and pipe replacement to mitigate GHG 
16 
emissions from its system. However, this investment, and the larger 
17 
strategy, puts the Companies on a path that is inconsistent with the 
18 
CLCPA. 
19 
• The use of satellites for leak detection is unproven. 
20 


--- Page 8 ---
8 
 
• The Companies’ proposed negative incentive for failure to achieve 
1 
minimum leak-prone pipe replacement (LPP) targets and unitary cost 
2 
performance incentive for LPP would provide additional incentives to the 
3 
Companies to make gas-system investments that are not in the public 
4 
interest. 
5 
• The final strategy for decarbonizing the Companies’ load will almost 
6 
certainly differ from what National Grid outlined in its CLCPA study or 
7 
the CEV, because the Companies have not completed their Long-Term Gas 
8 
Plan and the related process, and the Department of Environmental 
9 
Conservation has yet to promulgate sector-specific reduction mandates 
10 
pursuant to the CLCPA. A longer rate plan term would cement the 
11 
Companies’ proposed strategy, making it more difficult and more 
12 
expensive to change course.  
13

3.1. Policy Background  
10 
Q. 
Please describe the energy and climate policy background against which 
11 
you considered the Companies’ proposed investments. 
12 
A.  
The CLCPA requires statewide reductions in greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions 
13 
across all sectors. Relative to 1990 levels, it requires reductions of 40 percent 
14 
by 2030 and no less than 85 percent by 2050.  
15 
In addition, this law created the Climate Action Council (CAC), which is 
16 
charged with developing a scoping plan to meet these targets, released in 
17 
December 2022 (“Scoping Plan”). To inform the Scoping Plan, the New York 
18 
State Energy Research and Development Authority (NYSERDA) and New 
19 


--- Page 11 ---
11 
 
York State Department of Environmental Conservation commissioned an 
1 
analysis, called the Integration Analysis, which was released in December, 
2 
20211 and examined the economy-wide benefits, costs, and GHG emissions 
3 
reductions of pathways to achieve the CLCPA emission limits. It modeled 
4 
three scenarios that meet CLCPA emission-reduction requirements: Strategic 
5 
Use of Low-Carbon Fuels (Scenario 2), Accelerated Transition Away from 
6 
Combustion (Scenario 3), and Beyond 85% Reduction (Scenario 4). For 
7 
context, the Integration Analysis also included a reference case and a scenario 
8 
based on the Advisory Panel’s (AP) initial recommendations (Scenario 1) but 
9 
did not project that either will meet CLCPA requirements.2
