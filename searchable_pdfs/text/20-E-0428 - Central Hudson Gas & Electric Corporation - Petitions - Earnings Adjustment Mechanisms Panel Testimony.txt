III. 
INTRODUCTORY BACKGROUND 
7 
Q.    
What are EAMs? 
8 
A. 
EAMs are designed to incentivize the utility to facilitate specific outcomes 
9 
that are deemed beneficial to its customers and align with State policy.  
10 
Q.    
What was the Commission’s rationale for creating EAMs in the REV Track 
11 
2 Order?  
12 
A. 
In the May 19, 2016 REV Track 2 Order in Case 14-M-0101, the 
13 
Commission focused on establishing a “modern regulatory model” that 
14 
challenges utilities to take actions to achieve certain Commission and New 
15 
York State policy objectives by better aligning utility shareholder financial 
16 
interest with consumer interest.” The Commission identified the 
17 
establishment of additional earnings opportunities for utilities, including 
18 
EAMs, as an important component of a modern regulatory model. As 
19 
noted on page 2 of the REV Track 2 Order, the EAMs are “intended to 
20 
create a regulatory environment where utilities can create shareholder 
21 
value…by integrating third-party solutions and capital that improve the 
22 


--- Page 8 ---

MECHANISMS PANEL 
7 
 
efficiency, resiliency, and flexibility of the physical networks, reduce 
1 
consumer total costs and achieve the State’s policy objectives.” In the 
2 
REV Track 2 Order, the Commission identified multiple customer benefits 
3 
that can be achieved from utility actions taken to implement the policy 
4 
objectives of the REV proceeding including: 1) lower customer costs 
5 
through improved efficiency in capital investments; 2) better utilization of 
6 
existing assets; 3) lower energy usage through energy efficiency (“EE”); 
7 
and 4) reduced carbon emissions. Within the REV Track 2 Order, the 
8 
Commission highlighted that changes to the current state must occur in 
9 
order for the REV policy objectives to be achieved. These changes, as 
10 
noted on pages 2 and 3 of the REV Track 2 Order, include: 1) evolution of 
11 
the unidirectional grid into a more diversified and resilient model engaging 
12 
customers and third parties, 2) improvement of overall system efficiency, 
13 
and 3) improvement in consumer value and choice. Central Hudson’s 
14 
proposed EAMs align with the actions and changes adopted by the 
15 
Commission in the REV Track 2 Order. 
16
