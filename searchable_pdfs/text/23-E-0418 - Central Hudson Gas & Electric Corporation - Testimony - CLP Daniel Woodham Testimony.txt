DIRECT TESTIMONY OF DANIEL WOODHAM 
1 
I. Introduction and Qualifications 
2 
Q-1.  State your name, background, and affiliation. 
3 
A-1.  My name is Daniel Woodham and I am a caseworker with immigrants in Ulster 
4 
County NY with the non-profit Ulster Immigrant Defense Network (UIDN). Ulster Immigrant 
5 
Defense Network was founded in 2017 to counter the heightened national animosity against 
6 
immigrants in our corner of the world. Primarily a direct-service organization, as of Fall 2023 we 
7 
have served over 4,000 area immigrants, helping to fill their basic needs for food, rent, and utility 
8 
services when they are enduring hardships. We help them attain lawyers for asylum cases, and 
9 
assist them in navigating a healthcare system insufficiently responsive to non-English speakers. 
10 
In addition, when their needs are not being considered in the public sector, UIDN advocates for 
11 
the rights of our immigrant neighbors. 
12 
II. Purpose of Testimony and Recommendation 
13 
Q-2.  What is the purpose of your testimony in this case? 
14 
A-2.  The purpose of my written testimony is to oppose the rate hike which Central 
15 
Hudson is proposing, both as an individual as well as voicing a general opposition to the rate 
16 
hikes on behalf of the many immigrant families I work with as a caseworker at UIDN.

caseworker who helps immigrant families to understand bills, and to often help with the payment 
13

recent months I have encountered Central Hudson bills that: 
15 
 
1. Are not written in Spanish and therefore not understandable for Hispanic people, 
16 
2. Include two bills at a time instead of one, 
17 
3. Have bills totaling $400 or higher for one month for a one-bedroom apartment or even 
18 
a studio, 
19 
4. Are billed at a very high amount after a family hadn’t received a bill for severa 
20 
months,  
21 
5. Are not easily understandable even for educated English speakers,  
22 
6. Regularly state that additional charges will be levied if the bill is not paid by a certain

Q-5.  Has the inconsistent billing from Central Hudson impacted the lives of your 
7 
clients? If so, how? 
8 
A-5.  Many families will pay a Central Hudson bill, even if the charges are way above 
9 
what they normally have been or should be. Some families don’t pay bills because they cannot 
10 
afford the entire amount and don’t understand that they can pay less than the total. Most 
11 
immigrant families that have contacted me about bills are fearful of having their power shut off if 
12 
they don’t pay a bill and so pay it even if it means going without food! On two occasions one 
13 
family called for emergency food from UIDN because they had no money having just paid off a 
14 
Central Hudson bill of over $700 for one month. This happened one other time with another 
15 
family as well.

Q-10.  How has Central Hudson’s shortcomings on providing language access 
11 
affected your clients, and their families based on your experience and observation? 
12 
A-10.  Families that I have encountered remain confused about their bills. As already 
13 
stated, people do not access Central Hudson’s website and rely solely on the bill that comes in 
14 
the mail. These seemingly are always in English. Ability to read in any language is a challenge 
15 
for many. In addition, the indigenous Mayan language of Kekchi (from Guatemala) is the third 
16 
most common language in Kingston. There is no translation available in Kekchi for Central 
17 
Hudson bills as far as I am aware of. Additionally, it is important to point out is that reading and 
18 
writing are skills that many Kekchi people find challenging. 
19

Q-11.  Do most of your clients have basic literacy in their native language? What 
21 
percentage would you estimate are of low literacy or basically illiterate? How has literacy 
22 
been a barrier for Central Hudson customers who are clients of yours? 
23

A-11.  Though UIDN does not conduct literacy tests as part of our services, as an agency 
1 
we do have the experience of many if not most of our clients to be low-literacy or functionally 
2 
illiterate in any language, including their native languages. Low literacy could be as high as 
3 
40%-60%. Functionally illiterate could be 20-30% of the clients we serve. 
4 
Low literacy levels mean people cannot understand their bills. They cannot read about the 
5 
different charges between gas and electric, between supply and delivery charges. They cannot 
6 
understand on their printed bills what options they have if they cannot pay the amount due. Many 
7 
also do not await a message in Spanish on the CH recording if they only hear English. They 
8 
simply hang up. This is a technological illiteracy issue that must also be addressed. 
9 
 
10 
Q-12.  Based on your knowledge, experience, or observation, what improvements 
11 
could Central Hudson be making to provide more effective language access and services for 
12 
your affected clients and families?  
13 
A-12.  At Ulster Immigrant Defense Network, we would suggest the following 
14 
improvements: 
15 
 
1. Make no rate hikes at present, 
16 
2. Transition to being a public utility so people have more faith in their energy being at 
17 
fair market prices, 
18 
3. Dispense with estimated billing practices, making all bills reflect actual readings. This 
19 
will make bills less confusing month-to-month, 
20 
4. Compensate Central Hudson customers with refunds for extraordinary bills charged to 
21 
them in recent years, 
22

9 
5. Hire native Spanish and Kekchi speakers to staff the call center and review/update 
1 
digital resources such as the website and chatbots, 
2 
 
6. Provide bills in the native language of the client, 
3 
7. Make bills less wordy, more transparent, and less confusing for people with lower 
4 
literacy.

Q-14.  In your opinion, do you believe the proposed rate increases are appropriate 
14 
at this time? If not, please explain. 
15 
A-14.  Rate increases are not appropriate at this time. Rate hikes would erode the public 
16 
trust further. At a time when inflation has already crippled the ability of many families to remain

fed and housed, a Central Hudson rate hike would most likely result in an increase in homeless 
18 
and hungry people, both immigrants and native-born people.
