INTRODUCTION 
 
 
On October 29, 2021, the Signatory Parties1 filed a Joint Proposal recommending 
a comprehensive resolution of all issues raised in the above-captioned proceedings.  On 
November 19, 2021, Staff, Orange and Rockland, NYPA, NY-GEO and New Yorkers for Cool 
Refrigerant Management submitted statements recommending that the Commission adopt the 
terms of the Joint Proposal.  Public Utility Law Project of New York, Inc. (“PULP”), Alliance 
for a Green Economy (“AGREE”) and Intervenor Bruce Levine filed statements in opposition to 
the Joint Proposal.  This Reply addresses the issues raised in the Statements in Opposition filed 
by PULP, AGREE and Mr. Levine, including: 1) PULP’s opposition to provisions addressing 
return on equity (“ROE”), total residential bill impacts, amortization of electric and gas low 
income deferrals and excess unprotected deferred income taxes (“EDFIT”), residential customer 
charges for electric and gas, and arrears management;2 2) AGREE’s opposition to certain 
provisions regarding the Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act (“CLCPA”), total 
bill impacts and ROE; and 3) Mr. Levine’s opposition related to energy efficiency programs and 
electric vehicle (“EV”) charging stations.

DISCUSSION 
 
 
As explained in Staff’s Statement in Support, the Joint Proposal meets the 
requirements of the Commission’s Settlement Guidelines.  The Joint Proposal is consistent with 
the Commission’s goals and policies, compares favorably with the likely result of a litigated 
case, fairly balances the interests of ratepayers and investors, and provides the Commission with 
a rational basis for its decision.  The matters raised by PULP, AGREE and Mr. Levine, which are 
discussed in detail below, should present no barrier to the Commission adopting the terms of the 
Joint Proposal. 
1.  Cost of Capital 
 
 
PULP takes issue with the Joint Proposal’s ROE of 9.20%, arguing that it is 
unsupported by the record, unresponsive to the financial hardship experienced by ratepayers as a 
result of the COVID-19 pandemic and in contrast to the ROEs set by the Commission in recent 
rate case decisions.3  PULP’s arguments demonstrate a fundamental misunderstanding of both 
the cost of capital calculation and the Commission’s long-standing methodology for determining 
cost of equity.  For the reasons that follow, PULP’s arguments should be rejected.

record for agreeing upon such a high ROE” is wholly without merit.  Also lacking merit is

fact that the Joint Proposal is a negotiated agreement among a number of parties but seek to hold 
the Signatory Parties in these cases to a higher standard than those who have filed joint proposals 
in other rate proceedings.  The ROE is just one of many provisions contained in the Joint 
Proposal, all of which were the outcome of months of negotiations, including give and take by all 
Signatory Parties.  For this reason, neither this Joint Proposal nor the joint proposals filed in 
other recent rate proceedings provide the type of calculations PULP demands here, including the

5.  Electric and Gas Residential Customer Charges 
 
 
PULP objects to the increase in the fixed residential customer charge for both 
electric and gas, arguing that such increases “penalize[] conservation-minded low and moderate 
usage customers by denying them any ability to reduce their monthly energy costs for the part of 
their bills represented by such charges.”27  PULP’s argument, however, ignores two long-
standing principles of rate design: first, rates should reflect cost causation and second, fixed 
customer charges should cover costs that do not vary with usage.  Fixed customer charges 
recover the Company’s costs to serve its customers regardless of how much electricity or gas a 
customer consumes.  Meter reading, billing and administration costs, for example, do not vary 
based on a customer’s usage.  Under PULP’s proposal, the fixed charges incurred by Orange and 
Rockland to serve its low and moderate usage customers would be unfairly apportioned among 
higher use customers through the volumetric charge.  Notwithstanding the slight increase in the 
fixed customer charges, the incentive still remains for these low and moderate usage customers 
to reduce monthly energy costs through the volumetric portion of their utility bill.  For these 
reasons, PULP’s recommendation to maintain the existing electric and gas residential customer 
charges should be rejected.

the levels mandated by the Commission in the January 2020 NE:NY Order.  For these reasons, 
AGREE’s erroneous claims should be rejected. 
8.  Energy Efficiency and EV Charging 
According to Mr. Levine, the Joint Proposal demonstrates that the Company is 
relying on passive incentives and existing efficiency programs to address climate change in its 
territory.  This statement is patently incorrect.  Not only does the Company actively engage with 
its customers to promote its various clean energy programs, the Commission’s recent actions in 
the New Efficiency New York Proceeding42 and the EV Make Ready Proceeding43 obligate both 
Staff and the Company to support, within the terms of the Joint Proposal, the ramp-up of clean 
energy programs in order to meet the increased targets established by the Commission.   
Additionally, Mr. Levine’s proposal to establish a ratepayer-funded program in 
which local governments would collaborate with the Company to install EV charging stations at 
every municipally-owned parking lot in the Company’s service territory is duplicative of existing 
EV charging infrastructure programs and budgets already authorized by the Commission, as well 
as funding sources and incentives available through other entities.  Municipal governments are 
eligible to participate in the EV Make-Ready Program, as well as other existing programs and 
incentives offered by the New York State Energy Research and Development Authority and the 
New York State Department of Environmental Conservation.  For these reasons, Mr. Levine’s 
proposal should be rejected.

Correction to Statement in Support for the COVID-19 Related Adjustment 
 
 
Lastly, Staff provides one clarification to correct an inaccuracy in our Statement 
in Support.  Therein, Staff incorrectly stated that the $2.9 million in COVID-19 related 
adjustments was in part comprised of “a reduction in annual wage increases for non-senior 
management employees from 3% to 2% each year and senior management employees from 3% 
to 2% for RY2 and RY3.”44  The labor expense reflects the elimination of wage increases for 
Orange and Rockland’s senior management effective during the linking period and RY1, 
resulting in approximately 2.9% overall management wage increases in each of those periods.  
The $2.9 million COVID-19 related adjustment includes additional savings for the Annual Team 
Incentive Plan adjustment as explained in Staff’s Statement in Support,45 as well as the dollar 
equivalent of further modifications to the overall management wage increases.  These 
modifications reduced the increases in the linking period and RY1 from approximately 2.9% to 
2%.  No savings included in the $2.9 million were related to reductions in management wage 
increases in RY2 and RY3.  The Electric and Gas Rate Plans reflect 3% in each of those years.  
While the description of the savings as detailed within Staff’s Statement in Support was 
incorrect, the dollar value is accurate and remains a substantial benefit to customers and, 
therefore, should be adopted.
