2

3

customer counts may drop in the future as a result of political decisions to reduce the use

15 
being replaced today onto a diminishing number of future customers would be 
16 
particularly unfair.  Staff’s recommendation to extend responsibility for the cost of LPP

A. 
No.  Staff’s proposal looks further back in time to reflect a lower cost of removal.  
1 
However, there is no reason to believe that the cost of removal is likely to decrease in the 
2 
future.  To the contrary, this cost is more likely to increase because the cost is driven 
3 
primarily by labor costs that will continue to increase over time.  This is driven by both 
4 
the impact of general inflation and what is likely to be a tightened market for the skilled 
5 
labor needed to replace utility facilities.  Staff’s use of data from 2015 to reduce net 
6 
salvage costs makes it more likely that the Company will underrecover its net salvage 
7 
costs.   
8 
 
 
9 
 
The analysis presented in my direct testimony focused on looking at a span of years that 
10 
would capture annual fluctuations in reported cost of removal data as well as the most 
11 
recent data to capture as fully as possible the costs of the ongoing replacement programs.  
12 
The 2015 cost of removal data that Staff seeks to include was omitted because costs in 
13 
the first year of a 20-year replacement program are less likely to be representative of the 
14 
ongoing costs of a mature program. 
15 
 
 
16 
 
As with all these types of projects, time is required to ramp up the annual activity of a 20-
17 
year program.  My review started with the KEDNY data as shown on Exhibit __ (PMN-
18 
4), pages 3 and 4 that presents annual costs relating to retirements and cost to retire.  In 
19 
reviewing this data, I observed that the annual fluctuations were considerable, which is 
20 
not unusual because, in many instances, there is a lag in the reporting of data for different 
21 
years.  As a result of this data lag, I suggested that a span of several years would be 
22 
appropriate.  I chose three years as the most representative over four years as
