Division of Consumer Protection submits this Initial Brief concerning the Central Hudson Gas

proceedings, with a particular focus on residential and small commercial customers. UIU is 
tasked with “the power and duty to . . . represent the interests of consumers of the state before 
federal, state and local administrative and regulatory agencies engaged in the regulation of 
energy services.”1

PSC Complaint Rate; Residential Customer Satisfaction Survey; Percent of Calls Answered by a

Of these four (4) metrics, Collar made recommendations on the PSC Complaint Rate, 
Residential Customer Satisfaction Survey, and the Percent of Calls Answered by a Representative 
within 30 Seconds. Additionally, for the reasons explained below, UIU urges the Commission to

Customer service performance metrics provide utilities with 
incentives to provide acceptable levels of customer service. Quality 
service is important to customers and serves the public interest. Due 
to the monopolistic nature of utility service, customers cannot select 
another delivery service provider if they are not satisfied with the 
quality of service they receive from Central Hudson. Meaningful 
measures associated with appropriate financial risk ensure that 
utility companies provide adequate service to their customers. 
Structured correctly, customer service performance measures 
provide utilities with incentives that should result in the high level 
of service to which customers are entitled (Ev. Hr. Tr. 491-492). 
 
Many major investor-owned utilities in New York State are subject to a customer service 
performance incentive mechanism that imposes an earnings consequence on the utility if it fails to 
meet specified customer service quality performance standards. The terms of these mechanisms 
are established in individual utility rate cases. The rate plans assign a customer service quality 
performance target for each measure; if the company fails to meet the performance target(s), then 
it will incur a Negative Revenue Adjustment (“NRA”). The corresponding NRA provides financial 
relief to customers if a utility fails to provide the high level of service to which customers are 
entitled. If a utility fails to meet its target, the mechanism shows that customers have overpaid for 
the quality of service that was received. In this way, the NRA represents a refund to the customer 
for that overpayment.

Specifically, the Company’s Customer Experience Panel in their Direct Testimony

According to Collar, the proposal would require splitting the maximum NRAs currently at risk for

the two proposed metrics, thus leaving Central Hudson at risk of only 7.5 BPs per survey metric.  
(Ev. Hr. Tr. 496).     
UIU has a few concerns regarding the Company’s proposal to split the Residential 
Customer Satisfaction Survey. The proposal would result in shifting some of the Company’s NRA-
exposure due to the splitting of the transaction types, and thus reducing the Company’s exposure 
for each individual transaction type.  Making it easier for the Company to avoid an NRA carries a 
significant risk of diluting the survey measure and negatively impacting the level of customer 
service being provided due to a lack of financial incentive to maintain the target levels.  For 
example, if the survey had been split at the time of the CIS launch, it is likely that the “High 
Priority” target could have been met, and if the target for General Accounts & Billing was not met 
due to the problems with the launch, the NRA incurred would have been half of what it should 
have been.

customers who will experience delivery bill percent changes that are higher than average 
customers as a result of changes in the tail block structure.
