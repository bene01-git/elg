Q.    
Please explain Staff’s vacancy rate adjustment.  
7 
A. 
The Staff AP asserted that the Company’s projection of annual salaries 
8 
does not consider that some positions could be vacant during the year or 
9 
for a portion of the year. As such, Staff imputed a reduction to total labor 
10 
expense of 3.5%, based on a five-year average of the Company’s attrition 
11 
rate as provided by the Company in response to DPS-380, which is 
12 
included in Staff’s Exhibit __ (SAP-1).   
13 
Q.    
Please explain the Company’s interpretation of what Staff’s adjustment is 
14 
intended to do. 
15 
A. 
In testimony, the Staff AP noted that any period of vacancy would impact 
16 
the actual labor expense incurred by the Company. Therefore, it appears 
17 
that Staff’s adjustment is intended to protect customers from paying for 
18 
Company labor expense that may not ultimately be incurred.  
19 
Q.    
Does the Panel agree with Staff’s calculation and use of a vacancy rate for 
20 
the Company? 
21 
A. 
No. The Company disagrees with Staff’s imputation for multiple reasons. 
22 
First, the attrition rate of 3.5% used as a proxy for vacancy represents the 
23 


--- Page 11 ---
Case 23-E-0418; Case 23-G-0419 
 
       REBUTTAL TESTIMONY OF THE REVENUE REQUIREMENTS PANEL 
9 
 
number of people who left the Company for reasons other than retirement 
1 
as a percentage of the total number of employees at the end of the year.  
2 
Using this as a proxy vacancy rate falsely assumes that any vacated 
3 
position was empty for an entire twelve-month period. This theory may be 
4 
best explained with a simplistic mathematical example. If the Company 
5 
employed 1,000 employees on January 1st of a given year, lost 35 
6 
employees during the year, and rehired 35 new employees that same 
7 
year, the headcount at year-end would still be 1,000 and the attrition rate 
8 
would calculate out to be 3.5% (35 full-time equivalents (“FTEs”) 
9 
terminated divided by 1,000 FTEs at year-end). Applying Staff’s 
10 
methodology to this simple example would result in the Company being 
11 
allowed to recover expense for only 965 employees. Applying this 
12 
percentage to labor expense is overbroad, as it does not present what the 
13 
vacancy was at any point during the year or for how long that vacancy 
14 
lasted.  
15 
Q.    
What is the correct way a vacancy rate should be determined? 
16 
A. 
If the vacancy imputation is meant to protect customers from paying for 
17 
Company labor expense that may not be incurred, any determination of a 
18 
vacancy rate premised on employee count should be a comparison of 
19 
actual headcount to that provided for in rates, since that represents the 
20 
headcount for which funds are being provided by customers. In other 
21 
words, the focus of any adjustment to the projected level of labor expense 
22
