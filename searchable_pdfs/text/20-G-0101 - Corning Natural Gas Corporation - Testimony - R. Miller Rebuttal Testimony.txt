A. 
Not entirely. I agree that identification and pursuit of RNG supplies is within the purview 
of the gas supply group and is unlikely to result in any significant incremental expense. On 
the other hand, there are at least two types of costs that are incremental and that should be 
recovered: (a) the capital expenditure associated building a new RNG interconnect or 
upgrading existing facilities to allow for an RNG interconnect, and (b) the commodity cost 
associated with RNG supply. The capital cost component should be recovered via a 
surcharge mechanism for each applicable service class. The RNG commodity cost, 
whether local or upstream, should be recovered via the existing Gas Adjustment Clause 
mechanism in the same manner as other commodity costs. 
Q. 
At page 14 of his testimony, Mr. Riebel recommends that "the Company incorporate a plan 
to include NPAs as an alternative to traditional gas infrastructure investment since it may 
be a few years until they file for rates again." Do you concur with that recommendation? 
A. 
Generally, yes. The implementation approach described by Mr. Riebel appears reasonable. 
The Company believes, however, that it is important to clarify that acceptable NPAs may 
include both Demand-Side Solutions ("DSS") and Supply-Side Solutions ("SSS"). DSM 
can be further categorized as targeted demand response, targeted energy efficiency, heat 
pumps, thermal storage, other electrification and fuel switching, and behavior change and 
market transformation. SSS typically consist of alternate supply delivery methods such as 
on-system RNG supply, on-system liquefied natural gas ("LNG") peaking storage, 
compressed and LNG trucking (virtual pipelines) and LNG liquefaction port terminals.

Q. 
Beginning at page 14 of his testimony, Mr. Riebel refers to screening criteria and cost 
recovery for NPA projects. Do you have any comments on that discussion? 
A. 
Yes. The Company generally agrees with the suggestions Mr. Riebel makes. As part of 
the evaluation process the Company would expect to compare the cost of the NPAs to the 
cost of traditional solutions before proceeding with any new projects. The Company 
should be allowed to recover any capital costs associates with the NPA via a surcharge 
mechanism from the applicable service classes. Any associated NPA commodity costs 
should be recovered via the existing GAC mechanism. 
Q. 
Does that conclude your rebuttal testimony at this time? 
A. 
Yes, it does.
