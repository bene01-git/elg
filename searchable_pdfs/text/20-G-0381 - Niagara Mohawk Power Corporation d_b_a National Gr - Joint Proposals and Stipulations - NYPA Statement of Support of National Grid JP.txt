INTRODUCTION AND DESCRIPTION OF NYPA’S INTEREST 
 
The New York Power Authority (“NYPA”) hereby files this statement in support of the 
Joint Proposal (“JP”) filed with the New York Public Service Commission (“Commission”) on 
September 27, 2021. NYPA is a signatory to the JP with respect to the electric service issues set 
forth therein by Niagara Mohawk Power Corporation d/b/a National Grid (the “Company”) in 
Case 20-E-0380.1 
 
NYPA is a corporate municipal instrumentality and a political subdivision of New York 
State (“the State”), authorized under the New York Public Authorities Law (“NY PAL”) to, inter 
alia, finance, develop and implement energy-related projects, programs, and services for public 
entities in New York State.2  Consistent with the NY PAL, NYPA assists municipalities 
throughout New York in reducing their energy consumption from their street light facilities.  
Specifically, through the Smart Street Lighting NY Program created by the Governor in 2018, 
NYPA assists cities and towns in purchasing their street light facilities and retrofitting them with 
energy efficient Light Emitting Diode (“LED”) technology, dimming nodes, and other smart 
solutions to maximize energy efficiency and bill savings, including the adoption of “Smart City” 
devices that can be added to street lights.  Consistent with its statute, as well as New York State’s 
energy efficiency and greenhouse gas reduction goals set forth in the 2019 Climate Leadership 
and Community Protection Act (“CLCPA”),3 NYPA’s clean energy initiatives span the State, 
and include the installation, commissioning and financing of projects that promote energy

PRELIMINARY STATEMENT  
 
NYPA supports the electric service provisions of the JP as a fair and reasonable result 
consistent with the public interest, as it relates to the street lighting provisions.   
NYPA has relied upon the expertise of the Department of Public Service Staff (“DPS 
Staff”), the Company, and other interested parties with respect to the JP’s resolutions addressing, 
inter alia, COVID-19 pandemic relief provisions, including those addressing low income 
ratepayers, revenue requirements and bill impacts, rate of return on equity, revenue allocation 
and rate design, electric reliability and resiliency, and the myriad other provisions affecting the 
electric business of the Company and its customers.  
With respect to the JP’s resolutions on street lighting issues, the provisions adopting a 
street light dimming tariff, establishing a process to help facilitate the attachment of smart street 
lighting technologies to customer-owned street lights, and improvements to the street light sale 
process, and satisfy the standards of review set forth in the Commission’s settlement guidelines.5  
The JP reflects a compromise of competing interests that, in the context of setting rates, terms 
and conditions for three years (rather than for a single year that would emerge from a litigated 
case), produces an outcome that is superior to litigation.

The outcomes achieved in the JP related to street lighting issues – i.e. a street light

digital connectivity.  Such functions are viewed by municipalities as desirable benefits of 
converting their street lights to LEDs.  Thus, knowing how these smart city devices can be 
installed can encourage LED adoption.  The reverse is also true: if smart city devices do not have 
a clear path to being installed, this can inhibit municipalities from moving forward with their 
LED conversion plans.12  While it is possible to retrofit smart city technologies after LEDs have

3. Improvements to the Street Light Asset Sale Process 
NYPA’s testimony advocated for improvements to the street light asset sale process to 
improve transparency and efficiency of the process.  NYPA’s testimony advocated for greater 
detail on the calculation of the sale price, standardization of contracts, streamlining exchanges 
during the sale process, and limiting unnecessary maintenance that can increase costs.  Under the 
JP, the Parties reached a compromise on many of these points such as including greater 
transparency for large changes in purchase price, limiting maintenance to essential work prior to 
transfer of the street lighting assets, standardizing the purchase and sale agreement, and that the 
utility will provide an email to confirm key milestones.19 Accordingly, the resolution of the street 
light sale process issues is consistent with the Commission’s settlement guidelines and should be 
adopted.

B.  Joint Proposal Did Not Address Additional Electric Vehicle Programs 
 
 
Though not included in the JP, NYPA reiterates its support for the Company’s proposed 
electric vehicle (EV) program, which would have instituted reforms to reduce costs and barriers 
for residential, commercial and fleet customers to install electric vehicle chargers.  These 
programs were designed to address different market barriers than the programs instituted in the 
statewide generic EV proceedings. NYPA was supportive of the Company’s proposals as they 
were likely to encourage wider adoption of EVs which would help the State attain its clean 
energy goals. While disappointed that a reform package was not included in the JP, NYPA 
nonetheless recognizes that the exclusion of the EV programs was within the range of reasonable 
outcomes in a litigated proceeding, on balance NYPA supports the JP based on the inclusion of 
the provisions establishing the street light dimming, improving the sales process and the smart 
city technologies technical conference and report, noted earlier on pages 3-6, infra.  NYPA 
hopes that the Commission will consider the Company’s proposed EV program as part of the 
statewide strategy to boost EV adoption.   
CONCLUSION 
In sum, the JP balances utility and ratepayer interests and is consistent with sound 
environmental, social and economic policy, in accordance with the Commission’s settlement 
guidelines.  For these reasons, and the other reasons set forth in detail in this statement, the 
Commission should find that the JP is in the public interest and adopt it in full and without 
modification.
