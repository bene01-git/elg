OVERVIEW
Sane Energy Project opposes the Joint Proposal (JP) filed by the Brooklyn Union Gas Company
d/b/a National Grid NY (“KEDNY”), KeySpan Gas East Corporation d/b/a National Grid
(“KEDLI”) (KEDNY and KEDLI are collectively referred to as the “Companies”) and New York
State Department of Public Service Staff (“Staff”) on May 14, 2021.
Like National Grid’s original proposal filed over two years ago, the JP claims to be addressing
climate change and greenhouse gas emission reductions mandated by New York State climate
law. But just like the original proposal this JP is more greenwashing of dirty fracked gas and an
abject failure to serve the interests of the public. In 2021 it's dangerous climate denialism and it
extends a racist history of fossil fuel pollution at a time when marginalized communities are
disproportionately struggling with the economic and health impacts of the COVID pandemic.
The State of New York and National Grid have wasted two crucial years that should have been
spent transitioning to renewable energy hiding from the public in closed-door negotiations and
allowing expensive, contested fracked gas projects to be built.
We oppose this JP because instead of listening to tens of thousands of public comments and
input from elected officials, Staff and the Companies have failed to commit to reduce gas use in
a meaningful way and begin the morally necessary and legally mandated transition off fossil fuel
infrastructure. And have instead chosen to hide massive investments that expand capacity and
extend the life of fracked gas infrastructure and set customers up for higher bills through the
Demand Capacity Surcharge Mechanism1 and future rate years beyond this proceeding.
As Sane Energy Project laid out in our Initial Brief, in testimony throughout this case and in
other proceedings before the Public Service Commission (“Commission”), investments in new
fracked gas infrastructure are against public interest because:
●
They go against greenhouse gas emission reduction goals and Environmental Justice
protections of New York State policy mandated in the Climate Leadership and
Community Protection Act (CLCPA).
●
There’s no need for these projects.
●
There’s been massive public opposition to them.
●
Fracked gas infrastructure poses serious threats to the health and safety of
communities.

HISTORY
On April 30, 2019 the Companies filed new tariff filing and initial testimony in support of their
request to increase gas delivery rates.
On May 15, 2019 in response to the New York State Department of Environmental
Conservation’s (DEC) second denial of Transcontinental Gas Pipe Line LLC’s (Transco)
application for water quality certification permits for the Williams Northeast Supply Enhancement
(NESE) Pipeline, the Companies instituted a harmful and politically motivated moratorium,
denying customer connections.
On July 18, 2019 the CLCPA was signed into law by Governor Andrew Cuomo, setting legal
mandates to reduce emissions to 40% below 1990 levels by 2030 and then to 85% below 1990
levels by 2050.
On August 30, 2019 Sane Energy Project filed Direct Testimony outlining how National Grid’s
proposal failed to align with the CLCPA as well as New York City climate law.
On September 24, 2019 confidential settlement negotiation between parties commenced.
On October 10, 2019 Commissioner John Rhodes issued a one commissioner order instituting
proceeding 19-G-0678 investigating National Grid’s denials of service.
On October 10, 15, 16, 23, and 24, 2019 confidential settlement meetings occurred between
parties.
On November 12, 2019 Governor Andrew Cuomo issued a letter to the Companies notifying
them of his intent to revoke its certificate to operate its downstate territory.
On November 25, 2019 Governor Cuomo and the Companies announced in a press release a
settlement agreement to immediately lift the moratorium which included a $36 million penalty to
compensate customers impacted by the moratorium and in support of clean energy projects.
The next day on November 26, 2019 Commissioner John Rhodes adopted the settlement
through a one commissioner order which said “a long-term option or options should be identified
and agreed to by the State of New York by June 2020”.

stations or LNG facilities, but as a study by Linnaeus University found “radon and radon decay

Companies have presented the Greenpoint LNG projects to the community, a community that
has suffered from decades of industrial pollution. The Companies lied to community members at

The ALJs and Commission should also take into account the extreme vulnerability of the facility
to climate change and ongoing superfund remediation when considering if tens of millions of
dollars of investment extending the life of the facility are prudent.
CNG EXPANSION ON LONG ISLAND

CNG 3rd Site (Barrett CNG Off Loading Facility) and Portable CNG 4th Site (Inwood CNG Off

oppose them because in addition to the health and climate impacts we discussed above for
fracked gas projects, the CNG trucking presents an additional risk to the safety of the public.

As we saw with a recent explosion of a propane truck38 on a major New York highway, this is the
worst possible time for explosive trucks to be on the road driving through our communities.
Additionally The Synapse report’s findings show there is no need for these projects.
