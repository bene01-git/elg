Q. Please state your name and business address.
A. Harvey Epstein, 107 & 109 Avenue B, New York, NY 10009
Q:What is your occupation?
A: New York State Assembly Member, District 74
Q. Please summarize your background.
A:I represent the East Side of Manhattan in the State Assembly, including the neighborhoods of
the Lower East Side, East Village, Stuyvesant Town/Peter Cooper Village, Murray Hill, Kips Bay,
Tudor City and the United Nations in the New York State Assembly. I sit on the Assembly
committees on Agriculture, Environmental Conservation, Governmental Operations, Higher
Education, Housing, Small Business, and People with Disabilities. I chair the subcommittee on
Retention of Homeownership and Stabilization of Affordable Housing.
Q: On whose behalf are you testifying in this proceeding?
A: Sane Energy Project, a party to the rate case.
Q: What is your purpose in submitting this testimony?
A: To discuss concerns with the Company’s overall filing, impacts of the proposed steep rate
increases on customers in the district, and my opposition to fracked gas projects and especially
to the proposed $67 million investment in the East River Plant on East 14th Street.
Q: Please describe the demographics of AD 74.
A: The population of AD 74 is diverse. Just over half the residents are white, 19% are Hispanic,
16% are Asian, 8% are Black, and 3% identify with two or more races. The median household
income is under $90,000 a year; 18% of residents are below the poverty line, including a quarter
of children and 15% of seniors. Most are renters (81%), and almost all live in multi-family
buildings. 24% of the residents are immigrants.
Q: What proposed projects in the Company’s filing that would impact AD 74?
A: Virtually all residents and businesses in AD 74 will be affected, and many devastated, by the
proposed rate increase of 18.2% for gas and 11.2% for electricity. Because ConEd is a
regulated monopoly, it has no competition: residents are a captive market who cannot purchase
the energy that is essential for heat, light, cooking, electronics, and hot water from another
purveyor. Rates are already high, and this winter customers were shocked by spikes in the cost
of energy that forced many to choose between food, medication, and heat. Now, ConEd is

proposing institutionalizing additional steep increases in the delivery rates for the next three
years.
ConEd has requested the increase in gas rates to cover investments that would extend the life
of gas infrastructure in its territory. These projects include investing $500 million to replace
leak-prone pipes rather than to shift to electricity, and $75 million to hook new customers up to
gas. Expanding the gas system rather than expediting the transition to clean, renewable energy
will increase greenhouse gas emissions, increasing the risk of climate-related storm surges and
other catastrophes in Manhattan as well as harming the health of residents from indoor and
outdoor pollution from methane, particulate matter, and other toxins in fracked gas.
It is essential for the climate and our health that we immediately stop expanding the use of gas
and instead phase out these obsolete, harmful systems. Residents of District 74 know all too
well the dangers of fracked gas. In October, 2012, during Superstorm Sandy, the storm surge
caused a massive transformer explosion in the East River Generating Station on East 14th
Street, leaving much of Manhattan without electricity for days. In 2015, a gas explosion in the
East Village killed two people, injured a dozen others, and destroyed a large building. Multiple
recent studies, including several by RMI, have documented the risk to health from combusting
gas indoors for cooking and heating, which causes indoor air pollution at levels that would be
illegal outside, with severe consequences for respiratory health, especially of children, people
with asthma, and in communities of color (see, for example
https://rmi.org/insight/gas-stoves-pollution-health).
In particular, I oppose the proposed investment of $67 million in the East River Generating
Station in AD 74. Rather than extending the life of this fossil fuel plant, which is a blight on the
neighborhood that threatens our health, safety, and climate, ConEd should replace the energy
the plant produces with electricity from clean, renewable sources. It is long past time to retire the
East River Generating Plant.
Q: Is the East River project located in, or would it impact, state-designated
Disadvantaged Communities or Potential Environmental Justice Areas?
A: Yes. The facility is located on the border of Disadvantaged Communities along East 14th
street and there are Disadvantaged Communities a few blocks north.

Q:Do you support those projects? Why or why not
A: No; these gas facilities and infrastructure are not needed. They contribute to climate change
and threaten the health and safety of the community.
Q: Please describe the sources of fossil fuel pollution in AD 74.
A: Buildings that burn fossil fuels for heat and use electricity generated by gas are the most
significant source of air pollution in the district. Motor vehicles that use gasoline and Diesel fuel
are another major source of air pollution in AD 74.
Q: Did you vote for the CLCPA?
A: Yes; I was also a co-sponsor.
Q: Does the Company’s rate filing comply with the CLCPA?
A: No. In order to achieve the goals of the CLCPA–reducing greenhouse gas emissions by 40%
by 2030 and 85% by 2050, we must first of all stop increasing the use of fossil fuels. The
proposed investments in gas facilities and infrastructure will expand and prolong the lives of
fossil fuel infrastructure and result in increasing, rather than decreasing, GHG emissions now
and in the future. It is critically important that the Public Service Commission ensure that energy
