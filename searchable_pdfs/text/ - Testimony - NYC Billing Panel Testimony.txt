1 
Q. 
IN WHAT CAPACITY DOES THE CITY RECEIVE BILLS FROM CON 
2 
EDISON? 
3 
A. 
DCAS administers thousands of electricity and natural gas accounts on behalf of 
4 
City agencies.  For electric service, the City is a customer of the New York Power 
5 
Authority, or NYPA, and NYPA is a customer of Con Edison.  Con Edison bills 
6 
NYPA for its electricity usage each month, and then NYPA bills the City.  For gas, 
7

8 
Q. 
DOES THE CITY HAVE ANY CONCERNS WITH RESPECT TO THE 
9 
BILLS IT RECEIVES FROM NYPA? 
10 
A. 
Yes.  Approximately one third of the City’s bills for both electric and gas over the 
11 
last year have been based on estimated meter readings.   
12

13

14

16 
not the meter was actually read.

Q. 
TO THE YOUR KNOWLEDGE, ARE THERE ANY REQUIREMENTS 
1 
REGARDING ESTIMATED BILLING TO WHICH CON EDISON IS 
2 
SUBJECT? 
3 
A. 
Yes.  We have been informed by our counsel that the Commission has regulations 
4 
that govern utility estimated billing practices. 
5 
Q. 
PLEASE EXPLAIN. 
6 
A. 
Counsel has advised us that 16 NYCRR section 13.8 provides the conditions under 
7 
which Con Edison may issue an estimated bill to non-residential customers.  We 
8 
have read that regulation to understand its provisions and requirements.  A utility 
9 
may issue an estimated bill under one of eight circumstances.  
10 
First, if it is unable to access a meter.  Second, where obtaining an actual 
11 
reading is extremely difficult for reasons beyond the utility’s control despite the 
12 
utility having access to the meter.  Third, where the utility has good cause for 
13 
believing that an actual meter reading or reading by a customer will be erroneous.  
14 
Fourth, where circumstances beyond the utility’s control prevents a meter reader 
15 
from visiting the meter.  Fifth, where an actual reading was lost or destroyed.  Sixth, 
16 
where the Commission has authorized or directed an estimated bill.  Seventh, where 
17 
an estimated reading is the approved method in utility’s tariff.  Finally, if there was 
18 
an unmetered condition during the billing period.
