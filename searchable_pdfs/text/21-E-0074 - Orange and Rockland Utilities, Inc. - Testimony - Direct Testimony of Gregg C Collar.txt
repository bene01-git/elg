II. 
CUSTOMER SERVICE PERFORMANCE INCENTIVE MECHANISM  
1 
Q. 
What customer service performance metrics are included in the Company’s 
2 
CSPIM? 
3 
A.   
Currently, Orange & Rockland’s CSPIM includes the following customer 
4 
service performance measures: (1) PSC Complaint Rate; (2) Customer 
5 
Contact Satisfaction Survey; (3) Call Answer Rate; and (4) Residential 
6 
Service Termination and Uncollectible incentive performance mechanism. 
7 
For the PSC Complaint Rate, Customer Contact Satisfaction Survey, and 
8 
Call Answer Rate metrics, the Company is assigned a customer service 
9 
quality performance target for each metric, and if the Company fails to meet 
10 
the performance target, then it will incur an NRA. I further note that while 
11 
the Company’s performance on each metric is measured monthly, the target 
12 
threshold(s) are applied to the average of each Company’s twelve-monthly 
13 
scores. The Residential Service Termination and Uncollectible incentive 
14 
performance mechanism has both a Positive Revenue Adjustment (PRA) 
15 
and an NRA.  
16 
 
17 
Q. 
Could you describe the customer service performance metrics? 
18 
A. 
The PSC Complaint Rate metric measures the number of complaints per 
19 
100,000 customers “charged” to the Company on a monthly basis, but the 
20 
metric’s target is based on the average of twelve-monthly scores. Under the 
21

Company’s current rate plans, a complaint is considered “charged” when a 
1 
customer, after first contacting the Company to express dissatisfaction with 
2 
a Company action or practice, contacts the Commission to express their 
3 
dissatisfaction with the Company’s resolution of their complaint.  
4 
 
 
The Customer Contact Satisfaction Survey metric measures the 
5 
monthly satisfaction ratings resulting from a random telephone survey of 
6 
residential customers who have recently contacted the Company.  
7 
 
 
The Call Answer Rate measures the percentage of total calls that are 
8 
answered within 30 seconds by a Company representative.  
9 
 
 
The Residential Service Termination and Uncollectible incentive 
10 
performance mechanism is described later in my testimony.

12 
Q. 
What is the function of customer service performance metrics?  
13 
A. 
Customer service performance metrics provide utilities with powerful 
14 
incentives to provide exceptional levels of customer service. Quality service 
15 
is important to customers and serves the public interest. Due to the 
16 
monopolistic nature of utility service, customers cannot select another 
17 
delivery service provider if they are not satisfied with the quality of service 
18 
they receive from Orange & Rockland. Meaningful measures associated 
19 
with appropriate financial risk are required to ensure that utility companies 
20 
provide adequate service to their customers. Structured correctly, customer 
21

service performance measures provide utilities with these powerful 
1

2 
are entitled.

Q. 
Please explain your recommendation regarding the Company’s customer 
4 
service performance measures. 
5 
A. 
I propose to continue the Company’s current customer service performance 
6 
measures with modifications to the target level for its Call Answer Rate 
7 
metric. Customers deserve to see their utility’s performance improve over 
8 
time, especially where, as here, the utility asks its customers to pay higher 
9 
rates. Customer service performance targets should therefore be set at 
10 
levels that challenge utilities to achieve enhanced customer service, rather 
11 
than staying with the status quo. A target that a Company has no trouble 
12 
meeting fails to adequately incent the Company to improve its performance.    
13 
 
14 
Q. 
How do you typically arrive at your proposed service target levels for a 
15 
Company’s customer service performance measures? 
16 
A. 
I review a Company’s annual service quality performance data for the last 
17 
five calendar years (in this case, 2016 to 2020) that was provided by the 
18 
Company. (See Company response to UIU IR 2-15). I calculate the five-
19 
year average and then apply two standard deviations. I choose two 
20 
standard deviations because the result is a more reliably accurate reflection 
21

10

1 
well-established practice in this industry. I note that DPS Staff applied this 
2 
well-accepted rationale as recently as in their Consumer Service Panel 
3 
testimony in the National Grid rate case.1
