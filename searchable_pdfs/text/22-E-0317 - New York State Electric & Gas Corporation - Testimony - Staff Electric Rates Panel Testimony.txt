Embedded Cost of Service (ECOS) Studies 
4 
Q. 
Please briefly describe the purpose of the ECOS 
5 
study. 
6 
A. 
The purpose of an ECOS study is to assign the 
7 
costs of providing utility service to the 
8 
different service classes based on the costs 
9 
incurred to provide service to customers within 
10 
those classes.  The results of an ECOS study are 
11 
used as a guide in allocating revenue 
12 
responsibility between the different classes of 
13 
customers.  
14 
Q. 
What information does an ECOS study provide? 
15 
A. 
The results of the ECOS study are expressed as 
16 
both a total system rate of return and class-
17 
specific rates of return.  The results of an 
18 
ECOS study provide a summary of the individual 
19 
class rate of return which, in turn, shows the 
20 
level at which each class contributes to the 
21 
total system rate of return.  The rate of return 
22 
for a class is an effective way to measure how 
23 
well the class revenues align with costs 
24 


--- Page 16 ---
Case 22-E-0317 et al.                Staff Electric Rates Panel 
 
 
15 
 
incurred to provide service to that particular 
1 
service class. 
2 
Q. 
Please explain how the ECOS study is developed. 
3 
A. 
The ECOS study is generally a three-step process 
4 
that includes: (1) the functionalization of 
5 
costs (i.e., production, transmission, and 
6 
distribution); (2) the classification of costs 
7 
based on characteristics such as demand, 
8 
volumetric usage, and number of customers; and 
9 
(3) the allocation of each classified function 
10 
to each service class based on the appropriate 
11 
selected characteristics. 
12

Q. 
Explain the purpose of the minimum system study?   
4 
A. 
The minimum system study compares the cost of a 
5 
hypothetical minimum distribution system to the 
6 
total cost of the entire system that provides 
7 
power.  The minimum system study is used to 
8

9

10 
that are related to both the number of customers 
11 
as well as demand, such as poles, conductors, 
12 
and line transformers.   
13 
Q. 
Explain how the Companies use the minimum system 
14 
study to calculate the customer related costs.  
15 
A. 
For primary poles, the Companies multiplied the 
16 
total number of primary poles currently in 
17 
service, regardless of size, by the current 
18 
replacement cost of the minimum sized primary 
19 
pole currently installed by the Companies to 
20 
determine the minimum system cost.  The 
21 
Companies then use the current replacement cost 
22 
of a 40-foot pole since it is minimum sized pole 
23 
the Companies use to replace existing poles on 
24 


--- Page 19 ---
Case 22-E-0317 et al.                Staff Electric Rates Panel 
 
 
18 
 
the primary system.  Next, the replacement cost 
1 
of the total system cost was calculated by 
2 
multiplying the number of poles by the current 
3 
replacement cost for the respective pole sizes.  
4 
The replacement costs of the minimum system 
5 
costs were divided by the replacement costs of 
6 
total system costs to determine the customer 
7 
cost percentage, the remaining percentage is 
8 
considered demand related.  Finally, once the 
9 
percentage related to the number of customers 
10 
was determined, it was applied to book costs of 
11 
the applicable plant account for the ECOS study 
12 
period to determine the customer related costs. 
13 
Q. 
How are costs for these accounts allocated to 
14 
service classes? 
15 
A. 
Customer-related costs depend on the number of 
16 
customers attached to the utility system, 
17 
independent of the load, and are therefore, 
18 
should be allocated to based on the number of 
19 
customers.  In contrast, demand related costs 
20 
are a function of demand and are allocated to 
21 
each service class based on peak load.
