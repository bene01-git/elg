I. 
INTRODUCTION AND BACKGROUND OF NYPA’S INTEREST 
 
The New York Power Authority (“NYPA”) hereby files this statement in support of the 
Joint Proposal (“JP”) filed with the New York Public Service Commission (“Commission”) on 
October 29, 2021. NYPA is a signatory to the JP with respect to the electric service issues set 
forth therein by Orange and Rockland Utilities, Inc. (the “Company”) in Case 21-E-0074.1 
 
NYPA is a corporate municipal instrumentality and a political subdivision of New York 
State (“the State”), authorized under the New York Public Authorities Law (“NY PAL”) to, inter 
alia, finance, develop and implement energy-related projects, programs, and services for public 
entities in New York State.2 Consistent with the NY PAL, NYPA assists municipalities 
throughout New York in reducing their energy consumption from their street light facilities.  
Specifically, through the Smart Street Lighting NY Program created by the Governor in 2018, 
NYPA assists cities and towns in purchasing their street light facilities and retrofitting them with 
energy efficient Light Emitting Diode (“LED”) technology, dimming nodes, and other smart 
solutions to maximize energy efficiency and bill savings, including the adoption of “Smart City” 
devices that can be added to street lights.  Consistent with its statute, as well as New York State’s 
energy efficiency and greenhouse gas reduction goals set forth in the 2019 Climate Leadership 
and Community Protection Act (“CLCPA”),3 NYPA’s clean energy initiatives span the State, 
and include the installation, commissioning and financing of projects that promote energy

II. 
OVERVIEW OF THE JOINT PROPOSAL 
NYPA supports the electric service provisions of the JP, as it relates to the street lighting 
provisions, as a fair and reasonable result consistent with the public interest.   
NYPA anticipates that the Company and Staff will include detailed summaries of the 
Joint Proposal in their respective submissions herein. For this reason, NYPA will eschew 
providing an additional summary of the Joint Proposal’s numerous provisions and, instead, focus 
its Statement in Support on the primary reasons why NYPA elected to execute and support the 
Joint Proposal in these proceedings, and why its adoption by the Commission herein is in the 
public interest. 
The JP’s resolutions on street lighting issues will help street lighting customers better 
maintain their street lighting assets, provide better lighting service to their residents, and 
provides a path forward for street lighting customers to benefit from a potential future tariff or 
methodology that recognizes reduced energy usage through the dimming of street lights.  These 
outcomes will benefit street lighting customers, allowing them to better maintain their facilities 
and realize cost reductions, while helping the State achieve the CLCPA goals through reduced 
energy usage. The JP achieves these environmental and economic benefits while allowing the 
Company to continue its necessary technical review of attachments to its facilities. The JP also 
enables the Company and stakeholders to determine how to move forward with a street light

NYPA’s testimony advocated that the Company allow municipalities to install networked 
lighting control (NLC) nodes on LED street lights.7 Presently, it is NYPA’s understanding that 
the Company will not allow some or all NLC nodes to be installed.8 The NLC nodes serve as the 
same function as the photocell on existing street lights and are an essential component to the 
function of many LED street lights. The NLC nodes allow municipalities to have real time 
information on outages or other maintenance issues, as well as control the level of lighting.  
Under the JP, the parties agreed that the Company will establish and provide the technical 
requirements to install the NLC nodes and provide regular updates to customers during the 
technical review process.9 
NYPA supports the JP’s inclusion of the NLC installation standards.  Allowing NLC 
nodes to be installed has the potential to lead to increased penetration of LED street lights, 
reduction in street lights outage and maintenance costs, and other cost-effective social benefits 
for the municipalities that adopt them. NYPA believes the JP terms are within the range of 
reasonable outcomes that would have arisen from a litigated proceeding before the Commission. 
Accordingly, the resolution of installation of NLC nodes issue is consistent with the 
Commission’s settlement guidelines and should be adopted.

CONCLUSION 
In sum, the JP balances utility and ratepayer interests and is consistent with sound 
environmental, social and economic policy, in accordance with the Commission’s settlement 
guidelines. For these reasons, and the other reasons set forth in detail in this statement, the 
Commission should find that the JP is in the public interest and adopt it in full and without 
modification.
