New York has seen dynamic economic growth in the Downstate region, 
expanding residential and non-residential building space and thousands of oil-
to-gas conversions over the last 10 years.  These factors have resulted in a 
substantial increase in the demand for natural gas, placing stress on our 
existing gas network and threatening National Grid's ability to meet our 
customers' needs when demand is at its peak. 
 
 
This leaves little room for error in the face of unplanned supply interruptions 
or other contingencies.  And as part of the November 2019 settlement 
agreement with New York State that lifted the moratorium on new gas 
connections imposed in May of 2019, we're taking measures to ensure we 
have enough supply for the winters of 2019/2020 and also the winter 
2020/2021, including increasing our reliance on compressed natural gas, or 
otherwise known as CNG trucking, when needed to meet peak demand. 
 
 
Beyond the next two winters, however, continued growth in demand for 
natural gas creates a challenge that must be addressed.  The Long-Term 
Natural Gas Capacity Report presents 10 options to solve for that challenge.   
 
 
It's important to note that the long-term solution options we're presenting, and 
the various combination of these options working together, have different 
implications for how long we continue to rely on CNG trucking to meet peak 
demand and whether we might have future restrictions on new gas 
connections. 
 
 
Also of note, low-carbon solutions will be an important part of reaching 
longer-term clean energy goals.  While formally not part of tonight's 
discussion, National Grid is partnering with third parties to develop renewable 
natural gas solutions, exploring hydrogen blending, and power-to-gas to help 
de-carbonize the gas system.
