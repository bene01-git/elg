STATEMENT OF  
NIAGARA MOHAWK CORPORATION D/B/A NATIONAL GRID 
IN SUPPORT OF JOINT PROPOSAL 
 
Niagara Mohawk Power Corporation d/b/a National Grid (“Niagara Mohawk” or the 
“Company”) submits this Statement in Support of the Joint Proposal, dated September 27, 2021 
(“JP”), that outlines the terms of a multi-year rate settlement the above captioned cases.   The JP 
is the product of more than a year of rate case proceedings and negotiations, and the parties 
supporting the JP represent a broad range of customer, business and industry, government, labor, 
energy service companies, and energy policy interests.  The settlement will enable Niagara 
Mohawk to deliver its core mission of operating safe and reliable electric and gas distribution 
networks, while enabling continued delivery rate stability for the Company’s customers at a time 
when affordability is a priority.  Importantly, the JP also provides for a package of investments, 
programs, and commitments to advance the State of New York’s clean energy goals and policies

set forth in the Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act of 2019 (“CLCPA”).1  These 
outcomes would not be possible in a one-year, litigated proceeding.   
In determining whether a rate settlement is in the public interest, the Commission recently 
held that it must “balance reliability, public safety, and reasonable rates interests with emission 
reductions and clean energy objectives.”2  In this regard, the JP presents a comprehensive 
resolution of these rate proceedings that ensures continued access to safe, reliable, and affordable 
energy for the Company’s nearly two million customers in upstate New York, while at the same 
time advancing clean energy investments and programs to support the State’s energy and climate 
goals.  The JP reflects the input of numerous parties committed to achieving a reasonable outcome 
and, while the JP does not address all items to the full satisfaction of all stakeholders, the resulting 
settlement is an unquestionably positive outcome for the Company’s customers and other 
stakeholders.  For these reasons, the JP should be found to be in the public interest and adopted by 
the Commission in its entirety.

Prioritizing Affordability and Mitigating Customer Bill Impacts 
As a provider of essential energy services, the Company is very aware of the health and 
financial impacts that COVID-19 had (and is having) on customers in upstate New York.  At the 
time of the rate filing in July 2020, the Company recognized the sensitivity of updating rates when 
COVID-19 was impacting the physical and financial health of our customers, including businesses 
struggling to stay viable in the face of unprecedented economic upheaval.  Accordingly, the filing 
described various measures the Company took to reduce costs and provide needed customer 
assistance, including: filing emergency petitions to delay Commission-approved gas and electric 
rate increases that were scheduled for April 2020 and postponing this rate filing by three months; 
proactively suspending collection activities to ensure customers had uninterrupted access to 
electric and gas service; filing an emergency petition to temporarily waive various tariff fees, such 
as late payment charges, so customers experiencing economic hardships would not be burdened 
with these costs; and providing economic development assistance to business customers, including 
grants to manufacturers producing critical items to support the pandemic response.8  When this 
case was eventually filed in July 2020, the Company described various proactive adjustments to 
reduce the costs of service by tens of millions of dollars, such as moderating capital forecasts, 
deferring funding for management wage increases, delaying the recovery of depreciation expense, 
and accelerating the return of customer credits.9     
 Today, COVID-19 continues to impact the lives of the Company’s customers.  In 
recognition of these ongoing impacts, the Company and other Signatory Parties went to great 
lengths to mitigate costs and prioritize affordability in the proposed rate plans. The resulting

adversarial parties.  The negotiations fully complied with the Commission’s settlement rules35 and 
provided all parties the opportunity to participate.  In evaluating the JP, the Commission has 
consistently applied the following standards set forth in Settlement Guidelines:36 
• A desirable settlement should strive for a balance among (a) protection of the

economic policies of the Agency and the State and should produce results that were 
within the range of reasonable results that would likely have arisen from a Commission 
decision in a litigated proceeding; 
• In judging the settlement, the Commission shall give weight to the fact that a settlement 
reflects agreement by normally adversarial parties.37

• Whether the agreement compares favorably with the likely result of full litigation and
