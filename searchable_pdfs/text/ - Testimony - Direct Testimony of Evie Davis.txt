SALES FORECAST AND REVENUE ALLOCATION 
1 
Q. 
What led you to investigate the Company’s sales forecast? 
2 
A. 
As part of my responsibilities, I am familiar with the Con Edison surcharges billed to 
3 
NYPA monthly and oversee the further allocation to individual NYPA class customers. 
4 
These surcharges include the RDM surcharge.  Over the past ten years, the RDM 
5 
surcharges assessed to the NYPA class have been consistently positive, indicating an 
6 
initial under recovery of base revenues.  These RDM surcharges have therefore resulted 
7 
in significant charges to the NYPA customers. 
8 
Q. 
What is the purpose of the RDM? 
9 
A. 
The RDM reconciles the difference between actual billed delivery revenues and approved 
10 
revenues as adopted in the previous rate case.  In tariff terminology, the Company 
11 
reconciles the “difference between actual Pure Base Revenue and Allowed Pure Base 
12 
Revenue under this Rate Schedule.” (PSC No. 12, Leaf 21).  The reconciliation is 
13 
performed every six months, and the resulting balance is charged or credited to customers 
14 
with interest, over a six-month period that commences two months later.   
15 
Q. 
What has your review of the Company’s RDM balances shown? 
16 
A. 
It shows that RDM balances applicable to NYPA have been in a consistent deficit 
17 
position. In other words, the actual billed revenues have been lower than the RDM targets 
18 
set forth in the 2022 Joint Proposal and in the Commission’s “Order Correcting Revenue 
19 
Decoupling Mechanism Targets and Directing Tariff Amendments,” issued and effective 
20 
March 19, 2024, in Case 22-E-0064.  Since August 2023 (the first RDM assessment for 
21 
reconciliation to the Allowed Pure Base Revenues from the 2022 Joint Proposal), the 
22

11 
RDM charges described above.  The last time the RDM surcharge was a credit was ten 
12 
years ago in 2015, when the credit was approximately $300,000, indicating a consistent 
13 
over-forecast of NYPA load in Con Edison’s rate proceedings.

Q. 
What is your recommendation regarding the NYPA SC 91 demand forecast? 
4 
A. 
Based on my review of the load data in the EFP rate revenue model provided in response 
5 
to data request DPS 1-81 (April 2025 Update), I am recommending a -1.1% growth rate 
6 
be applied to the SC 91 demand forecast.  The -1.1% growth rate represents the annual 
7 
average change from the four-year period from 2019 to 2023 based upon actual demands 
8 
reported in the Company’s model provided in response to DPS 1-81.  Exhibit __ (ED-4) 
9 
(“Growth from 2019” column).  While NYPA SC 91 load decreases in 2020-2022 were 
10 
largely impacted by COVID-19, loads in 2023 materialized at a new normal below the 
11 
demand seen prior to 2020.  Further, the cumulative average growth rates (“CAGR”) for 
12 
each year starting in 2003 relative to 2023 are shown in the last row of Exhibit __ (ED-4) 
13 
(values highlighted in gold).  The CAGR for SC 91 demand has been consistently 
14 
negative since 2007, when excluding the CAGR between 2020-2022.

RECOMMENDATIONS AND CONCLUSIONS 
7 
Q. 
What are your recommendations? 
8 
A. 
Based on the evidence described above, I recommend a -1.1% growth rate be applied to 
9 
the SC 91 forecasted billing demands.  The Electric Forecast Panel’s SC 91 growth rate 
10 
does not consider the history of load patterns and should be adjusted.  If the Company’s 
11 
forecast load assumptions are adopted, NYPA’s projected revenues will be overstated and 
12 
result in continued RDM charges to customers.  Second, I request that the Commission 
13 
ensure that the Company’s previously filed updates and data responses, which appear to 
14 
have direct effects on the revenue allocation to NYPA with respect to both proposed 
15 
T&D revenues and proposed total revenues, are incorporated into the Company’s next 
16 
required updates in this proceeding.
