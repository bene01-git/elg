INTRODUCTION 
[ UNIFORM TOC I. ] 
 
 
Environmental Defense Fund (“EDF”) respectfully submits its post-hearing brief 
regarding the gas service rate plans of KEDNY and KEDLI (collectively, “National Grid” or 
“Companies”) in the above-captioned proceedings. Based on testimony and evidence submitted 
to the record in this matter, the New York Public Service Commission (“Commission”) should: 
(1) use this rate case to specify certain requirements to be completed and filed in the Companies’ 
next rate filing to ensure compliance with climate policy; (2) implement reforms to incentivize 
the Companies to fully identify leaks on their systems in order to achieve greater leak 
remediation and reduce methane emissions; and (3) require comprehensive long-term gas supply 
planning to avoid undesirable outcomes such as the moratoria on new firm gas service that were 
imposed by some NY gas distribution utilities during the past year. The State’s new climate 
legislation emphasizes the need for the Commission to implement the pragmatic, practical, and 
achievable proposals EDF presented in its testimony and this brief. 
The Commission has clear authority to take the actions recommended by EDF as part of 
this case. The setting of rates involves not only setting a price for service based on ratemaking 
principles, but also encompasses the setting of “terms, conditions” and “safeguards” that the 
Commission deems appropriate for such service.1 The burden of proof is on the utility to 
demonstrate that both its proposed rate changes and the terms under which it proposes to provide 
service are reasonable.2 Furthermore, any rate-setting or other order issued by the Commission 
must adhere to generally applicable state laws and policies, such as the State Administrative

I. 
The Commission’s Order Resolving the KEDNY/KEDLI Rate Case Must be

[ Uniform TOC IX(Q) ] 
 
The CLCPA was signed into law by Governor Cuomo on July 18, 2019 and took effect 
January 1, 2020. The CLCPA mandates that the State of New York adopt measures to reduce 
statewide greenhouse gas (“GHG”) emissions by 40% by 2030 and 85 percent by 2050 (from 
1990 levels), with an additional goal of achieving net zero emissions across all sectors of the 
economy by 2050 (the remaining 15 percent can come from carbon offsets).6 Specifically, the 
CLCPA recognizes the importance of addressing emissions of the greenhouse gas methane, 
which causes 84 times as much global warming as the equivalent amount of carbon dioxide over 
a twenty-year horizon.7 The Act ensures that methane will be accounted for in an emissions 
reporting system to be developed by the Department of Environmental Conservation (“DEC”).8  
The Commission’s order in this rate case will be one of its first major actions to address 
utility rates and the related terms and conditions of service since the CLCPA came into effect.

A. Section 7 of the CLCPA Applies to this Rate Case. 
 
 Section 7 of the CLCPA states that “all state agencies,” “[i]n considering and 
issuing . . . administrative approvals and decisions, . . . shall consider whether such decisions are 
inconsistent with or will interfere with the attainment of the statewide greenhouse gas emissions 
limits established in article 75.”10 If an agency concludes that its action is inconsistent with the 
GHG emission limits, the agency “shall provide a detailed statement of justification as to why 
such limits/criteria may not be met, and identify alternatives or greenhouse gas mitigation 
measures to be required where such project is located.”11 Additionally, state agencies are 
required to “not disproportionately burden disadvantaged communities” and “shall also prioritize 
reductions of greenhouse gas emissions and co-pollutants in disadvantaged communities.”12 
The Commission can address its Section 7 obligations by evaluating whether its actions 
are consistent with the emissions-reduction goals in the statute. The numeric, mass-based GHG 
emissions limit referenced in Section 7 has not yet been set—the DEC is currently undertaking 
the rulemaking13 that must be complete by January 1, 2021.14 But the CLCPA, including Section 
7, has been in effect since January 1, 2020. The statutory text establishes the GHG emissions-
reduction goals that will form the basis for the numeric statewide GHG emission limit: a 40%

C. The Commission Should Use the Relevant Information Presented in this Rate 
Case to Assess Tools to Quantify GHG Emissions. 
 
 
The Companies’ practices regarding the measurement of GHG emissions require 
improvements going forward. Leakage from natural gas distribution pipelines is a major source 
of methane emissions and it is crucial that those emissions be accurately quantified.26 National 
Grid reports methane emissions on its distribution system through the U.S. Environmental 
Protection Agency’s (“EPA”) GHG emissions inventory, commonly referred to as EPA Subpart 
W, a mandatory reporting program for local distribution companies (as well as many other 
industries).27 National Grid uses the EPA Subpart W emissions factors to calculate estimated 
systemwide emissions. The emissions factors are based on studies of emissions levels from 
different pipeline materials, so for example, National Grid multiplies the emissions factor 
specific to cast iron main by the number of miles of cast iron main on its system.28

D. The Commission Should Approve the Companies’ Proposed Future of Heat 
Engineering Group. 
 
The Companies propose to establish a new Future of Heat Engineering Group to 
“implement the gas decarbonization strategy,” consisting of a director and four engineers whose 
time will be evenly split between KEDNY and KEDLI.33  The Companies state that the Group is 
necessary because “additional resources will be needed to manage NPA market solicitations, 
review project proposals on a timely basis, and coordinate with project partners and other 
interested stakeholders.”34 The Companies intend that the Future of Heat engineering group will 
sit under the gas infrastructure and operations team and, when assessing the viability of new 
projects, will consider the GHG emissions reductions that a project is expected to achieve.35 
EDF submits that the Commission should find reasonable the proposed expenditures for 
the Future of Heat Engineering Group. The formation of the Group will better position the 
Companies to implement strategies to comply with the CLCPA, including quantifying and 
tracking GHG emissions reductions.

ALD technology and data analytics refers to the use of high-sensitivity methane detection 
equipment capturing data at high frequency by mobile vehicles equipped with global positioning 
systems. The vast amount of collected data is processed through advanced analytics to estimate 
methane flow rate (e.g., in liters per minute) that can indicate system gas leaks.37  
ALD has “been shown to help utilities find more leaks in their system than has been 
possible with traditional technology.”38 Academic publications and utility experience 
demonstrate that “utility crews using traditional technologies were able to locate only 35% of the 
pipeline leaks that were found using advanced leak detection methods.”39 And ALD technology 
and analytics can reveal more detailed information, reporting not just the presence of a leak, but 
also estimating leak size.40 Integration of ALD technology and analytics into the Companies’ 
leak management programs will provide substantial safety, cost, and environmental benefits and 
will move the Companies forward in their ability to track systemwide methane emissions.

B. The Commission Should Support the Continued Expansion of National Grid’s 
Use of Advanced Leak Detection.  
 
EDF, National Grid, and Colorado State University researchers have collaborated on pilot 
projects to advance the use of ALD technology and analytics—including operating, as described 
by the Companies, “a methane detection pilot program to gather and analyze leak flow data to 
prioritize system investments and leak repairs.”45 National Grid proposes to continue these

C. The Commission Should Approve the Positive Rate Adjustment for High-
Emitting Leaks and Reform its Leak Backlog Incentive Structure.  
 
The Companies’ current and proposed leak backlog performance metric financially 
penalizes the Companies if they add to their leak backlog by identifying additional leaks on their 
systems without immediately remediating the identified leaks. Thus, the mechanism incents the 
Companies to not use advanced leak detection (or any other innovation) in detecting leaks. 
Because this system penalizes the Companies for finding additional leaks, it requires reform. 
EDF submits that the Commission should maintain the incentive to address high-emitting leaks 
in this rate order. And the Commission should reform its leak backlog incentive structure to 
incentivize reductions in the volume of leaked methane rather than the number of leaks. First, the 
Commission should require a system-wide methane leak survey of the Companies’ service 
territories using ALD, to serve as the basis for an annual volumetric leak reduction target.     
The current leak backlog performance incentives for each Company were established in 
the 2016 rate cases and include (1) an annual leak backlog reduction target with a negative 
revenue adjustment if the Company fails to achieve the target, and (2) a positive revenue 
adjustment if the Company repairs additional Type 3 high-emitting leaks beyond the annual 
reduction target.54 The Companies propose to retain the same incentive structure in this rate case. 
The annual targets would continue to decrease the leak backlog, and the Companies could earn a 
positive revenue adjustment of 1 basis point for the repair of every 50 additional high-emitting 
Type 3 leaks beyond the annual backlog target, with a maximum of 5 basis points available.55 In 
response to an alternate proposal from DPS Staff, 56 the Companies explained why they “strongly 
believe” the leak backlog metric should include incentives for repairing high-emitting leaks:
