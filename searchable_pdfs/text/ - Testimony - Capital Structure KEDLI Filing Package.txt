II. 
Ratemaking Capital Structure 
1 
Q. 
What capital structure does KEDLI propose to use for ratemaking purposes in this 
2 
proceeding? 
3 
A. 
For a utility that is part of a holding company structure, New York Public Service 
4 
Commission (“Commission”) precedent supports setting rates using the utility’s stand-
5 
alone capital structure where the utility can demonstrate that it is financially insulated from 
6 
its parent and its capital structure is reasonable and in line with its own financial and 
7 
business risks.  KEDLI is proposing for ratemaking purposes to use a capital structure that 
8 
contains 48 percent common equity for the Rate Year.  However, as shown in the table 
9 
above, should this proceeding result in a settlement where rates are established for multiple 
10 
years, KEDLI proposes that the capital structure should reflect 49 percent common equity 
11 
in Data Year 1 and 50 percent common equity in Data Years 2 and 3.   
12 
 
13 
KEDLI believes an increase in common equity in the Data Years is appropriate given the 
14 
interest of maintaining its credit ratings and achieving continued favorable financing for 
15 
the benefit of customers.  KEDLI’s cash flow metrics have declined from March 2016 
16 
levels and are expected to decline further according to the credit rating agency Moody’s 
17 
Investor Services, Inc. (“Moody’s”).  In addition, Moody’s does not envision cash flow 
18 
generation to improve in the medium term, citing the impact of the passage of the Tax Cuts 
19 
and Jobs Act of 2017 (“Tax Reform Act”) as the main contributing factor.  Higher common 
20 
equity will improve cash generation during a period when Moody’s sees limited prospects 
21 
for improvement, reducing risks of further credit rating downgrades.   
22 
 
23

Moody’s has recognized that changes in utility capital structures to reflect more equity

could act as an offset to the negative cash flow impact arising from the Tax Reform Act.

It is important that the rates established in this proceeding ensure that KEDLI’s cash flow 
3 
metrics do not continue to decline to a level that would not support its current A3 rating.
