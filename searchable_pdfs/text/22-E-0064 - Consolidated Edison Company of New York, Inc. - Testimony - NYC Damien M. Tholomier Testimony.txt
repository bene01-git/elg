WHY IS VOLTAGE MANAGEMENT ON AN ELECTRIC DISTRIBUTION 
12

13 
A. 
Power systems must be designed based on voltage, current rating, and fault current values 
14 
according to recognized industry standards and major equipment manufacturers. Voltage 
15 
regulation and control in an electrical power system is important for proper operation of 
16 
the electrical power equipment to prevent damage such as overheating of generators and 
17 
motors.  Powering a sizable load block far from the main substation or power plant requires 
18 
special care. This is especially true if the load is supplied by an overhead line because it 
19 
has a higher impedance than an underground cable, which negatively impacts voltage 
20 
regulation performance. Choosing the optimal voltage level – an LV versus MV 
21 
distribution system – must be based on a comprehensive view of customers infrastructure

The voltage selection must incorporate both technical and financial aspects of the 
2

3 
 Quantity and type of customer loads to feed 
4 
 Voltage levels standardized in the industry and the region where the system is 
5 
located 
6 
 Expansion needs and new load additions 
7 
 The voltage requirements and cost of the electrical equipment and assets at 
8 
customer's infrastructure 
9 
MV distribution systems have many advantages over LV distribution systems, but 
10

11 
cost and safety are the prevailing factors. Advantages of MV systems include the use of 
12 
much less copper in the form of smaller conductors and fewer sets of conductors, fewer 
13 
power losses, lower voltage drop, and, as a result, distribution of much more power 
14 
capacity to the load.  
15 
Disadvantages of MV systems include larger equipment sizes, greater working 
16 
clearances required around the electrical equipment, more investments in training, and 
17 
longer maintenance periods to repair equipment.

A. 
An unbalanced three-phase systems can cause three-phase motors and other three-phase 
20 
loads to experience poor performance or premature failure because of the following 
21 
conditions: 
22

Case 22-E-0064

 Mechanical stresses in motor due to lower-than-normal torque input; 
1 
 Higher than normal current in motors and three-phase rectifiers; 
2 
 Unbalance current will flow in neutral conductors in three phase wye systems.

4

5

6

7

8

9

10

11 
voltage unbalance is 1.75 percent. 
12 
 
 
With motors, a voltage unbalance leads to a current unbalance. Voltage unbalance 
13 
at the motor terminals causes high current unbalanced, which can be 6 to 10 times as large 
14 
as the voltage unbalance. Unbalance currents lead to torque pulsation, increased vibration, 
15 
and mechanical stress, increases losses, and motor overheating.

Q. 
WHAT IS THE PURPOSE OF CVR/VVO? 
8 
A. 
The main objectives of CVR/VVO are to reduce reactive power flows on a circuit, and then 
9 
lower the voltage to reduce demand and customer energy consumption as well as utility 
10 
distribution system losses. VVO optimizes capacitor bank operations to improve power 
11 
factor and reduce system losses. CVR utilizes voltage regulators, transformer load tap 
12 
changers, and capacitors to control and reduce end-user voltages, which, in turn, lowers 
13 
customers’ demand and energy consumption. 
14 
Q. 
HOW DOES VVO OPTIMIZE VOLTAGE? 
15 
A. 
As shown in Exhibit__(DMT-13), VVO is accomplished by “flattening” a feeder line’s 
16 
voltage profile, or, in other words, narrowing the bandwidth of the voltage from the head-
17 
end of the feeder to the tail-end in concert with capacitors and other voltage regulating 
18 
devices such as load tap changers and line voltage regulators for voltage support.  
19 
 
 
In the standard VVO model, voltage will be monitored continuously along the 
20 
feeder and at selected end points rather than only at the substation, allowing the head-end 
21 
voltage to be significantly lowered at most times.

HOW DOES CVR REDUCE ENERGY CONSUMPTION AND DEMAND? 
1 
A. 
As shown in Exhibit__(DMT-13), CVR is the intentional operation of the distribution 
2 
system to provide customer voltages in the lower end of the acceptable range by reducing 
3 
voltage at the head-end of the feeder, with the goal of achieving energy and/or demand 
4

5
