II. 
Ratemaking Capital Structure 
8 
Q. 
What capital structure does the Company propose for rate-setting purposes in these 
9 
proceedings? 
10 
A. 
For a utility that is part of a holding company structure, Commission precedent supports 
11 
setting rates using the utility’s stand-alone capital structure in cases where the utility can 
12 
demonstrate that it is financially insulated from its parent and its capital structure is 
13 
reasonable and in line with its own financial and business risks.  Niagara Mohawk is 
14 
proposing for ratemaking purposes to use a capital structure that contains 48 percent 
15 
common equity for the Rate Year.  As shown in the exhibits referenced above, however, 
16 
should this proceeding result in a settlement where rates are established for multiple years, 
17 
the Company proposes that the capital structure should reflect 49 percent common equity 
18 
in Data Year 1 and 50 percent common equity in Data Year 2. 
19 
  
20 
 
Niagara Mohawk believes an increase in common equity in the Data Years is appropriate 
21 
as a means to enable the Company to maintain its credit ratings and achieve continued 
22 
favorable financing for the benefit of customers.  The Company’s cash flow metrics have 
23

Q. 
Are the Company’s current credit ratings consistent with the Commission’s policy on 
1 
optimal utility credit ratings? 
2 
A. 
Yes.  What is referred to as the “Generic Financing Proceeding” (Case 91-M-0509) resulted 
3 
in a 1994 Recommended Decision that, although never formally adopted by the 
4 
Commission, has often been referenced by the Commission when determining the cost of 
5 
capital to be used in setting rates.  The Recommended Decision proposed that the “A” 
6 
rating should continue to be the long-term target for utilities and that the Commission 
7 
should continue to offer utilities ratemaking support for an “A” rating.  The Commission 
8 
recently reaffirmed the importance of strong credit ratings for utilities in Case 17-M-0815, 
9 
which considered the impact of the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017 (“Tax Reform Act”) on 
10 
utilities, finding that “strong investment grade credit ratings benefit both utilities and their 
11 
customers.”  
12 
 
13 
 
Niagara Mohawk’s current ratings are in the “A” rating range, but any further downgrades 
14 
would move Niagara Mohawk into the “Baa/BBB” rating range.  Niagara Mohawk would 
15 
have difficulty maintaining an A3 rating under the cash flow metric guidance released by 
16 
Moody’s in June 2020, highlighting the importance of adopting credit enhancing 
17 
provisions such as progressing toward a higher equity percentage in the Company’s 
18 
ratemaking capital structure toward the outer years of any multi-year rate plan.  Recent 
19 
external pressures, including the effects of the Tax Reform Act and cash flow challenges 
20 
resulting from bill non-payment associated with COVID-19, exacerbate the risk of having 
21 
the Company’s financial metrics fall below the threshold ranges for its current rating.
