STATEMENT IN OPPOSITION OF JOINT PROPOSAL 
In accordance with the Ruling on Schedule issued by Administrative Law Judges (“ALJ”) 
Ashley Moreno and James Costello on March 6, 2023, WE ACT for Environmental Justice 
(“WE ACT”) and Alliance for a Green Economy (“AGREE”) hereby submit this statement in 
opposition to the Joint Proposal that Consolidated Edison Company of New York, Inc. (“Con 
Edison” or the “Company”) filed on February 16, 2023. 
PRELIMINARY STATEMENT 
New York State has taken the firm and clear stand that addressing human-induced 
climate change is vital and of the highest priority. The passage of the Climate Leadership and 
Community Protection Act (“CLCPA”) solidifies the path begun under the Reforming the 
Energy Vision (“REV”) framework and the State Energy Plan. The course is clear: New York 
must cut its carbon emissions to net zero on an extremely aggressive timeline—a 40% reduction 
from 1990 levels must occur in less than a decade and an 85% reduction must be achieved in just 
27 years.1 Cutting emissions will require an unwavering commitment to abandoning the use of 
fossil fuels and decarbonizing the State’s economy as quickly as possible.   
The Public Service Commission’s (“PSC” or the “Commission”) role in ensuring that the 
targets in the CLCPA are met cannot be understated. As the regulator of the State’s energy 
utilities, the Commission must oversee the transformation of New York’s energy sector to 
comply with the CLCPA. As is made crystal clear by the Final Scoping Plan, created by the 
State’s Climate Action Council, the PSC must step away from a business-as-usual approach to 
energy regulation and planning and require: that utilities to make deep and swift cuts to their use

Unfortunately, the Joint Proposal presented by Con Edison, the Department of Public

precisely the opposite of what the Commission should be approving under the New York Energy

necessary funding for gas infrastructure investments to support gas demand, instead of investing 
in the strategic downsizing of the gas system in line with the rapid transition to heat pumps 
called for by the Final Scoping Plan. Further, provisions would increase residential customer

clean energy to, Disadvantaged Communities, the Joint Proposal contains no provisions that will 
specifically benefit Disadvantaged Communities in this way. Adoption of the Joint Proposal 
would largely preserve the status quo for another three years, a status quo in which runaway 
climate change is inevitable and thousands of people suffer the health consequences of bad air 
quality. The Company stated that it is committed to clean energy and that its gas rate plan was

In addition to the CLCPA’s emissions mandates, the statute also includes provisions 
specifically addressing the disproportionate climate and environmental burdens that 
Disadvantaged Communities (“DACs”) experience. The CLCPA broadly defines DACs as 
“communities that bear burdens of negative public health effects, environmental pollution, 
impacts of climate change, and possess certain socioeconomic criteria, or comprise high-
concentrations of low- and moderate- income households.”16 The CLCPA protects DACs from 
discrimination through two key statutory provisions. CLCPA § 7(3) requires all state agencies, 
including the PSC, to avoid taking administrative actions that disproportionately burden DACs.

With regards to DACs, the Commission is obliged to consider burdens on DACs 
individually and as a whole29 including environmental, social, and economic burdens.30 Before 
the Legislature passed the CLCPA, the Commission already had a legal duty to provide safe and

requires more action from the Commission than was required under the Public Service Law. 
Where a term in the Joint Proposal disproportionately burdens any DAC, the Commission is 
legally obligated to modify the term such that the disproportionate burden is eliminated.33 Where 
a term proposes to reduce emissions, the Commission must point to language in said term stating 
that DACs will be prioritized,34 or else modify the term.

gas.178 Both hydrogen and NOx are indirect GHGs that lead to ozone formation in the 
atmosphere and creates health-harming pollution.179 Hydrogen combustion’s most significant 
public health threat is NOx, a pollutant that damages hearth and respiratory function, impairs 
lung growth in children, and leads to higher rates of emergency room visits and even premature 
deaths.180 By withholding this information, neither the Commission nor the public can know 
whether Con Edison’s R&D around hydrogen is an economical and environmentally beneficial 
resource option or an unnecessary additional investment in the continuation of the fossil gas 
distribution system that should be phased out. In addition, the Company does not provide for any 
mechanisms to ensure that hydrogen investments will not also promote and expand fossil gas use 
and infrastructure.

CONCLUSION 
The Legislature’s establishment of aggressive binding mandates for greenhouse gas 
emissions for New York State and lawmakers’ corresponding push for electrification require the 
Commission to embark on a dramatic shift away from the business-as-usual regulation of the 
operations of the utilities. In light of all of the reasons outlined in this Statement of Opposition, 
the Joint Proposal, as a whole, is at odds with both the CLCPA and the Public Service Law 
because the agreement permits the Company to use ratepayer dollars to invest in the GIRR 
program and pay a premium for alternative fuels that are unsafe, unhealthy, and further extend 
the State’s reliance on fossil fuel. A Joint Proposal that complies with shifts in New York State 
law would instead focus on the obvious need to reduce the demand for and use of gas and 
transition to electrification.  
The Joint Proposal also fails to abide by legislators’ instructions to agencies to rationally 
and equitably distribute the costs of the economy-wide transition away from fossil fuels. The 
Joint Proposal locks in immense costs for the fossil fuel infrastructure and increases the customer 
charge in a manner that will inevitably lead to an increase in energy insecurity for customers in 
Con Edison’s service territory. The Joint Proposal’s also structures rates in a manner that 
prevents rate payers from aggressively reducing their energy usage–a primary objective of the 
CLCPA. Finally, despite the steep increases in residential rates, the Joint Proposal provides 
minimal protections and programs to address disproportionate impacts in disadvantaged

Commission reject and not adopt the Joint Proposal.
