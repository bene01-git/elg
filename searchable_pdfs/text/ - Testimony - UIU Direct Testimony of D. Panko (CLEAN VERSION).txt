C.  
RATE DESIGN 
5 
My testimony provides general recommendations for rate design that can be 
6 
further explored, as appropriate, depending on the Companies’ final revenue 
7 
requirements.  The starting point for my recommended rate design approach is my 
8 
proposed revenue allocation process, which I used to assign costs to individual 
9 
service classes (e.g., setting a revenue target for each SC).  However, I observe 
10 
there are a variety of factors that may change the revenue target allocated to each 
11 
SC, such as a change in the Companies’ revenue requirements that requires more 
12 
or less revenues to be collected from all SCs.  The revenue target allocated to 
13 
each SC together with the fixed monthly customer charge and portion of the 
14 
revenues that flow through each of the volumetric rates (e.g., increasing the ratio 
15 
of tail-block to mid-block) can potentially have an impact on the optimal rate design 
16 
for each SC.  As such, my rate design recommendations are explanatory at this 
17 
time, since my recommendation will be dependent upon the final revenue 
18 
requirements, which will influence the magnitude of any potential changes to the 
19 
customer charge and volumetric rates.

1 
A. 
Background 
2 
Q. 
Can you briefly explain the revenue allocation process in a typical utility rate case? 
3 
A. 
Yes.  The results of one or more ECOS studies guide decisions regarding how 
4 
proposed rate changes should be allocated across various service classifications.  
5 
The specific process can vary, but ECOS results are generally examined to see 
6 
whether certain classes have been identified as having a high ROR (paying more 
7 
than the costs they have been allocated – sometimes referred to as “overearning”).  
8 
If a high ROR is identified, an effort is typically made to assign a somewhat smaller 
9 
share of the rate increase or revenue requirement to that service classification, 
10 
thereby moving in the direction of greater uniformity in the service classification 
11 
RORs.  Similarly, if certain classes are identified as having a low ROR (paying less 
12 
than the costs that have been allocated to them, which is sometimes described as 
13 
“underearning”), a somewhat greater than average share of the rate increase or 
14 
revenue burden may be assigned to that class during the revenue allocation step. 
15 
In carrying out the revenue allocation process, tolerance bands are often 
16 
used around the system average ROR to decide whether the service classification 
17 
ROR deviates far enough from the average ROR to merit special treatment.  The 
18 
width of the tolerance band (e.g., +-10%, 20%, 30%, etc.) can vary widely, but the 
19 
effect is generally the same – to ameliorate disputes concerning the ECOS results, 
20 


--- Page 33 ---
CASE 19-G-0309 and 19-G-0310  
 
             
 DIRECT TESTIMONY OF  
                                                                                      DANIELLE PANKO 
32 
since service classifications that fall within the tolerance band generally receive 
1 
the same percentage change.43  
2 
An analyst must exercise judgment when determining the size of the 
3 
tolerance band, and this is sometimes a topic of dispute.  The tolerance band can 
4 
be important because it helps determine which service classifications will receive 
5 
revenue changes that are less than, or more than, the other service classifications.  
6 
Finally, some restrictions are typically imposed on how much any service 
7 
classification will deviate from the average (e.g., no class is given a decrease, no 
8 
class is given more than 150% of the average increase). 
9 
The typical process just described is not the only method that has been 
10 
used in New York. Over the years in various proceedings, the Commission has 
11 
sometimes approved other revenue allocation methods.  For example, in the last

KEDNY and KEDLI gas rate cases (16-G-0058 and 16-G-0059) the Commission

approved system average increases for most of the Companies’ service
