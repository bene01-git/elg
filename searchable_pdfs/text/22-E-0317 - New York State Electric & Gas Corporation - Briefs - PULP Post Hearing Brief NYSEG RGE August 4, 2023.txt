Introduction  
 
The Public Utility Law Project of New York, Inc. (“PULP”) submits this post-hearing 
brief in response to the evidentiary hearing held on July 17th and 18th 2023. PULP hereby limits 
our discussion to two topics so as not to repeat arguments raised in prior pleadings but instead 
focus on the subjects discussed during the Evidentiary Hearing. First, the sheer affordability 
crisis rate payers will face due to the New York State Electric & Gas Corporation (“NYSEG”) 
and Rochester Gas and Electric Corporation (“RG&E”) (or the “Companies”) rate increases as 
included in the Joint Proposal (“JP”). Second, the negotiated 9.20% return on equity (“ROE”). 
PULP believes that using the generic financing model to set the ROE and recalculating that ROE 
annually could save rate payers considerable costs.  Moreover, PULP addresses assertions that 
implementation of the Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act (herein after 
“CLCPA” or “Climate Act”) warrants the 9.20% ROE. PULP provides greater detail below. 
 
Background  
 
The Companies filed their request for new rates on May 26, 2022. On September 26, 
2022, PULP and other parties filed direct testimony and on October 18, 2022, PULP and other 
parties filed Rebuttal Testimony. On October 19, 2022, the Companies filed with the parties and 
the Secretary to the Public Service Commission (“PSC” or “Commission”) a “Notice of 
Impending Settlement Negotiations.” Settlement negotiations began on November 2, 2022. On 
June 14, 2023, Settling Parties filed a Joint Proposal. Multiple conferences were held virtually 
before the Administrative Law Judges (“ALJs”).1 The ALJs then issued a “Ruling on Process 
and Schedule” on June 14, 2023. On June 27, 2023, parties filed “Statements on the Joint 
Proposal.” On July 7, 2023, Reply Statements were due. An evidentiary hearing was held on July 
17th and 18th, with witnesses from the Companies and the Department of Public Service (“DPS”), 
who were present for cross-examination. Following the end of the Evidentiary Hearing, the ALJs 
issued a ruling, with a deadline of August 4, 2023, for parties to submit post hearing briefs. 
PULP provides the following arguments below.

Argument 
 
I. THE 9.20% ROE SHOULD BE UPDATED, AND AN ANNUAL 
RECALCULATION ORDERED TO HELP SAVE CUSTOMER COSTS.  
 
PULP remains concerned about the negotiated 9.2% ROE included in the JP, as it has a 
significant, direct impact on customers’ bills.2 The Companies are looking to increase from the 
current ROE of 8.8%, to the 9.2% ROE, which substantially adds to the already historic rate 
increases included in this JP.3 PULP generally urges careful consideration of every aspect of the 
JP when looking for ways to cut costs. Specifically, we urge the ALJs and the Commission to 
modify the JP so that the ROE is not set through confidential settlement negotiations, but rather 
through calculations using the generic finance model, while also providing for an annual 
recalculation.4  
 
A.  Implementation of the Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act should not be 
justification for a higher ROE as the Companies have mechanisms in place that protect 
their business.  
 
PULP urges the ALJs and the Commission to proceed carefully with any assertion that 
the unknowns surrounding implementation of the CLCPA are justification for a macroeconomic 
claim that a higher ROE is warranted. During the Evidentiary Hearing, the panel admitted that 
they are still lacking specifics relating to aspects such as the required capital investment, the 
estimated time to transition customers off natural gas, and the estimated quantification of the 
early retirement of the assets.5 Simply arguing that these factors are unknowns due to the 
CLCPA should not alone be justification for a higher ROE.  
 
Furthermore, as a general principle, it is reasonable to assume that the risks presented by 
the CLCPA would be addressed in a manner that is consistent with past Commission policy that

iv. CLCPA-Related Capital Expenditures 
 
While the JP generally provides for only downward reconciliation of net plant and 
depreciation during the Rate Plan, it also offers the Companies the flexibility over the term of the 
Rate Plan to modify the type, timing, identity, priority, nature, and scope of capital projects from 
those currently forecasted,11 thus allowing them to manage any CLCPA-related capital projects 
in an economical and efficient manner. 
 
More generally, as DPS Staff observed in the most recent Con Edison rate case, the 
Commission has tools at its disposal to mitigate the impact of business risks on the Companies’ 
earnings and cash flow: 
 
New York State regulation offers a myriad of benefits to the utilities it 
regulates. In addition to fully forecasted test years and the ability to earn 
their allowed return, New York regulation also allows for risk-reducing 
deferral and true-up mechanisms, a full pass through of commodity costs to 
ratepayers and an approach to setting ROEs that is very transparent and 
predictable. These attributes of New York State regulation reduce the risk 
borne by the owners of New York utility equity, thereby reducing the 
return required and the cost of equity. These risk-reducing regulatory 
mechanisms are not employed in all jurisdictions, and those jurisdictions 
that use them may not do so to the same extent as New York.12 (Emphasis 
added)

From this perspective, there is no inherently distinctive risk presented by the CLCPA. 
Even in regard to the possibility that “stranded assets” could be created due to the shortening of 
useful lives in the gas business, the Commission would arguably be required to provide an 
accelerated mechanism by which the utilities could recover their investments. It’s important to 
note here that at the same time, the Companies should continue to be pushed in the rate case to 
limit the capital projects on the gas side to help prevent the bloating of future stranded assets, 
thereby requiring an important balance.   
 
B. PULP’s ROE Proposal Should Be Considered and Ultimately Adopted. 
 
Finally, during the Evidentiary Hearing, PULP asked the witness panel whether any 
discovery was conducted on PULP’s proposal to implement an annual ROE recalculation.13 The 
panel answered “no.”14 PULP acknowledges that our proposal did not appear in our Initial 
Testimony. However, we strongly believe that any opportunity to work towards cutting costs and 
saving rate payers financially is important and deserves consideration. Unfortunately, that did not 
happen in this case. Due to the unprecedented rate increases proposed by this proceeding, as well 
as the increasing financial pressures being applied from outside the rate case, the public deserves 
more. PULP stands by our proposal. In closing, PULP urges the ALJs and the Commission to 
consider setting the ROE based on the generic finance model while also providing an annual 
recalculation, thereby modifying the JP. 
 
II. 
CUSTOMERS OF NYSEG/RG&E WILL NOT BE ABLE TO AFFORD THE 
DOUBLE-DIGIT RATE INCREASE INCLUDED IN THE JP. 
 
PULP continues to urge caution when it comes to the rate increases themselves, as we 
fear that many of the Companies’ customers, and not just those who are low-income, will be 
unable to afford the significant increase. The Companies’ recent collections activity reports are a 
good indication of whether residential customers are currently able to pay their bills.

Unfortunately, these numbers are striking. As of June 2023, 130,637 residential NYSEG

During the Evidentiary hearing, Counsel for Multiple Intervenors (“MI”) engaged in 
cross-examination of the witness panel on the revenue requirements and considerations of 
affordability.17 The discussion that ensued only further solidifies PULP’s own concern that the 
rate increases included in the JP are far too high.18 In particular, when MI asked the panel 
whether they were “aware of any rate case involving a major electric and gas utility ... where the 
Commission approved larger delivery rate increases than what's being proposed here today for 
NYSEG electric?”19  the panel first answered they were “unaware” if it was likely one of the 
most expensive.20 However, DPS Staff then answered that they were unaware of a prior NYSEG 
electric case, over the last four cases, having a levelization of 17.1 percent.21 This line of 
questions and answers is concerning, as one would hope that the signatory parties representing 
the panel that day would know whether or not the rate increases included in the JP are the highest 
in recent history. It’s an important factor that must be taken into account and something that 
must be relayed to their customers, if the rate is approved as is. The public deserves to know the 
truth and be provided with time to prepare for the high increases that could be coming.  
 
Conclusion 
PULP remains concerned that residential customers, and not just low-income households 
but others, will be unable to afford the rate increases included in the JP. With such a substantial 
proposed rate increase, PULP believes that all parties should continue to find ways to cut costs. 
This includes thinking outside of the box. PULP believes that our proposal to institute an annual 
ROE recalculation has the ability to provide rate payers substantial savings each year. As a
