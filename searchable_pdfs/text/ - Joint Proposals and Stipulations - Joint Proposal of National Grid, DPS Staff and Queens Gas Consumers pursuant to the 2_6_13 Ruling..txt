JOINT PROPOSAL 
 
 
This Joint Proposal (“Joint Proposal”) is made this 22nd day of February 2013 by and 
among The Brooklyn Union Gas Company d/b/a National Grid NY (“Company” or “KEDNY”), 
the New York State Department of Public Service Staff (“Staff”), and Queens Gas Consumers  
(collectively, the “Signatory Parties”).1 
 
This Joint Proposal resolves all aspects of the examination of KEDNY’s earnings 
computation provisions and other elements of the Company’s rate plan addressed in the above 
referenced proceeding.  Except as otherwise noted, this Joint Proposal will cover a two-year 
period commencing January 1, 2013 and will continue in effect until modified by the 
Commission.  This Joint Proposal provides for the continuation of the Company’s existing rate 
plan provisions, as adopted by the Commission in Cases 06-M-0878 and 06-G-1185 (the “Rate 
Plan”), with the specific modifications provided below, and no change in base gas delivery rates 
(other than revenue neutral changes previously authorized by the Commission) during the rate 
years ending December 31, 2013 and December 31, 2014 (“Rate Year Six” and “Rate Year 
Seven,” respectively), or until new base rates are established by the Commission.

I. 
Procedural Context 
 
KEDNY is currently operating under a Rate Plan that was adopted by the Commission by 
Orders dated December 21, 2007,2 September 17, 20073 and August 23, 2007.4  That Rate Plan 
had a primary term of five years ending December 31, 2012 and remains in effect until modified 
by the Commission.  When the Rate Plan was adopted, the Commission expressed concerns 
about the continuing build-up of deferred debit balances on KEDNY’s balance sheet.  
Accordingly, when the Commission adopted the Rate Plan, it directed KEDNY to file 
incremental revenue increases of $5 million annually in Rate Years One to Five, and to propose 
to reopen its rates during Rate Year Three (the year ended December 31, 2010) to examine 
whether the level of KEDNY’s revenues should be changed in Rate Year Four and beyond to 
address the deferral balances.  Following this reopener filing, the Commission, by order dated 
November 28, 2012, directed KEDNY to implement a $25 million annual surcharge for each 
year beyond December 31, 20125 and to apply the revenue from that surcharge to reduce 
KEDNY’s existing Site Investigation and Remediation (“SIR”) deferred balances.  The 
Company and Staff also engaged in exploratory discussions to determine whether changes 
should be made to KEDNY’s Rate Plan, inter alia, to further the Commission’s goal of

2.2  
Earnings Sharing Mechanism   
 
Following each of Rate Year Six and Rate Year Seven, the Company will prepare and file 
with the Commission a computation of its ROE for the preceding rate year consistent with 
current practices.  At the end of Rate Year Seven, KEDNY will allocate 80 percent of the 
revenue equivalent of its cumulative (over Rate Years Six and Seven) earned common equity 
return in excess of 9.4 percent, if any, as a credit against its SIR deferral balance for the benefit 
of customers.  An example of the earnings report reflecting this sharing mechanism is attached as 
Appendix 2.  Earnings for periods beyond Rate Year Seven will be measured and, if necessary, 
shared on an annual basis and will be pro-rated for any partial year. 
It is the shared understanding of the Signatory Parties that the Company will file new 
base rates that will go into effect no later than January 1, 2016.  If the Company does not file 
new base rates by January 31, 2015, then this earnings sharing provision will be modified to 
require that 100 percent of the revenue equivalent of any earned ROE above 9.4 percent be 
credited against the SIR deferral balance for calendar year 2015 and prospectively until new base 
rates are in effect.

3.2 
Capital Expenditures and Net Utility Plant and Depreciation Expense 
Reconciliation Mechanism 
 
 
The Company’s current capital tracker mechanism is modified to be a downward only net 
utility plant reconciliation mechanism that includes allocated common plant and depreciation 
expense.  This reconciliation mechanism will operate on a cumulative basis over the two-year 
term ending December 31, 2014.  The capital investment levels detailed by project in Appendix 
1, Schedule 9 and reflected in the revenue requirements set forth in Appendix 1 of this Joint 
Proposal  are as follows: 
Rate Year Six - $320.1 million.   
Rate Year Seven - $293.7 million.   
   
The Company will reconcile its cumulative annual actual average net utility plant and 
depreciation expense revenue requirement for Rate Years Six and Seven to the combined target 
average net utility plant and depreciation expense revenue requirement ($360.284 million for 
Rate Year Six plus $387.480 million for Rate Year Seven).  
 
The cumulative average net utility plant and depreciation expense revenue requirement is 
calculated by applying the Company’s pre-tax weighted average cost of capital in the respective 
Rate Years (10.07% percent in Rate Year Six and Rate Year Seven) to the average net utility 
plant balance and adding the depreciation expense to the product.   
 
The difference between the actual annual average net utility plant and depreciation 
expense revenue requirement and the target average net utility plant and depreciation expense 
revenue requirement will carry forward from Rate Year Six and be summed with that of Rate 
Year Seven.  As illustrated in Appendix 4, Schedule 1, if, at the end of Rate Year Seven, the

expense revenue requirement is negative, the Company will defer the revenue requirement 
impact for the benefit of customers.  If, at the end of Rate Year Seven, the cumulative difference 
between the actual and target average net utility plant and depreciation expense revenue 
requirement is positive, there will be no deferral. 
 
The reconciliation mechanism will apply to the Company’s aggregate total average net 
plant and depreciation expense combined, and not to individual components.  The reconciliation

Commission.

3.3 
Low Income/Re-Establishment Fee Waiver Deferral 
 
The revenue requirement for Rate Year Six reflects $10.2 million for low income 
discounts and $0.2 million to fund one-time waivers of re-establishment fees for customers who 
are eligible for the low income program and have had their services disconnected for non-

Year Seven.  The annual re-establishment fee waivers are a component of the Company’s low 
income discount program for the purpose of calculating the deferrals.    
 
4. 
Gas Safety Performance Metrics 
 
The Company’s gas safety performance will be measured for each calendar year (“CY”) 
against the Gas Safety Performance Metrics described herein.  A total of 65 basis points in CY 
2013, 80 basis points in CY 2014, and 150 basis points in CY 2015 and any subsequent CY will 
be at risk for the Company’s performance under the Gas Safety Performance Metrics as set forth 
below. 
4.1 
Leak Prone Pipe (“LPP”) Removal 
 
The Company will continue to use its risk based prioritization model to identify and rank 
segments of LPP to be removed from service.  The Company will target LPP removal from 
service of 85 miles in CY 2013 and CY 2014, with a minimum of 40 miles during each calendar 
year, including at least 10 miles per year outside of City/State Construction-driven work.  The 


--- Page 15 ---
 
 
 
 
11 
 
Company will incur a negative revenue adjustment of 8 basis points should it fail to remove from 
service a minimum of 40 miles of LPP in each of CY 2013 and CY 2014 or a cumulative two-
year total of 85 miles of LPP by the end of CY 2014.  For any CY after CY 2014, the Company 
will incur a negative revenue adjustment of 8 basis points should it fail to remove from service a 
minimum of 40 miles of LPP. 
The Company will submit a semi-annual report to Staff detailing LPP removal mileage, 
main locations and costs.  The Company will submit these semi-annual reports no later than 30 
days following the six-month reporting periods ending June 30 and December 31. 
 
 
4.2 
Leak Management  
 
 In CY 2013, the Company will incur a negative revenue adjustment for failure to achieve 
year-end leak backlog targets for Type 1, 2 and 2A leaks.  In CY 2014 and CY 2015, the 
Company will incur a negative revenue adjustment for failure to achieve year-end leak backlog 
targets for Type 1, 2, 2A and 3 leaks.   
For CY 2013, the Company will incur a negative revenue adjustment if the year-end leak 
backlog exceeds the target of 45 for Type 1, 2 and 2A leaks in the backlog.   
For CY 2014, the Company will incur a negative revenue adjustment if the year-end leak 
backlog exceeds the target of 4,200 for all leaks (Type 1, 2, 2A and 3 leaks) in the backlog.  For 
CY 2015 and any subsequent CY, the Company will incur a negative revenue adjustment if the 
year-end leak backlog exceeds the target of 4,000 for all leaks (Type 1, 2, 2A and 3) in the 
backlog.   
Failure to meet the leak backlog target in a CY will result in a negative revenue 
adjustment of 12 basis points.  The Company will submit an annual report to Staff detailing all 
leaks in its year-end backlog by type no later than 30 days following the end of each CY.

5.2 
Low Income Discount Program 
 
 
Funding for the Company’s low income discount program will be increased to 
$10.4 million annually during Rate Years Six and Seven.  The costs associated with this increase 
in funding will be deferred for future recovery based on the thresholds stated in Section 3.3 
above.  The Company will provide low income discounts of $10.2 million in total on an annual 
basis through (i) a one time low income bill credit of $15.00 per eligible residential heating 
customer in Rate Year Six only and (ii) enhanced low income discounts for the Rate Years.  
From the $10.4 million annual funding for the low income discount program, $0.2 million 
annually will be used to fund one-time waivers of re-establishment fees for customers who are 
eligible for the low income program and have had their service disconnected for non-payment.  
The revised discount and waivers will take effect on the first day of the month following the
