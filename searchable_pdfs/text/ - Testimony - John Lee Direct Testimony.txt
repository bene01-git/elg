1 
Q. 
WHAT IS THE ORIGIN OF LOCAL LAW 84? 
2 
A. 
In 2009, the City enacted a comprehensive effort, called the Greener, Greater 
3 
Buildings Plan (“GGBP”), to proactively reach its sustainability goals.  The GGBP 
4 
enacted a local Energy Conservation Program and includes ordinances that target 
5 
energy efficiency in properties over 50,000 square feet.  There are roughly 15,000 
6 
properties, on which there are roughly 23,000 buildings in the City that fall into this 
7 
category.  Although these buildings account for less than two percent of all the 
8 
buildings in the City, they represent as much as 48 percent of the City’s total energy 
9 
use.  GGBP is designed to ensure that information about energy usage is provided 
10 
to decision-makers in large buildings so that the most cost-effective energy 
11 
efficiency measures are pursued.  The GGBP consists of four regulatory pieces 
12 
supplemented by education and training opportunities and a financing entity called 
13 
the New York City Energy Efficiency Corporation.  The regulations include LL84. 
14 
Q. 
PLEASE EXPLAIN LL84. 
15 
A. 
LL84 requires owners of large buildings to annually measure their energy and water 
16 
consumption and report it to the City.  LL84 standardizes this process by requiring 
17 
building owners to enter their annual energy and water use in the U.S. 
18 
Environmental Protection Agency's (“EPA”) free online tool, ENERGY STAR 
19 
Portfolio Manager, (“Portfolio Manager”)® and to use the tool to submit data to 
20 
the City.  LL84 provides a building owner with information about its building's 
21

1

4 
1) check the “Covered Buildings List” for their property; 2) collect whole building 
5 
energy and water use data from their utilities and fuel vendors; and 3) measure and 
6 
record their energy and water use into Portfolio Manager.

Q. 
WHAT IS THE PROCESS FOR COLLECTING THE BUILDING 
8

10 
properties that are required to comply with LL84 for that calendar year, based on 
11 
property records and eligibility criteria established in LL84.  If the building is on 
12 
the “Covered Buildings List,” the owner is required to comply with LL84 and report 
13 
their energy consumption, inclusive of owner controlled and all tenant 
14 
consumption, to the City.  In order to collect the building energy consumption data 
15 
for benchmarking, building owners are required to submit a written request to the 
16 
Companies.  Once the requisite form is submitted and checked, the Companies will 
17 
release the energy consumption data to the building owner.

In order to improve the flow of information and allow building owners, and the 
12 
City, to more easily identify and understand energy consumption data, the 
13 
Companies should automate the process.  Specifically, the Companies should 
14 
automate three distinct areas of the process in order to maximize the benefits of 
15 
LL84.   
16 
 
 
First, the data request process should be automated.  Providing aggregated 
17 
building data currently involves a process that first requires the building owner to 
18 
proactively log on to the Companies’ website in order to complete, sign and submit 
19 
a Letter of Authorization.  Then, in addition to completing the Letter of 
20 
Authorization, the building owner must call or email the Companies to request 
21

            16-G-0059

aggregated consumption data.  All of this is required before the Company will begin 
1 
its process of data aggregation.  Rather than require the building owners to take an 
2 
additional manual step to request the necessary data, the Companies should fully 
3 
automate the process so that a building owner can make the data request 
4 
electronically, either on the Companies’ websites or through the EPA’s Portfolio 
5 
Manager website.   
6 
 
 
Second, the Companies should streamline the internal processes required to 
7 
aggregate the consumption data.  Once the request for data has been submitted by

12 
City is aware of a number of commercially available tools that the Companies can 
13 
use to automate the customer data exchange.  Many of these tools are being used 
14

15

16 
this has yielded one of the most successful data automation solutions for city 
17 
benchmarking ordinances in the country.  In Seattle, the municipally-owned 
18 
utilities have also developed internal solutions that have proven successful.  These 
19 
automated tools improve the timeliness, accuracy and granularity of data uploaded 
20 
to the Portfolio Manager, at a reasonable cost.  In short, the Companies’ data
