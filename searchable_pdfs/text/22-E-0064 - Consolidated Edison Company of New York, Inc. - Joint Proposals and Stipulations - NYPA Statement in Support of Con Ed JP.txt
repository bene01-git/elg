INTRODUCTION 
On February 16, 2023, Consolidated Edison Company of New York, Inc. (the 
Company) and trial staff of the Department of Public Service (DPS Staff) filed a Joint 
Proposal (JP) with the New York Public Service Commission (the Commission) seeking 
approval of new rates, which would be in effect for three years.  The New York Power 
Authority (NYPA) submits these comments in support of the JP. NYPA has been active 
in these proceedings principally in relation to the electric service issues raised by the 
Company in the above-captioned matter. 
NYPA is a corporate municipal instrumentality and a political subdivision of the 
State, authorized under the New York Public Authorities Law to serve as the full 
requirements power supplier to a number of governmental entities in the New York City 
metropolitan area and Westchester County. NYPA’s interest in these proceedings arises 
from NYPA’s payment of Con Edison’s delivery charges which it passes through to its 
governmental customers. NYPA’s customers include the City of New York, County of 
Westchester, Metropolitan Transportation Authority, Port Authority of New York and 
New Jersey, New York City Housing Authority and the New York State Office of 
General Services, among others. NYPA seeks to ensure that the electric delivery rates 
paid by these state, county and municipal agencies, performing vital services for public, 
are just and reasonable. NYPA also sells electricity to numerous business customers in 
the Company’s service territory under the ReCharge New York power program. 
The JP was filed after an extensive settlement negotiation process. With respect 
to the issues raised by NYPA in this proceeding, the JP represents a fair and reasonable 
outcome which arose from a compromise of NYPA’s litigation positions, as described in

COMMENTS 
I. Embedded Cost of Service Study and NYPA Service Class 
In general, NYPA believes that the 2019 Embedded Cost of Service (“ECOS”) 
study, including the selection of its allocation methodologies, is based on reasonable and 
well-established cost of service principles.  The 2019 ECOS study resulted in a $20 
million “deficiency” charge on the NYPA service class.  In the JP, the $20 million 
charge will be phased-in equally over the three-year rate period. NYPA accepts the JP’s 
treatment of the NYPA service class deficiency and believes that the 2019 ECOS study 
appears to be based on reasoned judgment.  
In the prior rate case NYPA believed that the Company underestimated the energy 
efficiency achievements of the NYPA service class, which would have resulted in a higher 
revenue requirement allocation. This was addressed in the prior rate case and NYPA 
believes that the load forecast and energy efficiency achievement assumptions for the 
NYPA service class used in this JP was appropriate.  
 
II. 
Clean Heat and Energy Efficiency Program Surcharges  
 
In the Company’s case in chief, the Company proposed that all NYPA service 
class customers must begin to pay the surcharges that support the Company’s clean heat 
and energy efficiency programs regardless of whether or not the customers participate or 
if there are programs designed for the customer’s energy use. NYPA customers have 
historically been exempt from such surcharges under Commission precedent and instead

participate in NYPA’s energy efficiency services that are designed to meet NYPA 
customer energy usage and profiles. Both NYPA and the City of New York opposed the 
proposal in pre-filed testimony.  The Joint Proposal keeps with long-standing 
Commission precedent and does not begin to impose surcharges on NYPA customers for 
programs that they may not benefit from. 
 
III. 
Transportation Interconnection Timeline Earnings Adjustment 
Mechanism (EAM) 
The Transportation Electrification Interconnection Timeline (“TE 
Interconnection”) EAM incentivizes the Company to reduce the average timeline for 
transportation electrification projects from application to energization, relative to a 
historical baseline, for transportation electrification projects 300 kilowatts (kW) and larger 
each rate year.  In NYPA’s experience with its own direct current fast chargers for electric 
vehicles and for its customers, the interconnection timeline for large DCFCs for EVs can 
be very lengthy, which is a barrier to the deployment of the EV chargers necessary to 
achieve the State’s clean transportation goals.  In NYPA’s experience when the utility is 
involved and engaged with the interconnecting customer early, it can greatly expedite the 
timeline.  NYPA believes that the TE Interconnection EAM provides the right incentive 
for the utility to be engaged and help the customer through the interconnection process in 
a timely manner.

IV. 
Estimated Billing 
It has been publicly recognized that the use of estimated electricity bills can lead to 
inaccurately charging utility customers.1  In some circumstances, the Company has used 
estimated data rather than acquiring information from an actual meter read, and in some 
instances, the Company did not issue a bill at all.  The reliance on estimated data can 
extend beyond the window of time within which the New York Independent System 
Operator (NYISO) tariff allows for updates and challenges to the NYISO billing data and 
calculations. As a result, Energy Service Companies (ESCOs) and NYPA are not able to 
verify or challenge the NYISO’s billing data, and are forced to issue bills to customers and 
pay NYISO charges that may be generated using inaccurate data. In some instances, this 
creates cross subsidies between customers within classes and can cost customers more 
than what they should be paying. 
Within the Joint Proposal, the Company has agreed to a performance metric to 
calculate its performance with estimated and delayed billing each rate year. The Company 
will report the percentage of bills estimated or delayed over 125 days as of the end of each 
quarter.  The performance level will be the average of the four quarters of each rate year.  
The Company will report its performance by January 31st the following year.  The 
Company will then have targets to achieve based on a percentage reduction from the 
initial calculated baseline.  If the Company fails to reduce the estimated or delayed bills, 
the Company will receive negative revenue adjustments (NRA).  This creates a strong 
financial incentive for the Company to bring its billing up to date.
