Findings 
12 
Q.  
What is the purpose of your testimony in these proceedings? 
13 
A. 
 
My testimony examines, provides evidence of, and offers recommendations in 
14 
several areas: 
15 
1. Evidence that, based on data from the U.S. Census Bureau, the average energy cost 
16 
burden (“energy burden”) of Orange & Rockland Utilities (“Orange & Rockland”, 
17 
“O&R”, or “Company”)’s low-income residential customers is approximately 13% 
18 
of their household income.  This means that New York State has failed to achieve 
19 
its goal of limiting the energy burden of such customers to no more than 6% of 
20 
household income.  
21 
2. Evidence also provided by Census Bureau data that shows in 2019 approximately 
22 
42,700 households in the Company’s service area were presumptively eligible for 
23 
Home Energy Assistance Program (“HEAP”) grants, which along with the receipt 
24 
of Department of Social Services (“DSS”) grants were the sole eligibility-
25 
conferring criteria, thereby helping the Company to focus its outreach efforts on 
26 
communities whose households are most in need of financial assistance, energy 
27 


--- Page 7 ---
 
 
- 7 - 
 
efficiency programs and other initiatives of the Climate Leadership and Community 
1 
Protection Act (“CLCPA”).1 
2 
3. Evidence that the COVID-19 crisis had an immediate and severe impact on the 
3 
Company’s residential utility customers when Governor Cuomo issued his “New 
4 
York on Pause” Order on March 20, 2020. By October, the number of O&R 
5 
residential accounts in arrears had increased 6%, to 17,481 accounts; while the 
6 
amount these customers owed had spiked 86%, to $21 million. The arrears of 
7 
customers not enrolled in the Company’s Low Income Bill Discount Plan rose 
8 
much faster than those of LIBDP participants during this period. 
9 
4. Evidence that “rate mitigation” is proposed by the Company to offset its requested 
10 
rate increases which, when exhausted and if not replaced by other mitigation, would 
11 
lead to effective rate increases substantially higher than those proposed for the Rate 
12 
Year.    
13 
5. Evidence that the Company’s proposed electric and gas delivery rate increases, 
14 
which – after moderation -- would increase average electric and gas delivery rates 
15 
by 9% for customers not enrolled in the Company’s LIBDP, would result in a much 
16 
larger percentage increase for electric discount plan participants, ranging from 12% 
17 
to 107%, depending upon participant tier and usage. Approximately 6,500 “Tier 3” 
18 
LIBDP customers (54% of all participants) would experience the highest electric 
19 
percentage increases. 
20 
6. Evidence that changes in volumetric electric and gas rate design could, if 
21 
responsibly implemented, reduce greenhouse gases by incentivizing energy 
22 
conservation; and reduce the bills of low- and moderate- usage residential 
23 
customers while also protecting high-usage LIBDP participants from the effects of 
24 
higher bills by providing such participants with energy efficiency resources that 
25 
would have the effect of lowering their usage.
