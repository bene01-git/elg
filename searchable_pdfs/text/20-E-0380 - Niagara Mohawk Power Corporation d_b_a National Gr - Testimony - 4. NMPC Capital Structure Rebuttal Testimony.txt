III. 
Financial Integrity 
18 
Q. 
The Staff Finance Panel (“SFP”) (at 64-65) asserts that its capital 
19 
structure and return on equity recommendation, when combined 
20 
with other quantitative and qualitative factors, produces credit

1 
ratings.  Do you agree? 
2 
A.  
No, we do not agree.   Staff’s analysis ignores certain factors that threaten 
3 
the Company’s existing credit ratings.  Niagara Mohawk is currently 
4 
under credit watch negative by Moody’s and has shown weak credit 
5 
metrics under Moody’s methodology for the last couple of years.  As 
6 
noted by the SFP (at 74), Moody’s October 2020 credit opinion report 
7 
warned that Niagara Mohawk could be downgraded if the Company’s 
8

10 
consistent basis.  The credit metric analysis provided in Exhibit___(SFP-
11 
15) shows that Staff’s capital structure, return on equity, and other revenue 
12

13 
15.5 percent, which falls short of the high teen threshold enumerated by 
14 
Moody’s.  Based on additional input from Staff, correcting the calculation 
15 
in Exhibit___(SFP-15) to capture the nuclear and the unamortized debt 
16 
expense values would lower the implied CFO pre-WC/Debt metric to 14.9 
17 
percent.  Consequently, Staff’s recommended equity ratio and return on 
18 
equity fall short of supporting the Company’s current A3 rating from 
19 
Moody’s.  In fact, Staff’s estimated CFO pre-WC/Debt ratio falls toward 
20 
the middle of the 11.0 percent to 19.0 percent range required to achieve a

Q. 
Is the scorecard analyses presented in Exhibit__(SFP-15) sufficient to 
11 
determine what the Company’s credit ratings would be under Staff’s 
12 
ROE and equity ratio proposals?  
13 
A. 
No.  The matrix scorecard methodologies published by Moody’s and 
14 
Standard & Poor’s are only indicative guidelines for estimating a credit 
15 
rating.  Other factors play a key role in the rating agencies’ credit rating 
16 
assessment processes, and the ultimate credit rating can deviate from the 
17 
rating implied by the scorecard calculations.  For example, Moody’s 
18 
explains its scorecard is just one component of its ratings methodology 
19 
and it can only be used to approximate ratings assignments:

3

4

In the case of Moody’s credit rating assessment of Niagara Mohawk, the

rating agency has clearly indicated the core metric of CFO pre-WC/Debt

is a threshold issue outside of the general matrix scoring approach.  In the 
16 
case of Standard & Poor’s, Niagara Mohawk’s ultimate rating is 
17 
influenced by the agency’s group rating methodology.  Standard & Poor’s, 
18 
however, has indicated that the Company’s stand-alone credit profile

20

Q. 
Do you have any concerns with the Staff Efficiency and Sustainability 
6 
Panel’s recommendation (at 68-70) to recover the costs of the 
7 
Commercial Make-Ready Program for electric vehicles through a 
8 
surcharge implemented after costs have been incurred rather than  by 
9 
including those costs in base rates in these proceedings? 
10 
A. 
Yes.  Recovery of expenses through forward-looking test years is a well 
11 
known component of New York’s regulatory framework that is looked on 
12 
favorably by rating agencies as it provides for the timely recovery of costs.  
13 
Moving away from that approach would likely be considered a step 
14 
backward in the regulatory paradigm because it would create a lag in 
15 
recovery and have the effect of reducing current cash flow.  Although 
16 
surcharges that include an allowance for carrying charges on net balances 
17 
can make a company’s earnings whole over time, they still reduce near-
18 
term cash flow and put strain on cash flow-related credit metrics.  
19 
Consequently, we support the Electric Vehicle Panel’s proposal to recover

Q. 
Do you have any other comments on the importance of providing an 
4 
equity ratio and rate of return that support the financial strength and 
5 
creditworthiness of the Company? 
6 
A. 
Yes.  As noted by the SFP (at 75), Moody’s has downgraded a number of 
7 
utilities over the past year and a half.  The wave of downgrades is in large 
8 
part due to the effects of the 2017 Tax Reform Act, which led to reduced 
9 
operating cash flow for utilities such as Niagara Mohawk.  More recently, 
10 
COVID-19 pandemic-related bill non-payment issues have caused further 
11 
cash flow challenges.  Allowing a reasonable rate of return and, in the 
12 
event a multi-year rate plan is authorized, allowing for modestly higher 
13 
equity ratios in the later years of the plan will help mitigate these cash 
14 
flow pressures and demonstrate stable and consistent support for 
15 
maintaining Niagara Mohawk’s credit metrics.  In contrast, we believe the 
16 
deterioration in credit metrics that results from Staff’s proposal would 
17 
weaken investors’ assessment of the Company’s regulatory framework 
18 
and put further downward pressure on credit ratings.

IV. 
Cost of Debt 
1 
Q. 
Please describe the SFP’s recommendation concerning the cost of the 
2 
Company’s planned issuance of long-term debt in the Rate Year? 
3 
A. 
In its October 14, 2020 corrections and updates filing, the Company 
4 
recommended a 1.90 percent interest rate for new long-term debt to be 
5 
issued in the Rate Year based on an implied forward 10-year rate of 0.95 
6 
percent based on swap rate pricing (as discussed below the Company has 
7 
updated its recommended new debt rate for the Rate Year to 2.00 percent).  
8 
The SFP does not agree with the Company’s proposed use of forward rates 
9 
to determine the interest rate of the planned Rate Year debt, arguing (at 
10 
31) that “the Commission considers current interest rates to be the best 
11 
predictor of where interest rates will be in a given period going forward.” 
12 
 
13 
Q. 
Do you agree with the SFP’s methodology for determining the 
14 
Company’s cost of long-term debt? 
15 
A. 
No, we do not, for several reasons.   First, as the chart produced below of 
16 
recent ten-year U.S. Treasury bond yields shows rather clearly, a 
17 
momentary yield is hardly the “best predictor of where interest rates will 
18 
be going forward.” We recognize that the Federal Reserve has held the 
19 
federal funds rate, which is the inter-bank lending rate, low.  This action 
20 
by the Federal Reserve, however, clearly has not held the yields on the 
21 


--- Page 11 ---
Rebuttal Testimony of the Capital Structure Panel 
 
 
 
 
Page 9 of 12 
 
ten-year Treasury bonds consistently low, or stable.  As shown in the 
1 
figure below, while the yield on the ten-year Treasury bond has been 
2 
volatile, it has been increasing generally since early August 2020.  This 
3 
chart demonstrates that a point in time estimate, using recent market 
4 
conditions, is not the appropriate estimate of interest rates over the period 
5 
that the rates that are determined in this proceeding will be in effect.   
6 
 
7 
Figure 1: Ten-Year Treasury Bond Yield 
8 
 
9 
In addition, the implied ten-year rate used by the Company is derived from 
10 
market pricing of forward swap rates, which measure the current market-
11 
based cost of debt for that future period.  Market participants are aware of 
12 
historical and current interest rates, and the market-traded forward swap 
13 


--- Page 12 ---
Rebuttal Testimony of the Capital Structure Panel 
 
 
 
 
Page 10 of 12 
 
rates reflect the overall market’s combined assessment of future interest 
1 
rates.  If investors believed interest rates were most likely to remain at 
2 
their current level, that would be reflected in forward rate pricing.  For 
3 
these reasons, we strongly disagree with Staff’s recommendation to 
4 
assume current yields for future debt issuances.

V. 
Service Company Asset Recovery Charge  
7 
Q. 
Do you agree with the Staff Information Technology Panel’s (“SITP”) 
8 
rate of return recommendation (at 67-69) for the Service Company? 
9 
A. 
No, we do not.  We disagree with the SITP’s conclusion that the 
10 
Company’s weighted average cost of capital is the appropriate rate of 
11 
return for information technology investments held at the Service 
12 
Company.  The SITP ignores the fact that the Service Company maintains 
13 
a capital structure that is separate and distinct from Niagara Mohawk.  
14 
Moreover, the Service Company’s cost of long-term debt is lower than 
15 
Niagara Mohawk’s cost of long-term debt.  Therefore, the proposed rate of 
16 
return for the Service Company is reasonable and conservative. 
17 
 
18 
Q. 
Does the Panel agree with the ROE component of the SITP’s rate of 
19 
return recommendation for the Service Company? 
20 


--- Page 13 ---
Rebuttal Testimony of the Capital Structure Panel 
 
 
 
 
Page 11 of 12 
 
A. 
No, we do not.  We agree that the Company’s authorized ROE should be 
1 
used for the purpose of determining the cost of equity component of the 
2 
Service Company’s rate of return.  As discussed in the ROE Panel’s 
3 
rebuttal testimony, however, 8.70 percent is an unreasonable estimate of 
4 
the Company’s cost of equity.  As such, the SITP’s proposed pre-tax rate 
5 
of return of 7.40 percent (which translates to 5.93 percent after-tax) for 
6 
Niagara Mohawk is unreasonable.  Therefore, we continue to support the 
7 
Company’s 6.37 percent after-tax weighted average cost of capital for the 
8 
Service Company, subject to updated market data prior to the 
9 
Commission’s decision.
