Infrastructure Enhancement 
3 
Q. 
What is infrastructure enhancement?  
4 
A. 
Infrastructure enhancement concerns the 
5 
Company’s efforts to remove leak prone pipe.  
6 
Q. 
What is leak prone pipe?  
7 
A. 
Generally, leak prone pipe is unprotected steel 
8 
pipe, cast iron pipe, wrought iron pipe, and 
9 
some vintages of plastic pipe that may become 
10 
brittle.  As the name suggests, pipes made of 
11 
these materials are more prone to gas leakage 
12 
than coated and protected steel and newer 
13 
plastic pipe materials. 
14 
Q. 
What does “unprotected” mean in this context?  
15 
A. 
For purposes of this measure, “unprotected” 
16 
means that the pipe lacks adequate cathodic 
17 
protection, rendering it more susceptible to 
18 
corrosion.  It is also commonly referred to as 
19 
“bare steel” pipe.  Cathodic protection is a 
20 
method by which steel pipelines are protected 
21 
from corrosion by making the surface a cathode, 
22 
thereby inhibiting the formation of iron oxides 
23 
that can result in reduced wall thickness of the 
24 


--- Page 18 ---
Cases 23-E-0418 & 23-G-0419 
    
SPSP 
 
 
17 
 
pipe.  Unprotected pipe often has no surface 
1 
coating or inadequate coating, rendering efforts 
2 
to cathodically protect the pipe ineffective or 
3 
uneconomical.   
4 
Q. 
How does the leak prone pipe removal program add 
5 
to the safety of the Company’s gas system?  
6 
A. 
Leaks on underground piping can create safety 
7 
risks to the public and can potentially lead to 
8 
gas-related incidents, such as fires or more 
9 
serious events that could impact health and 
10 
safety, especially under frost conditions.  Leak 
11 
prone pipe generally leaks at a higher rate than 
12 
coated and cathodically protected steel or most 
13 
plastic pipes.  The removal of leak prone pipe 
14 
reduces these safety risks.  
15 
Q. 
Explain the importance of removing unprotected 
16 
or bare steel and cast-iron pipe.  
17 
A. 
Unprotected or bare steel pipes are more prone 
18 
to leakage because they are more susceptible to 
19 
corrosion.  Removal of these pipes reduce risks 
20 
associated with leakage.  As for cast iron pipe, 
21 
it is brittle, susceptible to graphitization, a 
22 
form of corrosion, and has low beam strength.  
23 
Low beam strength means that the material can 
24 


--- Page 19 ---
Cases 23-E-0418 & 23-G-0419 
    
SPSP 
 
 
18 
 
fail if it is subjected to heightened loads or a 
1 
loss of ground support, which makes the material 
2 
particularly susceptible to stresses resulting 
3 
from underground disturbances.  These 
4 
disturbances include ground settlement, freeze-
5 
thaw cycles, soil erosion, and nearby excavation 
6 
activities.  Cast iron’s physical 
7 
characteristics therefore make it more prone to 
8 
catastrophic failure than cathodically protected 
9 
steel and most plastic pipe.  Because cast iron 
10 
was installed many decades ago, cast iron pipe 
11 
tends to be in densely populated areas where 
12 
there are many enclosed structures and 
13 
continuous pavement.  In the event of a major 
14 
leak or failure, these circumstances often lead 
15 
to increased migration of below ground gas and 
16 
pose a greater risk of fires or explosions.  
17 
Removal of this type of pipe reduces the 
18 
potential for leaks and incidents resulting from 
19 
its failure.  This will in turn improve the 
20 
public safety.  
21 
Q. 
What are other benefits associated with removing 
22 
LPP?
