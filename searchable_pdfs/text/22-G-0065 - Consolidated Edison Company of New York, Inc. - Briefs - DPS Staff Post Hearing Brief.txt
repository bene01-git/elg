INTRODUCTION 
 
 
Pursuant to the Ruling on Evidentiary Hearing Process issued April 4, 2023, an 
evidentiary hearing was held in these proceedings on April 18, 2023.  The purpose of the hearing 
was to receive into evidence and evaluate the Joint Proposal filed on February 16, 2023.  The 
hearing also provided an opportunity to address the reasonableness of the Joint Proposal and to 
further develop the record to determine whether the Joint Proposal is in the public interest in 
accordance with the well-established Settlement Guidelines of the New York Public Service 
Commission (Commission).  At the hearing, AARP and the Public Utility Law Project (PULP) 
engaged in limited cross-examination of the Department of Public Service Trial Staff (Staff) and 
Consolidated Edison Company of New York, Inc. (Con Edison or the Company) Panels who 
testified in support of the Joint Proposal.1  
ALJs Costello and Moreno established a schedule for post-hearing briefs, which 
are limited to addressing issues raised at the evidentiary hearing and limited to ten pages.  This 
post-evidentiary hearing brief addresses the following issues raised during the evidentiary 
hearing through cross-examination: 1) AARP’s cross-examination concerning Return on Equity 
(ROE); 2) PULP’s cross-examination regarding Earning Adjustment Mechanisms (EAMs); and 
3) PULP’s concerns regarding alleged ERP under enrollment.  
 
ARGUMENT 
I.   The ROE is Reasonable and Not Predetermined 
At the evidentiary hearing, AARP’s cross-examination was in part based on a claim that 
the Staff Finance Panel states in its testimony that a 20 to 50 basis point stay out premium is 
typical.2  Staff takes issue with this mischaracterization.  In its testimony, the Staff Finance Panel 
states that rational investors are well aware of the Commission’s preference for a formulaic 
approach and of the Commission’s preference for multi-year plans.  As such, after accounting for 
the additional financial and business risks associated with a multi-year plan, a rational investor 
would expect the result to likely be similar to the range of recent joint proposals adopted by the

 III.  Energy Affordability Program Enrollment   
As explained in the Staff Consumer Services Panel direct testimony, enrollment in Con 
Edison’s Energy Affordability Program (EAP) is largely based on the receipt of public assistance 
administered by the New York City Human Resources Administration (HRA) and Westchester 
County Department of Social Services (Westchester DSS).9  For example, a customer who has 
applied for and been approved for the Home Energy Assistance Program (HEAP) is eligible for 
the EAP program as a utility customer.  To facilitate an automatic EAP enrollment process, the 
Company entered into a data sharing agreement with HRA and Westchester DSS which provides 
that the agencies validate the customer’s income for public assistance and provide to the 
Company through file matches whether a customer qualifies for automatic enrollment in the 
utility discount program.  Additional public assistance programs, such as Medicaid and Lifeline 
Telephone Service Program, also allow for a customer to enroll in the EAP program and the 
Company has implemented a process that allows a customer to self-certify, consistent with other 
major electric and gas utilities in the State.

The Commission directed that issues concerning utility EAPs be determined and 
addressed on a generic basis.  To that end, the Commission issued its Energy Affordability 
Policy Phase 2 Order (Phase 2 Order) that, among other things, established a working group 
(EAP Working Group) that consists of Staff, utilities and interested stakeholders.10  The 
Commission tasked the EAP Working Group to identify areas for improvements, to collaborate 
on solutions and to ultimately provide recommendations to the Commission to improve EAPs on 
a generic basis.  The Commission directed that reports be filed twice per year regarding the EAP 
Working Group activities and that they provide any updates or recommendations for 
Commission consideration.  In the report filed in July 2022 in Case 14-M-0565, the EAP 
Working Group developed a list of priorities with input from stakeholders for the group to 
address and develop solutions for improvements.  The priority list includes the identification of 
low-income customers as the first priority and discussions on the topic have already commenced.  
Staff recognizes that EAP enrollment is statewide issue and that efforts to increase program 
participation need to be addressed collaboratively by all interested stakeholders through the EAP 
Working Group.  In order to address EAP enrollment concerns, we need to understand the 
universe of low-income households in New York that receive a public assistance benefit and are 
a utility customer.

EAP.  Submetered electric customers do not take electric service from Con Edison, they take 
service from their landlord and the landlord would be responsible for providing the customer a

CONCLUSION 
 
 As explained in Staff’s Statement in Support and Reply to Opposition, the Joint Proposal 
meets the requirements of the Commission’s Settlement Guidelines.  The Joint Proposal is 
consistent with the Commission’s goals and policies, compares favorably with the likely result of 
a litigated case, fairly balances the interests of ratepayers and investors, and provides the 
Commission with a rational basis for its decision.  For all of the above reasons, and those stated 
in Staff’s Statement in Support and Reply to Opposition, Staff respectfully recommends that the 
Joint Proposal be found to be in the public interest and adopted by the Commission in its 
entirety.
