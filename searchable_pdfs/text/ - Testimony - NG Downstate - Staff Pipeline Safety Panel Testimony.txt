LEAK PRONE PIPE REMOVAL 
17 
Q. 
What type of pipe is considered leak prone? 
18 
A. 
LPP generally includes cast iron, wrought iron, 
19 
unprotected steel, and some early vintages of 
20 
plastic pipe that can become brittle.  Pursuant 
21 
to the Direct Testimonies of KEDNY and KEDLI’s 
22 
Gas Infrastructure and Operations (GIOP) Panels, 
23 
the Companies’ population of LPP consists of 
24 


--- Page 13 ---
Cases 19-G-0309 & 19-G-0310 
SPSP 
 
12 
unprotected steel, cast and wrought iron, 
1 
copper, and certain early vintages of plastic 
2 
pipe including Aldyl-A. 
3 
Q. 
Please explain the importance of removing cast 
4 
iron pipe. 
5 
A. 
In general, cast iron pipe is brittle, 
6 
susceptible to graphitization, which is a form 
7 
of corrosion, and has low beam strength.  Beam 
8 
strength refers to the amount of load a 
9 
structure can withstand before it fails.  The 
10 
low beam strength of cast iron pipe means that 
11 
the material can fail if it is subjected to 
12 
increased loading from a force pushing up or 
13 
down on it, or from a loss of ground support.  
14 
This characteristic makes the material 
15 
particularly susceptible to stresses from 
16 
underground forces or disturbances.  Such 
17 
disturbances include, but are not limited to, 
18 
ground settlement, freeze-thaw cycles, soil 
19 
erosion or nearby excavation activities.  The 
20 
physical characteristics of cast iron pipe make 
21 
it more prone to catastrophic failure than 
22 
cathodically protected steel or plastic pipe.  
23 
In addition, segments of cast iron pipe are 
24 


--- Page 14 ---
Cases 19-G-0309 & 19-G-0310 
SPSP 
 
13 
joined by hub joints, also known as bell and 
1 
spigot joints.  Packing material, such as jute, 
2 
was used in the joints to prevent leakage when 
3 
cast iron pipelines were first installed.  
4 
Unlike steel welded joints, these cast iron 
5 
joints develop leaks over time. 
6 
Q. 
How does the removal of cast iron pipe 
7 
contribute to the safety of the natural gas 
8 
system? 
9 
A. 
Cast iron pipe tends to be located in densely 
10 
populated areas where there are many enclosed 
11 
structures, subsurface structures, and 
12 
continuously paved areas.  In the event of a 
13 
major leak or pipeline failure, these 
14 
environmental circumstances may lead to greater 
15 
volumes of below-ground gas migration, 
16 
particularly under paved areas and through 
17 
connected subsurface structures, which creates 
18 
an increased risk for fires or explosions.  
19 
Removing this pipe reduces the potential for 
20 
incidents resulting from major leaks and 
21 
failures, thereby improving public safety. 
22 
Q. 
What is meant by the term unprotected? 
23 
A. 
Unprotected means that the pipe lacks adequate 
24 


--- Page 15 ---
Cases 19-G-0309 & 19-G-0310 
SPSP 
 
14 
cathodic protection, thus rendering it 
1 
susceptible to corrosion.  Unprotected steel 
2 
pipe often has inadequate or no coating, which 
3 
makes efforts to cathodically protect such pipe 
4 
ineffective and uneconomic. 
5 
Q. 
How do LPP removal programs improve the safety 
6 
of natural gas systems? 
7 
A. 
Leaks on underground piping can create safety 
8 
risks to the public and potentially lead to 
9 
serious incidents, resulting in injuries to 
10 
persons or property damage.  Therefore, removing 
11 
LPP reduces these safety risks. 
12 
Q. 
Please explain the importance of removing 
13 
unprotected steel pipe. 
14 
A. 
Data collected by the United States Department 
15 
of Transportation, Pipeline and Hazardous 
16 
Materials Safety Administration (PHMSA) shows 
17 
that corrosion is a leading cause of pipeline 
18 
failures, and that unprotected steel is 
19 
typically the pipeline material that is most 
20 
susceptible to corrosion. 
21 
Q. 
What other benefits are associated with the 
22 
removal of LPP? 
23 
A. 
Removing LPP should reduce the number of active 
24 


--- Page 16 ---
Cases 19-G-0309 & 19-G-0310 
SPSP 
 
15 
leaks, lead to a decline in leakage rates on the 
1 
distribution system, and reduce the operating, 
2 
maintenance, and overtime costs associated with 
3 
surveying leaks and responding to emergency 
4 
reports. 
5

6

Q. 
How do the Companies prioritize the removal of 
19 
LPP? 
20 
A. 
The pipe to be removed from service is 
21 
identified and ranked using a risk assessment 
22 
model-based approach. 
23 
Q. 
What is a risk assessment model? 
24 


--- Page 17 ---
Cases 19-G-0309 & 19-G-0310 
SPSP 
 
16 
A. 
A risk assessment model prioritizes all segments 
1 
of LPP according to attributes that pose the 
2 
highest associated risk.  The model uses several 
3 
weighted factors, such as material type, 
4 
diameter, pressure, date of installation, and 
5 
leak history, to determine a ranking.  This 
6 
allows the Companies to provide a higher level 
7 
of public safety by focusing its efforts on 
8 
pipeline segments with higher risk.  Although 
9 
there are commonalities between the State’s 
10 
LDCs, it is important to note that the model has 
11 
been tailored to the Companies unique mix of 
12 
assets and may not be appropriate for other 
13 
LDCs.  A uniform approach cannot accommodate the 
14 
characteristics that must be considered for each 
15 
system. 
16

22 
addition to minimizing the time a customer is

without service.
