RESPONSE OF CONSOLIDATED EDISON COMPANY OF NEW YORK, INC., 
TO AARP NEW YORK’S PETITION FOR REHEARING 
 
 
On August 22, 2023, AARP New York (“AARP”) petitioned for rehearing of the Rate 
Order1 in these proceedings on the narrow factual ground that the Commission allegedly erred in 
concluding that Con Edison’s Energy Affordability Program (“EAP”) for low-income customers 
was under-enrolled by a significant number of customers. Because AARP has not identified any 
actual error of fact in the Rate Order, much less provided any competent evidence in support of 
its preferred policies, the Commission should deny AARP’s petition. 
Background 
Con Edison’s EAP provides discounts on electric and gas service to low-income 
customers in its service territory. Customers may participate in the EAP only if they are enrolled 
in one or more qualifying public assistance programs, including Medicaid. One way that Con 
Edison enrolls customers in the EAP is by working with the New York City Human Resources 
Administration (“HRA”) and the Westchester County Department of Social Services (“DSS”) to

Discussion 
Commission regulations allow a party to seek rehearing of an order “only on the grounds 
that the Commission committed an error of law or fact, or that new circumstances warrant a 
different determination.”12 The regulations further require the requesting party to “specifically 
explain and support each alleged error or new circumstance said to warrant rehearing.”13

In this case, AARP argues only that the Commission committed an “error of fact” by 
concluding that Con Edison’s EAP “low-income discount programs are not under enrolled by a 
significant number of customers.”14 In support, AARP contends that the Commission “erred” by

actual error of fact. Moreover, the alleged evidence that AARP trumpets is based only on

finally, AARP’s petition ignores Con Edison’s significant ongoing efforts to reach out to low-
income customers (especially Medicaid-only customers) about the EAP. 
I. 
AARP’S PETITION IS IMPROPER BECAUSE IT FAILS TO IDENTIFY ANY 
ALLEGED ERROR OF FACT BY THE COMMISSION. 
The Commission should deny AARP’s petition because it has not identified an error of 
fact in the Rate Order. 
AARP’s petition contends that “the Commission erred in concluding that Consolidated 
Edison’s Energy Affordability Policy (EAP) low-income discount programs are not under 
enrolled by a significant number of customers.”16 But the Commission never made this finding. 
On this issue, the Commission found only that “this record does not support PULP and AARP’s 
assertions regarding the level of customers that may be eligible and unenrolled in Con Edison’s 
EAP.”17 In other words, the Commission could not—and did not—say how many eligible

II. 
AARP HAS NOT PROVIDED ANY EVIDENCE OF THE NUMBER OF 
MEDICAID-ONLY CUSTOMERS NOT ENROLLED IN CON EDISON’S 
ENERGY AFFORDABILITY PROGRAM. 
The Commission should also deny AARP’s petition because AARP has failed to produce 
any evidence that a significant number of Medicaid-only customers are not enrolled in Con 
Edison’s EAP. 
As a threshold matter, Con Edison acknowledges that it is “possible,” as AARP’s witness 
testified, that some Medicaid-only customers who are eligible for the EAP are not enrolled in the 
program. The Company, however, has no data on how many such customers there are. More 
importantly, neither does AARP. Its contention that there are more than 100,000 stacks 
conjecture and supposition on top of nearly decade-old data. Without any concrete evidence, the 
Commission did not err in declining to adopt AARP’s demands for additional outreach. 
AARP’s analysis relies exclusively on data collected in March 2014. At the time, 
Medicaid was not a qualifying program for the Company’s electric EAP. In its 2014 rate order, 
the Commission directed the Company to file a report with the number of “Medicaid eligible 
customers… served by Con Edison that are not already participants in the electric low-income 
program.”24 In response to requests from Con Edison, HRA and DSS determined how many

Conclusion 
The Company, the Commission, and AARP all share the same goal: having as many 
eligible customers as possible receive the benefits of enrollment in Con Edison’s EAP. All three 
also agree that robust outreach and education are crucial to achieving that goal. The only 
difference in opinion is over whether Con Edison’s ongoing efforts are up to that task. The 
Company submits they are. AARP has not identified any deficiencies in the Company’s outreach 
efforts, nor has it provided any evidence that Medicaid-only customers are under-enrolled to any 
significant degree. Absent such evidence, the Commission was correct to conclude that the JP’s 
provisions regarding the EAP are reasonable and in the public interest and to deny AARP’s 
request for further targeted outreach to Medicaid-only customers. Because the Commission did 
not commit an error of fact warranting rehearing, it should deny AARP’s petition.
