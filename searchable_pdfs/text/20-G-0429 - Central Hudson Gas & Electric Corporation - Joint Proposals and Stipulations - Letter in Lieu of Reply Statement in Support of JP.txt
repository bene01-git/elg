Dear Secretary Phillips: 
 
 
Pursuant to the Ruling on Schedule issued on September 1, 2021, by Administrative Law 
Judges Anthony Belsito and Michael Clarke in the above-referenced proceedings, Central 
Hudson Gas & Electric Corporation (“Central Hudson” or the “Company”), by and through its 
undersigned counsel, hereby submits this letter in lieu of a Reply Statement in Support of the 
Joint Proposal (“Letter Reply Statement”).1    
 
Given the unique and challenging circumstances surrounding these rate proceedings, 
including the COVID-19 Pandemic and navigating the goals and objectives of the CLCPA, the 
Company is extremely pleased that after nearly seven months of active negotiations the Parties 
were able to reach a global settlement.  The Joint Proposal signifies a thoughtful compromise 
among the Parties and carefully balances various interests including, but not limited to, providing 
customers with financial assistance and relief in light of the COVID-19 Pandemic, furthering the 
CLCPA’s environmental goals, and achieving consistency with State and Commission policies 
while simultaneously providing the Company with the rate relief needed to continue providing 
safe, adequate and reliable service.

In addition, the Joint Proposal received broad support among normally adversarial 
parties, which is reflected by the numerous statements in support filed by a wide range of 
Parties.2  In fact, the sole opposition was filed by a single party representing an individual whose 
objections address only a few discrete provisions of the JP.3  The near universal support of the JP 
highlights the various compromises and concessions contained in the Joint Proposal and 
demonstrates that the JP has a rational basis and is within the reasonably expected range of 
potential litigated outcomes.   
Consistent with the Ruling on Schedule, this Letter Reply Statement is limited to 
responses to the following arguments and statements raised in the Parties’ initial statements 
regarding the Joint Proposal:4 
Geothermal Rate Impact Credit (“GRIC”):  The JP reasonably continues the GRIC, with 
modifications, until an alternative technology agnostic rate structure is developed or until 
December 31, 2025.  This provision is in the public interest and was supported by the 
Signatories, including parties representing environmental interests.  Contrary to Mr. Wyman’s 
statement, the JP’s treatment of the GRIC in no way results in unjust, unreasonable or 
discriminatory rates to heat pump customers.  In addition, the JP’s provision that the GRIC will 
be funded through the Heat Pump budgets authorized by the 2020 Energy Efficiency Order 
rather than the RDM is also rational because the purpose of the GRIC, i.e., to remove barriers 
limiting increased penetration of geothermal heat pumps in the Company’s service territory, is 
consistent with the intended use of the Heat Pump budget funds. 
The Company acknowledges Mr. Wyman’s disappointment that the JP’s treatment of the GRIC 
does not comport with his litigation position.  However, as with any settlement document based 
on multi-party compromise, each provision of the JP does not always reflect the interest of any 
single party and the JP’s treatment of the GRIC overall is rational, reasonable, supported by the 
record, and serves the public interest.  Mr. Wyman’s objection to the Joint Proposal’s treatment 
of the GRIC, therefore, is baseless and should be rejected.   
Depreciation:  The depreciation rates set forth in the JP reflect a compromise among the Parties 
and are supported by the record evidence, including empirical data (e.g., life tables, mortality 
studies and net salvage studies), and thus are reasonable - contrary to Mr. Wyman’s contention.  
In addition, Mr. Wyman’s assertion that the Company’s gas asset lives should be accelerated to

Leak Prone Pipes (“LPP”):  With respect to any criticism regarding the Joint Proposal’s terms 
regarding LPP,7 the Company maintains that both the 15-mile LPP elimination target as well as

public interest because they promote gas safety and system reliability.  Further, as LPP releases 
methane, its elimination is consistent with Commission policy and the CLCPA’s GHG reduction 
goals as the Commission recognized in the KEDLI-KEDNY Rate Order.8  This provision is 
therefore rational and should be adopted without modification. 
Arrears Management:  The Company recognizes the importance of helping customers 
financially impacted by the COVID-19 Pandemic, and the JP provides a framework for the 
Company to develop a plan for managing residential and small business arrears in the event the 
Commission does not issue broad or uniform guidance on this issue through a generic order or in 
a generic proceeding.  To that end, any suggestion that the JP does not adequately address 
customer arrears is groundless.9   
The Joint Proposal recognizes that the best path for resolving customer arrears issues is through a 
generic proceeding applicable to all utilities in the State where all stakeholders will have an 
opportunity to provide input and feedback.  Further, existing generic Commission proceedings, 
with collectively over 30 active parties, are already examining issues related to energy 
affordability and the COVID-19 Pandemic’s impact on utility service.10  It would be premature 
to commence an arrears management plan specifically for Central Hudson that could be 
inconsistent or incongruous with a future generic Commission order.  Accordingly, the JP
