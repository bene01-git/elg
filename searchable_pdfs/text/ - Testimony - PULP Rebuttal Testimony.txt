Credit Card Payment Fees  
2 
Q.  
Please summarize the Companies’ proposal relating to the elimination of processing 
3 
fees on individual customers.  
4 
A. 
In National Grid’s Downstate Customer Service Panel Testimony, the Companies propose 
5 
to implement a “no-fee” model for credit and debit card payments.7 Their proposal is to 
6 
include the estimated cost to recover the fees incurred by KEDNY and KEDLI when 
7 
residential customers make CC/DC payments in rates rather than charging a fee each time 
8 
a customer makes a credit or debit card payment.8 The Companies explained that their 
9 
proposal comes in response to customer surveys indicating a preference for payments with 
10 
no fee.9 The Companies also believe transitioning to a no-fee model will benefit customers 
11 
who receive public assistance benefits via pre-paid debit cards, as under the no-fee model, 
12 
these customers will no longer be required to use a portion of their benefits to pay the 
13 
administrative fee that is currently incurred.10  
14 
Q. 
What was Staff’s position on the Companies’ proposal to implement a no-fee model?  
15 
A. 
Staff’s Consumer Panel testified that they were not in favor of the Companies’ proposal.11 
16 
Staff recommended that the Commission deny the proposal because they are concerned 
17 
about a likely increase in transaction volumes and associated fees, and removing the fee by 
18 
socializing the cost into the rate base would decrease transparency as well as remove the 
19 
incentive for customers to provide feedback that could encourage further negotiation with 
20 
card processors.12 Staff did not comment on the Companies’ belief that a no-fee model will 
21 
benefit customers who receive public assistance benefits via pre-paid debit cards.

Site Investigation and Remediation Costs  
1 
KEDNY SIR Recovery Surcharge Mechanism  
2 
Q.  
Please describe the KEDNY Site Investigation and Remediation (SIR) Recovery 
3 
Surcharge Mechanism.  
4 
A.  
The SIR Surcharge Recovery Mechanism was first ordered in KEDNY’s 2016 rate case 
5 
(16-G-0058). It is a mechanism by which the Company can recover – from ratepayers – 
6 
clean-up costs related to the Gowanus Canal and the Newtown Creek Superfund sites as 
7 
they are incurred. The mechanism includes a cap on any single year recovery of 2% of the 
8 
Companies’ prior year aggregate revenue.  
9 
Q.  
Has Staff stated a position on the KEDNY SIR Recovery Surcharge Mechanism?  
10 
A.  
Yes. The Staff SIR Panel stated that “the SIR Recovery Surcharge Mechanism would 
11 
enable KEDNY to appropriately recover significant incremental costs associated with [the 
12 
Gowanus Canal and Newtown Creek Superfund] sites in a manner that would not 
13 
unreasonably impact ratepayers."17  
14 
Q.   
Do you agree with Staff’s statement that the Mechanism “would not unreasonably 
15 
impact ratepayers”?  
16 
A.  
No, not as proposed.  
17 
Q.  
Why not?  
18 
A.  
It is unreasonable to expect KEDNY’s customers to potentially have to pay 100% of the 
19 
Company’s share of the clean-up costs of Newtown Creek and the Gowanus Canal.  
20 
Q.  
Is there an Earnings Share Mechanism (ESM) in the Companies’ most recent Joint 
21 
Proposal that provides for some form of environmental clean-up cost sharing between 
22 
ratepayers and shareholders?

Yes, but this ESM only goes into effect if KEDNY’s ROE exceeds 9.8 percent – an amount

2

10 
environmental costs (Account 182.3) recoverable from ratepayers by any such 
11 
amounts, and;  
12 
2. In the event that these proceedings result in a multi-year settlement that includes an 
13 
ESM;  
14

15 
• Shareholders and ratepayers share any earnings derived from actual returns in 
16

17 
• Both KEDNY’s share and the customer share of any ESM adopted in these 
18 
proceedings be used to pay down Deferred Environmental Costs.
