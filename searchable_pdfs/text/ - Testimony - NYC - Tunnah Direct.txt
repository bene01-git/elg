STREETLIGHT REGISTRY 
1 
Q. 
WHAT IS THE STREETLIGHT REGISTRY? 
2 
A. 
There are hundreds of thousands of streetlight and traffic signal poles 
3 
throughout New York City, and every pole has a discrete identifier.  That 
4 
identifier allows both Con Edison and DOT to track the poles and identify 
5 
the poles when work is needed, whether due to scheduled maintenance, a 
6 
crash, or other purpose.  The Registry is essentially a list of every pole and 
7 
its identifier. 
8 
Q. 
ARE THERE OTHER USES OF THE REGISTRY? 
9 
A. 
Yes.  As I explained previously, DOT is charged for the electricity used by 
10 
its streetlights based on the wattage of the bulbs and the hours of operation.  
11 
Another important element of the calculation is the number of bulbs of each 
12 
wattage.  That information is taken from the Registry. 
13 
 
 
Additionally, when either DOT or Con Edison is planning to work 
14 
on a particular pole and needs to coordinate with the other party, or one 
15 
party needs the other party to do something before it starts work, both 
16 
parties need to know which pole or poles are at issue.  It is not sufficient to 
17 
simply refer to the street or avenue, or to provide a street and cross street, 
18 
because there likely are multiple poles on each block.  The Registry is 
19 
intended to allow for both parties to pinpoint individual poles. 
20 
Q. 
HOW IS THE REGISTRY SUPPOSED TO WORK? 
21 


--- Page 18 ---
Case 25-E-0072 
FELICIA TUNNAH 
18 
A. 
Both Con Edison and DOT have access to the Registry, and both parties are 
1 
supposed to update the Registry any time a new pole is added or replaced, 
2 
or attachments are added or removed, or bulbs are replaced with different 
3 
bulbs, or other actions occur.  The City and DOT are not placing blame on 
4 
anyone, but over time the accuracy of the Registry has diminished.  
5 
Q. 
ARE THERE OTHER PROBLEMS WITH THE REGISTRY? 
6 
A. 
Yes.  Geolocation systems did not exist, or were very rudimentary, when 
7 
the Registry was created.  Therefore, locations were often contextual.  That 
8 
is, one pole was identified based on its relative location to another pole or 
9 
some other landmark.  However, over time, some reference points have 
10 
either changed or been removed. 
11 
Q. 
WHAT DO YOU PROPOSE? 
12 
A. 
There is a need for a new Registry, and one which leverages modern 
13 
technology such as global positioning system devices to allow for very 
14 
precise geocoding.  DOT recognizes that this will be significant undertaking
