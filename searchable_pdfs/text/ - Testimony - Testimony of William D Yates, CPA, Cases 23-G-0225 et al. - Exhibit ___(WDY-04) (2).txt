SUBJECT:
HEFPA-compliant Deferred Payment Agreements 
Request: 
Unless otherwise specified, please provide responses separately for both KEDNY and KEDLI. 
Additionally, all references to "customers" are for residential customers only. 
Please provide an explanation of any changes that have been made to policies and procedures 
involving the issuance of HEFPA-compliant Deferred Payment Agreements ("DPAs") for both 
EAP and Non-EAP customers since January 1 2019. 
Response: 
Since January 1, 2019, the most significant process change for HEFPA-compliant DPAs (i.e., 
Standard DPAs and Minimum DPAs) was the addition of a “verbal consent” option for both 
KEDNY and KEDLI customers. This option allows customers to verbally consent to their DPA 
terms over the phone to activate their payment arrangement.  With verbal consent, active status 
customers can immediately activate their DPA; likewise, “Cut Out Non-Pay” status customers 
can activate their DPA and have a reconnect order issued at the time of verbal consent (provided 
the customer can pay their down payment over the phone). The customer will still receive the 
DPA terms via email (as well as regular mail when the DPA is activated). Verbal consent is 
typically only used for the Negotiated DPA (15% down and 10 installments) as Minimum DPAs 
require the customer to complete a financial statement to determine eligibility. If a financial 
statement is waived based on the customer receiving E-HEAP, verbal consent may be used when 
the Customer Service Representative is adding the Minimum DPA to the account. This process 
change enhances the customer experience and reduces the need for follow up from the customer 
prior to activating DPA. A verbally consented DPA is treated the exact same way as a DPA that 
is signed, during the life of the payment plan, and subsequently if the customer defaults.  Verbal 
consent started at KEDNY in October 2022 and KEDLI in February 2023. 
The Companies have also periodically updated their DPA procedures and guidelines to reflect 
minor clarifying language and updates to menu navigation specific to each jurisdiction.

disabled under HEFPA all declined from 2018 through the most recent twelve- month period, as 
shown in the Companies' response to I/R PULP-04. 
Response: 
The decline in participation in the Elderly, Blind & Disabled Program in KEDNY has followed a 
steady rate of decline since 2015, despite continued promotion and marketing of the program. The 
Companies are not aware of any particular reason for the decline in participation, but the decline 
could be attributable to changes in eligible populations and households. 
As for changes in the eligible populations, it would require reliable third-party data to determine 
whether the KEDNY service territory has seen a decline in households that are made up entirely
of elderly, blind, and/or disabled residents. Trends that might cause a decline in eligible 
households could include any increase in non-senior children living with elderly parents, or any 
increase in the rate of senior couples moving into care facilities.  
As for eligible households, the COVID pandemic may have suppressed participation due to the 
moratorium on field collections and service terminations. Arrears forgiveness programs such as 
RAS, ERAP and the NYS Arrears Forgiveness Program may have lessened the urgency for 
eligible households to participate.

communications pertaining to situations in which residential customers contacting the Company 
indicate that they have experienced or are currently experiencing domestic or intimate partner 
violence. 
Response: 
Consumer Advocates support the Companies’ most vulnerable customers by connecting them 
with programs and services offered by the Companies and service organizations within their 
communities. They assist customers who have special circumstances such as domestic violence. If 
a Customer Representative or Consumer Advocate receives a report of domestic violence, the 
Companies notify the police and the NY State Department of Social Services. There are no 
written procedures specifically for domestic violence.

HEAP Tip Sheet - NYC
 Summary 
We encourage all residential customers to apply for all the Home Energy Assistance Program (HEAP) benefits
they are eligible to receive during the HEAP Season regardless of whether they heat with National Grid.
Customers are eligible to receive the low-income program, the Energy Affordability Program (EAP), which is a
monthly credit with proof they receive HEAP with another utility. Customers must be income eligible to
receive HEAP, reference HEAP Income Eligibility Guidelines.
As soon as a HEAP payment is applied to a customers account, the account is automatically enrolled in the
low-income Energy Affordability Program (EAP).
Based on the level of income, and the amount of the HEAP payment received, qualified customers
are placed in an EAP tier which determines their monthly bill credit.
Customers enrolled in EAP with no past-due balance at the time of enrollment, will automatically be
enrolled in Budget Billing with an opt out option available.
Customers that receive a HEAP grant for another utility will be identified via a file match from Human
Resource Administration (HRA).
It is important to understand how HEAP works because we want to avoid giving customers false hope about
receiving HEAP on their bill in cases where they may not be eligible.
Customers should always be directed to continue paying their bills as normal to avoid possible service
interruption. For customers who have already applied for HEAP and are looking to check the status of
their grant - refer to the Already Applied for HEAP tab for more information.

Program Information
HEAP Basic Program (Opens November 1, 2022)
The Home Energy Assistance Program (HEAP) is available to income eligible (low income) customers to help
them pay for their home heating costs.
The HEAP program is a federal grant and does NOT have to be paid back.
If customers are told they cannot get HEAP because they have an outstanding DSS loan that is false and
a National Grid Consumer Advocate can assist.
You don't need to be behind on your account or have an account balance to apply for Regular HEAP!
Your household may be eligible for a Regular benefit:
Have gross household income that falls within the HEAP guidelines, or
Receives assistance from SNAP / Food Stamps, Temporary Assistance or Code A Supplemental Security
Income (SSI Living Alone)
Have household members that are US Citizens or qualified aliens
Add-on benefits are provided to:
Customers with a vulnerable household member - under 6, age 60 or over or permanently disabled $35
If household income is in the Tier 1 range - $41
Emergency HEAP Program  (Opens January 3, 2023)
You do need to have an emergency to qualify for Emergency HEAP - meaning you have to meet the HEAP
qualifications and have a utility shut-off notice or be currently shut-off.
DO NOT Place a Hold on the Customers account, a hold in cases of E-HEAP will not indicate there is an
emergency need to the counties when the Customer applies for the assistance.
Your household may be eligible for an Emergency benefit:
The electric is necessary for the heating system or thermostat to work and is either shut-off or in threat of
shut off.
The electric or natural gas heat is shut-off or scheduled to be shut-off
If a customer is out of fuel, or they have less than one quarter of a tank of fuel, kerosene or propane, or
have less than a 10-day supply of wood, wood pellets, corn or other deliverable heat source 
And
Household income is at or below the current income guidelines or they receive Family Assistance, Safety
Net Assistance, SNAP/Food Stamps or Code A Supplemental Security Income (SSI)
The heating and/or electric bill is in the customers name
Household available resources are
less than $2,000 if no member of the household is age 60 or older; or

National Grid (KEDNY KEDLI) 
Request: 
Do the Companies currently have any additional cold weather protections in place other than those 
in HEFPA that they must comply with? If so, please explain. 
Response: 
The Companies voluntarily comply with additional cold weather protections other than those in 
HEFPA. Annually, National Grid has agreed to additional protections requested by the Department 
of Public Services (PSC), local service agencies, and other groups to the following voluntary 
winter measures regarding terminations for non-payment, including: 
• For the period from November 1 through April 15, the Companies will accept all Home
Energy Assistance Program (HEAP) payments and provide the customer with continued or
restored service regardless of the amount due and/or the customer’s payment status.  The
Companies’ Customer Advocate outreach programs will continue with focus on HEAP, fuel
funds, and the Energy Affordability Program.
• During the Cold Weather Period, the Companies will consider any Regular and/or
Emergency HEAP payment as entitling the applicant to a fair and reasonable Deferred
Payment Agreement, regardless of any previous DPA defaults.
• The Companies refrain from scheduling residential service terminations on days when the
local weather forecast predicts below-freezing temperatures.
• The Companies establish a voluntary moratorium on winter terminations for customers who
are elderly, blind or disabled.

responsibility related to the account.

violence to our website. The intent is to inform survivors of the law and make them aware that 
National Grid has options available to work with them in their unique circumstances.
