I. 
INTRODUCTION 
On July 31, 2023, Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corporation ("Central Hudson" or 
"Company") filed with the Public Service Commission ("Commission") a request to revise its 
tariffs along with supporting testimony and exhibits ("Initial Filing").  By way of its Initial Filing, 
Central Hudson requests revenue increases of $144.5 million for its electric business and $43.4 
million for its natural gas business.  The Company's revenue increase requests are inclusive of a 
proposed return on equity ("ROE") of 9.80 percent for both its electric and natural gas businesses.       
Walmart Inc. ("Walmart"), by its attorneys, respectfully submits this Initial Brief to the 
Commission.  Walmart actively participated in this proceeding and caused to be admitted into the 
evidentiary record the Direct Testimony and seven Exhibits of Alex J. Kronauer, Walmart's Senior 
Manager, Utility Partnerships ("Walmart Kronauer Direct") and the Direct Testimony and one 
Exhibit of Lisa V. Perry, Walmart's Director, Regulatory-Utility Partnerships ("Walmart Perry 
Direct").

E. 
Electric Revenue Allocation and Rate Design 
Walmart advocates that rates be set based on the utility's cost of service for each rate class, 
as doing so produces equitable rates that reflect cost causation, sends proper price signals, and 
minimizes price distortions.  Walmart advocates for rates to be set based on cost causation on both 
an interclass and intraclass basis.  In that regard, while Walmart does not oppose the Company's 
proposed revenue allocation methodology in this proceeding, Walmart does have a 
recommendation to modify the rate increase for specific charges within the Secondary Demand 
and Primary Demand rate schedules.    
1. 
Revenue Allocation 
Walmart does not oppose the Company's proposal to move classes towards cost of service 
with rate mitigation.  Using the results from its COSSs, the Company aims to bring the rate of 
return for each customer class to within 15 percent of the total system average rate of return.28  To 
mitigate impacts on customer classes outside of that band, the Company proposes to apply the 
following limitations: (1) for classes currently earning less than 85 percent of the system average 
rate of return, the overall increase was limited to 1.25 times the overall system increase; and (2) 
the overall increase for classes currently earning more than 115 percent of the system average rate 
of return is limited to 0.75 times the overall system increase.29  Walmart does not oppose the 
Company's proposed revenue allocation banding methodology.30

2. 
Rate Design 
Walmart primarily takes electric service on the Company's Secondary Demand rate with 
some facilities served on the Primary Demand rate.31  Those two rates are comprised of a fixed 
$/month customer charge, a $/kWh energy delivery charge, and a $/kW demand charge.32  The 
Company is proposing to maintain that structure and Walmart does not oppose that rate structure 
for Secondary Demand or Primary Demand rates.   
While Walmart does not oppose the structure of those rates, it does recommend 
adjustments to the increases of the individual charges.  When determining how costs are incurred 
and then allocated to customers, the Company first assigns costs to the portion of the Company's 
system that created the relevant cost (production, transmission, or distribution), a process called 
functionalization.33  Within each function, the Company then classifies each cost as either demand-
related, energy-related, or customer-related.34  The Company's rate structure for Secondary 
Demand and Primary Demand do not align with how customer, demand, and energy charges are 
incurred for customers.35
Certain customers on Second Demand and Primary Demand, including Walmart, take only 
delivery service from the Company.  Delivery service costs are typically classified as demand-
related costs since such costs do not vary with the amount of electricity provided.36  In order to 
align recovery of those costs with how they are incurred, delivery service charges should be

XV. 
CONCLUSION 
The Commission should thoroughly and carefully consider the impact on customers when 
examining the overall revenue requirement that it may approve for Central Hudson in these 
proceedings, which includes the impact of the overall ROE that it may authorize.  Regarding the 
ROE, the evidence presented in this case does not support approval of an ROE that would 
significantly exceed the ROEs approved by this Commission for similarly situated utilities.  As a 
retail electric and natural gas consumer with a national presence, Walmart submits that the 
Commission should approve an ROE for Central Hudson that more closely reflects the prevailing 
trend for distribution-only electric utilities and natural gas utilities throughout the country.
Therefore, Walmart respectfully recommends that the Commission reject Central Hudson's request 
for a 9.80 percent ROE and instead approve an ROE that is consistent with ROEs awarded to 
similar utilities nationwide and in New York.  This would be consistent with the actions taken by 
this Commission and other state regulatory commissions for utilities like Central Hudson.
