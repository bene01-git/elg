 Benefits Analyses 
10 
Q.    
What are the components of the two benefit analyses?  
11 
A. 
There are two components to the benefits value analysis.  The first component is the 
12 
employee benefits design analysis, which compared the design features of the 
13 
Companies’ benefits programs (e.g., health plan co-payments, deductibles, and 
14

15 
companies.  The second component is the benefit design value analysis which includes a 
16 
pay-weighted assessment of the program features based on salary (e.g., pension benefit 
17 
accrual formulas, savings plan company match percentages and the definition of covered 
18 
pay).   
19 
Q.    
Please explain how annual benefit design values were utilized in the Study.  
20 
A. 
The Companies’ annual benefit design value was measured against the annual benefit 
21 
design value of the 2021 Peer Group companies to compare how compensation-based

benefit programs affect the total value of the benefits packages.  If, for example, an 
1 
employee at Company A earns more pay than an employee at Company B in the same 
2 
position, then the value of the savings plan company match (e.g., 5% of pay) to the 
3 
employee at Company A will be higher.  The employee benefit analysis performed in this 
4 
manner allows for a more accurate comparison of the value of a benefits package than an 
5 
analysis that is performed on a pay-neutral basis. 
6 
Q.    
What process was used to benchmark the benefit designs of the Companies’ benefits 
7 
programs? 
8 
A. 
The benchmarking of employee benefits design was done using Aon’s Benefit Index© 
9 
(“Benefit Index”).  The Benefit Index is a premier tool for comparing the relative worth 
10 
of one company’s benefits programs to those offered by a group of other companies.  It 
11 
has been used by companies since the 1970s to make such assessments. 
12 
Q.    
How were benefit design competitiveness assessments made? 
13 
A. 
Benefit Index results are reached using a very specific process.  Actuarial techniques 
14 
measure the total value a representative population of employees would derive from the 
15 
Companies’ benefits program and the benefits programs of each of the 2021 Peer Group 
16 
companies.  All retirement income, death, disability, health, and paid time-off benefits 
17 
offered to employees are included.  This actuarial analysis reflects the benefits that each 
18
