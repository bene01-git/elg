Q. 
What is the role of underground storage in satisfying customer 
16 
requirements? 
17 
A. 
Approximately 31 percent of the Companies’ normal winter supply obligation and 
18 
28 percent of their design day demand requirement are met by deliveries of gas 
19 
withdrawn from storage.  Under the Companies’ storage contracts, storage 
20 
deliverability typically declines as inventory decreases (known as “withdrawal 
21 
ratchets”).  Once reached, these ratchets cannot be reversed until the following 
22 
year.  Therefore, the Companies establish a storage withdrawal plan prior to the 
23 
winter season to maintain inventories at levels that allow sufficient storage 
24 
deliverability to meet forecast winter peak conditions (storage rule curve).    
25 
 
26 
 
Market area storage provides the Companies with services that cannot be easily 
27 
duplicated with other assets.  The most important attribute of storage assets is 
28 
flexibility, which is vital in serving changing customer requirements.  The 
29 
Companies’ Transco storage service contracts provide end-of-day balancing that 
30 


--- Page 11 ---
Testimony of Elizabeth D. Arangio 
 
Page 9 of 45 
 
minimize the risk of over/under run penalties.  Whereas typical supply purchases 
1 
must be dispatched at the same volume for each day of a weekend or holiday, 
2 
storage allows enhanced flexibility by allowing intraday adjustments to meet 
3 
changing conditions.  In addition, storage improves the load factor of flowing 
4 
pipeline assets and is critical in meeting design weather conditions.  This value 
5 
cannot be duplicated by replacing storage with Marcellus production.  The 
6 
Companies utilize a least-cost dispatch to fill storage.  Where Marcellus shale 
7 
supplies present an opportunity to displace long haul supplies for storage refill, 
8 
the Companies take advantage of the economically priced supply. 
9 
 
10 
The Companies also have approximately 20 billion cubic feet (“Bcf”) of Gulf 
11 
Coast storage capacity.  These storage fields are made available to ESCOs as part 
12 
of the optional Gulf Coast storage release that occurs every April.  Assuming no 
13 
ESCOs opt-in for the Gulf Coast storage release, approximately 4.5 Bcf of the 
14 
total capacity is reserved for force majeure purposes.  The remaining 15 Bcf of 
15 
capacity, usually the entire KEDNY Washington Storage Service (“WSS”) field, 
16 
is utilized for off-system sales (“OSS”).  If all ESCOs opt-in, the capacity utilized 
17 
for OSS would be reduced accordingly.

IV. 
Portfolio Management 
1 
Q. 
Please describe the Companies’ gas supply planning goals.   
2 
A. 
The Companies’ primary gas supply planning goals are to: 
3 
(i) 
Dispatch the gas supply portfolio assets under a least-cost strategy to 
4 
reliably meet projected primary firm demand; 
5 
(ii) 
Maintain a diverse portfolio of gas supply, storage, and transportation 
6 
capacity contracts with varying terms and pricing provisions; and 
7 
(iii) 
Implement a formal hedging program to mitigate price volatility. 
8 
 
9 
These goals are consistent with the Commission’s “Statement of Policy Regarding 
10 
Gas Purchasing Practices” issued in Case 97-G-0600 and updated by letter issued 
11 
March 31, 2011.  The Companies maintain a portfolio that meets requirements

VII. 
Gas Cost Volatility Management 
8 
Q. 
What steps do the Companies take to mitigate the impact of gas cost 
9 
volatility on their customers? 
10 
A. 
The Companies mitigate volatility in the gas commodity markets in several ways.  
11 
First, they maintain a balanced portfolio that includes contract storage.  This 
12 
allows the Companies to inject gas during the summer for withdrawal during the 
13 
winter, which enables the Companies to mitigate price volatility during the winter 
14 
when demand is greatest.  Second, the Companies maintain a geographically 
15 
diverse gas supply portfolio that helps to reduce exposure to volatility in any 
16 
single supply region.  Third, the portfolio incorporates pricing diversity that 
17 
minimizes exposure to volatility at a single pricing point or market index.  
18 
Finally, the Companies mitigate price volatility with a formal hedging program. 
19 
 
20 
Q. 
Please describe the Companies’ hedging program.  
21 
A. 
The Companies’ overall pricing diversity and volatility mitigation plan is to 
22 
protect prices for approximately 50 percent, but no more than 60 percent, of 
23 


--- Page 39 ---
Testimony of Elizabeth D. Arangio 
 
Page 37 of 45 
 
forecast winter firm sales and to allow prices to float with the market for the 
1 
remaining forecast winter sales.  For this purpose, the “winter period” is defined 
2 
as November through March.  Prices are fixed through a combination of planned 
3 
storage withdrawals, which provide a natural hedge at the average price of 
4 
summer period injections, and financial hedging using NYMEX gas futures 
5 
contracts or NYMEX Over the Counter (“OTC”) financial settled swaps and 
6 
options using bilateral master agreements.  Beginning in the 2015-2016 winter, 
7 
the Companies are also using basis hedges for forecast purchases in the Northeast 
8 
producing region.  Locational basis swaps in conjunction with NYMEX hedges 
9 
are being used because of an observed disconnect between prices in the Marcellus 
10 
and Utica supply basins and NYMEX prices.  The use of these locational basis 
11 
swaps ensures that the Companies are mitigating volatility in the markets where 
12 
they purchase supplies. 
13 
 
14 
To determine the quantity to be hedged, the Companies forecast firm sales for 
15 
each month November through March, assuming normal winter weather 
16 
conditions, and multiply the results by 50 percent (Step 1).  Next, monthly storage 
17 
withdrawals to meet system operational needs are forecast and subtracted from 
18 
the result obtained in Step 1 (Step 2).  The results from Step 2 equal the quantity 
19 
of gas to be financially hedged to achieve the 50 percent target in each month.   
20 
 
21 
Once the volume of gas to be financially hedged each month is known, a monthly 
22 
hedging plan is created.  The purchases are spread evenly over 16 months starting 
23

2

3 
gain is included in the gas cost for that month.

19
