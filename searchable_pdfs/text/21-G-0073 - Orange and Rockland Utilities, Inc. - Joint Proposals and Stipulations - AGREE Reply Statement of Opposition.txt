1. Introduction
Alliance for a Green Economy (“AGREE”) offers this short reply to Department of Public Service
Staff’s (“Staff”) characterization of the Joint Proposal’s compliance with the Climate Leadership
and Community Protection Act.
2. Reply to Staff
A. CLCPA Compliance
Staff’s statement of support for the Joint Proposal makes unsubstantiated claims about the Joint
Proposal’s compliance with the CLCPA and erodes the clear meaning of the statute.
Staff correctly notes that state agencies must “consider whether [their] decisions are
inconsistent with or will interfere with the attainment of the statewide greenhouse gas emissions
limits established in [ECL §75-0107]” and that “where a decision is found to
be inconsistent with the GHG emissions limits, an agency must ‘provide a detailed statement of
justification as to why such limits/criteria may not be met, and identify alternatives or
greenhouse gas mitigation measures to be required where such project is located.’”
Staff also correctly observes that “complete CLCPA regulations have not yet been promulgated,”
an issue that we, too, pointed out in our statement of opposition when we called on the


--- Page 4 ---
Commission to provide clearer guidance and regulations for utilities to follow so that Section 7 of
the CLCPA can be met.
Staff then cites recent Commission rulings on the applicability of CLCPA to rate proceedings.
Most importantly, Staff points to the the Commission's order on the gas rates for The Brooklyn
Union Gas Company d/b/a National Grid NY (“KEDNY”) and KeySpan Gas East Corp. d/b/a
National Grid (“KEDLI”), in which the Commission found that §§7(2) and (3) applied to rate
proceedings, which are “other administrative approvals” under the statute. Staff writes:
“Concerning the substance of the joint proposal in those cases, the Commission
determined that the requirements of the CLCPA do not exist in a vacuum and must be
interpreted consistently with the Commission’s statutory duty to ensure safe and
adequate service. In this context, the Commission found the joint proposal to be
consistent with the CLCPA even though some proposed capital projects would meet
increased peak customer demand for gas because these projects were needed to
ensure continued safe, reliable, and adequate service to ratepayers.”
Staff goes on to claim that “under this framework, the Joint Proposal in these cases clearly
meets the requirements of the CLCPA,” using the commitments the Company has made to
reduce gas sales, increase energy efficiency and demand response programs, make use of
non-gas non-pipe alternatives (“NPAs”) for some LPP replacements and customer connections,
and the promotion of geothermal and electric heating options to new customers. The Staff
incorrectly claims that “Orange and Rockland will eliminate all promotional and marketing
programs related to the expansion of gas service and its future energy efficiency marketing will
encourage electrification and not mention any environmental benefits of natural gas.”
These marketing commitments were made by National Grid Upstate, but Orange & Rockland’s
commitments were less aggressive, a point we made in our statement of opposition when we
argued that the Commission should push O&R to go further in its CLCPA alignment. While the
Company committed to ending marketing for oil-to-gas conversions, it did not make any broader
commitments to end more general gas marketing -- for instance to new buildings -- nor did it
make any commitments around how it would or would not market gas as environmentally
friendly.

Staff claims that “the Company’s LPP program will also contribute to the CLCPA’s goals, most
directly by reducing the amount of gas escaping from the Company’s infrastructure, but also
through the Joint Proposal’s requirement to consider NPAs when replacing LPP.” While this may
be true, the Staff provides no greenhouse gas emissions analysis to support or quantify the
claim that leak prone pipe replacement will contribute to the CLCPA goals. We don’t necessarily
disagree that there may be some greenhouse gas emissions reductions associated with LPP
replacement; analysis filed in the National Grid case, first by AGREE and then later by National
Grid itself shows relatively small emissions reductions associated with LPP replacement.1 2
Further, unlike most other gas utilities that have had rate cases before this Commission, O&R
has not committed to pursuing a certain number of LPP replacements with NPAs and has not
put into place a robust NPA and BCA framework. We object to the reliance on LLP replacement
with additional gas infrastructure as a core strategy for CLCPA compliance, given the lack of any
analysis showing the greenhouse gas impacts of such a strategy.
Without any analysis, it is premature for Staff to claim definitively that “the Joint Proposal in
these cases clearly meets the requirements of the CLCPA.” It is true that the Commission
approved the Joint Proposal in the KEDNY/KEDLI case without an emissions analysis, but the
Commission also noted in its order that the record for that case was largely built before the
CLPCA went into effect and that no party filed a motion to require the utilities in that case to
supplement the record.3 In this O&R case, the Company filed its proposal well after the CLCPA
was in effect, but before the Commission ruled in KEDNY/KEDLI that CLCPA Section 7 is
applicable to rate cases. This puts this case in somewhat of a grey area for what CLPCA
compliance should look like. However, we call the Commission’s attention to the fact that
National Grid Upstate, in a case with similar timing to this O&R case, that company did file some
greenhouse gas emissions analysis with its statement of support for its Joint Proposal. Though
not an ideal time for such a filing, and though the filing was far from robust, it did demonstrate

that such an analysis can be done in rather short order and that that company did attempt to
comply with the new standard that the Commission had set.
We sincerely hope that the Commission will not erode the clear meaning of Section 7 of the
CLCPA by allowing parties to make unsubstantiated claims about greenhouse gas reductions or
by failing to require utilities from filing emissions analyses with their initial filings and with any
Joint Proposals that are reached in future rate cases. Now that the Commission has clearly
stated that Section 7 does apply to rate cases, going forward, the Commission should clarify for
future rate cases coming up in 2022 what will be expected for the development of a complete
record including a full greenhouse gas assessment of the Company’s current operations,
proposed projects, and proposed emissions reduction strategies to support the required Section
7 findings.
In this case, we have noted that while the Company has taken the important step of committing
to reducing gas sales -- a major aspect of CLCPA compliance -- the gas sales reductions
contemplated will need to be dramatically scaled upward in future years to reach the goals that
the Climate Action Council is coalescing around. In order to hit the bar of CLCPA compliance,
the Company needs to do more in the next three years to set itself up for that rapid
decarbonization, namely: stop relying on false solutions that are unproven, more aggressively
change its marketing behavior to try to avoid new gas hookups, and commit to trying several
non-pipe alternative projects so that it can learn how to retire segments of its gas system in the
future.
Without putting these building blocks in place -- blocks that other utilities have committed to and
that do not interfere in any way with other laws the Commission and utilities must comply with --
CLCPA compliance is jeopardized because three years of delay may well make reaching the
2030 mandates impossible or very costly to reach. The Staff uses the ongoing CLCPA process
as an argument for vague action now. We believe that same lengthy process is an argument for
why the Commission cannot and should not wait for the Climate Action Council (“CAC”) to
conclude its process before ensuring early and aggressive steps are taken to meet the
greenhouse gas emissions mandates defined by the CLCPA. As we outlined in our initial
statement of opposition, the CAC analysis is already clear that millions of buildings must be
converted to heat pumps in less than a decade. Failure to act on this information now will
interfere with the state’s ability to comply in the future.

3. Conclusion
For the reasons stated above, we call on the Commission not to erode the clear meaning of
Section 7 of the CLCPA or to upend its own standards of evidence and the development of a
record in rate cases. The Commission should require Orange & Rockland to take more
aggressive steps to set itself up to aggressively reduce emissions in future years by bringing
their NPA and marketing standards up to par with recent other Joint Proposals. Further, the
Commission should clarify that the standard of evidence for CLCPA compliance set in
KEDNY/KEDLI was unique, due to the timing of that case, and that going forward, utilities will be
required to accompany their rate case filings with greenhouse gas emissions analysis, starting
in 2022.
