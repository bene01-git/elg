I. 
INTRODUCTION 
On June 14, 2023, New York State Electric & Gas Corporation ("NYSEG") and Rochester 
Gas and Electric Corporation ("RG&E") (collectively, "Companies") filed with the New York 
State Public Service Commission ("PSC" or "Commission") in the above-referenced proceeding 
an executed Joint Proposal memorializing the settlement achieved between multiple parties.  
Walmart Inc. ("Walmart") actively participated in the settlement negotiations and is a signatory to 
the Joint Proposal as it pertains to the above-captioned electric base and gas base rate proceedings 
initiated by the Companies.  Walmart hereby respectfully submits this Statement in Support of the 
Joint Proposal.

Although Walmart did not address, and therefore does not take a position on, every

reasonable, and fair resolution of the issues in the electric and natural gas cases.  Walmart believes 
the Joint Proposal is in the best interest of the Companies, its customers, and the New York public.  
Accordingly, and for the following reasons specific to the issues raised by Walmart in the course 
of these proceedings, Walmart respectfully requests that the Joint Proposal be approved without 
modification.

General Service – Time-of-Use Rates ("SC 8");5 (6) the revenue requirement and rate design for

The Companies originally requested an authorized ROE of 10.2 percent for all components 
of their operations.  In response to this request, Walmart submitted testimony noting concerns that 
the Companies' requested ROE was counter to recent ROEs awarded both in New York and 
nationwide and that the ROE determination would have an impact on the Companies' customers.8
After many months of settlement discussions addressing extensive issues, the settling parties 
agreed to a 9.20 percent ROE, which is considerably lower than the Companies' original proposal.9
Walmart believes that a 9.20 percent ROE reflects a significant compromise between the parties 
and provides the Companies the opportunity to earn a fair return while still protecting customers' 
expectations of safe and reliable service at just and reasonable rates.       
Regarding electric rate design, the Companies' originally proposed rate design for 
NYSEG's SC 7-110 would gradually (but not fully) move the SC 7-1 rate closer to cost of service 
by increasing the customer charge to $271/month, which represented an increase of 25 percent.  
The Companies' marginal cost of service study results, however, supported a customer charge of 
$528.23/month.  Walmart recognized the Companies' proposal to increase the customer charge 
would help move rates closer to cost of service and thus did not oppose the NYSEG's SC 7-1 rate

rates is revenue neutral, impacting only the SC 7-1 class without harming other classes.
