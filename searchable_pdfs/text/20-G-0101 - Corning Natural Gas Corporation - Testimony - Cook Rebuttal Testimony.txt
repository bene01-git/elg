Q. 
Does Corning agree with Staffs Leak Prone Pipe (LPP) replacement target 
recommendation? 
A. 
Yes. Corning agrees to establish the LPP target at 10.0 miles each calendar year, 2021 
through 2023. However, Corning would like it noted that the leak prone pipe should 
include Lines 11 and 15. 
Q. 
In its testimony, Staff discusses the leaks existing on piping traversing the flood 
control lands in the Corning area. Does Corning agree with Staff's proposal? 
A. 
Corning agrees that the leaks should be addressed through our leak prone pipe 
replacement program and has maintained that as our position since originally 
approached by the DEC. Many of these Type 3 leaks are in areas where we may not 
be allowed to perform direct replacement (i.e. new pipe placed parallel and adjacent to 
the existing pipe) but instead will be forced to place the new pipe onto municipal or 
private property. Doing so likely requires longer lengths of pipe and far greater 
expense than if we were able to install the new facility adjacent to the existing one. 
We have informed the DEC that any one of these projects would be a significant 
portion of our annual capital budget and as a result we may perform only one of these 
projects per year. Therefore, the leak repairs on flood control lands may take several 
years to complete. Corning has been and will continue to work with the DEC on these 
leak repairs. Corning appreciates being allowed to count the entire length of pipe 
installed on these leak repair installations within the flood control lands towards the

main replaced within these flood control areas. We understand that we will be

EMERGENCY RESPONSE 
Q 
Does Corning agree with Staffs proposal to increase the negative revenue adjustments 
(NRA) from six, four and two basis points to twelve, eight and five basis points for 
failure to meet the 30, 45, and sixty minute targets, respectively? 
A. 
Staff states that it is reasonable to increase these NRAs to incentivize Corning to avoid 
backsliding from its current performance. Corning disagrees with Staff's position that 
the NRAs should be increased and with the justification for the increase, i.e., as an 
incentive to prevent our backsliding. Corning wishes to maintain the NRAs at their 
current levels of six, four, and two basis points. Staff stated in its testimony (page 30, 
line 23) that historical data shows that Corning has constantly met the targets for this 
metric. We have shown no evidence of backsliding or missing our Emergency 
Response targets in the past. We are already incentivized to meet our targets and have 
done so. We believe penalizing us for consistently meeting a target is inappropriate.

THE 1.1 MILES OF PIPE LOCATED AT SULLWAN PARK SCIENCE CENTER

within 60 days of the filing date of Staff's direct testimony (i.e., by August 25, 2020).

A. 
Corning understands the importance of assuring the integrity of this natural gas system 
and will do what it must to achieve this objective. We have begun the necessary 
O&M work to assimilate this segment of pipe into our system. To date we have 
performed a leak survey over the entire system including bar holes in the ground at the 
service line entrance. We have initiated a complete locate and mark-out process in 
cooperation with Corning Incorporated of the 1.1 miles of pipe. This system will have 
CP readings taken and remediation as needed, this summer. The below-ground valves 
will be excavated by Corning Incorporated this summer/fall to assure operability. The 
basic system map provided by Corning Incorporated has been transferred to Corning 
Natural Gas' mapping. The mapping will be updated as system locating progresses.

MONTHLY LEAK REPORT 
Q. 
Pursuant to Case 05-G-1539, Corning has been required to submit a monthly Leak 
Repair Status Report to the Secretary. This report contains system leak survey status, 
quantity of leaks discovered, quantity of leaks repaired, and quantity of main and 
services replaced since last report. Although Staff did not address the subject of the 
monthly leak report in its testimony, do you have any recommendation regarding this 
report? 
A. 
Yes. Corning would like to discontinue sending this report to the Department of 
Public Service. This report was required by Safety Staff during a time in the 
Company's history when system maintenance and leak repairs were minimal and the 
number of leaks in the Corning system were much greater. None of these conditions 
exist today. Since the time this report was first required we have replaced more than 
60% of our leak prone pipe and almost 40% of our leak prone services, and we have 
repaired over 3,700 leaks. With the Company's current leak repair trajectory and 
facility replacement program, we will soon be maintaining a small to non-existent leak 
backlog. Accordingly, this report has become obsolete and should be discontinued.
