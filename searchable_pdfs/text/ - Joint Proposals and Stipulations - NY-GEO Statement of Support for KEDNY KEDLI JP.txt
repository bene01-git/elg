We have done so, but we need to start this letter by expressing our belief and grave 
concern that, relative to gas utilities, New York State is NOT currently on track to 
adequately contribute to meeting the Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act 
(CLCPA) legal mandates to reduce carbon emissions by 40% by 2030 and 85% by 
2050.  Fossil gas is currently the largest contributor to New York’s greenhouse gas 
emissions.  State policy over decades has created momentum behind gas use and 
expansion that can be compared to the momentum of an aircraft carrier.  The climate 
crisis has created a shoreline that looms too close for that momentum to be stopped for 
a crash to be avoided.   
 
We need all hands on deck to apply full brake and steering power to minimize the 
impact of this crash.  This includes New York’s citizens, its civic organizations, its 
utilities, its executive and legislative branches of government, and its Public Service 
Commission.  Rate cases such as this one take place within the context of policy and 
practice from the above institutions that has largely supported gas use and expansion.  
This context is starting to be turned around in some ways, but too slowly to allow 
adequate progress in rate cases such as this in relation to the CLCPA. 
 
That being said, NY-GEO believes that rate cases are an important real-world forum for 
making change.  It is here, as well as other Commission proceedings, where the “rubber 
hits the road” and changes of various sizes are actually implemented.  As such, we 
have worked with other parties in this case, including the companies and staff, to 
negotiate important changes that can be built upon and have signed on to the Joint 
Proposal in order assure acceptance and implementation of those changes.   
 
These include:

Reporting the Cost of the 100-Foot Subsidy: 
The 100-Foot Rule provides free infrastructure to new gas customers and clearly tilts 
the market playing field against alternatives such as ground and air source heat pumps.   
 
The Companies will file a report with the Secretary to the Commission, within nine 
months of the issuance of a Commission order adopting the terms of this Joint 
Proposal, regarding the cost of facilities (e.g., main and service lines for connecting 
customers) provided to new customers pursuant the Commission’s regulations and 
Companies’ tariffs and how all or a portion of those costs could be used to provide 
applicants with an incentive to select electric thermal options (e.g., heat pumps) 
rather than gas service. Such incentive would be in addition to federal and state 
incentives. The report could form the basis for a petition by any interested party to 
implement such a program, or could be utilized to discuss such a program in the 
next rate proceedings for the Companies.   Page 83 – section 7.57

NY-GEO believes the sum total of the advances listed above justifies signing on to this 
agreement.  However, our opening statement to this letter remains top of mind.  This 
Joint Proposal accomplishes many worthwhile things and sets up future positive 
development.  It however does not put us on track to meet the urgent climate mandates 
of the CLCPA.  We recommend the following to accompany Commission acceptance of 
the Joint Proposal: 
 
• The Commission to develop a recommendation to the Legislature to quickly wind 
down the 100-Foot Rule as a gas subsidy and perhaps replace it with a 
renewable heat subsidy.  In addition, the Commission to repeal regulatory 
additions it has made that extend past the letter of the law that created the 100-
Foot Subsidy. 
• The Commission to develop a recommendation to the Legislature to quickly wind 
down the obligation to serve language in the Public Service Law as it pertains 
solely to fossil gas.  Gas utilities need clear guidance on their future role in the 
context of the need to substantially and quickly reduce GHG emissions.  Will their

role be expanded to include renewable heat sources such as ground loops and 
how will they begin decapitalizing gas assets that will become stranded as gas 
use is substantially reduced? 
• The Commission to require each rate case to result in the creation or updating of 
a glide path to reach CLCPA goals that includes verifiable projections of CO2e 
reductions per year, MMBTU reductions in billed annual fossil gas usage, and 
reductions the numbers of customers heating with gas in residential, commercial 
and industrial classes.  
• The Legislature and Governor to take action to resolve the above issues through 
appropriate legislation  
• The Climate Action Council (CAC) and the Governor to make clear that the 
CAC’s upcoming scoping plan is NOT an excuse for inaction.  Too often 
initiatives for reducing carbon are rejected in policy circles and in rate cases by 
citing the need to wait for the CAC’s recommendations.  The Council needs to 
communicate the importance of stopping fossil gas expansion and reducing gas 
use wherever possible.  The Council also needs to lay out reasonable 
expectations for limits on the degree to which hydrogen and renewable natural 
gas can be expected to substitute for fossil gas.  This is key guidance needed to 
inform the degree to which gas infrastructure is likely to become a stranded 
asset.   
 
For the steps above that would be taken by the Commission, NY-GEO expected to see 
these points included in the white papers developed for the Gas Planning Proceeding.  
We request the Commission to order these issues to be included in that proceeding and 
worked through in that context.
