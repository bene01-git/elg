The Standard of Review 
 
The Public Service Commission has the statutory obligation to review, and approve (with 
or without modifications), or reject settlement agreements.  The Commission’s Settlement 
Guidelines, which provide the standards within which such an approval or disapproval must be 
analyzed, provides that the terms and conditions of a joint proposal must be just and reasonable 
and in the public interest.5 
While the Commission can be deferential to settlements agreed upon by normally 
adversarial parties, that deference is not without limits.  In addition to compliance with proper 
procedures, determining whether the terms of a joint proposal are in the public interest involves 
substantive consideration of the following: 
1. consistency with the law and regulatory, economic, social and environmental State and 
Commission policies;

II. 
THE JP MUST BE REJECTED AS IT IS NOT IN COMPLIANCE WITH THE 
MANDATES OF THE CLCPA IN REGARD TO GHS GAS REDUCTIONS 
 
The JP ostensibly “commits” the Company to achieve a “net-zero increase in bill gas 
usage” during the rate term, unless this “commitment” is “impacted by economic conditions and 
other factors beyond the Company’s control.”8  Such a “commitment” is patently insufficient 
under the CLCPA. 
The CLCPA, enacted in 2019, became effective on January 1, 2020.9  The Act mandates 
a 40 percent reduction in GHG emissions by 2030 and an 85 percent reduction in greenhouse gas 
(“GHG”) emissions by 2050.10 The 85 percent target is consistent with what the

The JP in this case does not set the Company on any such pathway: quite the opposite.  
First, as previously stated, it does not commit the Company to freezing billed gas usage, even 
assuming (which we dispute) that a freeze is sufficient under the CLCPA. This “commitment” is 
subject to the gaping loophole that “economic conditions and other factors beyond the 
Company’s control” may preclude the Company from achieving this target.18 Second, the JP 
makes no projection (and does not commit the Company to make this projection during the rate 
term) as to how much GHG emissions will increase or decrease as a result of the plans and 
programs contained in the JP, but leaves this assessment for the Company’s next rate filing.19 
Third, as further developed in the next section, the CLCPA Study defers any actions to reduce 
net GHG emissions until the next rate filing, and in any case, does not guarantee any reductions 
during that period.20 
Citizen Action does not propose a specific standard for judging the pace at which a 
regulated utility subject to the Commission’s jurisdiction must reduce GHG emissions.  But we 
respectfully submit that, in order for the state to have any chance of meeting the CLCPA targets, 
at a minimum, the JP should ensure that the Company make GHG emissions reductions during 
the current rate three-year rate period, and put the utility on a glidepath towards meaningful year-
by-year reductions to permit it to comply with the CLCPA by the first statutory target: 2030.  
Clearly, the JP does not meet this test: the JP gives the Company wide discretion as to what 
capital projects it undertakes, including those that impact gas supply.21  Further, the JP provides 
no guarantee of any net GHG reductions for the first three years of the roughly nine-year period 
within which the state must achieve a 40% reduction in GHG emissions.  The lack of any 
concrete mandates as to specific outcomes to address the climate crisis during the three-year rate 
period, or plan for future GHG emissions reductions after the current rate period does not 
constitute compliance with the CLCPA.   
The JP is therefore contrary to the public interest and should be rejected by the 
Commission in its present form.  In his decision denying an application for a Section 401 Water 
Quality Certification (“WQC”) for the proposed Northeast Supply Enhancement Project

III. 
THE LACK OF COMPLIANCE WITH THE CLCPA IN THE JP IS NOT 
CURED BY THE PROPOSAL TO DO A CLCPA STUDY 
 
The Parties signing the Joint Proposal, including the Company and the Department of 
Public Service staff (“Staff”) have included in the JP a requirement that a study be performed, 
“that evaluates how the Company’s businesses may evolve in the future to support the emission 
reduction and renewable energy goals of the CLCPA and any emission reduction goals the 
Company has independently adopted” (the “CLCPA Study” or “Study”).28   
The CLCPA Study in the JP does not cure the defect set forth in section II, namely that 
the JP does not set the Company on a pathway to contribute to CLCPA’s the mandated GHG 
reduction targets.  First, the Study, as already suggested, does not guarantee any future 
reductions in GHG emissions by the Company, it “evaluates how the Company’s businesses may 
evolve in the future” to support the CLCPA targets.  Second, no specific GHG emissions targets 
are specified in the JP, nor any methodology or standards set forth to assure the Commission that 
the Study will result in reductions significant enough to make a fair contribution to the CLCPA’s 
GHG reduction targets.29 Finally, although the JP has limited public input processes, such as a 
scoping meeting to obtain public input into the plan for the Study, ultimately, control rests with 
the Company: the Company has the discretion to select any needed consultants (with limited 
input by Staff and stakeholders on the “type of technical and/or consulting experience required to 
complete the study”) and is ultimately responsible for completing it.30 
 
IV. 
THE JP IS NOT IN COMPLIANCE WITH THE MANDATE ON THE 
COMMSSION IN CCLPA SECTION 7 TO DO A CLIMATE SCREEN  
 
The JP should also be rejected on the separate ground that section 7(2) of the CLCPA, 
sometimes known as the CLCPA “climate screen,” directs “all” state agencies, including the 
Commission, to consider whether decisions on “permits, licenses, and other administrative 
approvals and decisions” are “consistent with or will interfere with the attainment” of the 
greenhouse gas (GHG) reduction limits in the CLCPA. If the decision is inconsistent, section

VI. 
CONCLUSION 
 
The Joint Proposal should not be approved in its present form for the reasons articulated 
above.  The JP contains policies that are not in the public interest in that they are inconsistent 
with the CLCPA.  As detailed above, we recommend that the following actions be undertaken to 
modify the JP to address our objections: 
 
• The JP should mandate that the Company make GHG emissions reductions during the 
three-year rate period, and establish standards to ensure that Company is on a glidepath to 
fully comply with the CLCPA emissions reduction mandates by the first statutory target: 
2030.

rate term as to the impact of the JP on the GHG emissions reduction limits in the CLCPA,
