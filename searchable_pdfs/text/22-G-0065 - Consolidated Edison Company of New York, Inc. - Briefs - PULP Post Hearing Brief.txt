Introduction  
The Public Utility Law Project of New York, Inc. (“PULP”) submits this post-hearing 
brief in response to several topics discussed at the evidentiary hearing held on April 18, 2023. 
These topics include: Consolidated Edison’s (“Con Edison” or “Company”) noncommitment to 
direct any new outreach or education efforts to help low-income households and those located in 
disadvantaged communities (“DACs”) in light of significant rate increases; the lack of any 
additional relief that addresses the disproportionate burdens that DACs will face due to the 
increases in rates under the Joint Proposal (“JP”); the absence of additional support to remedy 
under-enrollment in the Company’s Energy Affordability Program (“EAP”), as identified by 
PULP; the Company’s lack of transparency in applying for certain federal funds made available; 
the lack of transparency behind the 9.25% return on equity (“ROE”); and procedural frustrations 
and concerns of PULP that stem from Earthjustice’s motion to strike, filed on July 15, 2022. 
PULP provides greater detail below. 
 
 
Background  
Con Edison filed its request for new rates on January 28, 2022. On May 20, 2022, PULP 
and other parties filed direct testimony and on June 17, 2022, PULP and other parties filed 
Rebuttal Testimony. On June 17, 2022, Con Edison filed with the parties and the Secretary to the 
Public Service Commission (“PSC” or “Commission”) a “Notice of Impending Settlement 
Negotiations”. Settlement negotiations began on July 6, 2022. Following negotiations, the 
Settling Parties filed a Joint Proposal on February 16, 2023. A procedural and technical 
conference was held virtually before the Administrative Law Judges (“ALJs”) on March 2, 2023. 
The ALJs then issued a “Ruling on Process and Schedule” on March 6, 2023. On March 29, 
2023, parties filed “Statements on the Joint Proposal.” On April 5, 2023, Reply Statements were 
due. An evidentiary hearing was held on April 18, 2023, and witnesses from Con Edison, the 
Department of Public Service (“DPS”), PULP, AARP of New York, and New York City (“the 
City”), were present for cross-examination.

Argument 
I. 
Con Edison has not sufficiently committed to provide any additional outreach 
and education efforts to low-income customers not already participating in the 
EAP that could help expedite their enrollment and offset some or all of the 
increase in rates. 
 
Parties supporting the Joint Proposal argued that outreach and education surrounding 
EAP are adequate, citing the numerous outreach undertakings Con Edison participated in 
throughout 2022.1 PULP has been supportive of past undertakings, as we hoped that they would 
maximize the number of customers eligible for the Phase I Electric and Gas Bill Relief credit by 
enrolling eligible customers in EAP. However, by December 2022, PULP estimates that there 
were still nearly 440,000 customers2 who could be enrolled in EAP that were not captured by 
Con Edison’s efforts. Moreover, Con Edison’s own Collections Activity Report shows that for 
March 2023, 410,321 residential accounts are behind on their bill by 60 days or more, for an 
arrearage total of $494,555,595.3 PULP remains concerned that residential customers in general 
will not be able to afford another rate increase.  
During the course of cross-examination, Con Edison attested that the Company would 
continue the outreach undertakings that took place in 2022, to their knowledge, in the first rate-
year and beyond.4  However, the Joint Proposal contains no commitment to expand upon the 
current efforts. While Signatory Parties point to the 2022-2023 Outreach and Education Plan 
(“O&E Plan”) filed by Con Edison on April 3, 2023, PULP remains concerned that the Company 
has not included their plans in the JP itself. The O&E Plan lists goals for programming, not 
commitments, and does not cover the entire term of the Rate Plan as it ends in December 2023 
(emphasis added). PULP advocated in its Statement of Opposition (“SOO”) that proactive, 
targeted outreach be conducted to customers whose account histories and interactions with the 
Company indicate a potential for EAP eligibility.5 PULP cited numerous indicia of utility 
unaffordability. The JP only states that internal controls over file matching will be improved

II. 
Home Energy Assistance Program (“HEAP”) Households without Con Edison 
heating accounts may still be eligible for EAP enrollment using their HEAP 
award, and their potential lack of enrollment is a great concern that must be 
addressed. 
  
Mr. William Yates, PULP’s Director of Research, prepared a number of responses to 
interrogatories from the City of New York that were entered into the record at the Evidentiary 
Hearing.9 These responses supported PULP’s assertion that approximately 440,000 low-income 
households are eligible but not currently enrolled in Con Edison’s EAP.10 Mr. Yates also 
provided verbal answers that support PULP’s claim that more needs to be done.  
The City sponsored Exhibit 615 in order to show different living situations where an 
individual may receive a HEAP grant but not be a Con Edison heating customer, including living 
situations where, “Eligible households that live in government subsidized housing or a group 
home setting with heat included in their rent” or “Eligible households who have heating costs 
included as part of their rent.”11 Mr. Yates indicated that it is possible that some HEAP recipients 
may not have a Con Edison heating account in their own name.12 During cross-examination and 
re-direct, Mr. Yates explained how there are some households that may not have Con Edison 
heating accounts, however, they may still in fact be Con Edison electric only customers and 
would be entitled to EAP benefits based on their receipt of a HEAP award.13 During re-direct, 
Mr. Yates mistakenly referred to submetered customers as having EAP eligibility but corrected 
himself during cross-examination by the City.14 At the time of cross-examination by Staff, Mr. 
Yates referred to these households where tenants do not have their own heating account as 
“master-metered” households. Though this term is understood generally to refer to customers 
who are sub-metered electric customers, the intention of Mr. Yates’ testimony on the record was 
for the purpose of indicating that tenants in a building where the sole heating account is in a 
building owner’s name and heat is included in rent, may have individual electric accounts

III. 
Con Edison’s Disadvantaged Communities Report Fails to Provide Immediate 
Benefits to help households keep their energy bills affordable. 
 
The DAC Report included in the JP will provide annual data and an annual stakeholder 
meeting related to the different programs that may provide benefits.16 However, PULP remains 
concerned that this is simply not enough. During the Evidentiary Hearing, PULP asked Con 
Edison to identify which of the programs included in the DACs Report would specifically 
address the increased financial burden that will occur from the rate increase.17 Con Edison could 
not identify any specific programs that would provide DACs with relief to offset the rate 
increase. Instead, the Company answered generally that there are, “programs available to all 
customers, to the extent that the impact low- and moderate-income customers, those can and/or 
may have a direct impact on the bills.”18 Both the Company and Staff are aware of the bill 
impacts under the JP, so the Company’s response is particularly troubling to PULP.   
 
IV. 
The JP Lacks Any Substantive Remedies for Addressing Disproportionate 
Energy Burdens on DACs. 
 
PULP remains concerned that neither the Company nor Department Staff appropriately 
address the increased and disproportionate energy burdens that households living in DACs will

identified during the Rate Plan, arguing that the question was speculation and requiring a legal 
opinion.20

Act”). New York’s Climate Act requires State Agencies, including DPS and the Commission, to

V. 
The Public Remains in the Dark About Con Edison’s Plans to Apply for Vital 
Federal Funds. 
 
The JP states that the Company will apply for federal funding for projects through the 
Smart Grid Grant and the Grid Resilience Programs under the federal Infrastructure Investment 
and Jobs Act. It further provides that, if the Company’s application is successful, customers will 
receive a sur-credit equal to the amount of federal funding received once the projects are 
completed. The Signatory Parties have argued that this arrangement, absent any further 
disclosures from the Company regarding the nature of the projects themselves or the sur-credit 
that customers could stand to receive, is reasonable. However, considering the significant rate 
increases included in the JP, Con Edison has a duty to its customers to be as transparent as 
possible regarding its applications for federal funding given that a successful application will 
have a measurable beneficial impact on future customer bills.

regarding Con Edison’s applications for federal funding were, “fair and reasonable” because, 
“the Company has researched the opportunities to obtain federal funding and developed a

VI. 
The 9.25% Return on Equity is Not Transparent to the Public and it Follows a 
Long, Concerning History in Rate Cases that Must be Addressed and Changed.  
 
PULP remains concerned that the 9.25% ROE will have significant bill impacts on Con 
Edison customers. Moreover, in PULP’s cross-examination during the Evidentiary Hearing and 
in our filings in the rate cases, we demonstrated how the “decades-old approach” for establishing 
the ROE lacks public transparency and accountability.24 PULP conducted research and offered 
an alternative ROE proposal in our SOO, which we believe could have saved ratepayers,“ up to 
an estimated $1.8 billion in unnecessary, excessive delivery charges since the 2010 rate year.”25 
Versus the proposed multi-year settlement, PULP’s analysis demonstrates ratepayers could 
receive significant financial benefits by having an annual adjustment or “refresh” to the ROE.26 
Although this proposal appeared in PULP’s SOO and our Reply Statement, neither the Company 
nor the Department Staff conducted any discovery to learn more about the proposal, which has, 
in our opinion, the ability to save ratepayers significant costs over the rate plan. In fact, the 
Company admitted at the Evidentiary Hearing that they did not conduct any discovery on 
PULP’s proposal.27

questions about the ROE process to learn how Signatory Parties moved away from their

troubling precedent, essentially leaving Motions to Strike powerless in the context of Testimony 
and JPs. As a result, PULP raises our concern herein and respectfully requests that the 
Commission remand the ALJ’s April 18th decision for the purposes of hearing the Motion on its 
substance.
