Another problem is bulk billings; the process of sending a single bill for several 
1 
accounts that can cover periods that range from 45 days to over 365 days. When a customer 
2 
receives a bulk consolidated bill for multiple months, it is difficult to allocate monthly, on 
3 
an account level basis, usage, and demand (that have been billed together). Among other 
4 
problems, this can lead to incorrect budget projections. For example, a large NY 
5 
commercial real estate company’s natural gas account received estimated bills for many 
6 
months and then was bulk billed for April 5, 2021, to August 2, 2021. 
7 
 
To address these issues, Con Edison should be required to provide detailed records 
8 
of its use of estimated meter readings and the Commission should adopt a penalty 
9 
mechanism that would incent Con Edison to reduce the frequency and duration of estimated 
10 
billing. In addition, Con Edison should reconcile its usage estimates with its own metered 
11 
data and with data provided by its customers. Con Edison should report that information to 
12 
the Department of Public Service on at least a quarterly basis. Disparities between 
13 
estimated and actual meter readings of more than 5% should trigger a penalty payment by 
14 
Con Edison to the customer equivalent to 10% of the cash disparity whether Con Edison 
15 
has either overestimated or underestimated consumption. Con Edison should be motivated 
16 
to ensure accurate metering of its products and such penalties should provide such 
17 
motivation.
