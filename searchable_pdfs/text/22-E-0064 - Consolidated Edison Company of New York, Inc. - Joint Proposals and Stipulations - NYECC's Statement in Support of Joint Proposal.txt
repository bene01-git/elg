NYECC's STATEMENT IN SUPPORT OF THE JOINT PROPOSAL 
 
Dear Judges Moreno and Costello: 
 
Pursuant to your Ruling on Process and Schedule, Issued March 6, 2023, in the above referenced 
proceedings, please consider this letter as the Statement in Support of the Joint Proposal ("JP") 
on behalf of the New York Energy Consumers Council, Inc. ("NYECC") due by March 29, 
2023. The NYECC and its predecessor organizations1 have represented the energy interests of 
commercial property owners and managers in New York City before State agencies for nearly 70 
years. NYECC also continues to represent hospitals, colleges, governmental agencies, cultural 
and financial institutions, housing cooperatives, and real estate organizations. NYECC's 
membership includes landmark member properties such as One Bryant Park, PENN 1, 345 Park 
Ave., 7 World Trade Center and Rockefeller Center.

NYECC is a signatory to the JP in these proceedings, which covers a three year Con Edison 
electric and gas rate plan from January 1, 2023 through December 31 , 2025, inclusive.  
 
By this letter statement in support, the NYECC respectfully requests, that your Honors 
recommend to the Commission adoption of the JP in the above referenced proceeding as agreed 
to by the party signatories and filed with the Commission's Secretary on February 16, 2023. 
NYECC believes that the provisions of the JP are in the best interest of the public in general and 
of the signatories in particular because of the significant negotiated reductions by the Active 
Parties from the amounts originally sought by the Company over the three year Rate Plan as well 
as other provisions in the JP which reflect negotiated compromise.  
 
Among the salient features of the JP include the following: 
 
• A revenue requirement increase in the electric and gas rate cases for each of the three rate 
years which is significantly less than Con Edison’s initial and preliminary update asks:

Accordingly, ratepayers in both rate plans will pay far less under the revenue requirements in the 
three-year rate plans in the JP than under the original or updated amounts previously sought by 
Con Edison.  
 
• For electric and gas, the JP provides for the following customer favorable sharing mechanisms 
as between customers and the Company when the following Return on Equity ("ROE") 
thresholds are attained or exceeded:  
 
Greater than 9.75% to less than 10.25% ROE- equal sharing between the customers and the 
Company;

• The JP also contains an 86%/14% customer/company sharing mechanism of benefits obtained 
for tax refunds and reductions attained through Company efforts.  
 
• The JP provides for the electric and gas revenue requirements including a 1% labor-
productivity adjustment from the end of the Historic Test Year through RY1 and a 1.5% labor-
productivity adjustment for RY2 and RY3.   
 
• In RY1, the JP provides for annual investment in capital projects and programs by the 
Company of approximately $2.85 billion in electric and approximately $1.12 billion in gas. In 
RY2, the JP provides for annual investment in capital projects and programs by the Company of 
approximately $2.88 billion in electric and approximately $1.12 billion in gas. In RY3, the JP 
provides for annual investment in capital projects and programs by the Company of 
approximately $2.8 billion in electric and approximately $1.1 billion in gas.   
 
• The JP provides for numerous Company performance metrics designed to measure various 
activities that are applicable to the Company’s Electric, Gas, AMI, and Customer Service 
Operations, and assess negative and/or positive revenue adjustments where performance targets 
are not met or are exceeded, including the creation of the Estimated and Delayed Billing Metrics 
with a Negative Revenue Adjustment for residential and commercial metrics measuring the 
percentage of customer bills that have been estimated or delayed longer than 125 days as of the 
end of each calendar quarter, which is an issue of importance to NYECC and numerous other 
stakeholders. 
 
• Under the JP, the Company is working with high tension customers to help protect customer 
equipment by providing power quality information, including, but not limited to, voltage interval 
readings at customer connection points. This information can be used by high tension customers 
to understand the voltage level they are receiving from the Company at the meter. The Company 
will strive to begin providing the power quality information on January 1, 2024. The information 
will be used to help develop the tool for sharing voltage data with high tension customers. 45 
days prior to increasing CVO in a network beyond the prior CVO peak, the Company will send a 
written notice to all high-tension customers fed by that network. If the Company receives 
notification from any of these customers within the 45 day period that customer work is required 
to prepare the customer equipment for the voltage change, the Company will delay the planned 
additional voltage optimization for the network until the customer has made necessary changes 
to its equipment, up to three months from the original notification date. The Company can 
change CVO levels up or down depending on circumstances, and is only required to provide 
prior notice to high tension customers: (1) if it is increasing the CVO level beyond the prior 
CVO peak level or (2) making a "permanent" change to the existing CVO level. 
 
• Regarding the Company’s requirement to provide data to building owners under the City of 
New York Local Laws 84 and 97, the JP contains a provision in which the Company will 
facilitate building owners’ compliance by taking certain steps enumerated in the JP, including,

but not limited to, redesigning its software architecture, conducting quality assurance and quality 
control activities on a statistically significant portion of the data set each year to confirm its 
completeness and accuracy, and providing an annual informational report to the Commission 
within 60 days of the end of each rate year. 
 
• The three-year electric rate plan revenue allocation in the JP also includes compromise on the 
phasing in (approximately $6.8 million per year) of the NYPA deficiency as reflected in Con 
Edison's embedded cost of service study.  
 
• The JP extends the Business Incentive Rate ("BIR") application period during the term of the 
rate plan and updates the Biomedical Research Program by extending by two years the term for 
BIR rate reductions for existing customers and for new customer applications through December 
31, 2025 applying a 12 year term, which will revert back to a 10 year term after January 1, 2026. 
 
• The JP contains new provisions to the Monthly Adjustment Clause (MAC) and the NYPA OTH 
Statement section in the PASNY Tariff to credit customers for the revenue requirement impact of 
any federal funding received under the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act once the 
underlying project is in-service. 
• The JP modifies MAC component 39 and the NYPA OTH Statement section in the PASNY 
Tariff to indicate that, in addition to charges, the Company may receive refunds from PJM 
Interconnection L.L.C. related to its former 1,000 MW firm transmission service agreement and 
credit customers for such refunds, which is possible under pending federal litigation. 
 
• The JP includes a provision that costs incurred by the Company for implementation of new 
Non-Wires Alternative (ones that are not included in base rates) during the Electric Rate Plan, 
including the overall pre-tax rate of return on such costs, will be recovered over ten years. 
 
• The JP includes a provision that costs incurred by the Company for implementation of new 
Non-Pipeline Alternatives (i.e., those that are not included in base rates) during the Gas Rate 
Plan, including the overall pre-tax rate of return on such costs, will be recovered as a regulatory 
asset over twenty years. 
 
• The JP includes a provision that the Company will implement a price guarantee for residential 
customers commencing billing for the first time under SC 1 Rate IV during the term of the rate 
plan. 
 
• The JP includes a provision that the Company will exclude from costs chargeable to the major 
storm reserve an amount equal to two (2) percent of the costs incurred (net of insurance and 
other recoveries) due to the occurrence of a major storm. 
 
• The JP includes a provision that the Company is subject to a $350,000 per event deductible for 
Pre-Staging and Mobilization Costs (i.e., up to $350,000 per event will not be chargeable to the 
major storm reserve). 
 
• The JP includes a provision that the Company’s gas rate design reflects a 10-year phase-out of 
declining block rates in SC 2 and SC 3.

• The JP provides that during the term of the Gas Rate Plan, the Company will file annual reports 
with the Secretary under Case 22-G-0065 to provide information on the interruptible discount for 
each rate year. The report, to be filed by May 31 of each year, beginning May 31, 2023, will 
include, inter alia, information on the number of interruptible customers and customers 
switching from interruptible service to firm service and customers switching from firm service to 
interruptible service. 
 
• During the term of the Electric Rate Plan, Con Edison will implement two front of the meter 
energy storage projects, Freshkills in Staten Island and Glendale in Queens. Both will provide 
peak shaving, distribution and substation contingency support, voltage support, renewable 
energy support, and participate in the wholesale market. The Queens project will also contribute 
to the reliability and resiliency of the distribution system serving disadvantaged neighborhoods 
in Maspeth, Queens. 
 
• The JP contains a pilot program authorized at $75 million over the term of the Electric Rate 
Plan to identify and prioritize sections of Con Edison’s overhead distribution system, where 
customers frequently experience outages caused by severe weather, for undergrounding. 
 
• Under the JP, the Company will implement a pilot program designed to allow for the 
procurement of certified gas (a/k/a differentiated gas), i.e. gas produced which mitigates methane 
emissions, during the rate period, limited to an annual cost above traditional supplies of 
$800,000 per year. The Company will file an annual report each May with the Secretary under 
Case 22-G-0065, describing the progress of the pilot to date and review the report with Staff 
during regularly scheduled meetings on gas supply issues. 
 
• The JP contains provisions supportive of and in furtherance of the objectives of the Climate 
Leadership and Community Protection Act. 
 
Based upon the atypically extended and extensive period of negotiations and discussions among 
the Active Parties and Con Edison, held over a period of more than seven months in these cases, 
and after weighing the probable costs of further litigation, the probability of success, the 
litigation's complexity, further attendant expense, inconvenience and delay, NYECC believes 
that the JP confers upon the public and the signatories in particular significantly greater benefits 
than costs and falls within the expected range of the fully litigated case and therefore the JP 
should be approved.  
 
Finally, NYECC commends the hard, tenacious, and thoughtful work of all of the Active Parties 
in this proceeding and of the Department of Public Service Staff in particular, without whose 
efforts the filed JP would not have been possible.  
 
Accordingly, the NYECC respectfully requests that your Honors recommend to the Commission 
that the Joint Proposal be adopted as filed.
