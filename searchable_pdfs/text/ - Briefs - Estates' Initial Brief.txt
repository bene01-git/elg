VIII. REVENUE ALLOCATION AND RATE DESIGN ISSUES 
D. 
Bill Impacts.   Estates NY’s building stock in New York City (“NYC”) is 
primarily comprised of rent stabilized apartments regulated by the New York 
State Division of Housing and Community Renewal (“DHCR”). For that reason, a 
high proportion of Estates’ tenants are low- and moderate-income residents.  The 
higher natural gas rates proposed by the Company in Case 19-G-0309 will burden 
each property, creating upward pressure on rents for those residents who do not 
reside in rent stabilized apartments. The Company’s rate increases have typically 
outpaced rent rate increases, reducing funds necessary to maintain or upgrade 
buildings in the Portfolio and provide resident amenities or services. Importantly, 
in order to achieve necessary carbon emissions reductions from this building 
stock, Estates and its peers must make substantial capital investments over the 
coming decade. These investments would be reduced in the event of significant 
increases in operating expenses of which the Company’s proposed increases in 
the costs of natural gas would be a significant portion.  
The Company is proposing an increase in base distribution rates of over 30% in 
the tail block for the SC-3 customer class. The overall increase in proposed bills 
to the typical SC-3 customer is 11%. customer is 11%.  This large increase in base

E. Demand Response. 
 
Gas demand response an effective means of helping the State and City to reach its 
goals for maximizing the use of the existing gas distribution system and reducing 
the overall amount of fossil fuels consumed. In that regard, the Company’s pilot 
gas demand response program should be significantly expanded to help address 
the capacity issues and enable the State, City and the Company to reach their 
respective goals. 
 
Gas demand response permits the Company to maximize its gas network to bring 
additional commodity gas to its Customers, thereby offsetting carbon intensive 
liquid fossil fuels and reducing carbon emissions. A gas demand response 
program incentivizes Customers to limit usage at times of capacity constraints via 
direct payments to participating Customers which incentivize them to limit gas 
use generally and especially during such periods. This, in effect, monetizes timely 
responses during system constraint periods.  Customers may utilize electrification 
and gas-to-electric switching as a means of achieving demand response goals, 
which not only helps to alleviate capacity limitation issues but also reduces 
carbon emissions.  Peak period gas consumption, is often driven by morning 
domestic hot water (“DHW”) demand layered on top of space heating demand; 
DHW should be an appropriate target for electrification or partial electrification in 
the near term.

In its Testimony, Staff approves of the Company’s demand response program but 
opposes additional FTEs be approved for the DR project.  The Companies do not 
agree with Staff’s recommendation and urge the addition of dedicated FTEs.2  
Estates supports these additional FTEs to administer and expand the Gas Demand 
Response and implement and administer the Green Gas Tariff requested by the 
Future of Heat Panel in its testimony in this Case.3 
 
In light of above considerations, enhancing or incentivizing gas demand response 
via electrification is an effective means of achieving decarbonization goals, 
reducing overall costs to gas ratepayers, maximizing gas network efficiency and 
utilization, improving local air quality, and fostering a market for the 
development of new methods or technologies.  We also urge the Company to 
expand its existing pilot gas demand response program and ultimately make the 
program permanent. 
 
Electrification would assist in avoiding expanding the existing, capacity-
constrained natural gas network.   One favorable method of managing 
decarbonization by electrification is expanded use of electric domestic hot water 
equipment. Specifically, as noted above, natural gas demand for the sole purpose 
of producing domestic hot water leads to network capacity constraints during 
periods of cold weather. This is caused by the peaking nature of domestic hot 
water demand in the morning coinciding with likely high natural gas demand 
response periods.  
  
We acknowledge that when carbon-producing fossil fuels including natural gas 
are used to generate electricity associated power generation emissions partially 
reduce decarbonization effectiveness. We also acknowledge that such 
electrification may necessitate the construction of additional generation plants and

G. Geothermal. 
 
The development and implementation of geothermal NPA-eligible heating 
systems helps Estates and the New York State energy marketplace via energy 
efficiency improvements, carbon emissions reduction and network resiliency. 
 
Geothermal technology offers highly energy efficient production of domestic hot 
water and space heating. The technology, particularly shared loop geothermal 
energy systems, if widely implemented, would play a significant role in

reinforcing the Company’s ability to meet decarbonization goals via heating 
electrification and reducing peak gas capacity.   
 
Without addressing potential legal steps necessary for the Company to engage a 
line of business producing, distributing and selling geothermal energy, we support 
the Company’s development of alternative revenue sources with an affiliated 
thermal company selling thermal energy as a means of offsetting possible losses 
incurred by an overall reduction in fossil-based natural gas consumption.  
Alternatively, or concurrently, the production and sale of geothermal energy 
would also provide private businesses with opportunities resulting in job creation 
in New York State. Utility management of thermal distribution networks would 
provide a platform for new and vastly increased private thermal energy generation 
activity. Geothermal technology helps Estates meet regulatory requirements in 
that it provides a cost-effective low- to zero-carbon source of heat and remains a 
technologically feasible alternative, or supplement, for the wholesale 
electrification of large multifamily and commercial buildings. 
 
A thermal company would build, maintains and manages shared infrastructure 
(which would include locations in public rights of way and on private property) 
that provides a heating and cooling medium for water source heat pumps or 
similar technologies. A thermal company’s distribution facility would most likely 
be comprised of a horizontal water pipe network, heat exchangers, pumps and 
pumping stations, and physical connections to customers and heat sources or 
sinks. The thermal company, would provide a platform for sharing thermal energy 
across various customers, and might offer the possibility of establishing a bi-
direction thermal distribution system similar to the bi-directionality of the electric 
distribution network envisioned by New York States Renewing the Energy Vision 
(“REV”) plan.

J. Hydrogen Blending Study.  
Hydrogen and RNG blending would help to achieve decarbonization goals by 
further moving natural gas toward a regime harmonized with the REV framework.  
 
Reducing the carbon intensity of the natural gas supply via hydrogen blending is a 
possible solution to avoiding the substantial waste associated with the 
abandonment of any portion of the natural gas infrastructure (which infrastructure 
has been increasingly adopted in recent years by Customers) which would be a 
desirable outcome of State decarbonization efforts. Such solutions should also 
include the use of renewable hydrogen sources such as renewable power to gas, 
agricultural, dairy or solid waste industry-sourced gaseous fuels like biomethane.    
 
Q. 
Consistency of Rate Design and Revenue Allocation with Climate 
Leadership and Community Protection Act. 
 
Recent legislation includes the Climate Leadership and Community Protection 
Act (2019 N.Y. SB 6599), the New York Independent Service Operator’s 
(“NYISO”) IPPTF Carbon Pricing Proposal, NYC Local Law 97 of 2019 together 
with and additional efforts to modify New York Stated Energy Research and 
Development Authority (“NYSERDA”) or utility-managed incentive programs to 
further buildings’ energy decarbonization and efficiency.  Compliance with 
statutes, regulations and orders relating to these initiatives will require large 
capital expenditures at properties such as those in the Portfolio to modify or 
replace building equipment.  
 
It is important to bear in mind that property owners from smaller multi-family 
residential to large commercial have substantial sunk costs related to natural gas 
infrastructure and heating plant equipment which burns natural gas as a primary 
fuel.  The full lifecycle costs that Customers will face include substantial costs in 
connection with decarbonization.  In addition, customers unable to comply with
