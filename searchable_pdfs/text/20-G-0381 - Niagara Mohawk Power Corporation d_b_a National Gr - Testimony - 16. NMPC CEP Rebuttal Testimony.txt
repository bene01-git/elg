Q. 
Please address Staff’s recommendation (SESP at 33-34) for a downward-only 
1 
reconciliation for EE spend at end of the Rate Year and that the Company be allowed 
2 
to carry unspent funds from year to year in the event of a multi-year settlement.  
3 
A. 
A downward-only reconciliation is not appropriate for the EE spend in light of the 
4 
flexibilities already afforded to EE spend in the January 2020 NENY Order.  On page 69 
5 
of the January 2020 NENY Order, the Commission continues the practice of allowing 
6 
“utilities to shift budgets from year to year within a portfolio to accommodate the timing 
7 
concerns that arise with commitments and outlays and to avoid artificial program stops 
8 
and starts.”  Appendix A of the January 2020 NENY Order further approves budgets and 
9 
energy savings targets respectively for the electric and gas EE portfolios for the period 
10 
2021 to 2025 in total.  The Company understands this flexibility allows the Company to 
11 
pull forward funds from future years within the 2021-2025 period as long as it does not 
12 
spend more than the total budget authorized for all five years.  With this, a downward-
13 
only reconciliation at the end of the Rate Year, or a multi-year settlement that does not 
14 
extend to December 31, 2025, would be more restrictive than the current authorizations 
15 
afforded to the EE spend in the January 2020 NENY Order.  
16

To be clearer:  The Company’s Gas Peak Reduction metric would measure the difference 
1 
between the highest observed daily consumption by firm customers during the winter, 
2 
defined as November to March, and the summer, defined as April to October.  The 
3 
difference between these two readings represents the increase in consumption associated 
4 
with temperature, which can be considered as a reasonable proxy for the heating 
5 
requirements of the Company’s customers.  Because the temperature on which the peak 
6 
winter day occurs can vary, the metric would divide the difference described above by the 
7 
heating degree day (“HDD”) measurement for the day on which the winter peak was 
8 
recorded.  This would help to account for variation and, assuming that the increase in 
9 
consumption is correlated with an increase in temperature, help to evaluate change in the 
10 
metric year over year. 
11 
 
12 
The general formula used to calculate the metric is as follows: 
13 
[winter peak consumption (Dth) - summer peak consumption (Dth)] ÷ winter peak heating 
14 
degree day (HDD) 
15 
 
16 
The daily consumption would be measured of an average of all sendout at the main West 
17 
and East pooling areas, netting out interruptible gas consumption within the Company’s 
18 
service territory. 
19 
 
20 
It is worth noting that it is the Company’s intent to largely mirror the methodology 
21 
employed by Con Edison in the Joint Proposal submitted on October 18, 2019, in Cases
