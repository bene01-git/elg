B. 
Use of Excess Depreciation Reserve as a Rate Modifier 
1 
Q. 
PLEASE DISCUSS THE ISSUE OF EXCESS DEPRECIATION RESERVE. 
2 
A. 
In general, a Book Depreciation Reserve account is maintained for each plant account and 
3 
reflects a running balance of the depreciation related items related to a plant account. Each 
4 
year, annual depreciation expense plus proceeds of gross salvage when utility property is 
5 
retired are positive additions to the Book Reserve account, while the value of the plant that 
6 
was retired and the cost of removal are negative changes to the Book Reserve account. The 
7 
Theoretical Reserve is a calculated amount of the reserve balance which should have been 
8 
accumulated for the plant existing at the time of the calculation and assuming the current 
9 
depreciation rates had been in effect over the whole life of the utility plant in service at the 
10 
time of the calculation. In actuality, variations occur between what was assumed would be 
11 
recovered through depreciation (i.e., the “Theoretical Reserve”) and what is actually 
12 
booked to the various accounts (i.e., the “Book Reserve”). If the Book Reserve is greater 
13 
than the Theoretical Reserve, the difference is known as an Excess Depreciation Reserve 
14 
(“EDR”) and indicates that ratepayers provided the utility too much in depreciation 
15 
expense in the past. Conversely, if the difference between the Book Reserve and 
16 
Theoretical Reserve is negative, this difference is known as a Depreciation Reserve 
17 
Shortfall and implies that ratepayers provided the utility too little in depreciation expense 
18 
in the past. As utility plant can typically depreciate over fifty (50) years or more, slight 
19 
differences between the Book Reserve and Theoretical Reserve are expected, but material 
20 
deviations warrant an adjustment in setting rates. In New York, when the difference is 
21 


--- Page 16 ---
Page 14 
greater than 10% in total plant, a corrective adjustment is often made to reduce or eliminate 
1 
a Reserve excess or shortfall.24
