BACKGROUND 
 
 
The Commission initially approved the Companies’ Firm 
DR Programs in the October 15, 2020 Order Directing 
Implementation Plan Filing, Annual Reporting, and Tariff 
Modifications in Cases 20-G-0086 and 20-G-0087 for KEDNY and 
KEDLI, and in the October 13, 2022 Order Authorizing Tariff 
Amendments Regarding Gas Demand Response Programs for Firm Gas 
Customers in Case 20-G-0381 for NMPC (Implementation Orders).  
In the Implementation Orders, the Commission directed the 
Companies to file Annual Reports following the end of each Firm 
DR Program season, and alongside those Annual Reports to request 
authorization for improvements to the Firm DR Programs as 
needed.2  KEDNY, KEDLI, and NMPC each operate three common 
programs: the Load Shedding Program, the Load Shifting Program, 
and the Bring Your Own Thermostat (BYOT) Program.  In addition 
to the three common programs, KEDNY and KEDLI also operate a 
Behavioral Demand Response Program.

industrial, and multi-family firm service customers capable of 
reducing their peak gas load for up to an eight-hour period on 
Event days.  Though focused on daily load reductions, the Load 
Shedding DR Program event windows correspond to the Companies’ 
typical system peak hours to ensure that the peak day load

Both the Load Shedding and Load Shifting Programs use a 
baselining methodology to measure participant performance during 
Events.  This baselining methodology compares each participant’s 
gas usage during Event days to the participant’s gas usage 
during a number of recent similar days, plus a weather 
adjustment to account for differences in weather between the 
Event day and the comparison days.  The number of days used to 
determine the baseline differs between whether the Event day 
falls on a weekday or weekend day.  For weekday Events, the 
Companies compare each participant’s average gas usage during 
the Event period to the average of the participant’s five 
highest usage days within 10 days of the Event.  For weekend or 
holiday Events, the Companies compare each participant’s average 
gas usage during the Event period to the average of the 
participant’s four highest usage weekend or holiday days within 
the last six weekend or holiday days.  The Companies base the 
weather adjustment on a comparison of the number of heating 
degree days (HDD) during the Event day relative to the weekday 
or weekend/holiday days used to establish the baseline.  The 
Companies cap the weather adjustment at 15 percent of the 
unadjusted baseline.5

Baseline Methodology 
 
 
The Companies propose four modifications to the 
baseline methodology they use to calculate a participant’s 
performance during Events.  First, the Companies propose to 
raise the upward Baseline Weather Adjustment cap from 15 percent 
to 35 percent.  The Companies explain that the 2022/2023 winter 
capability period was the first season that DR events were held 
during weekends or holidays, and that several events were 
characterized by a period of mild temperatures followed by a 
rapid drop in temperature.  The Companies state that the 
2022/2023 winter capability period revealed that events are not 
always preceded by days with cold enough temperatures to provide 
a reasonable indicator of cold weather utilization.  The 
Companies note that the 15 percent cap on weather adjustments 
during the 2022/2023 winter period caused performance calculated 
during some events to be artificially low, and propose a higher 
weather adjustment cap of 35 percent to establish a more 
accurate baseline. 
 
 
Second, the Companies propose to omit instances of 
zero consumption captured in the Unadjusted Baseline.  The 
Companies note that some customers predominantly use alternate 
heating sources while temperatures are mild, and only use gas 
for heating when temperatures fall below a certain threshold, 
resulting instances where a baseline might include days of zero 
consumption if the weather before an event was mild.  In the

DISCUSION AND CONCLUSION 
 
 
The Firm DR Programs are an integral part of the 
Companies’ system planning in future years, and align well with 
both longstanding Commission policy to make the best and most 
effective use of existing infrastructure possible as well as 
more recent goals and requirements under the Climate Leadership 
and Community Protection Act.  The reduction in peak-day and 
peak-hour gas usage from existing customers improves the gas 
utility’s ability to maintain system reliability during peak 
winter heating events, and allows the utilities to continue 
meeting customer energy needs while simultaneously reducing the 
need to build additional infrastructure.  While both bulk and 
distribution-level electric demand response programs are 
commonplace, and have been in place in place throughout New York 
for nearly a decade, gas demand response programs for firm 
customers remain in their infancy.  The Companies’ Firm DR 
Programs are among the first of their kind, and represent an 
innovative way of addressing both gas supply and distribution 
constraints affecting the NMPC East Gate service area and 
helping to meet KEDNY and KEDLI’s well-documented supply 
constraints in their respective New York City and Long Island 
service areas.

As shown in their annual reports, KEDNY and KEDLI have

Because there is little firm customer gas demand 
response program experience to draw on, either from New York or 
other States, the Companies’ Firm DR Programs are expected to 
continue to operate in a “test and see” regime for the 
foreseeable future.  To that end, the Commission expects that 
the Companies will continue to design their Firm DR Programs to 
operate cost effectively, but recognizes the need for 
flexibility so that the Companies can continue to make 
improvements to the programs as they identify issues.  
Therefore, the Commission finds the Companies’ proposals to 
modify baseline calculation methodologies based on their 
observations of actual program operations to be reasonable.
