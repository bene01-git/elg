Attachment #4 - Outage Event Report Addendum - National Grid 4-977 Line 
11/7/2025
RG&E -Station 168, Canandaigua Division
Description of Station 168 Project
RG&E’s project at Station 168 is a complete rebuild of the substation that moves from the previous
configuration — two 115 kV / 34 kV transformers tapped off National Grid’s Trunk #2 and Line 4-977 —
to an arrangement that bisects two nearby transmission lines to have four 115 kV sources into the
Station 168 load pocket.
At the time of this event, Station 168 was in an abnormal configuration due to ongoing project
construction activities. The abnormal configuration had a single 115 kV feed into Station 168 being
provided by National Grid’s Line 4-977. The other three 115 kV transmission sources into the new 115 kV
bus at Station 168 were de-energized as part of a planned transmission outage to maintain safe
electrical clearances during ongoing construction activities. This loss of foreign supply event
occurred on a Friday, September 26, at approximately 11:58 AM; at this time construction crews were
working in and around Station 168 to complete construction work and restore the new facility to a
configuration with additional transmission sources later that day.
Additional Event Description
The cause of this event was the loss of foreign supply caused by a trip of the National Grid Trunk 4 I
977 Line between Border City Substation and Station 168. The distance from Border City Substation
to the fault was estimated at approximately 5.8 miles (i.e., about 13 miles from Station 168)1.
Following the trip of National Grid’s Trunk 4 1 977 Line, the NMP422 breaker at Station 168, correctly,
did not reclose as not all the required conditions (i.e., live 115 kV bus) were met. Energy Control Center
(ECC) Operators responded by reviewing alarms and sequence of events, as well as contacting field
personnel to determine what had occurred. In circumstances where a facility trips with construction
crews nearby, extreme care is taken to ensure that no personnel were involved in, or affected by, an
incident, and that all crews are safely clear of hazards. To safely to close the NMP422 breaker
remotely through the SCADA system it was necessary for ECC operators to:
• 
Determine what type of fault had occurred,
• 
Where the fault had occurred and what equipment was involved,
• 
Determine if there were any field crews or first responders in proximity to the fault, and
• 
Receive permission from the NYSIO to close the breaker, as this 115 kV breaker is under their
jurisdiction.
Given the ongoing construction status of Station 168, the presence of field crews, and the
coordination required with National Grid and NYISO it would have been nearly impossible to evaluate
all conditions and determine it was safe to manually close the NMP422 breaker from the ECC within 5
minutes.

Discussion of Automatic Reclosing Settings
The Station 168 project team had considered utilizing temporary reclosing settings for the abnormal
configuration at Station 168, but determined that this temporary relay logic would only be beneficial
for temporary faults and, in fact, could increase exposure due to the use of a non-standard and
unfamiliar reclosing design.2 It is important to note that, for this event, the use of a custom reclosing
configuration would not have had mitigated this event as Station 168 had been approved to return to
a more normal configuration with additional transmission sources approximately 90 minutes before
this event occurred. One step of this “return to normal" process would have been reverting to the
normal reclosing settings, the same that were in place during the event.
The reclosing for the new NMP422 breaker that tripped for the 115 kV line fault is correctly configured
to reclose for either a “hot-bus, hot-line” condition or a “hot-bus, dead-line” condition.3 The Operating
Instructions for the NMP422 breaker at Station 168 indicated that it did have the ability to reclose, but
the instructions did not enumerate the conditions required for reclosing. Station Operating
Instructions used by operators are drafted based on the System Protection Requirements (SPR) for a
facility. During design and construction there can be multiple revisions made to the SPR and,
consequentially, the Operating Instructions based on as-built conditions — this may be particularly
true during large or complex projects. The project at Station 168 included a significant amount of new
equipment, including six 115 kV circuit breakers and all associated protective and control relaying.
Reconfiguration of this area’s 115 kV system required a significant update to the station Operating
Instructions for the new equipment, including insertion of Station 168 into the line protection and
reclosing schemes in the area. Accordingly, updates to the station operating instructions had not yet
been finalized at the time of this event.
Finally, Operating Instructions are generally written to describe how the facility will respond when
moving from a system normal (N-0 state) to an N-l state; operating instructions typically do not
include station behavior for loss of additional elements when already in an N-l state (i.e., N-1-0). At a
substation like Station 168 with multiple 115 kV sources, the “dead-bus, live-line” condition that
preempted reclosing for this event requires the Loss of at least two system elements (e.g., both line
breakers connected to one of the 115 kV buses being tripped).4

Customer Outage Count
Following the event, RG&E's Operational Performance group continued to review outage records for
9/26/25 to identify the proper number of customers that were outaged due to the loss of foreign
supply. The final, accurate, number of customer outages for this event following data scrubbing is
22,743.
Discrepancies between the final affected customer count (22,743 customers) and the initial data
included in the Utility Event Dashboard (21,056 customers), the “Preliminary Notice of Non-Utility
Controlled Outage Events - September 2025” (18,789 customers) and the Outage Event Report
(22,999 customers) can be attributed to initial customer outage quantities that were generated
automatically after the event occurred (the UED and Outage Event Report data) or to outages that
had incorrect cause codes, so they were not initially attributed to the Loss of Foreign Supply event
(the Preliminary Notice). Table 1, below, includes a summary of the finalized outage data for the
9/26/25 Loss of Foreign Supply Event.
