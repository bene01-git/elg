Statement of Opposition to  
Case 19-G-0309 – Proceeding on Motion of the Commission as to the Rates, Charges, 
Rules and Regulations of The Brooklyn Union Gas Company d/b/a National Grid NY for 
Gas Service  
Case 19-G-0310 – Proceeding on Motion of the Commission as to the Rates, Charges, 
Rules and Regulations of KeySpan Gas East Corporation d/b/a National Grid for Gas 
Service 
 
I am a Long Island resident and executive Director of All Our Energy, a Long 
Island nonprofit focused on stopping climate change and promoting environmental 
protection.  I myself, and on behalf of All Our Energy, strongly oppose this Joint 
Proposal. Here on Long Island, our communities have been fighting against the 
Companies’ CNG infrastructure expansions in Glenwood, Barrett, and Inwood because 
they pose both immediate and long-term health risks to us. The trucks that they intend 
to bring through Long Island in order to inject CNG into the transmission system at 
these sites are highly explosive and so dangerous that they are currently illegal in New 
York City. This gas that the companies call “natural” is potent fracked methane gas 
whose emissions warm Earth’s atmosphere at a rate many times that of carbon dioxide. 
Moreover, the supply/demand gap that the Companies claim as justification for this new 
infrastructure is drastically overestimated and does not take into account the mandates 
of the CLCPA to radically reduce GHG emissions by 2030. It also ignores the fact that 
the coming of offshore wind power in 2025, 2026, 2027 and beyond will displace much 
of the need for these dirty fossil fuels for electricity generation in our region- freeing up 
supply that has not been accounted for.  It is clear that the Companies are 
overestimating demand forecasts because it is in their financial interest to continue to 
build out fossil fuel infrastructure. An 8.8% return on equity for $50 million worth of new 
CNG infrastructure will profit them well. But my Long Island community is certainly not 
here to be profited off of for the benefit of an international corporate monopoly.
   I understand that rate recovery for the aforementioned Long Island CNG 
injection infrastructure projects will be through the Demand Capacity Surcharge 


--- Page 2 ---
Mechanism and that it is dependent upon the Companies’ meeting certain Energy 
Efficiency and Demand Response metrics. While this is a concession and a step in the 
right direction, it is not nearly rigorous enough. My community demands nothing short of 
an outright denial of rate recovery for unnecessary projects that have been justified with 
exaggerated and unsubstantiated demand forecasts. We had professed that these 
projects will inevitably become stranded assets before too long as the Climate Crisis 
worsens, and emissions reduction mandates become more severe. But how could it 
possibly be prudent or in the public interest to allow even the possibility of rate recovery 
for a Company’s solely self-serving construction investment profits, which the company 
itself has already abandoned immediately after their completion?  The company’s recent 
public announcement of their abandonment of their own plans to build the CNG filling 
plant in Greenpoint, which was to supply compressed gas to these new injection plants, 
strands these assets by the company’s own hand. That the parent project has been 
publicly announced by the company to be CANCELED, while also aggressively seeking 
to be paid for it in this rate case anyway, and while its child projects will not be used, is 
in-your-face arrogance of impunity. It is not that we endorse the CNG supply project-far 
from it. The point is this cancellation immediately, right now, strands these Long Island 
assets with no supply mechanism for their use before even being used once and that 
lost investment is immediately socialized to ratepayers who never had a say.  
  
In Appendix 7 Schedule 11.11 of the Joint Proposal, we see these Long Island 
CNG projects with their annual Capex breakdowns and three teeny tiny asterisks 
leading to a teeny tiny footnote with massively problematic implications. It reads: “Due 
to these projects being in-service or nearing completion, KEDLI will be allowed to 
recover the associated costs without undergoing additional independent assesment 
(sic).” This statement effectively sets an expectation for the Company that they will be 
able to recover the costs of any of their investments, no matter how imprudent or 
harmful they are to New Yorkers, as long as the construction of those infrastructure 
investments is either complete or nearly complete before a new rate case is filed. The 
same exact story is taking place in the KEDNY territory with MRI Phases 1-4. Staff must 
acknowledge and listen to the tens of thousands of people who have filed public 
comments against the project. Staff must acknowledge and listen to the dozens of 
elected officials, including the Mayor of New York City who have publicly opposed the 
project. Apparently, all that concerns staff is the fact that construction was started in the 
beginning of this rate filing and now, over two years later, it is complete. What type of 
precedent does this set? This is not accountability. This is the blatant enabling of 
incredibly harmful behavior from a multibillion-dollar corporation. This State regulatory 
agency must be the Department of Public Service, not the Department of Corporate 
Service and needs to act to protect the interests of the public.   
  
This building of unnecessary projects, gambling with the public’s money, and 
then abandoning that investment to cover up the potential illegal segmentation that the 
overarching project this rate case represents, is a miscarriage of environmental law. In 
this sprawling, unapproved project, where the North Brooklyn Pipeline meets the 
Greenpoint LNG Plant, meets the Greenpoint CNG plant, to the additional infrastructure 
built on Long Island through a pipeline on wheels, the CNG filling plant links all that 


--- Page 3 ---
segmented, built infrastructure. Now the company has withdrawn the CNG supply 
project to obscure the fact that it is the CENTRAL HUB of the continuous, contiguous, 
single gigantic gas pipeline project at the heart of this rate case. They have somehow 
avoided proper and required environmental review-which it would NEVER pass, and to 
further injure us, seek to have us pay for it anyway.  Or were their claims of the CNG 
filling plant’s cancellation just more lies and obfuscation to distract from this issue? If it 
was canceled, why has it not been removed from the rate case? Again, like the injection 
plants, this begs the question of who is in charge of allowing this reckless behavior and 
why are they allowing it?  
This case deserves the decisive outcome of receiving no ratepayer 
recovery which would simply reward the freewheeling use of public funds without 
accountability, prior review, nor any long-term public return-on-investment.  We should 
not pay for building projects that no one asked for and then whose sole supply line is 
then chosen to be canceled, so as to avoid proper environmental scrutiny of what really 
is a far-reaching, single project that is expansion of new infrastructure.  In fact, there 
should be penalties assessed for this obfuscation, which so blatantly acts against the 
public interest.  
  
I request, first and foremost, that rate recovery for the Glenwood, Barrett 
and Inwood and all Long Island CNG and related facilities be denied. At the very 
least, it should be subject to the full independent consultant assessment as well as the 
EE and Demand Response metrics. On page 44 of the JP it states that the independent 
consultant responsible for determining whether or not the infrastructure projects subject 
to the DCSM are needed will “be selected by Staff.” In rebuttal, I request that the 
selection of the Independent Assessor is not a decision made by Staff alone but 
that it is, instead, a highly transparent process in which members of the public 
play an integral role. The Joint Proposal goes on to say that the consultant shall be 
nationally recognized “in the area of gas engineering.” However, on page 42 it states 
that two of the consultant’s criteria for determining whether or not the projects are 
necessary shall be “alternative options [and] GHG emissions.” It is imperative that the 
consultant is more than just an expert in soon-to-be-obsolete “gas engineering” 
but more importantly is an expert in moving us to the renewable energy required. 
I request that the language of the JP be altered to express this. We are both legally and 
morally mandated to find renewable energy solutions that have a net-zero emissions 
impact. The consultant must have a forward-thinking perspective that can envision less 
harmful ways of providing New Yorkers with thermal energy.
