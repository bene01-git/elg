III. 
RATIONALE FOR EAMs 
8 
Q.    
What are EAMs? 
9 
A. 
EAMs are designed to incentivize the utility to facilitate specific outcomes 
10 
that are deemed beneficial to its customers and align with State policy.   
11 
Q.    
What was the Commission’s rationale for establishing an EAM framework 
12 
within the REV Track 2 Order?  
13 
A. 
In the May 19, 2016, REV Track 2 Order in Case 14-M-0101, the 
14 
Commission focused on establishing a “modern regulatory model” that 
15 
challenges utilities to take actions to achieve certain Commission and New 
16 
York State policy objectives by better aligning utility shareholder financial 
17 
interest with consumer interest.  The Commission identified the 
18 
establishment of additional earnings opportunities for utilities, including 
19 
EAMs, as an important component of a modern regulatory model.  As 
20 
noted on page 2 of the REV Track 2 Order, the EAMs are “intended to 
21 
create a regulatory environment where utilities can create shareholder 
22

7 
 
value…by integrating third-party solutions and capital that improve the 
1 
efficiency, resiliency, and flexibility of the physical networks, reduce 
2 
consumer total costs and achieve the State’s policy objectives.” The 
3 
Commission has identified multiple customer benefits that can be 
4 
achieved from utility actions taken to implement the policy objectives of 
5 
the REV proceeding including: 1) lower customer costs through improved 
6 
efficiency in capital investments; 2) better utilization of existing assets; 3) 
7 
lower energy usage through energy efficiency (“EE”); and 4) reduced 
8 
carbon emissions.   
9 
Q.    
How do Central Hudson’s EAMs benefit its customers?  
10 
A. 
Central Hudson’s EAMs benefit customers in a variety of ways.  Each of 
11 
the proposed EAMs are consistent with the Climate Leadership and 
12 
Community Protection Act (“CLCPA”) and support the State’s clean 
13 
energy policies, many of which deliver benefits to customers (and society 
14 
more broadly) through greenhouse gas (“GHG”) emissions reductions.  
15 
For instance, achievement of the DER Utilization EAM represents a 
16 
significant reduction in GHG emissions by integrating more renewable 
17 
generation onto the utility system that can displace existing fossil fuel 
18 
generation.  Certain EAMs also incentivize Central Hudson to maximize 
19 
utility programs that provide direct benefits to customers.  For instance, 
20 
achievement of the Electric Vehicle Supply Equipment and Infrastructure 
21 
EAM represents successful proliferation of EV charging infrastructure 
22

throughout Central Hudson’s service territory, improving access to vehicle 
1 
charging for all customers, providing incentives to customers who invest in 
2 
vehicle chargers at their site, and directing the highest incentives to those 
3 
within Disadvantaged Communities (“DAC”).  Other EAMs are aligned with 
4 
outcomes that reduce utility costs to customers.  For instance, 
5 
achievement of the Electric Peak Reduction EAM would drive a reduction 
6 
in the Company’s wholesale capacity requirement, which reduces capacity 
7 
costs passed on to customers and could also mitigate the need for load-
8 
driven infrastructure upgrades.  Most of the EAMs deliver some 
9 
combination of these and other types of benefits.

analysis demonstrates that if the Company were to reach maximum 
14 
achievement on each of its EAMs there would be a positive net benefit of 
15 
approximately $516 million and a benefit-cost ratio of 2.50.  The results of

this analysis are included in more detail within Exhibit __ (EAMP-1).
