customer satisfaction; improving community interactions; improving worker and public safety; 
and reducing customer trim requests and associated costs.

B. 
The Joint Proposal Is in the Public Interest and Should be Adopted by the 
Commission in Its Entirety Without Modification 
Based on the foregoing, and as explained more fully below, the Joint Proposal is in the 
public interest and should be adopted by the Commission because it: was entered into (in whole 
or in part) by normally adverse parties; strikes a fair balance among the interests of ratepayers 
and investors and provides the Companies with funds needed to build, operate and maintain safe 
and adequate electric and gas systems; is consistent with the legal, regulatory, economic, social 
and other policies of the Commission and the State; and compares favorably with the likely 
result of full litigation and is within the range of reasonable outcomes.  The Joint Proposal 
benefits customers in numerous other ways including: multi-year rate certainty (through April 
30, 2026); an opportunity for sharing via an earnings sharing mechanism (“ESM”); a multi-year 
commitment to capital spending, including investments in aging infrastructure; increased funding 
for EE and electric heat pump programs; enhanced electric vegetation management to reduce 
tree-related outages; additional workforce to enhance the customer experience; the continuation 
of Energy Affordability Programs; continuation of the Companies’ electric economic 
development programs; various downward-only reconciliations; and negative revenue 
adjustments (“NRAs”) should the Companies miss established targets for certain customer 
service, electric reliability and gas safety performance metrics.  While providing these customer 
benefits, the Joint Proposal also provides the Companies with needed rate relief to support 
continued significant investment in their electric and gas systems to maintain safe and adequate 
service.

II. 
CRITERIA FOR REVIEWING SETTLEMENTS 
As discussed in Section III of this Statement, the Joint Proposal clearly meets the 
Commission’s criteria for the adoption of a settlement.  The Commission has consistently 
applied the following public interest standard as set forth in its Settlement Guidelines:7  
• A desirable settlement should strive for a balance among (a) protection of the customers; 
(b) fairness to investors; and (c) the long-term viability of the utility.  Additionally, a 
settlement should be consistent with sound environmental, social and economic policies 
of the Agency and the State and should produce results that were within the range of 
reasonable results that would likely have arisen from a Commission decision in a litigated 
proceeding. 
 
• In judging the settlement, the Commission shall give weight to the fact that a settlement 
reflects agreement by normally adversarial parties.8

The Order adopting the Settlement Guidelines enumerates the following factors to be

• The settlement’s consistency with law and the regulatory, economic, social and

• The existence of a rational basis for decision; 
• The completeness of the record; and 
• Whether the settlement is contested. 
The first four of the foregoing factors, according to the Commission, are elements of the 
public interest standard, while the last two “simply guide [the Commission] in [its]

III. 
THE JOINT PROPOSAL MEETS THE COMMISSION’S SETTLEMENT 
STANDARD FOR ADOPTION 
The Joint Proposal fully satisfies the Commission’s Settlement Guidelines.  First, the 
Joint Proposal is consistent with the New York State Public Service Law (“PSL”) and 
Commission and New York State economic, social and environmental policy.  For example, as 
discussed in Section IV, the Joint Proposal contains a number of provisions and items that are 
consistent with the Commission’s and New York State’s commitment to reducing GHG 
emissions as set forth in the CLCPA.  In addition, the Joint Proposal contains a number of 
features that are consistent with the Commission’s goals of reducing customer bill impacts and 
decreasing the number of service terminations (e.g., through the Joint Proposal’s robust Energy 
Affordability Programs). 
Second, the Joint Proposal produces a result that is rational and well within the range of 
reasonable outcomes had these proceedings been fully litigated.  As discussed below, certain 
aspects of the Joint Proposal reflect positions advocated by the Companies, Staff and other 
Signatory Parties in pre-filed testimony, while other aspects represent a compromise among 
various Parties.  Taken as a whole, therefore, the Joint Proposal compares favorably with the 
likely result of full litigation.

Third, the Joint Proposal strikes a fair balance among the interests of customers, the 
Companies, other Signatory Parties, and the long-term soundness of the utilities.  For example,

Certified Natural Gas Pilot (Section XXXI.D)  
In testimony, the Companies proposed a Certified Natural Gas (“CNG”)127 Pilot Program 
that would permit them to purchase up to $250,000 of CNG, that is incremental to traditional 
supply costs, during each of the next three calendar years to allow the Companies to learn, adapt, 
and adjust as the CNG market develops.128  Staff testified in support of the proposal but 
recommended more frequent reporting requirements. 
The Joint Proposal authorizes the Companies to implement a CNG Pilot Program 
designed to allow for the procurement of certified natural gas, during the Rate Plan, limited to an 
annual cost above traditional supplies of $250,000 per year per Company.  Procured certified 
natural gas will be recovered similarly to other natural gas purchases through each Company’s 
Gas Supply Charge.  The Companies agree to limit purchases CNG that has obtained one of the 
following ratings: (1) Project Canary Trustwell Platinum rating; (2) MiQ Grade A rating; and/or 
(3) OGMP 2.0, Level 5 rating.  The Companies will file an annual report describing the progress 
of the pilot and review the report with Staff during regularly scheduled meetings on gas supply 
issues.   
The CNG Pilot Program should be adopted because it is within the reasonably expected 
range of potential litigated outcomes and will provide the Company an opportunity to purchase 
and quantify emission benefits from natural gas producers that operate and meet environmental 
standards and best practices for methane emissions reductions in their operations.  This

Financial Reporting Requirement Change (Section XXXI.E) 
The Joint Proposal discontinues certain reporting requirements from Case 07-M-0906 and 
continued in Case 12-M-0666.  These requirements included certain periodic financial and 
reporting conditions related to Security and Exchange Commission (“SEC”) reporting rules and 
requirements because Energy East was being delisted from the New York Stock Exchange and 
was no longer going to be a public registrant.  However, Avangrid, which was formed in 2015, is 
a public registrant and is subject to all of the SEC reporting rules/requirements covered by the 
referenced requirements.  As a result, continuation of this reporting requirement is unnecessary 
as the information is already publicly available.  This provision is reasonable and should be 
adopted because it removes duplicative reporting requirements, which should allow the 
opportunity for efficiencies.

Broadband Expansion (Section XXXI.F) 
In 2022, Governor Hochul announced the start of the $1 billion ConnectALL initiative, 
which is intended to deliver affordable broadband to millions of New Yorkers statewide.  As a 
result of this initiative, the Companies have begun to see an increase in pole attachment 
applications by broadband companies impacting the Companies’ infrastructure.  During the 2021 
Historic Test Year, the Companies received requests to attach to approximately 65,000 poles.  
This grew to over 100,000 applications in 2022 and could grow to over 140,000 pole attachment 
applications by the end of 2023. 
As a result of these applications, the Companies may experience extraordinary increases 
in the level of pole attachment requests, which would have a corresponding material increase in 
the Companies’ costs to accommodate broadband expansion initiatives.  The Joint Proposal
