TARIFF FILINGS 
On January 23, 2018, based on the consensus 
recommendation in the Phase Two Report, National Grid filed 
proposed tariff amendments, listed in the Appendix, to KEDLI’s 
Service Classification (SC) No. 14 – Transportation Service and 
KEDNY’s SC No. 20 – Non-Core Transportation, to eliminate the 
penalty charge of $10 per dekatherm applied to certain customer 
imbalances in the Companies’ daily balancing charges. 
The Companies’ current tariffs state that for 
quantities of natural gas consumed by a customer that exceed the 
quantities of natural gas delivered by more than 10 percent but 
less than 20 percent, the excess shall be purchased by the 
customer at 140 percent of the Company’s Daily Gas Price plus 
$10 per dekatherm.  “The Collaborative parties agree that this 
$10 per dekatherm charge should be deleted because the applicable 
multiple of the Daily Gas Sales Price provides a sufficient 
disincentive to power generation customers to take all available 
steps to avoid underdelivering gas by more than 10 percent per 
day.”6  National Grid requested waivers of the requirements of

NOTICES OF PROPOSED RULEMAKING 
Pursuant to the State Administrative Procedure Act 
(SAPA) §202(1), Notices of Proposed Rulemaking were published in 
the State Register on February 21, 2018 [SAPA Nos. 16-G-0058SP5 
and 16-G-0059SP4].  The time for submission of comments pursuant 
to the Notices expired on April 23, 2018.  LIPA submitted 
comments. 
In its comments, LIPA stated its support of the 
recommendations in the Phase Two Report, with two exceptions.  
First, LIPA acknowledges that, in November 2017, LIPA and the 
other collaborative parties agreed that further action regarding 
National Grid’s non-firm transportation rates, including any 
changes to the Value Added Charge (VAC) and Minimum Bill, should 
be tabled until Staff issues its White Paper in Case 17-G-0011.

regarding the Company’s proposed Bypass Parameters, LIPA states 
that the Company’s bypass analysis should be more transparent by 
including appropriately calculated ceiling and floor prices.

DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION 
The Companies’ four recommendations discussed in the 
Phase Two Report were developed in a collaborative process and 
the proposed tariff modifications reflect the issues 
contemplated for this phase of the collaborative.  With regard 
to the four recommendations, the Commission: (1) approves the 
proposed tariff amendments to eliminate the $10 per dekatherm 
penalty charge; (2) rejects the proposal to incorporate bypass 
parameters into the Companies’ respective tariffs; (3) agrees 
that rate design for power generators should be addressed 
holistically in Case 17-G-0011; and (4) agrees that, at this 
time, the Companies’ firm delivery rates for distributed 
generation resources should not be modified. 
The Companies’ propose tariff modifications to 
eliminate the $10 per dekatherm penalty charge that applies to 
under-deliveries of gas.  An imbalance of at least 10 percent but 
less than 20 percent already requires the customer to purchase 
gas at 140 percent of the Companies’ cost of gas.  The $10 
penalty is in addition to this imbalance penalty structure and, 
as such, is unnecessary.  Another reason to eliminate the charge 
is because no other gas utilities in the state have this 
additional penalty in their tariffs.  Furthermore, during the 
Phase Two Collaborative no parties objected to eliminating the 
$10 per dekatherm penalty.  The Commission, therefore, approves 
the Companies’ proposed tariff revisions. 
The Phase Two Report recommends adding bypass 
parameters to the Companies’ tariffs.  The bypass parameters 
that the Companies included in the Phase Two Report 
recommendation do not reflect a consensus recommendation of the 
collaborative parties.  Specifically, as noted in the Phase Two 
Report, “LIPA asserts that in addition to the bypass parameters, 
the Companies should also be required to develop and implement a

formula for determining rates and terms of contracts responsive 
to individual bypass requests as well as principles that would 
govern subsequent extensions of such contracts” but, “the 
Companies (and others) disagree with this recommendation.”7  
Moreover, in its comments, LIPA asks that these parameters 
include a ceiling and floor price. 
While the sharing of information between a utility and 
a bypass customer is necessary to analyze any bypass proposal, 
the necessary data will change depending on the specific 
situation.  Also, the specific data requested should be flexible 
to fit the situation and should not be too onerous for the bypass 
customer to provide.  With regard to LIPA’s request that the 
parameters include a ceiling and floor price, this is 
unnecessary.  The ceiling price for bypass negotiations is simply 
the tariffed delivery rates otherwise available to the customer.  
The floor price should equate to the marginal cost to provide 
the customer with service, which will be discussed in  
Case 17-G-0011, as it is an important factor in the development 
of gas delivery rates to electric generators.  The Commission 
therefore rejects the Phase Two Report’s recommendation to add 
additional language to National Grid’s tariffs. 
The Phase Two Report recommends tabling further actions 
related to value-based and cost-based rate design for power 
generators until Staff releases its White Paper in the Generic 
Generator Proceeding.  LIPA asks that the Commission eliminate 
the VAC immediately.  LIPA, along with the other collaborative 
parties, had already “agreed that further actions related to the 
rate design for power generators should be tabled until Staff 
releases its White Paper in the Generic Generator Proceeding.”8

It is anticipated that the Staff White Paper will provide 
comprehensive recommendations for each of the individual rate 
design comments applicable to generators.  All stakeholders will 
be able to provide comments on the Staff proposal.  Moreover, 
while the VAC may ultimately be eliminated, such a change may be 
feasible only in concert with changes to other aspects of rate 
design for natural gas service to electric generators.  The 
Commission will not, therefore, eliminate one charge without 
holistically evaluating all other generator rate design 
components. 
The Phase Two Report recommends that no changes to the 
Companies’ distributed generation rates be made at this time.  
Further, the Companies state that, any changes to the firm 
delivery rates for distributed generation resources should be 
considered generically by the Commission, and then implemented 
in later utility rate proceedings.  At this time, the Commission 
agrees that the Companies’ firm delivery rates for distributed 
generation resources should not be modified and declines to 
institute a new generic proceeding. 
The Commission also grants waivers of the requirements 
of Public Service Law §66(12)(b) and 16 NYCRR §720-8.1 as to 
newspaper publication of the changes proposed by the Companies’ 
filings.  This is reasonable because the tariff filing 
effectuates changes affecting a small number of customers.  The 
Companies shall individually notify the affected customers of 
the tariff changes.
