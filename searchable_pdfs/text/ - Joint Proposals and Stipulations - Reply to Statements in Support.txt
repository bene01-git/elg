PRELIMINARY STATEMENT 
 
In accordance with the April 11, 2024, Ruling on Schedule, WE ACT for Environmental 
Justice (“WE ACT”) hereby submits this Statement in Reply to the Initial Statements on the Joint 
Proposal filed on May 1, 2024, in the above captioned proceeding. 
 
WE ACT argues that the Brooklyn Union Gas Company d/b/a National Grid NY for Gas 
Service and KeySpan Gas East Corporation d/b/a National Grid for Gas Service (together, the 
“Companies”) and the Signatory Parties to the Joint Proposal have not met their burden of 
proving that the Joint Proposal is in the public interest,1 and more specifically, that the Joint 
Proposal complies with the Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act (“CLCPA”). 
Upon reading the statements submitted by the parties in these proceedings, WE ACT believes 
that having the Commission approve the Joint Proposal without any modifications will result in 
both environmental and financial harm, particularly to residential low-income and disadvantaged 
customers. 
 
In the Statement of Opposition to the Joint Proposal, WE ACT described its concerns as 
they pertained to unjust and unreasonable rates: the imprudent investments found in Section 10 
of the Joint Proposal—Gas Safety Performance Metrics and removal and replacement of leak 
prone pipe; nonbinding Non-Pipeline Alternatives (“NPAs”) targets; four new, polluting 
biomethane interconnection facilities that will almost certainly disproportionately burden 
Disadvantaged Communities (“DACs”) in contravention of the CLCPA; and requests for rate 
recovery that conflict with State and Federal law and the Commission’s guidance regarding 
utilities’ political activity. For all of the reasons discussed herein, WE ACT once again urges the

B. 
The Commission Cannot Find that the Joint Proposal Complies with CLCPA 
Section 7(3). 
The Commission cannot make a finding that the Joint Proposal is consistent with Section 
7(3) because the Companies have not provided the information needed to determine if the Joint 
Proposal’s terms would disproportionately burden disadvantaged communities, such as the 
baseline of existing burdens or the pollution that will result from any capital investments 
authorized by the Joint Proposal.20 
Section 7(3) of the CLCPA requires that state agencies ensure that their decisions do “not 
disproportionately burden disadvantaged communities” and “prioritize reductions of greenhouse 
gas emissions and co-pollutants” in such communities. In addition to prohibiting 
disproportionate burdens on, and mandating pollution reductions in, disadvantaged communities, 
the CLCPA calls for disadvantaged communities to enjoy the benefits of clean energy and energy 
efficiency investments. Specifically, the CLCPA mandates that at least 35%, with a goal of 40%, 
of the benefits of spending on clean energy and energy efficiency accrue to disadvantaged 
communities.21 The prohibition on decisions that would disproportionately burden disadvantaged

Staff’s Statement in Support of the Joint Proposal fails to articulate a clear application of 
the Section 7(3) requirements, seeming to conflate the provision with Section 7(2) by discussing

imposes new requirements on the Commission. Moreover, Section 7(3) instructs agencies such

disadvantaged communities and prohibits any decision that would. Even if gas service 
constituted a benefit under the CLCPA—which it does not—that would not relieve the 
Commission of the obligation to prevent any disproportionate burdens on disadvantaged 
communities.

III. 
NEIGHBORS OF THE NEWTOWN CREEK BIOMETHANE PROJECT NEED 
ADDITIONAL PROTECTIONS.  
As detailed in WE ACT’s Statement in Opposition to the Joint Proposal and recognized 
by the City of New York, the Newtown Creek Project has been “plagued” with problems “since 
[KEDNY] began operations in 2023, after several years of delay,” leading to biogas flaring when 
the Project is offline.48 The Joint Proposal requires KEDNY to negotiate a Service Level 
Agreement with the City within 30 days requiring: (1) automated notifications of system outages 
to the City Department of Environmental Protection (“DEP”) within one hour; (2) that KEDNY 
assess any outage within three hours during business hours and within 4.5 hours during off-
hours, weekends, and holidays; and (3) that KEDNY provide DEP and Community Board No. 1 
with information on remedial action and the anticipated restoration time if the Project will be 
offline for more than 24 hours continuously or 48 hours in a given month.49 The Companies’ 
assertion that these requirements represent “a reasonable resolution of concerns expressed about
