INTRODUCTION 
 
 
On November 18, 2022, Niagara Mohawk Power Corporation 
d/b/a National Grid (Niagara Mohawk or the Company) filed a 
petition (the Petition) requesting a limited waiver of Rules 
57.1.3 and 57.1.3.3 of the Company’s electric tariff schedule, 
P.S.C. No. 220 – Electricity (Electric Tariff).  Rules 57.1.3 
and 57.1.3.3 address the time period during which the Company is 
allowed to reconcile actual billed delivery service revenues to 
its annual target revenues through the revenue decoupling 
mechanism (RDM).  The requested limited waiver would allow 
Niagara Mohawk to recover Electric Energy Efficiency Transition 
Implementation Plan (ETIP) credits that were inadvertently 
excluded from billed delivery service revenues for certain

BACKGROUND 
 
 
The Company’s Electric Tariff Rule 57 allows the 
Company to reconcile actual billed delivery service revenues to 
annual targets for each specific customer group, or 
Reconciliation Group.1  The Commission approves the annual target 
revenues for each Reconciliation Group through rate orders.  
Rule 57.1.3 specifies that the RDM reconciliation period 
reflects a 12-month period for which actual billed delivery 
service revenues will be reconciled to the annual target 
revenues for each Reconciliation Group.  Rule 57.1.3.3 specifies 
that the RDM adjustment will be effective over a 12-month 
period, July 1 through June 30, commencing with the July billing 
month in the year following the RDM reconciliation period.  This 
period is known as the recovery period.  Allowing the Company to 
recover the under-collection for certain customer groups, 
specifically for SC Nos. 2D, 3, and 3A through its RDM would 
require a limited waiver of Rules 57.1.3 and 57.1.3.3 of the 
Company’s Electric Tariff.

reasonably should have known that the original billing was

DISCUSSION 
 
 
In its Petition, the Company proposes to recover 
$18,411,174 of under-collected ETIP costs from April 2018 to 
March 2021 from customers in SC Nos. 2D, 3, and 3A.  The Company 
states that the under-recovery was unintentional and resulted 
from an error in how it recorded actual billed delivery service 
revenues when performing its RDM reconciliation calculation.  
Specifically, the Company failed to consider ETIP credits 
provided to NYPA/RNY customers in its determination of actual 
billed revenues.  Because the 2017 Rate Order approved recovery 
of ETIP costs in base delivery rates, and the Company was not in 
an overearning position, the full recovery of these costs is 
appropriate.   
 
 
MI argues that the proposed recoveries could, in part, 
be barred by 16 NYCRR §13.9(c)(1), which limits the period 
during which a utility is allowed to backbill its customers.  
However, 16 NYCRR §13.9(c)(1) applies to upward revisions to 
previously issued bills.  The RDM reconciles aggregate actual 
billed revenues to annual target revenues for each 
Reconciliation Group through a surcharge or surcredit, as 
applicable.  As such, Niagara Mohawk’s request does not seek to 
adjust previous bills sent to customers. 
 
 
The Commission has addressed an analogous issue in the 
2004 CAC Order and in the 2011 Gas Cost Order, as described in 
Niagara Mohawk’s reply comments.  The 2011 Gas Cost Order 
explained that NYSEG made a calculation error in the context of 
its gas adjustment clause, and gas cost charges are not subject 
to the limitations on backbilling.  The Order goes on to explain

that “charges in the [Gas Supply Charge] GSC are made with the 
understanding that they are subject to future reconciliation, 
which, even though done on an annual basis, may exceed the 12-
month time period allowed under our backbilling rules.”7  RDMs 
are specifically designed to provide the respective company with 
specified delivery service revenues, or revenue targets, for 
each Reconciliation Group.  Reconciliations between actual 
billed revenues and revenue targets are performed at the same 
frequency as GSC reconciliations and with the understanding that 
they are subject to future reconciliation.  Thus, we similarly 
find here that granting the proposed limited waiver to rectify 
the RDM under-recovery does not run afoul of the limitations on 
backbilling set forth in 16 NYCRR §13.9(c)(1).  
 
 
MI also raises the issue of rate impacts, specifically 
to SC No. 3A, and proposes extending the collection time period 
for that class from 27 months to 63 months.  In its comments, MI 
estimates that a 63-month collection period would keep bill 
impacts below two percent, whereas MI calculates that the 27-
month collection period would result in delivery bill impacts of 
between 3.9 percent and 9.14 percent, depending on customers’ 
voltage level.

RDM adjustment period.”  However, the estimated bill impacts 
associated with a 27-month recovery period for SC No. 3A 
customers are excessive.  In its reply comments, the Company

CONCLUSION 
 
 
The Commission grants Niagara Mohawk a limited waiver 
of Rules 57.1.3 and 57.1.3.3 of its Electric Tariff, subject to 
the conditions contained herein.  The Company shall include the 
recovery of $22,923 from the SC No. 2D Reconciliation Group in 
its upcoming RDM filing.  According to Tariff Rule 57.3, Niagara 
Mohawk shall make that filing no later than June 28, 2023, to be 
effective for the 12 months beginning July 1, 2023.  
Additionally, the Company shall file an amended RDM Statement, 
to revise the current RDM reconciliation rate for the SC No. 3 
and the SC No. 3A Reconciliation Groups.  The revised rate for 
the SC No. 3 Reconciliation Group is to be calculated to recover 
the $2,147,615 under-recovery over a 15-month time period.  The 
revised rate for the SC No. 3A Reconciliation Group shall be 
calculated to recover the $16,240,636 under-recovery over a 60-
month time period.  The revised RDM statement shall be filed on 
not less than three days’ notice, with an effective date of 
April 1, 2023. 
 
 
Concerning customer outreach, the Company requests a 
waiver of newspaper publication requirements and proposes to 
send direct mail and/or email to impacted customers in SC Nos. 3 
and 3A.  In addition, due to the minimal nature of the impacts, 
the Company proposes no notification should be required for SC 
No. 2D.  Because the impacts on SC No. 2D customers are de 
minimis and because the Niagara Mohawk will directly notify
