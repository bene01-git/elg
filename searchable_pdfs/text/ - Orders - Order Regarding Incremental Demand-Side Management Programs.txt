Demand Response 
 
 
The Companies’ gas demand response programs for firm 
customers (Firm DR Programs) are aimed at reducing customer gas 
consumption during select peak winter days and hours with 
extreme cold temperatures (Event Days).  The Companies seek to 
utilize these programs to address future gas system supply gaps.  
In this filing, the Companies seek approval of the recovery of 
funds associated with the Firm DR Programs for the 2023-2024 
heating season.  The proposed budgets for the 2023-2024 heating 
season will enable the Companies to continue operating and scale 
up the current Firm DR Programs. 
 
 
On October 15, 2020, the Commission issued its Order 
Directing Implementation Plan Filing, Annual Reporting, and 
Tariff Modifications in Cases 20-G-0086 and 20-G-0087.9  The DR 
Implementation Order directed the Companies to file an Annual DR 
Filing by June 15 of each year which includes a report 
describing the performance during the prior winter and an 
Implementation Plan fully describing the operations, terms, and 
conditions related to the Firm DR Programs. 
 
 
The Companies launched a set of Firm DR Programs for 
the 2019-2020 and 2020-2021 winter seasons.  These programs were 
an extension of programs developed during the 2019-2020 winter 
heating season as part of the Settlement Agreement and described 
in the Companies Implementation and Contingency Plan described 
in the Companies “2020-2021 Expanded Gas Demand Response 
Implementation Plan.”10

The Companies operate four Firm DR Programs: the Load 
Shedding DR Program, the Load Shifting DR Program, the Bring 
Your Own thermostat (BYOT) Program, and the Behavioral DR 
Program.11  The Load Shedding DR Program focuses on commercial, 
industrial, and multi-family firm service customers capable of 
reducing their peak gas load over an eight-hour period on Event 
Days.  Under this program, the Companies ask Customers to reduce 
gas consumption during pre-determined hours, either by shutting 
off non-heating gas equipment or switching to a backup non-gas 
heating fuel source during events.  Though focused on daily load 
reductions, the Load Shedding DR Program event windows 
correspond to the typical system peak hours to ensure that the 
peak day load reductions occur during the gas system’s most 
critical periods.  The Load Shifting DR Program focuses on 
commercial, industrial, and multi-family firm service customers 
capable of reducing peak hours gas load over a four-hour period 
on Event Days.  The primary goal of the Load Shifting DR Program 
is to provide peak hour usage reductions during the early 
morning hours, which is the period in which the system typically 
experiences the highest customer demand.  To participate in the 
Load Shifting DR Program, participants must shift load to 
alternate hours prior to or following the event window and are

Capacity Demand Metrics 
 
 
The Rate Order established five weighted Capacity 
Demand Metrics that the Companies must meet to fully recover the 
costs of long-term capacity projects during the rate plan.  The 
Companies are required to submit quarterly reports documenting 
their performance in meeting the Capacity Demand Metrics. 
 
 
In the Filing, the Companies propose an updated 
methodology for calculating the Capacity Demand Metric for EE.  
The Companies propose to calculate this metric for calendar year 
2023 by adjusting the 2023 savings target authorized in the 
NE:NY Order by the Companies’ under- or over-performance 
relative to both NE:NY targets in prior years and the 2022 EE 
capacity demand metric, and dividing the total by the number of 
years remaining in the NE:NY period.  The Companies state that 
the 2022 EE Capacity Demand Metric set by the Commission was 
substantially higher than the savings they projected their 
programs would be able to achieve, and that their proposed 
methodology for 2023 would result in a target that would be 
challenging but possible to meet.  The Companies do not propose 
any changes to the Capacity Demand Metrics related to DR, 
Electrification, and Third-Party Solutions.

DISCUSSION 
EE Programs and EE Capacity Demand Metric 
 
 
The Commission agrees with the Companies’ proposal to 
prioritize weatherization programs within their EE portfolios.  
Doing so will help to address the forecasted gap between gas 
supply and demand within their service territories.  In their 
recent filing regarding the proposed Vaporizers 13 & 14 
project,15 the Companies noted changes in their peak demand 
forecast, which may push out the forecasted date of a gap 
between supply and demand.  Prioritizing weatherization programs 
presents an opportunity to shift the forecasted date of a 
potential gap between supply and demand further into the future.  
Furthermore, it is consistent with the broader direction for EE 
portfolios that the Commission indicated in the 2020 NE:NY Order 
and is contemplating with renewed attention in the NE:NY Interim 
Review.  The Commission especially supports the Companies’ 
proposal to make use of previously collected, unspent EE funds 
and projected unspent EE funds to pay for its weatherization 
programs.  This use is consistent with the use of unspent energy 
efficiency funds previously authorized by the Commission.16 
 
 
When the Companies proposed the DSM weatherization 
programs in the 2021 Annual DSM Filing, they stressed that these 
programs would need to scale up rapidly to address peak-day and 
peak-hour capacity constraints.  The Commission does not see 
evidence that the Companies have taken the necessary steps to 
achieve this rapid deployment.  The Companies project that as of

Finally, the Commission approves the Companies’ 
proposed methodology for calculating the EE Capacity Demand 
Metric.  The Commission set the 2022 EE Capacity Demand Metric 
based on the Companies’ planned expenditures related to the EE 
programs described in the 2021 Annual DSM Filing.  The 
Commission understood the ambitiousness of the Companies’ 
planned expenditures on weatherization programs to match the 
magnitude and the urgency of the work required to avert a gas 
supply shortfall in future winter seasons.  The 2023 savings 
target resulting from the Companies’ proposed methodology 
represents a more modest acceleration of the Companies’ NE:NY 
savings achievement than the Capacity Demand Metric set in 2022.  
Setting the EE Capacity Demand Metric as proposed by the 
Companies would not reduce the amount of savings to be achieved, 
but simply distribute it over the remaining years of the NE:NY 
period (through 2025).  Given the Companies’ dramatic 
underperformance on DSM EE programs relative to their stated 
ambitions, this redistributed target better matches the 
Companies’ current trajectory while still remaining a meaningful 
marker of the Companies’ efforts to realize the EE components of 
the Distributed Infrastructure Solution.  However, the 
Commission may revisit the EE Capacity Demand Metric 
calculations in future years if the mechanism remains in place 
beyond the term of the current rate plan.

For consecutive seasons, the programs have shown

At the close of the 2021/2022 winter heating season, 
approximately 6,500 thermostat devices were enrolled in the BYOT 
Program, an increase of approximately 189 percent over the 2,251 
devices enrolled during the 2020/2021 winter heating season.  
The Companies did report that there continued to be a high 
number of customer opt-outs over the course of the four-hour 
event windows.  To address this issue, the Companies made two 
modifications to the BYOT Program for the 2022/2023 winter 
heating season based on inputs from internal planning and 
engineering teams.  First, the event window hours for the
