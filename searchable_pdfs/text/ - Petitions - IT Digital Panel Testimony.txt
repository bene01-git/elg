10 
Q.

11 
A. 
National Grid IT&D delivers services and technology solutions that range from critical gas 
12 
transmission and distribution support systems to desktop applications. Through these 
13 
services, the IT&D function enables National Grid to safely and reliably deliver energy to 
14 
millions of customers, balance energy inputs and demands, enhance customer service, and 
15 
take action to advance the energy transition.  
16 
 
17 
These services underpin the safe, reliable, and secure physical and commercial operation 
18 
of KEDNY and KEDLI's gas distribution businesses.  IT&D also provides a suite of 
19 
software applications, asset and work management systems, as well as customer support 
20 
systems that allow KEDNY and KEDLI to efficiently and effectively operate the gas 
21 
system.

Q. 
Please describe how National Grid develops the IT&D investment plan. 
4 
A. 
National Grid’s jurisdictions identify project priorities with technology components.  As 
5 
part of that prioritization effort, business needs, infrastructure requirements, sequencing, 
6 
resource availability and capacity, size and impact of investment requests, and the overall 
7 
benefit of the investment compared to other proposals is assessed.  
8 
            
9 
Once engaged, IT&D considers existing multi-year investments and identifies the amount 
10 
of work remaining, and the delivery timeline for those projects.  IT&D will also support 
11 
new investments needed to comply with legal or regulatory requirements and mandates, or 
12 
other critical corporate objectives.  Lastly, projects to address IT&D infrastructure are 
13 
targeted, including investments to upgrade, replace, and add functionality to ensure system 
14 
reliability and to establish foundational capabilities required for future operational needs 
15 
of the business.   
16 
 
17 
Working as part of the senior leadership team in each jurisdiction, IT&D collaborates on 
18 
creating an investment plan in line with jurisdictional priorities. A final investment plan 
19 
review occurs each fiscal year with the jurisdictional presidents, jurisdictional delegates, 
20 
and functional executives.  Upon the review and approval of the investment plan by the 
21 
jurisdictional presidents and functional executives, consolidated financial information 
22 
from the investment plan, by jurisdiction and company, is incorporated with IT&D

IV. 
IT&D Cost Allocation and Historic Test Year Costs 
6 
Q.       How are IT&D projects and investment costs charged to KEDNY and KEDLI? 
7 
A. 
IT&D capital projects and investments that are shared across operating companies are 
8 
implemented and owned by the Service Company.  The costs of these investments are 
9 
allocated to KEDNY and KEDLI (and the other operating companies that utilize and/or 
10 
benefit from the investments) in the form of rent expense.  Exhibit ___ (ITDP-2) provides 
11 
the allocation factors utilized to determine the forecast annual rent expense for planned 
12 
IT&D projects in the Rate Year and Data Years. 
13 
 
14 
Q. 
How are the costs of IT&D capital projects recovered? 
15 
A. 
The costs of IT&D capital projects are amortized over a period of years.  The average 
16 
service lives of the assets determine the amortization periods.  In general, many of the 
17 
IT&D-related assets on the books of the Service Company are either software intangibles 
18 
or portable general plant equipment that are being depreciated, amortized, and recovered 
19 
over a period equal to their planned usefulness or expected lives.  The actual physical 
20 
replacement of assets is determined by business decisions to maintain, refresh, replace, or 
21 
decommission assets.  That evaluation is conducted based on variables such as operational 
22 
risk, the cost to be incurred, and the availability of replacement options.  Considering 
23 


--- Page 12 ---

technological and functional obsolescence, the recovery of hardware and equipment and 
1 
amortization of software intangible assets will approximate the new system’s useful life.
