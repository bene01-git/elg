III. 
EMBEDDED COS STUDIES 
5 
Q.    
What is the purpose of an embedded COS study? 
6 
A. 
The basic purpose is to determine the earned rate of return (“ROR”) on 
7 
rate base by service class in order to evaluate the relationship between 
8 
current rates being charged to each customer class and the costs incurred 
9 
to serve each customer class.  Basically, revenues less expense divided 
10 
by rate base yields the ROR. 
11 
Q.    
What is the difference between an historical COS study and a pro forma 
12 
COS study? 
13 
A. 
An historical COS study uses actual data as recorded on the Company's 
14 
books for a particular calendar year, in this case 2018.  A pro forma COS 
15 
utilizes Rate Year forecasts for system loads, revenues, expenses, and 
16 
rate base to develop an estimated ROR by service class.  Together, the 
17 
historical and pro forma studies allow for a comparison of estimated 
18 
realized to estimated expected rates of return based on the current rate 
19 
structure adopted in Cases 17-E-0459 and 17-G-0460, the Company’s 
20 
prior rate cases. 
21

Q.    
Please outline the methods used to prepare an embedded COS study. 
1 
A. 
The basic procedure is to allocate rate base, expenses, and revenue 
2 
among the various service classes via a three step process of: (1) 
3 
functionalization; (2) classification of investment and expenses; and then, 
4 
(3) allocation of those “costs” among the service subclasses. 
5 
Q.    
Please explain what is meant by the term “functionalization.” 
6 
A. 
Functionalization characterizes the analysis and re-grouping of the various 
7 
plant investment and expenses according to the activity or "function" for 
8 
which the cost was incurred rather than strictly by the Federal Energy 
9 
Regulatory Commission (“FERC”)/PSC account number to which it was 
10 
booked.  The conventional "functions" are production-related, 
11 
transmission-related, distribution-related or customer-related (“PTDC”). 
12 
Q.    
How did you determine the function of a cost? 
13 
A. 
Company accounting records and data maintained for engineering 
14 
purposes are the principal sources of information used in the 
15 
functionalization process.  Personnel from the Company's accounting and 
16 
engineering areas also provided assistance in identifying the particular 
17 
function or functions of specific investments. 
18 
Q.    
Would you please provide an example of a functionalized cost? 
19 
A. 
Costs recorded in the Electric Plant Account 362, Distribution Station 
20 
Equipment, averaged approximately $156.6 million in 2018.  Records as 
21 
to the type and location of the equipment represented by this investment 
22 
indicate that about $642,000 served a production function; $6.8 million 
23

12 
 
served a power supply transmission function; $135.7 million served a 
1 
distribution function and about $13.5 million served a specific class of 
2 
customers.  As a result, the $642,000 would be functionalized to 
3 
production, the $6.8 million to transmission, the $135.7 million to 
4 
distribution, and the $13.5 million is specifically assigned to a specific 
5 
class of customers. 
6 
Q.    
Please explain what is meant by the term “classification.” 
7 
A. 
Classification refers to the process of separating the functionalized costs 
8 
into amounts related to demand, energy or number of customers.  
9 
Demand-related costs vary in proportion to the rate (expressed in kW or 
10 
Mcf) at which customers utilize electricity or natural gas.  Energy-related 
11 
costs vary in proportion to the total volume of the electricity or natural gas 
12 
delivered.  Customer-related costs vary in proportion to the number of 
13 
customers served.  A smaller, fourth category of costs, specifically-
14 
assigned, defines costs that can be attributed to a particular service class 
15 
of customers. 
16 
Q.    
Regarding your example of the functionalization of Electric Plant Account 
17 
362, how did you proceed to classify these costs? 
18 
A. 
Since virtually all of the equipment included in Account 362 must be sized 
19 
to meet the expected maximum collective demand of all customers, the 
20 
non-specific functional categories (production, power supply transmission 
21 
and distribution) were classified as demand-related investment.  The 
22 
remainder, about $13.5 million, was specifically assigned.  
23

Q.    
Please explain what is meant by the term “allocation.” 
1 
A. 
Allocation refers to the process of distributing the functionalized and 
2 
classified costs among the service classes on the basis of service class 
3 
contribution to peak demand (in kW); or, class contribution to total volume 
4 
of electricity delivered (i.e., energy in MWh); or, class contribution to total 
5 
number of customers; or, by direct assignment to a specific rate class 
6 
when appropriate.  To accomplish this step, several types of “demand” 
7 
allocators are used, including Summer/Winter Coincident Peak (“CP”) 
8 
demand, Class NCP (non-coincident peak kW); Sigma NCPi (sum of 
9 
individual customer’s peak demand). 
10 
Q.    
Continuing with your Account 362 example, how was the plant investment 
11 
ultimately allocated? 
12 
A. 
The Company allocated investments functionalized as “production” and 
13 
“power supply transmission” to the various service classes on the basis of 
14 
their respective contribution to the average of the summer and winter 
15 
coincident peak demands.  Investment functionalized as “distribution-
16 
related” was allocated on the basis of per class contribution to the class 
17 
non-coincident peak demand at the substation level of service.  
18 
Investments functionalized as “specific” were directly assigned to the rate 
19 
classes of customers to be served by that investment.
