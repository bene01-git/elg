A. KEDNY’s Current Gas Safety Metric

ending December 31, 2012.  The 2013 Joint Proposal approved by the Commission extended 
KEDNY’s rate plan and modified various aspects, including the gas safety performance metrics.  
In addition to providing for more stringent performance targets for most of the Company’s 
existing gas safety metrics, the 2013 Joint Proposal introduced the Metric, which imposed 
negative revenue adjustments based on the number of instances of non-compliance with the 
Commission’s gas safety regulations identified by the Department of Public Service Staff 
(“Staff”) in its annual field and records audits.3  Under the Metric, KEDNY is subject to the 
following maximum negative revenue adjustments:  15 pre-tax basis points ($2.7 million) in CY

paper and electronic records to assess whether the Company is documenting code compliance

C.  Proposed Amendments to the Gas Safety Metric  
To mitigate overly punitive results from the Metric and to encourage further performance 
improvements in gas safety compliance, the Company is proposing to amend the 2013 Joint 
Proposal to (i) modify the Gas Safety Violations Metric for CY 2015 and thereafter, (ii) provide 
that the Company will be able to reduce the negative revenue adjustments for the Gas Safety 
Metric for CYs 2013 and 2014 if the Company achieves certain performance targets in Staff’s 
CY 2016 audits, and (iii) allow KEDNY to reinvest assessed negative revenue adjustments in gas 
safety and compliance programs.  Specifically, the Company proposes to modify the Metric for 
CY 2015 and thereafter, as follows: 
1. Cap on the number of violations used to assess the negative revenue adjustment 
a. 
Beginning with the Metric results for CY 2015, the number of occurrences of 
violations for each high risk or other risk regulation subject to a negative revenue 
adjustment will be capped at ten per calendar year. 
b. 
If the Company is cited for more than ten violations of a particular high risk or 
other risk regulation in a calendar year, the Company will provide Staff with a 
compliance improvement plan that contains: (a) a root cause analysis of the 
Company’s compliance deficiency, and (b) a proposed mitigation plan to address 
future performance.  The Company proposes that it will meet with Staff to 
develop a mutually agreeable mitigation plan that will include provisions for 
tracking and reporting the Company’s efforts to address the compliance 
deficiency.

D.  KEDNY’s Proposal to Modify the Gas Safety Metric is  
Reasonable and Advances Public Safety  
 
 
Over the last several years, as the gas industry has intensified its focus on safe operations, 
KEDNY has increased its efforts to promote safety and compliance.  Since the Metric was 
implemented, KEDNY commenced a series of measures to improve compliance with the 
Commission’s regulations.  The Company has initiated a Compliance Analysts Program to 
monitor and document the Company’s compliance with the Commission’s safety regulations and 


--- Page 10 ---
 
8
internal work procedures.  This new function has contributed to improved safety performance, 
and will provide additional benefits as the Company expands the program over the next few 
years.  The Company has also engaged a nationally recognized pipeline safety expert to conduct 
an independent assessment of its gas operations to identify any compliance gaps and to help 
develop remediation plans.  In the long term, the Company is enhancing its systems supporting 
gas operations to improve compliance tracking and documentation capabilities.    
KEDNY submits that its proposal to modify the Metric will support further compliance 
improvements, and is in the public interest for several reasons.  First, as discussed above, the 
current Metric is overly punitive in cases where a procedure or systems failure results in 
numerous violations of the same code provision, and a multi-million dollar negative revenue 
adjustment, for just one compliance issue.  While a negative revenue adjustment capped at ten 
occurrences (as much a $1.8 million) is still significant for audit violations, it is a far more 
reasonable result than the negative revenue adjustments that result under the current Metric.  The 
Metric can and should be more effectively focused on addressing compliance deficiencies 
prospectively.  KEDNY believes that the Metric should encourage the Company to develop root 
cause analyses in instances of non-compliance and plans for addressing future compliance, not 
just assess significant financial penalties.  Thus, the Company proposes to require compliance 
improvement plans as a means to focus more attention on addressing compliance deficiencies 
going forward and, importantly, reducing the likelihood of repeat violations in future audits.   
Second, the ability to earn reductions to the assessed negative revenue adjustment will 
provide KEDNY with an incentive to further improve its compliance performance.  KEDNY’s 
proposed modification to the Metric would allow for reductions only if the Company made very 
significant improvements in its performance in 2016.  Indeed, to reduce the negative revenue 


--- Page 11 ---
 
9
adjustments arising from CY 2013 and CY 2014 audits, the Company will need to achieve 
performance improvements of 40 to 70 percent.  This is consistent with the intent of the Metric 
to encourage improvements in compliance performance.   
Third, KEDNY’s proposal is beneficial for both the Company and its customers because 
it would fund significant safety investments by reinvesting any negative revenue adjustments in 
gas safety improvement programs at the Company’s expense.  The Company proposes to work 
with Staff to develop mutually agreeable plans to invest any negative revenue adjustments in 
targeted gas safety programs that will directly benefit customers in New York. 
KEDNY takes compliance with the Commission’s regulations very seriously.  KEDNY 
also recognizes that its compliance performance needs to improve.  Accordingly, KEDNY’s 
proposal to modify its Gas Safety Metric is focused on encouraging and achieving better safety 
performance.  KEDNY believes that its request to modify its Gas Safety Metric is reasonable and 
in the public interest because it will advance public safety and compliance.
