Q.
The NYPA SLP proposes (at 23) that, when billing usage for smart 
9 
city devices, “[t]he Company should use an average power draw 
10 
value, rather than the maximum power draw.”  Does the Company 
11 
agree? 
12 
A.
The Company does not agree that an average power draw value more 
13 
accurately reflects a device’s power usage.  First, the Company’s practice 
14 
for providing unmetered electric service to small load device applications 
15 
is to utilize the maximum power draw operating on a full-time schedule.  
16 
Second, establishing “average” power draw for any device would be 
17 
difficult.  The power draw on smart city devices is highly variable, 
18 
depending on the communication load, ancillary equipment loads, and 
19 
the control of street lights.  The load also can change over time, generally 
20 
trending upward as additional applications are found for the 
21 


--- Page 14 ---
Rebuttal Testimony of the Outdoor Lighting Panel 
Page 12 of 20 
communication infrastructure.  Without metering many of these devices 
1 
over an extended period of time, there is no basis for establishing an 
2 
“average” energy usage for the devices.  Third, the power draw for many 
3 
devices is not necessarily “stable and predictable” – e.g., surveillance 
4 
cameras, which may use built-in heaters to keep the camera warm and 
5 
the lens clear in cold weather.  Finally, some devices can use more 
6 
electricity than the device’s rating might indicate.  Using the average will 
7 
not account for true energy usage.
