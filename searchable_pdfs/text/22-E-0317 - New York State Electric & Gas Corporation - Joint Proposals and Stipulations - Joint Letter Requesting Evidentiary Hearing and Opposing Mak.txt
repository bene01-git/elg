Dear Secretary Phillips and ALJ Bergen: 
 
AARP New York (AARP), the Public Utility Law Project of New York (PULP), Alliance for a 
Green Economy (AGREE), Climate Solutions Accelerator of the Genesee-Finger Lakes Region 
(Accelerator), Fossil Free Tompkins (FFT), Campaign for Renewable Energy (CRE), and 
Ratepayer and Community Intervenors (RCI) request reconsideration of ALJ Bergen’s ruling to 
adjourn the evidentiary hearing scheduled to commence on January 3, 2022.  We also 
strenuously object to the request of New York State Electric & Gas Corporation (NYSEG) and 
Rochester Gas and Electric Corporation (RG&E), in their letter of December 22, 2022, for a 
further 60-day “make whole” provision.  We also fully agree with Multiple Intervenor’s 
discussion in its letter of December 22, 2022 regarding the lack of due process. 
AARP has hundreds of thousands of members living in the Companies’ service territories, the 
vast majority of whom live in households that pay utility bills. AARP works on a range of energy 
issues at the state level. The core principles from which this work is approached include:  
• Ensuring service affordability for all—utility rates should be based on prudent use of 
ratepayer money. Costs and savings should be distributed fairly among consumers. All 
households with lower incomes should be enrolled in utility low-income rate assistance 
programs and should also be able to access robust deferred payment arrangements and 
arrearage management programs.  
• Maintaining and improving quality—all consumers should have access to high quality 
utility services regardless of factors such as geography, race, ethnicity, and income.

Enhancing accountability—any changes in utility service, policy, or rates should be open 
and transparent. In New York’s case, this includes also informing ratepayers of all the 
factors that are causing higher bills and not just costs associated with their utility’s rate 
plan.  
PULP’s primary focus is to promote and defend the legal rights of residential low-income utility 
consumers by educating the public, regulators, and elected officials about the impacts of utility 
rates, conducting research on the rights and energy burden of utility consumers, and, through 
advocacy, emphasizing the rights and needs of low-income utility consumers. PULP’s work over 
the last 40 years has been instrumental in identifying utility practices that inadvertently harm 
low-and moderate-income customers. 
 AGREE has advocated statewide for more than a decade in a variety of forums including all 
major electric and gas utility rate proceedings as well as relevant Commission policy 
proceedings for an equitable transition to a renewable-energy-based economy that promotes a 
healthy and sustainable physical and social environment.  
The Accelerator is a non-partisan organization whose mission is to build healthier, more 
equitable, and environmentally sustainable communities by catalyzing local efforts to eliminate 
greenhouse gas emissions and address the effects of climate change. The organization serves the 
Genesee-Finger Lakes region, which includes the counties of Genesee, Livingston, Monroe, 
Ontario, Orleans, Seneca, Wayne, Wyoming, and Yates.  
FFT is an unincorporated renewable energy advocacy campaign in Tompkins County that aims 
to ensure that the county and state meet their greenhouse gas and renewable energy targets while 
ensuring a just, orderly, and affordable transition. Since 2013, FFT has been involved in a variety 
of activities including policy making at the local and state levels, community education, and 
grassroots organizing.  
CRE is a nonprofit public interest organization formed in 2010 to conduct public education 
programs promoting energy conservation, energy efficiency, and renewable energy. 
RCI was formed by a coalition of individuals, municipal officials, engineers, and researchers in 
the Finger Lakes to participate in energy related matters before the PSC in order to ensure just, 
equitable, community-minded progress towards regional and state GHG reduction and renewable 
energy goals.  
 
We disagree with any suggestion or assumption contained in NYSEG/RG&E’s letter that 
settlement discussions are proceeding smoothly and are likely to result in a widely supported 
resolution of the issues in these rate proceedings.  Quite the contrary, the discussions compel the 
conclusion that the likelihood of achieving a settlement that is in the public interest from an 
affordability, reliability, or environmental perspective and satisfies the legal standard of safe and 
adequate service at just and reasonable rates is close to null.  
In our view, continuing settlement talks is a poor use of the scarce resources of our organizations 
as well as the ample resources, fully funded by ratepayers, of DPS Staff and the Companies.  A
