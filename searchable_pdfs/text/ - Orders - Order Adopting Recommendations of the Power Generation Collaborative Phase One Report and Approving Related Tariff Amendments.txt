BACKGROUND 
 
 
The Commission’s Rate Order in Case 16-G-0058, et al.,2 
adopted the terms of a Joint Proposal that established a three-
year rate plan and, among other things, required the 
establishment of a two-phase collaborative to consider power 
generation issues.  Phase One would address the implementation 
of no harm/no foul balancing provisions under which daily 
imbalances greater than two percent would be cashed out only if 
there also is a system-wide imbalance for the entire pool of 
electric generators; monthly trading of imbalances; and, 
implementation of load following or other enhanced balancing 
services.  The Rate Order directed the parties to file a report 
concerning Phase One issues by April 30, 2017, with either 
recommendations for consideration by the Commission or the

COLLABORATIVE REPORT 
 
 
On June 30, 2017, National Grid submitted the 
Collaborative Report, explaining the results of Phase One.  The 
Collaborative Report contained recommendations agreed to, in 
part,3 by the participants, which included National Grid, 
Department of Public Service Staff (Staff), the Long Island 
Power Authority (LIPA), the New York Power Authority (NYPA) and 
other interested parties.  The recommendations as described in 
the report propose for the implementation of: (1) “no-harm/no-
foul” balancing; (2) extended balancing service; (3) revisions 
to the daily balancing service demand charges, or “Swing 
Charge;” and, (3) clarifications of other aspects of National 
Grid’s services to provide electric generation customers greater 
balancing flexibility on the gas network.

PUBLIC COMMENT 
 
 
Pursuant to the State Administrative Procedure Act 
(SAPA) §202(1), Notices of Proposed Rulemaking were published in 
the State Register on August 16, 2017 [SAPA Nos. 16-G-0058SP4 
and 16-G-0059SP3] for the Collaborative Report and KEDLI’s and 
KEDNY’s respective tariff filings.  The time for submission of 
comments pursuant to the Notices expired on October 2, 2017.  
Comments were received from LIPA on October 2, 2017.  LIPA 
supports the recommendations in the Collaborative Report, with 
certain exceptions.  Specifically, LIPA opposes the revised 
Swing Charge methodology, which increases the existing daily 
balancing service demand charges.  LIPA bases its opposition on 
the following: (1) the swing charge was not scheduled to be 
analyzed until Phase Two of the collaborative; (2) the swing 
charge and new pricing methodology were not adequately reviewed; 
(3) the collaborative is an inappropriate process to propose a 
charge on a contested basis; and (4) the rate methodology has 
not been approved and incorporated into the revenue requirement,

LEGAL AUTHORITY 
 
 
Under PSL §66, the Commission has general supervision 
of all gas corporations in New York State, which includes the 
ability to regulate the terms under which gas corporations 
provide gas service to their customers. 
 
DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION 
 
 
The Companies’ recommendations discussed in the 
Collaborative Report and proposed tariff modifications provide 
for a number of changes that address the issues contemplated for 
this phase of the collaborative.  National Grid examined all the 
Phase One issues in an open forum for all Parties including 
Staff to have a clear understanding of each of the issues being 
addressed and the proposed solutions to provide “no-harm/no-
foul” balancing provisions and enhanced balancing services for 
power generators.  In addition, supporting data was provided to 
all parties, to the extent possible, for transparency with 
regards to the costs of portions of National Grid’s commodity 
and capacity portfolio that are necessary in providing these 
services to non-core power generation customers and the extent 
to which they are relied upon.  The review of both the 
methodology and calculation associated with the Enhanced 
Balancing service finds National Grid’s proposed methodology and 
rate to be appropriate and equitable among core firm customers 
and non-core power generation customers.  These changes ensure 
that all customers are being charged appropriately for their use 
of the assets secured by National Grid on their behalf, or for 
which they avail themselves of through use of National Grid 
facilities.

Moreover, National Grid’s Enhanced Balancing service 
is solely associated with commodity and demand costs that are 
not revenue requirement related or associated with customer 
delivery charges.  Therefore, LIPA’s objection to the change in 
the methodology for the Swing Charge on the basis that it has 
not been incorporated into the Companies’ revenue requirements 
is unsubstantiated. 
 
 
Furthermore, LIPA’s objections that the Swing Charge 
was not scheduled to be analyzed until Phase Two of the 
collaborative, and that the Collaborative Report is not an 
appropriate place to propose this charge, absent consensus among 
the parties, are unpersuasive.  First, with regard to whether 
the Swing Charge was scheduled for analysis in Phase Two, the 
Rate Order does not require that timing.  The Rate Order 
specifies that Phase One of the collaborative was to address 
implementation of load following or other enhanced balancing 
services.  Consideration of implementation of enhanced balancing 
services necessitates review of the costs of providing balancing 
services.  The Collaborative Report proposes to expand daily 
balancing to four percent when practicable, which LIPA supports.4  
Second, the collaborative process, while perhaps aiming for 
consensus, provides for resolution of contested issues, such as 
the Swing Charge.  Specifically, the Rate Order notes that the 
then anticipated collaborative report would include “either 
recommendations to us or the positions of the parties in dispute 
for our resolution.”5
