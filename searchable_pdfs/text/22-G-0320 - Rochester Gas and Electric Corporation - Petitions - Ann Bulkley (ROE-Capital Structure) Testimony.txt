Q. 
Please describe the guiding principles to be used in establishing the cost 
23 
of capital for a regulated utility.  
24 
 
The United States Supreme Court’s precedent-setting Hope and Bluefield 
25 
cases established the standards for determining the reasonableness of a 
26 
utility’s allowed ROE.  Among the standards established by the Court in 
27 
those cases are:  (1) consistency with the returns on equity investments in 
28 
other businesses having similar or comparable risks; (2) adequacy of the 
29 
return to support credit quality and access to capital; and (3) an 
30

understanding that the means of arriving at a fair return are not controlling, 
1 
only that the end result leads to just and reasonable rates.2F3  
2 
  
3 
Based on those standards, the Commission’s order in these cases should 
4 
provide the Companies with the opportunity to earn a ROE that is (1) 
5

6 
continue to provide safe, reliable service; (2) sufficient to support the 
7 
financial soundness of the Companies’ operations; and (3) commensurate 
8 
with returns on equity investments in enterprises having comparable risks.

The authorized ROE should enable the Companies to finance capital

the period during which rates are expected to remain in effect.

IX. MULTI-YEAR RATE PLAN  
1 
Q. 
Would a multi-year rate plan impact your ROE recommendation?    
2 
 
Yes, it would.  The Commission has in many cases approved three-year rate 
3 
case settlements that often include stay-out premiums. It is my 
4 
understanding that the Companies will provide three-years of forecast data 
5 
in their rate filings.  In keeping with Commission precedent, a stay-out 
6 
premium would reflect the increased risk faced by the Companies under a 
7 
multi-year rate plan.  
8 
  
9 
Q. 
How has New York typically estimated a stay-out premium?   
10 
 
The New York approach has typically set the measure of the risk and return 
11 
trade-off using one half of the yield spread between a one-year and three-
12 
year Treasury securities.    
13 
  
14 
Q. 
Does one half of the yield spread between one-year and three-year 
15 
Treasuries sufficiently reflect the risk to equity investors inherent in a 
16 
multi-year stay-out?   
17 
 
No.  Any stay-out premium associated with a multi-year rate plan should 
18 
not only compensate investors for changes in the level of interest rates or 
19 
inflation, but also for the potential risk of under-earning that is introduced 
20 
by “staying out.”  By staying out of rate cases, the utility may not fully 
21 
recover material amounts of capital expenditures and may be required to 
22 
absorb losses due to differences between the cost of service established in 
23

the rate plan and actual levels of revenue and expense.  The premium should 
1 
compensate the utility and its investors for these additional risks over and 
2 
above interest rate risk.   
3 
 
4 
Q. 
Have current market conditions increased the risk of a multi-year stay-
5

6 
 
Yes. As noted earlier in my testimony, the Federal Reserve is currently 
7

8

9 
Investors will rotate out of long-term government bonds to avoid being 
10

11

12

13

17
