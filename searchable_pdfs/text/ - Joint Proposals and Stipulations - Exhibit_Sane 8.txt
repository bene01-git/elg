Commissioners, 
 
I write as a party in Case 19-G-0678 to express my concern—echoed by numerous other groups and 
parties therein—that the PSC is in danger of reneging on the duties required of it in this proceeding. 
Those duties include keeping the public informed about its final decision on National Grid’s long-term 
infrastructure options—a decision that would conclude a months-long ordeal in which thousands have 
invested both time and effort, all with the understanding that a democratic, transparent process would 
yield a democratically determined final result. As we enter July with no such decision made, I want to 
remind the commission of the continued requirement for transparency in this case. To provide the public 
with anything less—particularly at this late date—would be a betrayal both of the public’s trust and of the 
terms of the settlement.  
 
I also want to remind you that, unlike a rate case, Case 19-G-0678 is implicitly punitive in nature and 
exists to right the corporate wrongs inflicted on the public by National Grid, whose moratorium, in your 
words, caused “undue hardship to more than 3,700 existing and new customers.” Moreover, it is intended 
to do this by way of the very public participation, input, and reciprocity that National Grid denied its 
customers when it unilaterally decided to shut off their gas. Over 8,000 of those customers and other 
members of the public have filed comments towards that end, to say nothing of the hours spent reading 
documents and attending public meetings related to it. They deserve continued communication regarding 
the status of this case, and, ultimately, to have this process seen through to the end.  
 
It is particularly troubling that the PSC’s silence is coming as it reopens confidential rate case 
negotiations with National Grid, whose profits have suffered because of the COVID-19 pandemic. It is 
not lost on the public that long-term infrastructure is lucrative to utilities, nor that, as this decision has 
been delayed, construction has continued on the widely opposed MRI Pipeline in North Brooklyn (and 
without approved funding). It would not be unreasonable for the public to assume that all of these things 
are related, and that the PSC has decided to ignore both public opinion and climate science in the name of 
protecting shareholders amid a COVID-related state of exception. The PSC must prove this suspicion 
wrong. It must honor the terms of the settlement in Case 19-G-0678, beginning with an effort to update 
the public on the status of the decision.

And please: be wary. The ongoing pandemic must not be used as cover for capitulating to corporate 
interests at the expense of democratic demands, particularly when those demands concern a public health 
crisis—climate change—whose impact stands to be far greater than this virus. I trust that you will not 
abdicate your responsibilities as a regulator in such a way. The public has made its demands perfectly 
clear: no new fossil fuel infrastructure, both for its sake, the planet’s sake, and the sake of your 
grandchildren. We hope that you will honor your commitment to transparency and democracy in Case 
19-G-0678 by seeing it through and delivering on this demand.
