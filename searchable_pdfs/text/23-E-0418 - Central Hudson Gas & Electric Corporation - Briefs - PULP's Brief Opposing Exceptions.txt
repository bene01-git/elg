I. 
INTRODUCTION 
Pursuant to the Notice of Schedule for Filing Exceptions issued May 1, 2024, in the above-
referenced proceeding, the Public Utility Law Project of New York, Inc. ("PULP") hereby 
submits its Brief Opposing Exceptions to the Recommended Decision (“RD”) by Administrative 
Law Judges (“ALJs”) in this proceeding. The Brief Opposing Exceptions does not repeat the 
arguments presented by PULP in its Initial and Reply Briefs, except to the extent necessary to 
respond to the RD. 
 
II.  OPPOSING EXCEPTIONS 
a.  A 9.2% ROE and 48% equity ratio is ample for Central Hudson.  
 
PULP opposes Central Hudson’s exception to the RD as it pertains to the ROE and equity 
ratio. Central Hudson claims in their exceptions brief that the RD is silent on a potential credit 
downgrade due to Staff’s proposal of a 9.2% return on equity (“ROE”) and 48% equity ratio.1 
However, the RD is meticulous in going through both Staff’s and the Company’s arguments and 
proposals regarding these issues.2 As stated by the RD, “the Commission’s overall decision in 
these matters and the resulting rate plans are more likely to impact Central Hudson’s credit rating 
than the adoption of any single element, such as an equity ratio.”3 Further, the RD’s 
recommendation to adopt a 9.2% ROE and 48% equity ratio is consistent with the record, as well 
as PULP’s litigation position through the entirety of this proceeding.4  
The Company’s lack of acknowledgment regarding its issues with the SAP CIS and its 
subsequent impact on its financial situation must be considered when evaluating the ROE. 
Central Hudson customers should not bear the burden of the Company’s self-imposed problems. 
Moreover, as the RD states, the financial strain that Central Hudson may be under does not 
“warrant a departure from the Commission’s express methodological precedent.”5 Therefore, 
PULP opposes Central Hudson’s exception to the 9.2% ROE and 48% equity ratio.

b.  The Company’s exception to the RD’s interpretation of Commission authority 
regarding performance metrics is unpersuasive.  
 
PULP opposes Central Hudson’s exception to the RD’s efforts to impose NRAs in this 
litigated rate case. First, the Company mischaracterizes both the Commission and the Third 
Department’s decisions in Hurley Water, as well as the record in these proceedings.6 In its Brief 
on Exceptions, the Company attempts to distinguish itself by stating that present circumstances 
provide a “series of after-the-fact penalties imposed retroactively based on arbitrary and differing 
standards.”7 However, in Hurley Water, the Commission approved a lower-than-average rate for 
a water utility who was found to have less than adequate service.8 In that matter, the Commission 
ordered that the rate would be adjusted should service levels improve, which means that the rate 
as proposed was meant to encourage the company to improve its service.9  
In the current case, NRA’s offer a similar option to the Commission as they seek to 
“incentivize utilities to provide adequate levels of customer service by aligning shareholder and 
ratepayer interests in assigning financial consequences where a utility fails to provide specified 
levels of service.”10 Similarly, so long as the Company meets the level of service necessary, it too 
will avoid penalty.11 NRAs are necessarily forward looking and ascribing such performance 
metrics without considering a utility’s past performance and a trajectory of growth would 
frustrate the intended purpose of “incentiviz[ing] utilities to provide adequate levels of customer 
service.”12 Therefore, the RD correctly recommended the use of NRAs, which is appropriately in 
line with the Commission’s authority in ordering the use of performance metrics. 
Second, Central Hudson wrongly indicates that the record lacks any evidence which 
supports the proposition that “[v]ery significant differences exist between the state’s utilities that 
create differing expectations for adequate service by each utility’s customer base, Department of 
Public Service Staff, and the Commission.”13 The record includes information demonstrating

c.  The RD appropriately concludes that the Company should post the Attestation 
relating to service terminations on its website. 
 
Central Hudson argues that the RD should not adopt PULP’s recommendation to modify 
the Billing Reporting Stipulation (hereinafter “Stipulation”) by posting the Attestation on their 
public website once it has been executed.23 The Company claims that if the Commission accepts 
this modification, it would undermine the Stipulation itself. Additionally, the Company states  
that there is “no record developed on the costs and benefits” included in the record warranting 
the inclusion of the Attestation on their website.24 PULP objects to the Company’s exception by 
providing the following four arguments. 
First, it is well within the Commission’s power to modify the Stipulation, as the 
agreement explicitly states, “[n]othing in this Stipulation shall be construed to limit the 
Commission's authority under the New York State Public Service Law.”25 The Commission’s 
overarching goal is to ensure that utilities provide safe and reliable service at just and reasonable 
rates. Including the Attestation on the Company’s website supports this goal by promoting 
transparency and accountability. The RD correctly acknowledges this and adds that it “may 
promote awareness about the care the Company will employ regarding service terminations…”.26  
PULP asserts that the simple act of including the executed Attestation on the Company’s 
website is both just and reasonable, aligning with the Commission’s mission. The Attestation 
aims to assure customers that a validated process is in place to prevent shut offs if a residential 
customer’s account has not received an actual meter reading before a final termination notice is 
issued.27 And given the widespread dissatisfaction among Central Hudson’s customer base,28 as 
well as the Company’s recent efforts to resume residential collections, including service 
termination,29 PULP agrees with the RD that posting such an Attestation will increase the 
likelihood that customers see Central Hudson’s commitment to responsible service management.

As such, PULP maintains is in the public interest to include the Attestation on the Company’s 
website, and the Stipulation can and should be modified to include this directive. 
Second, the RD correctly indicates that such a requirement would not be “unduly 
burdensome of the Company or its resources.”30 PULP felt it was important to note that Central 
Hudson failed to offer any sort of cost estimate in its exception to support its claim that posting 
on its website would in fact be cost prohibitive.31 At the same time, PULP doubts that the simple 
task of including an Attestation of the Company’s website would take very much time or cost to 
complete. Moreover, doing so will be in the public interest and outweigh the minimal cost of 
complying with the modification to the Stipulation.  
Third, PULP finds it troubling and confusing that the Company doesn’t intend to post the 
Attestation on their website. If the Company wants to help reassure customers that their bills are 
accurate and that they want to work with them to prevent a service termination, one hopes that 
they’d want to spread the word widely that they are taking steps to ensure accurate billing. 
Instead, their actions herein to limit its communication of important public information is 
worrisome.   
Fourth, PULP once again raises a procedural concern we have with Central Hudson’s 
plans to resume collections activities, including service terminations, while decisions on 
proposals by the Commission are forthcoming.32 PULP is troubled by this as it appears the 
Company is attempting to circumvent rate case process where proposals are still under active 
consideration and review. PULP felt compelled to re-elevate this procedural frustration.33 
 For the reasons stated herein, PULP objects to the Company’s exception and urges the 
Commission to support the RD by requiring Central Hudson to post its attestation on its website.  
 
III.  CONCLUSION 
For the foregoing reasons, the Commission should not grant the exceptions described in 
this Brief Opposing Exceptions and should approve PULP's positions on the matters discussed 
above.
