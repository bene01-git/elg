Q.    
What are SAE variables? 
1 
A. 
As noted in the Panel’s direct testimony, SAE variables are variables that 
2 
aid in addressing structural changes in end-use saturation and efficiency 
3 
trends.  These end-use variables include weather, price, economic drivers 
4 
and end-use saturation and efficiency trends.  Additionally, the electric 
5 
end-use variables constructed for the residential classes reflect changes 
6 
in housing square footage and thermal shell integrity. 
7 
Q.    
Does the SFP use an alternative to the SAE approach? 
8 
A. 
In their testimony, on page 20, the SFP states that they “separated out the 
9 
underlying components of these [SAE] variables and included them as 
10 
distinct explanatory variables on the right hand side of [their] models.” 
11 
However, in response to CH to DPS (061), the SFP clarified they did not 
12 
use any appliance saturation variables in their models and instead 
13 
included only economic, price or weather-related variables. 
14 
Q.    
Why does the Panel continue to support the use of SAE variables as the 
15 
preferred approach to forecasting sales? 
16 
A. 
The SAE approach of end use modeling is a widely recognized method of 
17 
forecasting energy sales.  As described in the Panel’s direct testimony, the 
18 
New York Independent System Operator (“NYISO”) leveraged the SAE 
19 
approach in 2019 when they completed the first phase of a multi-phase 
20 
Climate Change Impact & Resilience Study, which initially attempts to 
21 
assess the potential implications for load associated with climate change
