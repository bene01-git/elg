3

4 
A.  
Advanced leak detection technology and data analytics (“ALD+”) uses high-
5 
sensitivity methane analyzers deployed on survey vehicles to identify where 
6 
natural gas is leaking from distribution systems.  The “advanced” part of this 
7 
terminology refers in part to the methane sensors using a form of cavity-enhanced 
8 
laser-absorption spectroscopy20 that can more accurately and precisely determine 
9 
the concentration of methane in the air.  In contrast to “traditional technology,” 
10 
the sensors in ALD+ can quantify much smaller departures from the local 
11 
background concentration and thus find more leaks.  Moreover, ALD+ uses 
12 
advanced data analytics that examine the overall neighborhood patterns in the 
13 
methane concentration to discern when the concentrations are elevated.  These 
14 
sophisticated analyses can reveal subtler leak information than traditional 
15 
technologies.   
16 
It is important to note that the greater sensitivity of ALD+ allows utilities 
17 
to find more leaks of all sizes and risk categories.  For a variety of reasons, the

IV.   
The Mechanics, Benefits, and Cost Effectiveness of Advanced Leak Detection 
7 
Technology and Data Analytics (“ALD+”) 
8 
Q.   
Could you define what technology would qualify as ALD+? 
9 
A. 
Yes.  At the heart of ALD+ are devices for measuring methane concentration at 
10 
parts per billion (ppb) levels of precision. These devices use a technology known 
11 
as cavity-enhanced laser absorption spectroscopy.  At this time, I am aware of a 
12 
number of manufacturers that commercially produce such instruments for use in 
13 
the natural gas industry, including Picarro, ABB Los Gatos, LiCor and Aeris.23  
14 
These devices all have accuracy and precision better than 10ppb. Although some
