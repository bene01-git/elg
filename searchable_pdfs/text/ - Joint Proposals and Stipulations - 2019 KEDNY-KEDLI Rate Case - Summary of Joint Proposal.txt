Summary of Joint Proposal 
 
The Joint Proposal proposes three-year rate plans for The Brooklyn Union Gas Company 
d/b/a National Grid NY (KEDNY) and KeySpan Gas East Corporation d/b/a National Grid 
(KEDLI) (collectively, the Companies) for the term beginning April 1, 2020 and ending March 
31, 2023.  Rate Year (RY) 1 is April 1, 2020 through March 31, 2021; RY2 is April 1, 2021 through 
March 31, 2022; and RY3 is April 1, 2022 through March 31, 2023.  In addition, the Joint Proposal 
also proposes terms1 that would apply in a Stayout Period that would begin on April 1, 2023 and 
end on the earlier of March 31, 2024 or when the Commission next sets base delivery rates for the 
Companies.

To achieve these outcomes, KEDNY and KEDLI will amortize approximately $94.9 million and 
$4.8 million of regulatory liabilities (deferred credits), respectively, over the term of the rate plans, 
including $41 million from the resolution of enforcement actions,2 which will be used to offset the 
cost of the Companies’ energy efficiency and demand response programs. 
 
Return on Equity (ROE)/Capital Structure: The Joint Proposal proposes an 8.8% ROE and a 
ratemaking capital structure that reflects a common equity component of 48% for both Companies.  
The rate plans propose that customers will share earnings in excess of 9.3%.

Make Whole Provision: Because the Joint Proposal is being submitted in RY2, the Joint Proposal 
proposes a make whole provision to allow the Companies to recover the revenue shortfall resulting 
from the extension of the suspension period such that the Companies are restored to the same 
financial position they would have been in had rates gone into effect on April 1, 2020. 
 
Revenue Allocation/Rate Design:  
• Firm Service: For the firm service classes, the Joint Proposal recommends no increases to 
the minimum monthly charges.  Beginning in RY2, the Joint Proposal recommends rates 
designed in a manner that encourages energy efficiency by including more of the increase 
in the last rate block.  In connection with this change, the Companies will pursue an 
outreach and education program to inform customers of the rate design change, potential 
impacts, and target energy efficiency programs for high usage customers.   
• Non-Firm Demand Response Service: To encourage customers to remain on or switch 
to interruptible service, the Joint Proposal recommends Tier 1 and Tier 2 interruptible 
services that will have volumetric delivery rates set at discounts of 50 percent and 60 
percent, respectively, below the tail block volumetric rate of the applicable equivalent firm 
service class. 
 
Demand Capacity Surcharge Mechanism: To effectuate the recovery of costs associated with 
incremental demand response, energy efficiency, and Long-Term Capital Capacity Projects 
identified in Case 19-G-0678 as solutions to address the gap between the Companies’ supply of 
natural gas and the forecast of customers’ peak firm demand, the Joint Proposal proposes that the 
Companies be permitted to implement a surcharge beginning in RY2. 
 
Energy Efficiency: The Companies’ annual Energy Efficiency costs authorized in Case 18-M-
0084 will be collected in base rates.  The Companies will be permitted to recover any other 
approved Energy Efficiency costs through the Demand Capacity Surcharge Mechanism, including 
any additional or accelerated Commission-approved New Efficiency:New York (“NE:NY”)budget 
(or other Commission-approved energy efficiency programs) not reflected in base rates.   
 
Gas Safety and Reliability Surcharge: The Gas Safety and Reliability Surcharge will continue 
to allow the Companies to proactively replace LPP and repair leaks, but will not permit the 
Companies to earn a positive revenue adjustment for LPP replacement above the proposed targets.

• Long-Term Capital Capacity Projects: The Companies will be permitted to recover 
Long-Term Capital Capacity Projects identified in the Joint Proposal through the Demand 
Capacity Surcharge Mechanism.  These projects will be subject to an additional 
independent assessment process, and recovery is conditioned upon the Companies 
achieving metric targets for energy efficiency, demand response, non-pipe, third party 
solutions, electrification and NPAs for leak prone pipe.  
• MRI Phase 5: Work on MRI Phase 5 has been halted.  If KEDNY decides to pursue Phase 
5 of the MRI Project, it must file a petition seeking approval to recover the costs of the 
project. 
 
Gas Operations Items: 
• Relocation of Inside Gas Meters: KEDNY and Staff will use good faith efforts to meet 
with NYC on a periodic basis to discuss issues related to relocating inside meters in NYC 
and work to reach a mutually agreeable resolution.   
• Additional Gas Safety Programs: The Joint Proposal proposes that the Companies 
establish the following gas safety programs in RY2: a Pipeline Safety Management and 
Damage Prevention Advisor Program; an Instrumentation and Regulation Training and 
Test Lab; Gas Control Standard Operating Procedure Training; Field Evaluation Training; 
and a Plastic Fusion in Process Inspection Program. 
 
Information Technology: The rates proposed in the Joint Proposal reflect incremental IT capital 
investment of $150 million in each of the RYs.  These costs do not include those associated with 
the Customer Information System (CIS). 
• CIS: The Companies may file a petition with the Commission setting forth their proposed 
CIS project and seeking authorization to defer CIS-related developmental costs. 
 
Gas Business Enablement (GBE): For purposes of determining the Companies’ allocable shares 
of GBE Program costs, the total Service Company costs, comprised of capital and project operating 
expenses related to capital expenses for the GBE Program will be capped at $515.5 million for the 
work identified by the Companies to be conducted through Rate Year Three. 
 
Future of Heat: The Joint Proposal proposes that the Companies will operate their gas networks 
with the objective of reducing billed gas usage compared to forecasted usage, normalized for 
temperatures, over the term of the rate plans and provides for new programs and initiatives to 
promote long-term emissions reductions from the gas networks. 
• Marketing: The Companies are not currently conducting marketing for new gas 
connections and conversions, including any customer rebates for heating oil-to-gas 
conversions or new gas customers, and commit to not resume gas marketing through the 
term of the rate plans. 
• Alternate Heating Options: The Companies commit to developing educational materials, 
in coordination with their energy efficiency and demand response programs, that will 
inform customers of alternative heating options, including air- and ground-source heat 
pumps and district geothermal heating systems.   
• Newtown Creek Project: KEDNY’s Newtown Creek project, located at the NYC 
Newtown Creek Wastewater Treatment Plant, will capture the biogas generated from the

wastewater plant, filter it through a purification system, and upgrade it to pipeline quality 
natural gas.  The gas can then be odorized and injected into KEDNY’s distribution system. 
• Hydrogen Blending Research: The Companies will conduct a hydrogen blending study 
to consider the actions necessary to prepare the gas system for the introduce of hydrogen.  
A grant from NYSERDA will fund a portion of the study.   
• Geothermal Non-Pipe Alternatives: The Companies will support the deployment of 
geothermal heating as a non-pipe alternative in their service territories, including by 
identify instances where planned leak prone pipe replacement projects could be avoided by 
deploying shared geothermal loops or individual ground- or air-source heat pumps to serve 
affected customers.   
• CLCPA Study: Prior to their next base rate filings, but in no event later than the end of 
Rate Year Three, the Companies will complete a report that evaluates how the Companies’ 
businesses may evolve in the future to support the emission reduction and renewable 
energy goals of the CLCPA, NYC’s Local Law 97, and any emission reduction goals the 
Companies have independently adopted.   
• Depreciation Study: The Companies will complete and file, at least three months before 
their next base rate filings, a study on the potential depreciation impacts of climate change 
policies and laws – including the CLCPA and Local Law 97 – and the statewide GHG 
emission limits established therein – on their gas assets.   
• Additional Reporting Requirements including reporting on new gas connections costs 
and commitments for the next rate filing, including a climate assessment of investments 
and initiatives. 
 
Earnings Adjustment Mechanisms: The Joint Proposal proposes adoption of three EAMs: (1) 
Energy Efficiency Share The Savings, (2) Low and Moderate Income Energy Efficiency and (3) 
Gas Peak Reduction.  
 
Reconciliations: The Joint Proposal includes a number of reconciliations such as Energy 
Affordability Program, SIR expense, and Property Tax expenses, Negative and Positive Revenue 
Adjustments, Net-plant expenses, and City/State Construction capital costs. 
 
SIR Costs: The Joint Proposal provides for annual reconciliation of SIR costs and, for KEDNY 
only, a continuation of an available SIR surcharge to accommodate the variable costs associated 
with SIR projects in New York City.  Similar to what is currently applicable, the surcharge would 
be triggered if the reconciliation between the rate allowance and actual costs exceeds $25M (on a 
cumulative basis), and is capped at 2% of KEDNY’s prior year aggregate revenues. 
 
Customer Service Quality Program: The Joint Proposal continues and updates the following 
customer service metrics: PSC Complaint Rate, the Residential Customer Satisfaction Survey, 
adjusted customer bills, and telephone answer response within 30 seconds.  The Joint Proposal 
proposes total potential Negative Revenue Adjustments (NRAs) of $9.90 million (KEDLI) and 
$11.7 million (KEDNY) per year. 
 
Gas Safety Performance Metrics: The Joint Proposal continues and updates existing metrics: 
LPP removal; leak management; damage prevention; emergency response; and gas safety

Customer Initiatives:  
• E-Deferred Payment Agreement (“E-DPA”) Reports: The Companies will permit 
residential customers to negotiate E-DPAs over the telephone, with the resulting E-DPA 
provided to the customer for review and electronic signature via email or other electronic 
means. 
• Energy and Affordability Program (“EAPs”): The annual rate allowances for KEDNY 
and KEDLI EAPs increased to $38.45 million and $6.75 million, respectively.  The 
Companies’ EAPs and discount calculations are subject to modification in Cases 14-M-
0565 or 20-M-0266. 
• EAP File Match: The Companies agree to increase the frequency of their file match with 
NYC to four times per year and to collaborate with Nassau and Suffolk Counties and the 
Office of Temporary Disability Assistance in an effort to expand file matching in those 
counties. 
• Reconnection Fees: KEDNY will waive all reconnection fees for EAP participants as 
KEDLI does now. 
• Energy Efficiency Program Participation: The Companies will offer their energy 
efficiency programs to non-firm customers and Baseload Distribution Generation Program 
customers. 
• Economic Development Programs: Annual economic development funding will be set at 
$2.25 million for KEDNY and $2.75 million for KEDLI, which will be largely funded 
through the amortization of prior economic development deferral credits.  During the term 
of the rate plans, both firm and non-firm non-residential customers will be eligible to 
participate in the Companies’ economic development grant and discount programs.
