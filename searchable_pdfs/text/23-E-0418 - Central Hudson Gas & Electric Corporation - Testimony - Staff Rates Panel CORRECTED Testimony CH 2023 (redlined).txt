Embedded Cost of Service Studies 
11 
Q. 
Please briefly describe the purpose of an ECOS 
12 
study. 
13 
A. 
An ECOS study provides a comparative analysis of 
14 
the cost of providing utility services to each 
15 
customer class, and the rate of return, or ROR, 
16 
from each class.  It is based on an analysis of 
17 
the rate base, operating expenses, and revenues 
18 
for a prior calendar year or forecasted RY.  The 
19 
three major steps in an ECOS study are 
20 
functionalization and classification of costs to 
21 
operating functions, and allocation of those 
22 
costs to customer classes. 
23 
Q. 
What does an ECOS study provide? 
24 


--- Page 13 ---
Cases 23-E-0418 & 23-G-0419 
 
Staff Rates Panel 
 
 
12 
 
A. 
The final output of an ECOS study contains a 
1 
summary of the individual class rates of return, 
2 
which indicates the level each class contributes 
3 
to the total system rate of return.  The rate of 
4 
return for a class is an effective way to 
5 
measure how well the class revenues align to the 
6 
Company’s costs to provide service to that 
7 
particular customer class.  The study also 
8 
provides an analysis to establish the 
9 
appropriate revenue targets for bill printing, 
10 
mailing and receipt services, merchant function 
11 
charge, referred to as MFC, uncollectibles, and 
12 
credit and collection functions.   
13 
Q. 
What is a “tolerance band” and why is it used?  
14 
A. 
A tolerance band is a certain percentage above 
15 
and below the system average rate of return 
16 
determined through the ECOS study.  Service 
17 
classes are considered deficient or in surplus 
18 
if the service class’s specific rate of return 
19 
falls outside the tolerance band.  The purpose 
20 
of a tolerance band is to account for the 
21 
imprecise nature of an ECOS study.  Central 
22 
Hudson proposes to use a tolerance band of 15 
23 
percent for electric and gas.
