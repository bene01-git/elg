Purpose  
1 
Q. 
What is the purpose of the Panel’s testimony in this proceeding? 
2 
A. 
The purpose of the Panel’s direct testimony is to describe the investments the 
3 
Company has made and will continue to make to provide enhanced customer 
4 
engagement platforms and opportunities supporting Clean Energy initiatives and 
5 
strengthening its relationships with its electric and gas customers. 
6 
These investments can be classified into the following three categories: 
7 
1. Enhancements to Customer Facing Applications: Investments in 
8 
platforms and programs that are used by the Company to interact 
9 
and communicate directly with its customers (e.g., Customer 
10 
Engagement Marketplace Platform, Customer Online Community).  
11 
2. Upgrades to Underlying Foundational Technologies and Systems: 
12 
Investments in the technologies and systems used by the Company 
13 
to serve the needs of its customers (e.g., Customer Service System 
14 
Implement Project, Enterprise Scheduling System, and data 
15 
analytics).   
16 
3. Programmatic Modifications: Modifications to existing 
17 
operations/processes to improve the efficiency and convenience of 
18 
the services/programs the Company offers to customers (e.g., 
19 
Energy Efficiency Programs, and Low Income Bill Discount 
20 
Program).

4 
A. 
A regulatory asset is an accounting treatment arising in instances where a utility 
5 
incurs a cost that is typically not treated as a capital expenditure. However, 
6 


--- Page 67 ---
 
ORANGE AND ROCKLAND UTILITIES, INC.

4 
The regulatory asset appears on the utility’s balance sheet and represents the costs 
5 
that have been incurred by the utility but have not yet been recovered from 
6 
customers.

Work Description:  
The Company plans to test Oracle’s OPower behavioral demand response (BDR) software 
as a Pilot Program to reduce demand on peak days targeting the residential sector.  We are 
currently using Oracle’s OPower software to reduce annual energy use with customized 
messaging to customers using a treatment and control group.   
 
For our current behavioral program that reduces overall annual energy consumption, the 
treatment group receives print and electronic messaging including recommendations for 
easy steps to reduce usage, while the control group receives no messaging.  The usage in 
these groups is then compared to determine if the treatment group reacted to the messaging 
and lowered their usage.  For 2020, O&R electric and gas customer treatment groups have 
reduced their usage by 0.8% and 0.4% compared to the electric and gas control groups, 
respectively.   
 
For the behavioral software program targeting peak demand savings, messaging will be 
sent the day before a forecast peak day for the gas and electric system, with tips on how 
customers can avoid using energy during the period of the day that is designated to be a 
peak period.  Customers will then be notified after the event on how they performed to give 
near-real time feedback to customers and to encourage positive behavior.  
 
Gas and Electric Behavioral Demand Response 
The OPower BDR software programs use thoughtful communications that employ proven 
behavioral science tactics, which encourage customers to reduce their energy usage during 
peak events and lower energy demand on a large scale, without the need for devices or 
special services.

creates the lowest possible barrier to entry for residential customers to contribute to load 
reductions and helps utilities realize the full potential of available DR resources. This 
unique ability to reach targeted customers also offers equity among residential customers to 
participate in DR.

Hardware and Data Model:  As mentioned, the Con Edison and O&R legacy CSSs’ 
are based on a mainframe computing platform. Even though smaller computers have 
the necessary capabilities today, the legacy CSS and program applications are entirely 
mainframe dependent. The legacy CSS data model is a “premise-based” design in 
which the data collected indicates energy usage by a specific building or residence, as 
opposed to a modern “customer-based” approach used by more technologically 
advanced CSS solutions.  These customer-based data models focus on understanding 
energy usage on a customer-specific (rather than premise-specific) basis. Thus, the 
functionality required of the legacy CSS is limited to processing the monthly meter 
reads and providing basic details regarding the customer’s energy usage and location.   
• 
Antiquated Computer Languages:  The legacy software that runs and maintains the 
legacy CSS is written in Common Business-Oriented Language (“COBOL”) as its core 
programming language.  COBOL dates back to 1959, and its use today is limited to 
legacy mainframe systems in finance, banking, utilities and other sectors. Proficiency 
in other legacy programming languages such as ASSEMBLER and Random Access 
Management Information System (“RAMIS”), in which legacy CSS programs were 
originally developed, continues to diminish similar to COBOL.  Finally, modern CSS 
solutions available in the market employ a system configuration approach, rather than 
customized programming.  The advantage of a system configuration approach is that 
it is easier to implement changes rather than engaging in customized system 
reprogramming.  This is expected to translate into less costly system changes, 
improved knowledge transfer and access to a larger pool of system support resources. 
• 
People:  Maintaining the legacy CSS requires the Company to train and maintain 
technical staff competent in these older programming languages and mainframe 
systems. This is becoming more difficult as the availability of business analysts and 
application developers who are familiar with these languages and technology 
becomes more limited each year. This attrition of skilled developers makes it very

The Company has a significant amount of data about its customers, including data on energy 
consumption, payment history, field visits, rate/program enrollment (e.g., energy efficiency 
programs, time of use (“TOU”) rates, level payment plan, low income discounts), and the type and 
channel of historical interactions with the Company (including detailed interactive voice response 
(“IVR”) and web logs). This valuable data currently resides in numerous internal systems and 
databases. Through the Data Analytics program, O&R will employ modern data analytics platforms 
to connect these data sources, and sort through the resulting data to identify patterns, trends, 
correlations and relationships. The Company can use this connected data to develop a better 
understanding of customer pain points experienced while interacting with the Company and 
predict current and future customer needs. Examples of the types of actionable insights that the 
Company seeks to develop through the Data Analytics program include the following: 
 
• 
Customer segmentation on attributes such as behavior, sentiment, communications 
preferences, and energy needs, in order to target customers for programs and services that 
will meet their needs. 
• 
Propensity to adopt certain programs (e.g., eBill, auto-pay, payment assistance, time- 
variant rates) to achieve more timely and effective customer target marketing, program 
adoption and customer service. 
• 
Next Best Action (“NBA”) recommendations that can be offered to customers via self- 
service tools and Company employees. NBA is a customer-centric model that evaluates a 
customer’s past behavior, recent actions, interests, and needs to identify the most effective 
action to achieve desired outcomes. It considers the different actions that can be taken by a 
specific customer and decides the 'best' one. Examples include using NBA models to 
prompt a Customer Service Representative (“CSR”) to make specific program, service 
offering, or account resolution recommendations and presenting these same options to a 
given customer on the web. 
• 
Speech / natural language analytics to understand the purpose of a customer’s call, 
sentiment, resolution, and satisfaction.  
• 
Quality assurance / fraud reviews, to facilitate detection of employee or customer 
fraud. For example, data analytics can be used to identify accounts or employees who 
exhibit unusual types of behavior over time such as repetitive refunds, transfers, or 
improper supervisory approval.  
• 
Operational analytics to measure process efficiency and compliance, as well as identify 
areas for improvement or the need for new solutions. For example, deep call analytics 
can provide insight into which types of calls are resulting in back office cases, and what 
the average resolution time is for such cases. 
• 
Cross-channel analytics (e.g., web, IVR, email, phone, text, mobile apps) to understand 
better a customer’s path and intent, to allow for quicker service by a CSR, or to conduct 
a root cause analysis of customer pain points during a self-service process. For example, 
cross-channel analytics will be used to determine where in a given web transaction 
customers are experiencing a problem that in turn results in a CSR-based phone call. 
Such insights can be used by a CSR to understand that a customer was on the web, 
what they were trying to complete, and allow the CSR to resolve the issue more quickly 
and effectively. In addition, the Company can use this insight to identify an issue with 
the web transaction, which resulted in the customer needing to call instead of 
completing the transaction in a self-service channel

Work Description: 
 
The DCX program is a multi-year program that will continue to refine and build upon the digital 
platform developed during the current rate plan. The Company has already improved the online 
customer experience by redesigning the www.oru.com, www.coned.com, and www.conedison.com 
external websites and introduced a mobile-enabled design, My Account portal, and mobile apps 
(“IOS” and “Android”).  
 
DCX is an enterprise-wide joint program in which O&R and Consolidated Edison Company of New 
York, Inc. (“CECONY”) teams work closely together to refine the current platform and execute various 
initiatives, while also aligning initiative with each company’s unique needs. The project costs are being 
shared between CECONY and O&R based on the shared service accounting rules whereby the split is 
92.75%/7.25%. O&R will continue its successful collaboration with CECONY on the various focus 
areas for DCX in the upcoming Rate Years, as described further below.  
 
DCX will continue to be guided by these principles during Rate Years 1-3:  
 
•Customer First – Through surveys, in-depth interviews and journey mapping, customers are guiding 
the Company to a best-in-class digital customer experience. 
•Simplicity – Customers have indicated through usability testing and benchmarking that they expect 
simple and intuitive experiences when interacting with their utility. 
•Personalization – Utilizing data-enabled analytics and customer-driven optionality to provide 
customers with relevant content and tailored solutions. 
•One Company – DCX enables a collective, cross-functional approach and delivers a digital 
experience that is consistent across the entire website/app and for both O&R and CECONY.

•Security – Safeguarding customer information while maximizing usability is a central objective of 
DCX. 
 
The DCX program will continue to use a customer centric, “Agile” project management approach (i.e., 
an iterative and incremental method of managing the design and build of the digital platform) that will 
adapt project scopes to changing priorities based on customer feedback and analytics. The Company 
will engage customers in the development and design of strategic new offerings through various types 
of usability testing, including prototyping. In light of this flexible Agile project management approach, 
the Company will continue to update Department of Public Service Staff and stakeholders on the 
evolution of the DCX program through quarterly reports.  
 
Key focus areas of the efforts to optimize and expand O&R’s digital platforms during Rate Years 1-3 
include the following:  
 
• Ongoing Optimization: Embedded in the DCX work process is an ongoing review of customer 
feedback through the use of the “provide feedback” link throughout the web and mobile experience, 
post chat and transactional surveys, and focus groups. In addition, website analytics are continually 
reviewed to develop actionable insights. This information will the help Company identify 
opportunities to benefit customers and remove transactional friction points. Examples include changes 
to the placement of text, buttons and icons to make it easier for customers to find information and 
links, and streamlining of transactions flows (i.e. start service or report outage) to remove known pain 
points or to optimize business rules to allow for improved service.  
• Transactional Expansion: DCX will expand online self-service offerings to include transactions that 
are only available through a Customer Service Representative (“CSR”) today as well as new content to 
deliver additional value to specific customer segments. Examples include appointment scheduling and 
service reconnects, and expansion of commercial customer offerings such as improved data 
visualization and multi-account portfolio management.  
• Data Sharing: The Company seeks to expand business-to-business data sharing capabilities through 
the expansion of Green Button Connect (“GBC”) data sets and opening secure application 
programming interfaces (“API”) for additional data exchange opportunities with interested customers 
and third parties.  
• Technology Upgrades: To meet customers’ expectations, the digital platform must maintain a high 
level of reliability, and an error-free experience. To accomplish this, the Company will make upgrades 
to the Web Experience Management (“WEM”) platform. Upgrades to the WEM platform will unlock 
new features and capabilities of our Sitecore Content Management System that have not been utilized 
to date, such as recent improvements to Sitecore Analytics Platform that will enable greater 
personalization opportunities. DCX will evaluate future upgrade features and capabilities to plan for 
timely upgrades so that, as technology evolves, O&R sites have the capability to keep up with industry 
changes and customer expectations. DCX will also invest in the development of WEM functionality for 
content authors to allow for easier distribution of content across all digital channels and media, 
providing improved consistency across the digital experience.  
• Migrating remaining ancillary legacy web applications: The DCX program will continue to migrate 
remaining legacy web applications and customer experiences to its DCX platform.  
• Mobile App Services: Enhancements will also be made to the mobile app functionality, including 
development of key features as they are expanded across the digital platforms, as noted above (e.g., 
implementation of new transactional offerings, content optimization, etc.). The Company also plans to 
explore and leverage mobile specific services such as use of a Global Position System (“GPS”) to report 
an outage, push notifications for alerts, and use of mobile cameras to submit outage/safety (e.g., wires 
down) related issues. The initial mobile applications have been well received by customers with an

Work Description: 
 
The Company is requesting funding for one FTE to initiate the Customer Journey Mapping 
program. Customer journey mapping is a business improvement methodology that can deepen the 
Company’s understanding of its customers and their expectations. The customer journey mapping 
process is defined below. 
 
The first step of the Customer Journey process is the current state assessment. This requires 
identifying the critical activities that make-up the customer experience and documenting all the 
associated interactions for each activity. These interactions, displayed in graphical, chronological 
form, are the “touchpoints” that make up a “journey map.” It is important to identify touchpoints 
throughout the entire journey. For example, a customer journey map could show touchpoints 
across an entire planned outage – from the Company’s announcement of a planned outage, 
through the system shut down and reboot, and any follow-up communications. Other example 
journeys may include but could change over time: 
 
1. Sign-up for Service and Onboarding; 
2. Outage Communications; 
3. Billing and Payment Assistance; 
4. Billing and Payment Process; 
5. Energy Efficiency and Management; and 
6. Account Changes. 
 
Once the customer journey maps have been documented, data must be collected for each 
touchpoint throughout the map. This can be performed in a variety of ways, depending on rigor 
and data availability. For the Company, this could mean performing voice of the customer surveys, 


--- Page 149 ---
Exhibit___CSP-1 
Page 64 of 91 
 
64 
 
benchmarking the Joint Utilities3 and other peers, tracking key performance indicators, leveraging 
the proposed “Online Community”, and hosting customer workshops and interviews. Once data is 
collected, the next step is to analyze the data to better understand the most important and 
impactful interactions – and any associated paint points – between a company and its customers.  
 
Once the journeys have been mapped and data collected and analyzed, the next step of the effort is 
future state design. Based upon the findings during the current state assessment, companies design 
implementation plans for initiatives to address any discovered pain points and improve the 
customer experience. It is important to prioritize these initiatives based on a variety of factors, 
including the number of customers impacted, and the investment required. Timing must be 
considered as the Company works to deploy the new billing system in the middle of 2021.  Some 
examples of initiatives include: 
 
• 
Developing a standard payment engine across online channels; and 
• 
Deploying  improved outage communications.  
 
Beyond the future state design of each initiative is implementation, which is not included as part of 
this request.  
 
Beginning in 2022, the Company plans to design and initiate the Customer Journey Mapping 
program. The Company will learn from industry leaders to follow best practices and procedures 
for customer journey mapping. Con Edison, in particular, has a robust Customer Journey Mapping

Work Description: 
This project is to automate billing changes for Mass Market Net Energy Metering (“NEM”) customers 
and establish a Value Stack credit posting program that will accurately post manually calculated 
billing credits to Remote Crediting and Community Solar satellite accounts monthly. Billing 
automation of the Mass Market NEM Changes will include system changes made to our Customer 
Information Management System (CIMS) to provide accurate billing. These changes will allow mass 
market customers to continue to participate in Distributed Generation (DG) and provide those 
customers with optionality for how they are billed for their monthly usage and credited for their 
monthly generation. Per Case 15-E-0751: NEM Successor Tariff, mass market customers with system 
ratings of 750 KW or less are eligible to keep Phase One NEM compensation for their monthly 
generation, but the compensation will vary depending on how the customer chooses to be billed for 
their energy. The Phase One NEM Compensation will vary between Monetary and Value Stack 
Credits. All customers, regardless of billing and compensation rate, all new DG customers 
interconnected after 1/1/2022 will be assessed the new Customer Benefit Contribution (“CBC”) charge 
so that they are contributing to the funding of public benefit programs.  
 
The Value Stack credit posting program will assist Customer Accounting in posting large quantities of 
manually calculated credits. Credits will be calculated manually using the existing Value Stack 
Calculator. The new credit posting program will allow Customer Accounting to upload the credit 
information for each satellite account by component, where they will be held and tracked on a table to 
CIMS. These credits are then applied to the satellite accounts as they go through billing, applying the 
credits and debits to the correct General Ledger accounts associated with each Value Stack component. 
The table with value stack credit information can also be exported to excel and used in Customer 
Accounting monthly for reporting.
