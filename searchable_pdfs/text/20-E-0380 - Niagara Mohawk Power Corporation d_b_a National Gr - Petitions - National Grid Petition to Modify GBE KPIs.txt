INTRODUCTION 
 
Niagara Mohawk Power Corporation d/b/a National Grid, The Brooklyn Union Gas 
Company d/b/a National Grid NY, and KeySpan Gas East Corporation d/b/a National Grid 
(collectively, the “Company” or “National Grid”) submit this petition requesting that the New 
York Public Service Commission (“Commission”) approve revisions to certain Gas Business 
Enablement (“GBE”) program key performance indicators (“KPIs”).  National Grid’s current rate 
plans authorize the Company and Department of Public Service Staff (“DPS Staff”) to revise the 
GBE KPIs if they are no longer reasonable measures of performance.  The revisions, which are 
supported by DPS Staff, correct errors made in the original assumptions and definitions, making 
the KPIs more accurate measures of performance.

BACKGROUND 
The GBE program is a comprehensive framework of new information technology solutions 
to strengthen and improve National Grid’s performance.  Among other things, the GBE program 
replaces manual, paper-driven processes with modern technical solutions.  Under the terms of its rate 
plans, the Company is authorized to recover GBE program costs subject to certain customer 
protections designed to ensure the program delivers the intended benefits to customers.1  The 
protections include a GBE program cost cap, downward-only tracker, and the following six KPIs 
measuring operational, customer, and safety performance: 
i. 
Work Management – Customer Meter Services (“CMS KPI”) 
ii. 
Work Management – Maintenance and Construction (“M&C KPI”) 
iii. 
Engineering and Back Office 
iv. 
Customer Call Volume 
 
v. 
Customer Experience Rating (“Customer Experience KPI”) 
vi. 
Gas Safety and Compliance 
 
The GBE KPIs were first included in the 2017 NMPC Joint Proposal2 and then extended to 
KEDNY and KEDLI in the 2019 KEDNY/KEDLI Joint Proposal.3  The KPIs are measured one-
time, in the 12-month period ending March 31, 2024 (“FY24”), which is when the GBE program

Typically, KPIs are set based on an historic baseline of performance supported by 
established ways of working.  This traditional approach posed a challenge with the GBE KPIs 
because the system would not be in service for nearly seven years.  Therefore, the Joint Proposals 
contained a mechanism that authorized the Company and Staff, by mutual agreement, to propose 
adjustments to the KPIs, if necessary, to ensure they remained a reasonable measure of 
performance.  Specifically, the 2017 NMPC and the 2019 KEDNY/KEDLI Joint Proposals 
provide: 
In the event of any unforeseen circumstances, changes in law or regulation, or other 
events that impact the underlying assumptions used to develop the GBE KPI 
performance targets, such that the KPI targets are no longer reasonable measures 
of the Company’s performance, the Company and Staff may, by mutual agreement 
subject to Commission approval, equitably adjust the GBE KPI targets upward or 
downward to address such changes.4 
 
The 2017 NMPC Joint Proposal included a document entitled, “GBE Program Niagara 
Mohawk KPI Descriptions and Calculations,” that explained the methodology to calculate the 
KPIs specific to NMPC (“KPI Descriptions”).5  Additionally, NMPC made a filing in the 2017 
NMPC Rate Case, dated April 13, 2018, that provided additional detail on inputs to certain KPIs 
(“April 2018 NMPC Report”) (the KPI Descriptions and April 2018 NMPC Report are collectively 
referred to as the “NMPC KPI Document”).  To assist with monitoring the need for changes, the 
Joint Proposals contained annual KPI reporting requirements.

In preparing the report documenting KEDNY and KEDLI’s GBE program KPI 
performance for the first time in May 2022, the Company closely examined the data and inputs, 
and discovered inconsistencies in how the initial baseline targets were derived for the M&C and 
CMS KPIs along with how the calculation methodology was described in the NMPC KPI 
Document for NMPC.  The Company also discovered that the 2019 KEDNY/KEDLI Joint 
Proposal inadvertently did not include a corresponding KEDNY and KEDLI KPI Document to 
explain the calculations for KEDNY and KEDLI.  The Company convened a meeting with DPS 
Staff to discuss the issues and the Company’s proposed resolution.  DPS Staff conducted a 
thorough review of the KPIs, asked discovery questions, and proposed a change to the Company’s 
proposal.  
The revisions proposed in this petition reflect the combined efforts of the Company and 
DPS Staff.  Both parties agree that revisions are necessary to (i) the M&C KPI for NMPC, 
KEDNY, and KEDLI; (ii) the CMS KPI for NMPC; and (iii) minor clarifying edits to the Customer 
Experience KPI for NMPC, KEDNY, and KEDLI.  Further, the Company and DPS Staff agree 
that the NMPC KPI Document should be updated, and a corresponding KEDNY and KEDLI KPI 
Document created, to better document the process.  A redlined and a clean version of the NMPC 
KPI Document reflecting the updates agreed upon by the Company and DPS Staff is included in 
Attachments 1 and 2, respectively.  A KEDNY and KEDLI KPI Document is included in 
Attachment 3.  
The proposed KPI revisions and rationale are discussed in the sections that follow.  As will 
be seen, the changes adhere to the intent of the Joint Proposals and ensure that the KPIs are 
correctly measuring the Company’s performance.

In reviewing the source files for NMPC, the Company discovered three main flaws with

Document specifically excluded contractors from the calculation, the baseline included main 
installation mileage associated with both contractors and in-house crews.  Third, while the NMPC 
KPI Document references 240 working days, that variable was not used in the calculation to derive 
the baseline.

B. 
Revised Calculations for NMPC, KEDNY, and KEDLI 
In light of the issues identified above, the Company and DPS Staff considered the following 
three options before mutually agreeing that a revised calculation best met the intent of the GBE 
KPIs and the interest of customers. 
 Leave Calculation as is and do Nothing.  This option was rejected because the historic 
calculation utilized by NMPC does not align with the NMPC KPI Document.  The 
historic calculation includes both in-house and contractor crews, does not adjust crew 
size, and does not use 240 working days.  In addition, the historic calculation is based 
on an average feet per day assumption that came from Massachusetts data; therefore, it 
is not a reasonable measure of New York performance.  Further, the NMPC KPI 
Document methodology could not be applied to KEDNY and KEDLI because those 
companies primarily use contractor crews (as does NMPC) – and that data was included 
in the baseline.

and the Company also added as an appendix to both the NMPC and KEDNY and KEDLI KPI 
Documents a spreadsheet that details the calculation, further enhancing transparency.  Lastly, the 
revisions provide a consistent methodology across the three companies.  For all these reasons, the 
revisions to the M&C GBE KPI result in a more accurate measure of performance.

Revised Calculation for NMPC  
In light of these issues, the Company and DPS Staff considered the following three options: 
 Leave Calculation as is and do Nothing.  This option was rejected because the historic

 Calculate per the NMPC KPI Document.  This option was similarly rejected because 
it would change how the baseline was derived, resulting in NMPC’s actual performance 
greatly exceeding the target (i.e., NMPC’s FY22 performance would be 8.02).  Such a 
result would render the KPI effectively meaningless.   
 Revise the Existing Calculation.  This is the recommended option.  The revision adds 
electric and gas CMS jobs to the numerator, aligning it with the denominator, which 
already included electric and gas CMS FTEs.  This results in a like for like 
measurement.  The revised calculation is included in Attachments 1 and 2 for NMPC.   
The benefits of the Company and DPS Staff’s revision is that it is a simple fix that brings 
consistency to the calculation.  It also eliminates the need to include a collections assumption as 
now all jobs are included in the metric.  Inasmuch as the GBE program benefits both electric and 
gas CMS employees in NMPC’s service territory, the change aligns with the intent of the KPI, 
which is to measure NMPC’s actual performance.  The revision updates the “Jobs” as those listed 
in the April 2018 NMPC Report were pre-GBE and have changed since the GBE program was 
implemented.  While no change is required for KEDNY and KEDLI, there is a need for a KEDNY

3.  Customer Experience KPI 
 
The Customer Experience KPI measures the overall satisfaction of the Company’s 
residential customers captured in the “Ease of Doing Business” survey.  The Company and Staff 
propose two minor revisions to the NMPC KPI Document.   
The first revision reflects that all residential customers, both electric and gas, are included

CONCLUSION 
The 2017 NMPC Joint Proposal and the 2019 KEDNY and KEDLI Joint Proposals 
recognized the likelihood that changes would be needed to the GBE program KPIs given the time 
between when the metrics were set and GBE implementation.  The changes mutually agreed upon 
by the Company and DPS Staff correct inconsistencies due to enhancements in assumptions and 
data collection that, while reasonable at the time the KPIs were adopted, are no longer reasonable.  
Importantly, the changes ensure that the KPIs correctly measure the Company’s performance in a 
manner that fully adheres with the intent of the Joint Proposals.  The changes provide the added 
benefit of having documentation in place that conforms with how the KPIs are actually calculated, 
enabling a path forward to support any future reviews of the metrics.  For all these reasons, the 
Company and DPS Staff respectfully request that the Commission approve the instant petition.
