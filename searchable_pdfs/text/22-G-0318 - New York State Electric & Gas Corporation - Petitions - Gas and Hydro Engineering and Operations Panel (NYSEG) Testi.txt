V. 
CLEAN ENERGY AND METHANE EMISSIONS 
15 
Q. 
What actions has NYSEG taken to reduce methane emissions consistent with the goals of 
16 
the CLCPA? 
17 
A. 
The Company’s leak prone pipe replacement program continues to reduce methane 
18 
emissions.  NYSEG completed removing all of its cast iron pipe (a significant source of 
19 
natural gas leaks) in 2015, and has advanced removal of other types of leak prone pipe on 
20 
an aggressive schedule.  As a result, NYSEG has significantly reduced the number of 
21 
leaks discovered annually from over 1,300 in 2004 to approximately 150 in 2021.

4

Q. 
What clean technologies is NYSEG Gas considering for future implementation? 
7 
A. 
NYSEG Gas is exploring renewable natural gas (“RNG”) as a logical and actionable 
8 
technology.  Sources typically will include gas from landfills or anaerobic digestors.  
9 
Methane emissions from all sources (including conventional natural gas and renewable 
10 
natural gas) have a GHG emissions impact of 86 times carbon dioxide (“CO2”) in a 25-
11 
year timeframe (86 CO2e).  Therefore, every therm of methane sequestered from the 
12 
atmosphere in the form of RNG and used in the natural gas system, ultimately combusted 
13 
into CO2, reduces GHG emissions of the entire natural gas system.  NYSEG is also 
14 
looking at gas purging technologies and administrative processes to reduce and/or 
15 
prevent methane from venting to the atmosphere.  Innovative technologies and processes 
16 
will enable the Companies to comply with the “Protecting our Infrastructure of Pipelines 
17 
and Enhancing Safety Act of 2020” (“PIPES Act of 2020”) and the PHMSA Advisory 
18 
Bulletin ADB 2021-01, Statutory Mandate to Update Inspection and Maintenance Plans
