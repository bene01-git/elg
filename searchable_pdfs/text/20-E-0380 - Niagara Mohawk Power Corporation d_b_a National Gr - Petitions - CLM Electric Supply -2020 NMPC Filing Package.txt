Q. 
Please describe Niagara Mohawk’s electric supply procurement 
14 
objectives. 
15 
A. 
Niagara Mohawk’s procurement strategy is aimed at meeting its customers’ 
16 
supply needs at the lowest reasonable costs consistent with the need to 
17 
appropriately manage price volatility for certain customer classes and to ensure 
18 
safety, reliability, and adherence to applicable environmental and contractual 
19 
requirements.  Niagara Mohawk’s electric supply service cost can vary 
20 
significantly and unpredictably from year-to-year.  In a series of orders issued 
21

between 2004 and 2008 in Cases 00-M-0504, 06-M-1017, and 03-E-0641, the 
1 
Commission concluded that New York electric utilities should engage in 
2

3

(“Staff”).

12 
Q.  
Please provide an overview of the supply portfolio hedging that Niagara 
13 
Mohawk performs for its mass market customers. 
14 
A.  
The Company enters into physical and financial fixed-price procurement 
15 
contracts for energy to serve as hedges, thereby mitigating supply price 
16 
volatility for SC-1 and SC-2ND mass market customers.  These contracts are 
17 
procured through several competitive solicitations per year using a dollar cost-
18 
averaging approach.  The Company also hedges electric capacity costs through 
19 
the use of physical and financial fixed-price procurement contracts, as well as 
20 
through various NYISO capacity auctions.

Q. 
Please describe how the Company recovers its electric supply costs from 
4 
customers. 
5 
A. 
The Company currently recovers the costs to procure electricity to serve its 
6 
supply customers in both the commodity and delivery portions of its rates as 
7 
described in Rule 46 of the Electric Tariff.  The commodity portion of the rates 
8 
charged to supply customers consists of the market value of the electricity 
9 
commodity that the Company purchases to serve these customers (based on 
10 
hourly energy prices established in the NYISO’s day-ahead market, as well as 
11 
installed capacity and ancillary service prices).  The delivery portion of supply 
12 
customers’ rates contains the forecast net market value of legacy purchase 
13 
power contracts executed prior to June 1, 2001.   
14 
 
15 
These supply costs are subject to fluctuations.  Therefore, the Company 
16 
reconciles its electric supply costs through its electricity cost reconciliation 
17 
mechanisms, pursuant to Rule 46 of the Electric Tariff.  The Company has 
18 
made all required filings with the Commission under its electric cost 
19 
reconciliation mechanisms.

IV. 
Clean Energy Standard Obligations 
1 
Q.  
Please describe the Company’s obligations to meet the CES requirements.    
2 
A.  
By its August 1, 2016 Order Adopting a Clean Energy Standard in Case 15-E-
3 
0302 (“CES Order”), the Commission established a Renewable Energy 
4 
Standard (“RES”) and a zero emission credit (“ZEC”) Standard.  The RES 
5 
requires each Load Serving Entity (“LSE”) such as Niagara Mohawk, on behalf 
6 
of its customers, to procure renewable energy certificates (“RECs”) from 
7 
qualifying CES Tier-1 facilities and ZECs from upstate nuclear generators as 
8 
defined in the CES Order and subsequent implementation orders.  
9 
Additionally, on July 12, 2018 the Commission issued an “Order Establishing 
10 
Offshore Wind Standard and Framework for Phase 1 Procurement” in Case 18-
11 
E-0302 (“OSW Order”).  The OSW Order established a requirement for LSEs 
12 
to procure Offshore Wind Renewable Energy Credits (“ORECs”) to help the 
13 
State meet its offshore wind renewable energy goals.   
14 
 
15 
Q.  
How do LSEs procure RECs to meet the RES requirements?    
16 
A.  
Under the CES Order, LSEs may meet their customers’ RES obligations by 
17 
purchasing RES compliance-eligible RECs from the New York State Energy 
18 
Research and Development Authority (“NYSERDA”) or other third-party 
19 
sources; making Alternative Compliance Payments (“ACPs”) to NYSERDA; 
20 


--- Page 13 ---
Testimony of Christopher L. Meyer 
Page 11 of 13 
or any combination thereof.  These are the methods utilized by Niagara 
1 
Mohawk.   
2 
 
3 
Q.  
What are the RES Tier 1 obligations for the Historic Test Year through 
4 
2022?   
5 
A.  
For the Historic Test Year, the RES obligation target was 0.78 percent.  For 
6 
2020 through 2022, the annual RES LSE obligations are 2.84 percent for 2020, 
7 
4.20 percent for 2021, and 8.20 percent for 2022. NYSERDA has not yet 
8 
determined and published the RES obligations for 2023 and beyond. 1   
9 
 
10 
Q.  
How is the annual ZEC obligation determined for the Company? 
11 
A. 
The CES ZEC obligation is determined over an annual twelve-month period 
12 
that runs from April through March.  The ZEC requirement is determined using 
13 
each LSE’s load ratio share of the total New York State load as determined by 
14 
NYSERDA.  LSEs are allocated the ZECs acquired by NYSERDA under the 
15 
CES Order and make monthly payments to NYSERDA for this requirement. 
16

Q.  
How is the Company’s annual OREC obligation under the OSW Order 
1 
determined? 
2 
A. 
The OREC obligation is determined each calendar year.  Like the ZEC

4

5

6 
make monthly payments to NYSERDA for this requirement.  The OREC 
7

9 
operation.2

Company would adjust its CES procurement processes accordingly.
