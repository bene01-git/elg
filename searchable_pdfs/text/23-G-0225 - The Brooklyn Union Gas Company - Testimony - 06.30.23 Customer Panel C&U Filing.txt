Program Title: Consumer Advocacy 
Brief Description: 
The Brooklyn Union Gas Company d/b/a National Grid NY (“KEDNY”) and KeySpan Gas East 
Corporation d/b/a National Grid (“KEDLI”) (collectively, the “Companies”) are proposing to add 
five Consumer Advocates. 
 
The program costs include 5 full-time Consumer Advocates (2 KEDLI, 3 KEDNY) to assist LMI 
customers in obtaining more affordable energy by promoting programs and services to customers 
and agencies through various channels such as inbound referrals, outbound calls, on-site at 
agencies, using targeted outreach to reach customers in need, and increasing engagement in 
communities by holding webinars, presentations, and Expos. 
 
Program Justification: 
 
Consumer Advocates assist LMI households in making their energy more affordable, by: 
• Educating customers 
• Identifying unidentified low-income customers to increase EAP participation 
• Promote programs and services such as HEAP, EAP, Empower NY, personalized payment 
plans, budget billing, etc. 
• Hold expos, presentations, and webinars 
• Using purchased income data to find areas of disadvantaged communities to serve, to make 
outbound calls to customers based on specific criteria as a way to maximize efficiency 
 
The Companies believe the number of customers potentially qualifying for LMI programs is in the 
range of approximately 690,000 across KEDNY and KEDLI service territory. The additional 
Consumer Advocates are needed to interface with this large population of customers, reaching a 
significantly greater proportion of eligible customers than existing resources allow, providing 1:1 
assistance for a greater number of customers, and enrolling a larger proportion in our programs. 
The addition of five full-time Consumer Advocates by KEDLI would enable outreach and 
assistance to an additional 4,800 to 6,000 households and enable KEDLI to provide approximately 
72 community outreach events annually in KEDLI’s service territory.

Program Title: Clean Energy Advisors 
Brief Description: 
The Brooklyn Union Gas Company d/b/a National Grid NY (“KEDNY”) and KeySpan Gas East 
Corporation d/b/a National Grid (“KEDLI”) (collectively, the “Companies”) are proposing a Clean 
Energy Advisor program to assist low-to-moderate income (“LMI”) customers in accessing clean 
energy programs by informing them of the available opportunities and helping them navigate 
through the program processes such as applications and scheduling appointments. The Clean 
Energy Advisors would also be liaisons to the New York State Energy and Research Development 
Authority (“NYSERDA”) Regional Clean Energy Hubs. 
 
The program costs include 3 full-time equivalents (“FTEs”) to provide individualized assistance 
to LMI customers as they engage in clean energy programs and interface with NYSERDA and 
their Regional Clean Energy Hubs and communities. 
 
Program Justification: 
 
There are many barriers for LMI customers to participating in clean energy programs such as lack 
of awareness and information surround clean energy programs, technologies and benefits, 
confusion and time constraints surrounding the process for participating in clean energy programs 
such as filling out applications, income qualifications, scheduling services, understanding project 
scopes and needing to access multiple programs or funding sources to complete the full project. 
The goal of this program is to provide guidance to customers to help them overcome those barriers 
and encourage more LMI customers to participate in clean energy programs. Additionally, with 
the roll-out of NYSERDA’s Regional Clean Energy Hubs it is important for the Companies to stay 
closely connected to and support NYSERDA, the Regional Clean Energy Hubs and the 
communities they serve and ensure coordination and continuity amongst the organizations. 
 
The Clean Energy Advisors are an important step to providing the type of support LMI customers 
may need to access clean energy programs and aligns with New York State’s goals set forth in the 
New Efficiency: New York Proceeding that emphasizes the importance to LMI energy efficiency 
programs and dedicates 20% of incremental funding to LMI customers. Their activities will also 
help further investments in Disadvantaged Communities which aligns with the Climate Leadership 
and Community Protection Act (“CLCPA”) requirement of at least 35% of the benefits from 
investments 
 
The NENY Order emphasizes the importance of LMI energy efficiency programs and dedicates 
20% of all incremental funding to LMI customers. The CLCPA requires at least 35% of benefits 
of clean energy be directed to underserved communities. The Energy Affordability Proceeding 
additionally aims to keep household energy burden at or below 6% and weatherization and energy 
efficiency is one of the most effective ways to permanently lower customer energy bills. These 
resources will also help to support the achievement of the LMI energy efficiency targets and 
proposed LMI energy efficiency EAM.

Program Title: Energy Efficiency Full-time Equivalents (“FTEs’) 
 
Brief Description:  
 
The Brooklyn Union Gas Company d/b/a National Grid NY (“KEDNY”) and KeySpan Gas East 
Corporation d/b/a National Grid (“KEDLI”) (collectively, the “Companies”) recognize the 
importance of delivering on the ambitious goals set out in the Climate Leadership Council 
Protection Act (“CLCPA”). To deliver on these aggressive targets, initiatives and the New 
Efficiency: New York (“NE:NY”) targets, the Companies need to strengthen existing building 
envelope programs and to develop and deploy new programs, with a stronger ability to focus on 
LMI customers and Disadvantaged Communities.  
 
Since the issuance of the January 2020 NE:NY Order, which ramps up the energy efficiency 
savings targets by 449 percent and 296 percent for KEDLI and KEDNY, respectively, for 2021 
through 2025, the focus of energy efficiency programs has changed from programs that administer 
monetary incentives to install more efficient gas appliances to a far more labor-intensive programs 
with emphasis on building envelope enhancement. Although the Companies’ initial efforts in 
transitioning to the more labor-intensive building envelope measures ran into headwinds due to 
the pandemic and supply chain shortages, recent experience indicates that interest in these 
programs is intensifying dramatically. 
 
The cohort of existing FTEs were assembled to design the first layer of program build and delivery, 
without future expansion considered. Now that the portfolios are maturing and ready to deliver 
more to customers, they require a proportionate workforce to handle the dramatically increasing 
demand. Otherwise, the Companies will risk remaining stagnant toward achieving the CLCPA’s 
carbon reduction targets, both current and future. 
 
Program Justification: 
 
Meet Energy Efficiency Targets. Achieving the CLCPA goals of net zero emissions by 2030 will 
require accelerated energy efficiency and building decarbonization. To ensure the Companies’ 
ability to deliver on these goals, they need to grow a workforce proportionate to the exponentially 
increasing targets.  
 
Focus on Disadvantaged Communities. Appropriate levels of focus are required to address equity 
and a just transition, especially for customers in Disadvantaged Communities.  The CLPCA 
requires at least 35 percent of the State’s clean energy investments be directed to Disadvantaged 
Communities. Program delivery within these communities requires adequate attention to ensure 
that these levels are exceeded. 
 
Deploy Measures with Deeper Savings In order to drive the necessary uptick in measures that 
deliver longer lifetime savings, in a shorter time frame, appropriate staffing levels and expertise 
are required. Building envelope programs are much more labor intensive than historic energy 
efficiency programs. The current volume of projects is too high for existing Program Management 
resources, due to intentional levels of growth.
