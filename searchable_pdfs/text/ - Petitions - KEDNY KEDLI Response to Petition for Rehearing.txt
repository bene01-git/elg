Background 
By Order issued and effective August 15, 2024,2 the New York State Public Service 
Commission (“Commission”) approved with minor modifications and corrections a Joint Proposal 
that resolved, or provided a framework for resolving, all issues in the above base rate proceedings.  
One of the areas where the Commission made a minor modification was with respect to the

The Commission Did Not Commit A Legal Error In Establishing A Reporting 
Requirement That Applies To RNG Facilities That Will Be Located In A DAC 
In asserting that the Commission committed a legal error by requiring the Companies to 
submit an analysis of disproportionate impacts only for any RNG project located in a DAC, We 
Act ignores the fact that the Commission is not under any legal obligation to require the Companies 
to submit any particular analysis at all regarding the RNG interconnections.  Thus, the Commission 
had discretion to formulate the content of the additional reporting it wished to require.  The 
Commission cannot commit a legal error by failing to require an analysis that is not legally 
required.  As We Act has identified no error of law, its Rehearing Petition should be rejected. 
We Act’s request for a more comprehensive analysis appears to arise from a mistaken 
assumption that the CLCPA requires the Commission to perform a detailed environmental analysis 
of each RNG project before authorizing rate recovery of the Companies’ interconnection costs for 
such projects.7  In these proceedings, the Companies are seeking only to recover the costs they will 
incur to interconnect their distribution systems with RNG projects that will independently need to 
obtain all necessary permits to construct and operate the projects themselves.  While CLCPA 
Section 7(3) requires all State agencies to ensure that their administrative decisions and approvals 
will not “disproportionately burden disadvantaged communities,” and that their decisions will 
“prioritize reduction in greenhouse gas emission and co-pollutants in disadvantaged 
communities,” these legal obligations do not require the Commission, in approving rate recovery 
for the interconnection costs associated with an RNG project, to conduct an independent review

of other State and local agencies’ decisions to permit the projects in the first place.  In the case of 
the RNG projects, the agencies that are charged with permitting these facilities should conduct the 
analysis of projects to the extent required and the Commission’s CLCPA-related review should be 
limited to confirming that all necessary environmental permits have been properly obtained.  Thus, 
there is no need for the Commission to expand the reporting requirements that it established in the 
August 15 Order and, as such, the Commission clearly did not commit a legal error by imposing 
the reporting requirement included in the August 15 Order. 
The Commission Obviously Did Not Intend To Modify The Provision Of The Joint 
Proposal That Addresses The Sale Of Environmental Attributes Associated With 
Biomethane 
While the August 15 Order mis-stated the provision of the Joint Proposal that set forth the 
Companies’ obligations to engage with biomethane developers concerning the sale of 
environmental attributes, there is no indication in the August 15 Order that the Commission meant 
to modify the Joint Proposal with respect to this provision.  Moreover, because the environmental 
attributes are sold by biomethane project developers, not the Companies, the Commission lacks 
jurisdiction to regulate the sale of the attributes.  In addition, even if the Commission had 
jurisdiction to regulate the sale of environmental attributes, there is currently no viable market for 
their sale in New York and it would not make sense for the Commission to effectively eliminate 
the opportunity for project developers to monetize the attributes until a market develops.  Thus, 
there is no basis for the Commission to clarify its order other than to confirm that it did not intend 
to modify the Joint Proposal. 
Conclusion 
For all the foregoing reasons, the Companies respectfully request that the Commission 
reject We Act’s rehearing petition and confirm that it did not intend to modify the Joint Proposal’s 
provision that addresses the sale of environmental attributes associated with biomethane.
