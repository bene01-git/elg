Dear Secretary Phillips:
The Newtown Creek Alliance is opposed to the Joint Proposal for filing in the above-referenced proceedings for
the following reasons:
1.
The Proposal does not take appropriate actions in reducing fossil fuel consumption in New York City
and Long Island. The actions that are currently being undertaken by the Company and are part of this
rate case, namely the construction of the Metropolitan Reliability Infrastructure project and LNG
Greenpoint Vaporizers 13 and 14, only further a dependence on natural gas in the decades to come. We
firmly believe in the climate change goals set forth by New York City and New York State, and do not
believe that this proposal meets those demands.
2.
The Proposal puts an unjust financial burden on local ratepayers who have historically been exposed to
potential environmental harms relating to the siting of the Greenpoint Energy Center facility. Our
communities already pay a price that National Grid has forced upon us, with soil and groundwater
contamination (including Benzene, Toluene, Ethylbenzene and Xylene (BTEX); Semivolatile Organics:
Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAHs); and Polychlorinated biphenyl (PCBs); a federally designated
Superfund waterway in our backyard (of which the Company was an original potentially responsible
party); and air emissions of greenhouse gases and Nitrous Oxides from the Greenpoint facility. It is
unconscionable to make these local rate payers also pay for the expansion of the Company’s fossil fuel
infrastructure.
3.
The Newtown Creek biogas purification project utilizes NYC wastewater infrastructure to create a
cleaner and more local source of natural gas. While we believe there is significant potential in advancing
this technology at existing wastewater treatment plants, we are opposed to the Company recovering their
own costs for this project from local rate payers who have been impacted by the siting of the City’s
largest wastewater treatment plant; the increased air emissions from the facility since the construction of
the project began (original completion was 2019, and the project is still not operational); and are
impacted by the local truck traffic delivering bioslurry to the treatment facility.

About NCA:
The Newtown Creek Alliance (http://www.newtowncreekalliance.org/) is a community-based organization
dedicated to restoring, revealing and revitalizing Newtown Creek in the boroughs of Queens and Brooklyn. We
represent the interests of community residents and local businesses that are dedicated to restoring community
health, water quality, habitat, access and vibrant, water-dependent commerce along Newtown Creek. NCA
works to restore the Creek by securing mitigation and remediation of known environmental hazards – both in
the neighborhoods surrounding Newtown Creek and in Newtown Creek itself – reporting ongoing sources of
pollution and preventing new pollution. To restore the ecological functions of the waterway, NCA supports
investments in green infrastructure, bioremediation, and habitat restoration. We help revitalize watershed
communities by playing a leadership role in area-wide brownfield redevelopment planning, creating programs
that improve the environmental profile of industrial businesses and engaging in workforce development to
create local green jobs. Our work supports environmental, economic and human health.
