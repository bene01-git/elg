BACKGROUND 
Since March 2020, the Pandemic has significantly 
affected public health and caused substantial economic impacts 
in New York.  Accordingly, on March 7, 2020, the Governor 
declared a disaster emergency in the State of New York (COVID-19 
State Disaster Declaration).6 
 
 
On June 17, 2020, the Governor signed into law as 
Chapters 108 and 126 of New York’s Laws of 2020, the Parker-
Mosley Law, which, among other things, required utility 
corporations to provide residential customers a right to enter 
into deferred payment agreements “without the requirement of a 
down payment, late fees, or penalties, as such is provided for 
in this article.”  The provisions would remain in effect until 
180 days after the COVID-19 State Disaster Declaration is lifted 
or expires. 
 
 
On May 11, 2021, the Governor signed into law 
amendments to the Public Service Law (PSL) and General Business 
Law (GBL) that provide an expansion of the moratorium on 
terminations and disconnections of electricity, gas, steam, 
landline telephone, cable television, broadband, and water 
services to customers during and after the COVID-19 State 
Disaster Declaration.  The amendments to PSL §32 require service 
providers to offer deferred payment agreements without down 
payments, late fees, or penalties to eligible residential and

Under Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP),

financial statements if the utility will recover it within 24 
months from the end of the annual period in which it was 
recognized.  To address the discrepancy between the Commission’s 
traditional method of disposing of deferrals in the next rate 
proceeding and GAAP, the Companies propose to establish a 
surcharge mechanism that permits full recovery of the deferred 
Lost Fees during GAAP’s 24-month recovery timeframe.  
Additionally, KEDNY and KEDLI, seek approval of a 
surcharge/surcredit mechanism for Rate Years Two and Three under

any Unbilled Fees deferral through a surcharge or surcredit

its current rate plan, by which it would collect or refund any

DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION 
 
The Companies assert that they have deferred Lost 
Revenues, net of related savings, in accordance with the terms 
of the applicable rate orders but cannot wait until the 
resolution of their next rate proceedings to collect these 
monies due to GAAP requirements.  Accordingly, the Companies 
propose to collect these monies via surcharges.  Additionally, 
the Companies propose to continue their reconciliations in the 
two subsequent rate years.  We grant the Companies’ petitions, 
with modifications, for the reasons discussed below. 
 
With regard to the timing of the collection of these 
deferrals, we agree with the Companies’ assertion that GAAP 
requires that certain regulatory deferrals must be recovered 
within 24 months to be recorded on the income statement.  As 
such, recovery or refund of the deferrals via surcharge or 
surcredit mechanisms is appropriate. 
KEDNY and KEDLI deferred $12.959 million and $5.925 
million, respectively, of Unbilled Fees during Rate Year One of 
their rate plan.  NMPC deferred $17.143 million and $3.396 
million for its electric and gas operations, respectively, 
during the 15-month period identified above.  The Companies 
calculated these amounts by taking the Unbilled Fees for the 
periods stated and reducing those fees by related savings, as 
described above.11  The Companies also applied interest to the 
Lost Revenues, which they calculated using the pre-tax weighted

Given the significant increase in arrears on which 
late payment fees can be assessed in 2022 and 2023, we 
anticipate that there could be a considerable increase in these 
revenues in the Companies’ respective subsequent rate years.  
The Companies propose to reconcile these revenues for these same 
rate years.  However, the petitions do not clearly state whether 
the Companies propose that this reconciliation would be pursuant 
to the Exogenous Costs provision of the KEDNY/KEDLI Rate Order 
and the Externally Imposed Cost provision of the 2022 NMPC Rate 
Order, and, as such, be subject to the associated minimum 
financial impact threshold requirements set forth in the 
respective rate orders.  To ensure that ratepayers receive the 
full benefit of any increase in these revenues in the outer rate 
years, we direct that the Companies fully reconcile these fees, 
net of savings calculated consistently with the instant 
petition, in the two subsequent rate years without any threshold 
requirement and collect/pass back any variance through the 
surcharge/surcredit mechanism discussed herein.  Additionally, 
the Companies are required to submit a reconciliation of the 
Unbilled Fees, and related savings, for the deferral within 60 
days after the end of the two subsequent rate years.  These 
reconciliations will be subject to Staff’s review.  If concerns 
are identified and not resolved prior to the effective date of 
the surcharge, those concerns will be addressed in the Company’s 
next rate proceeding.

Lost Revenues is not the only issue resulting from the 
Pandemic.  Customer arrearages, or unpaid past charges, are also 
a problem that the Commission and Staff have been working to 
address.  On February 4, 2021, Staff issued a proposal that 
recommended that the Commission establish an arrears management 
plan at each utility to address the looming arrearage problem in 
New York State.12  Staff proposed that shareholders contribute to 
solving the arrearages problem, in particular because of the 
benefits to them provided by Revenue Decoupling Mechanisms 
(RDM).  These mechanisms were implemented specifically to 
address delivery rate disincentives to utility implementation of 
energy efficiency programs.  RDMs, however, capture all 
variances in revenue, including the repercussions of the

The Companies have customer arrears of approximately 
$88 million, $37 million, and $219 million for KEDNY, KEDLI, and 
NMPC, respectively.  The Companies have committed to using a 
portion of the deferred Unbilled Fees amounts toward addressing 
these customer utility arrearages, specifically $1.2 million, 
$0.4 million, and $1.9 million for KEDNY, KEDLI, and NMPC, 
respectively.  Given the significant amount of arrearages 
resulting from the Pandemic, each utility’s contribution toward 
an arrears management plan is critical to addressing the 
problem.  The Commission views the Companies proposed 
contribution to help address arrearages as the minimum to make 
this program workable.
