I. 
INTRODUCTION  
 
Pursuant to the Notice of Schedule for filing Exceptions issued by the New York State 
Public Service Commission (“Commission”) on May 1, 2024, Multiple Intervenors, an 
unincorporated association of approximately 55 large industrial, commercial, and institutional 
energy consumers with manufacturing and other facilities located throughout New York State, 
including the Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corporation (“Central Hudson” or “Company”) 
service territory, hereby submits its Brief on Exceptions to the Recommended Decision issued by 
Administrative Law Judges (“ALJs”) James A. Costello and Ashley Moreno in Cases 23-E-0418 
and 23-G-0419.1   
Multiple Intervenors hereby responds in opposition to the following exceptions filed by 
Central Hudson: 
• Central Hudson’s exception that the Recommended Decision did not recommend 
allowing the Company to defer potential future costs relating to the Climate Leadership 
and Community Protection Act (“CLCPA”) (Central Hudson at 30-31); 
• Central Hudson’s exception that the Recommended Decision erred in finding that a 
48% equity ratio would be reasonable (Central Hudson at 31-33);

C. 
CLCPA Deferral Mechanism 
 
In the Recommended Decision, the ALJs agreed with Multiple Intervenors and Staff that 
the deferral mechanism for potential future costs related to CLCPA compliance proposed by 
Central Hudson should be denied.2  Central Hudson objected to this recommendation in its Brief 
on Exceptions, arguing that the proposed deferral mechanism is necessary to mitigate the potential 
risk that the Company might incur CLCPA-related compliance costs in the Rate Year that it cannot 
recover.3  For the reasons set forth below, Central Hudson’s exception should be denied. 
Central Hudson’s rate filing reflects the revenue requirement impact of known CLCPA-
related costs in its rate filing.4  Those costs are substantial.  For instance, the Recommended

RATE OF RETURN / FINANCIAL ISSUES 
 
B. 
Capital Structure 
 
Judges Costello and Moreno recommended that the Commission utilize a capital structure 
with a 48% equity ratio for ratemaking purposes.9  The Company excepts to that recommendation, 
arguing instead that a 50% equity ratio should be utilized in this proceeding.10  For the reasons set 
forth below, Central Hudson’s exception to the recommended equity ratio should be denied. 
The Recommended Decision accorded substantial weight to Staff testimony that the 48% 
equity ratio did not impair the ability of other utilities to access capital.11  Central Hudson argued 
that the experience of other utilities is irrelevant because the question in issue relates to the record 
in this proceeding and whether the capital structure approved by the Commission might impact the 
Company’s financial metrics in a manner that contributes to a rating downgrade.12  Central Hudson 
then apparently contradicts itself by arguing that the 48% equity ratio is “inconsistent with the 
trend in other jurisdictions which has increased to an average equity ratio of 52.57% in 2022.”13  
The Company cannot have it both ways by, on the one hand, arguing that the equity ratios 
authorized for other New York utilities are irrelevant to the determination of its own capital 
structure but also argue, on the other hand, that the equity ratios authorized for utilities in other 
jurisdictions are relevant to, and support, the thicker equity layer that Central Hudson prefers.   
The ALJs considered the factors argued on exceptions by Central Hudson and found them 
to be unpersuasive.  They concluded, based on their weighting of an extensive record developed

XVIII. USE OF REGULATORY ASSETS TO MODERATE RATES 
 
Central Hudson excepted to the ALJs’ recommendation that a portion of available 
regulatory liabilities should be used to moderate delivery rate increases approved in these 
proceedings.35  According to the Company, regulatory liabilities “are best utilized” to levelize 
multi-year rate increases.36  Central Hudson also argued that there is no record support for the 
ALJs’ suggestion that it would be reasonable for the Commission to use one-third of the available 
regulatory net liabilities to moderate delivery rate increases in these proceedings, and that the use 
of such credits would degrade the CFO Pre-WC/Debt metric that rating agencies use in part to 
determine credit ratings.37  The Company’s exceptions to these recommendations should be denied 
for the following reasons. 
The regulatory liabilities that would be used to moderate delivery rate increases approved 
in these proceedings are funds held by the utility but owed to customers.  The funds eventually 
must be returned to customers and using the balances to moderate the impact of delivery rate 
increases resulting from this proceeding is in the best interests of customers.  It also is consistent 
with Commission practice, even if the moderators would be applied to blunt the impact of a 1-year 
rate plan instead of a multi-year settlement.  Using available moderators to reduce delivery rate 
impacts would address intergenerational equity concerns, which dictate that customer credits be 
returned as soon as possible in order to maximize the likelihood that the customers responsible for 
the creation of the credits also benefit from their return.
