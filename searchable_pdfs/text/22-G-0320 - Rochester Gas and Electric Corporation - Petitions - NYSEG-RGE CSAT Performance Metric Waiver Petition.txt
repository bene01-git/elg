New York State Electric & Gas Corporation (“NYSEG”) and Rochester Gas and Electric 
Corporation (“RG&E” and collectively with NYSEG, the “Companies”) hereby submit this letter 
petition for waiver of a customer service metric and associated negative revenue adjustments 
(“NRAs”) contained in the Joint Proposal adopted by the State of New York Public Service 
Commission (“Commission”) in Cases 22-E-0317, et al.1  (the “Joint Proposal”). Specifically, as 
part of the Customer Service Performance metrics, the Joint Proposal established targets 
associated with Contact Satisfaction (“CSAT”). The Joint Proposal established revenue 
adjustments based upon the Companies’ performance pursuant to that metric. 
 
For the reasons explained in detail below, the Companies request that the Commission waive the 
NRAs associated with the Companies’ CSAT results in 2025.

Legal Authority: 
The Commission has the legal authority to rule on this petition under Public Service Law (PSL) 
§§65 and 66, because the Commission has general supervision of all electric and gas corporations 
in New York State, which includes the ability to regulate the terms under which such corporations 
provide service to their customers and the authority to make ratemaking determinations. 
Background/Discussion: 
The Companies remain steadfast in their commitment to enhancing the customer experience. 
This commitment is demonstrated through the ongoing, coordinated efforts of the Voice of the 
Customer, Digital Customer Experience, and Customer Care teams. These teams engage in 
rigorous monitoring and comprehensive analysis of customer feedback and operational 
performance data, ensuring that actionable insights are identified and implemented to drive 
continuous improvement in every aspect of the customer journey. Efforts in this space include 
ongoing reporting on customer feedback, collaborative customer experience focused working 
sessions, and consistent ad hoc investigations into customer feedback that warrants additional 
exploration. These collective efforts have contributed to measurable improvements in several key 
operational and customer-focused metrics, reinforcing that the actions taken are driving 
meaningful enhancements across the broader performance landscape. Despite these efforts, due 
to increasingly adverse external conditions that remain beyond the Companies’ control, the 
Companies continue to encounter significant challenges in elevating customer satisfaction.  
The current CSAT thresholds have not been updated since 2016. These targets—89.5% for 
NYSEG and 88.0% for RG&E—were originally calculated using a five-year average of CSAT 
scores from 2010 to 2014, minus two standard deviations. 
Since that time, external influences—including economic conditions, heightened storm severity, 
and the lingering effects of post-COVID recovery—have exerted a sustained negative impact on 
customer satisfaction. Industry benchmarks reinforce this trend. For example, J.D. Power reports 
a widespread decline in utility satisfaction, largely driven by escalating financial pressures on 
customers. The J.D. Power 2024 U.S. Electric Utility Residential Customer Satisfaction Study 
found that 39% of respondents felt worse off financially compared to the prior year, 
underscoring the link between affordability and declining sentiment.2 
Internal and external analyses consistently point to cost—particularly volatile supply costs—as 
the primary driver of growing dissatisfaction.   The Companies’ performed an analysis that 
examined the relationship between supply and delivery costs and CSAT performance. Results 
showed that sharp increases in electric supply costs during 2023 through 2025—costs entirely 
outside the Companies’ control—were strongly and negatively correlated with CSAT scores. 
Moreover, supply costs exerted a more pronounced and earlier cumulative impact than delivery

In this context, it is reasonable to consider neutral customers as satisfied customers. Neutral 
responses typically indicate that the customer’s expectations for a necessary service have been 
met, as they are neither dissatisfied nor seeking improvement. For utilities, where the baseline 
expectation is reliable service at a fair price, neutrality reflects adequacy and acceptance rather 
than discontent. 
According to the American Customer Satisfaction Index (“ACSI”), energy utilities consistently 
score in the low to mid-70s on a 100-point scale—far below luxury or discretionary services.4 
ACSI notes that maintaining scores in this range is commendable given the complex priorities 
utilities must balance, such as increases in demand, infrastructure investment, and affordability. 
This demonstrates that customer expectations for essential services differ significantly from 
other industries and further supports the position that neutral responses indicate that expectations 
have been reasonably met and should be considered satisfied for performance evaluation 
purposes. 
The Companies’ reported CSAT values do not include neutral customers as satisfied in line with 
current requirements; however, if neutral customers were included, NYSEG and RG&E would 
have achieved CSAT scores of 89.5% and 88.6%, respectively—both above their CSAT 
thresholds. 
Conclusion 
The Companies have demonstrated a sustained and proactive commitment to improving the 
customer experience in the face of significant external challenges beyond their control. The 
CSAT thresholds, which have remained unchanged since 2016, no longer reflect current 
operating conditions or industry benchmarks. Customer sentiment in the utility context is heavily 
influenced by supply cost drivers, and if neutral responses were reasonably classified as satisfied, 
the Companies would have met the established targets. Given these circumstances, it is 
reasonably appropriate to waive penalties associated with not meeting the current CSAT 
thresholds, recognizing both the Companies’ continued efforts and the impact of external 
conditions on customer satisfaction outcomes.
