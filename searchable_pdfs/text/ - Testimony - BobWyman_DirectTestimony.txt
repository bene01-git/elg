Q. This all sounds complicated. Why can’t we wait to deal with these issues later?
2
A. The longer we allow the accumulation of gas asset to continue, the more expensive it
3
will be for ratepayers to support the gas system in the future. The key problem here
4
is that once a new gas capital investment is made, its costs are essentially ﬁxed over
5
the remaining life of the asset. For instance, if the Company invests in a new service
6
connection with a sixty-year life, it will recover one sixtieth of its up-front cost each
7
year, plus the cost of ﬁnancing the remaining un-recovered amount. Then, given that
8
the Company uses volumetric rates to recover its costs of delivery, the ﬁxed costs are
9
spread over the number of units of gas sold each year.
10
Q. Explain why ﬁxed costs are such a problem when sales decline.
11
A. As long as the number of units of gas sold remains unchanged, then the cost-
12
recovered per unit will stay essentially the same each year until costs are fully recov-
13
ered. However, if sales decline, then each year the ﬁxed asset costs will be spread over
14
a smaller number of units of gas than during the year before. That means that after a
15
sales decline, the per-unit cost recovery will increase.
16
For example, if gas consumption were to decline by 80% between now and 2050, then
17
the per-unit cost recovery for an asset installed in 2019 but still in use in 2050 would
18
be 500% greater than it was in 2019.12 This is because, in 2050, the ﬁxed costs would
19
be spread over just 20% of the number of units sold in 2019. If the decline in sales by
20
2050 is only 50%, then the per-unit cost would increase by a still substantial 200%.
21

A. The result will be a gas “death-spiral.”
1
As gas’ per-unit costs increase, alternatives to gas will become more competitive and
2
those ratepayers who have the capital or credit needed to invest in alternatives to gas,
3
such as heat pumps, will abandon gas and ﬁnd shelter with the cheaper alternatives.
4
However, by abandoning gas, they will drive sales even lower and that will increase
5
the per-unit costs to the point where even more ratepayers decide to abandon gas.
6
The cycle then repeats. As the per-unit costs increase, gas sales will decrease even
7
faster, forcing the per-unit costs increases to accelerate. This is referred to as a “death-
8
spiral.”
9
In time, we will ﬁnd that gas will become known as the “poor-man’s fuel” since the
10
remaining gas users will increasingly be those who can’t afford to abandon it — no
11
matter how much they might wish to. Low and moderate income ratepayers, who
12
have the most limited ﬁnancial resources and the greatest need for more affordable
13
energy, will end up carrying the burden of the stranded assets left behind by those
14
who are ﬁnancially more fortunate.
