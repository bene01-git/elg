A. BACKGROUND ON DISTRIBUTED INTELLIGENCE 
5 
Q.     WHAT ARE “DISTRIBUTED INTELLIGENCE” CAPABILITIES? 
6 
A.     “Distributed Intelligence” (“DI”) is a recently-developed capability of advanced 
7 
meters that is defined as a generalized computing capability inside the meter itself. DI-
8 
capable meters are different from traditional smart meters because traditional smart 
9 
meters are limited to collecting energy usage information on intervals ranging from 5 
10 
minutes to 60 minutes, and then transmitting that information back to the utility for 
11 
processing. DI-capable meters, in contrast, have an on-board computer that allows for 
12 
significantly enhanced information gathering at much shorter time intervals, providing 
13 
insights into power consumption.  
14 
 
Major meter manufacturers, including Itron and Landis+Gyr, now offer DI-capable 
15 
meters, which allow software applications, or “apps,” to be deployed over the advanced 
16 
metering infrastructure’s (“AMI”) radio network and executed on the meter’s computer. 
17 
For example, Itron’s website states that DI allows software apps to “have access to the 
18 
meter’s data, implement algorithms, and then send the results of these computations to 
19

meter manufacturer (Itron, Landis+Gyr, etc.) is involved in reviewing apps to ensure

Q.     WHAT IS LOAD DISAGGREGATION? 
1 
A.     Load disaggregation – also known as non-intrusive load monitoring – refers to 
2 
statistical inferences about the energy usage of individual devices or appliances in a 
3 
home by analyzing the whole-home’s energy usage. Algorithms of different types can 
4 
analyze energy usage at various time intervals. From my research and experience in 
5 
this field, the accuracy of load disaggregation is inversely proportional to the sampling 
6 
interval, meaning that shorter time intervals lead to greater accuracy. DI capabilities 
7 
promise to dramatically increase the accuracy of load disaggregation as compared with

traditional smart meters, as shown in Figure 1 below.

Q.     WHAT IS DIGITAL PLATFORM REGULATION? 
3 
A.     Digital platform regulation is a new way of thinking about government intervention 
4 
in digital sectors of the economy in order to promote competition and bolster benefits for 
5 
consumers. Recent attention to the power of large technology firms such as Facebook, 
6 
Apple and Google has led to academic scholarship and consumer advocacy on how 
7 
best to regulate digital platforms, as well as actions from government all around the 
8 
world, including the U.S., focused on curbing abuses of market power. The advent of 
9 
distributed intelligence capabilities in advanced metering systems calls for the 
10 
Commission to approach this issue based upon the dynamics of digital markets rather 
11 
than merely traditional cost-of-service regulation. 
12 
Several principles of digital platform regulation have been articulated by leading 
13 
consumer advocates. These principles stem from the recognition that digital platforms, 
14 
and app stores in particular, share many similarities, even when compared across 
15 
various industry sectors. The principles that support the greatest customer benefits 
16 
include: non-discrimination of app developers; supporting equal access to technical 
17 
information about the platform; preventing platform operators from duplicating 
18
