III. 
LEAK MANAGEMENT 
5 
Q.    
Does Central Hudson have a gas leak management program? 
6 
A. 
Yes. 
7 
Q.    
How does Central Hudson define its leak management program? 
8 
A. 
The Company defines its leak management program as a system wide 
9 
program to identify, respond to and mitigate underground gas leaks in a 
10 
safe and cost-effective manner.  Gas leaks result from unplanned releases 
11 
of natural gas from the Company’s transmission and distribution 
12 
infrastructure.  The causes for these releases may include damage by 
13 
excavation activities or outside forces such as frost induced ground 
14 
movement, obsolete joint designs such as those associated with cast iron 
15 
gas mains, material failures and obsolete construction practices such as 
16 
the installation of bare steel gas mains and services. 
17 
Q.    
Please describe the Company’s current safety performance measures for 
18 
leak management. 
19 
A. 
The Company has three: a Total Leak Backlog Performance Measure; a 
20 
Hazardous (used interchangeably with “Repairable”) Leak Backlog 
21 
Performance Measure; and a Type 3 high emitter leak elimination 
22 
Performance Measure. 
23

Q.    
What is the Total Gas Leak Backlog Performance Measure? 
1 
A. 
The Total Gas Leak Backlog Performance Measure tracks the total 
2 
number of active leaks, including Hazardous Leaks, at year end.  Leaks 
3 
eliminated during the year, those that have been repaired but are awaiting 
4 
confirmation of the repair in 14 to 30 days, and above grade leaks, are 
5 
excluded from the total leaks reported at the end of the year for this 
6 
metric. 
7 
Q.    
What is the Hazardous Gas Leak Backlog Performance Measure? 
8 
A. 
Similar to the Total Gas Leak Backlog measure, this measure tracks the 
9 
total number of Hazardous Leaks at year end.  Leaks eliminated during 
10 
the year, those that have been repaired but are awaiting confirmation of 
11 
the repair in 14 to 30 days, and above grade leaks, are excluded from the 
12 
total count of Hazardous Leaks reported at the end of the year for this 
13 
metric. 
14 
Q.    
Aren’t all gas leaks considered ‘hazardous’? 
15 
A. 
No.  16 NYCRR Part 255 codifies which leaks are considered hazardous.  
16 
Pursuant to this regulation, all leaks are assigned a classification of Type 
17 
1, 2A, 2 or 3.  These leaks are documented, tracked, monitored and re-
18 
evaluated at regular intervals.  Hazardous or potentially hazardous leaks 
19 
are classified as a Type 1, 2A or 2 and have a set repair timeframe 
20 
ranging from immediate for a Type 1 classification to within 12 months for 
21 
a Type 2 classification.  Type 3 leaks do not have an associated repair 
22 
timeframe and are considered to be ‘non-hazardous’ due to the location 
23 


--- Page 13 ---

11 
 
and magnitude of below grade natural gas readings.  Type 3 leaks are re-
1 
evaluated during the next required leakage survey or annually, whichever 
2 
is sooner; to verify that the leak hazard level has not changed.  The 
3 
Company’s total leak backlog is comprised primarily of Type 3 leaks. 
4 
Q.    
Please describe Central Hudson’s leak management program. 
5 
A. 
As leaks are identified through the Company’s inspection program, by 
6 
customers or by the public, Central Hudson investigates, classifies, 
7 
prioritizes and schedules the repair by severity.  Typically all hazardous 
8 
leaks are repaired until eliminated.  The repair prioritization of non-
9 
hazardous leaks is based on review of the leak parameters, experience 
10 
and coordination with the Company’s leak prone pipe (“LPP”) elimination 
11 
program.  Central Hudson utilizes both Company and contractor resources 
12 
to maintain an overall leak backlog consistent with the targets established 
13 
in Case 17-G-0460 of 10 Repairable (Hazardous) leaks and 100 Total 
14 
Leak backlog measured at any day during the last ten days of the year. 
15 
Q.    
Are there any factors outside the Company’s control that can impact 
16 
Central Hudson’s performance with respect to these metrics? 
17 
A. 
Yes.  External factors such as extreme cold causing ground movement or 
18 
third-party excavation practices may increase the total number of leaks 
19 
identified or found in a given year.

Q.    
Please discuss the Company’s recent performance with respect to the 
1 
leak management metrics. 
2 
A. 
Over the time period from 2015 to 2019, the Company was able to reduce 
3 
the total leak backlog from 126 leaks to 87 leaks.  Over that same period 
4 
of time, year-end Hazardous Leak totals remained relatively stable 
5 
between 3 and 5 leaks.  The reduction in total leak backlog was 
6 
accomplished through a combination of either repair to the existing gas 
7 
facility or its replacement.  The Company utilizes the feedback of its field 
8 
crews in determining whether a gas facility should be repaired or replaced.  
9 
If it is determined the condition of the gas facility has deteriorated to the 
10 
point where continued leakage will occur, the facility is scheduled for 
11 
replacement within the required repair period.  For those leaking gas 
12 
services which are considered LPP, the service is replaced in its entirety 
13 
with a plastic service.  If the meter set is located indoors it will be relocated 
14 
to the outside unless this placement would create more of a safety threat 
15 
to the facility (i.e., vehicles or pedestrians) then if it remained inside.  The 
16 
decision to allow the meter to remain inside is made by Gas & Mechanical 
17 
Engineering.

Q.    
Does LPP elimination program necessarily result in a reduction of leaks? 
3 
A. 
Based on the most recent leak repair data from 2019, the number of leaks 
4 
repaired per 100 miles of LPP was 138 per year.  Applying this to a 
5 
proposed LPP elimination rate of 15 miles per year results in 
6 
approximately 21 main leaks eliminated each year associated with the 
7 
LPP elimination program.  This number will vary from year to year since 
8 
leakage rate is not the only factor used in prioritizing LPP elimination 
9 
projects.  In addition, due to the inherent variability in the factors that affect 
10 
the number of leaks found each year as well as the uncertainty in the 
11 
quantity of specific LPP materials (i.e. cast iron, wrought iron and 
12 
unprotected steel), each with its own leakage rate, one cannot simply 
13 
apply this reduction to the backlog measure.  While the LPP elimination 
14 
program does act to reduce the leaks found in a given year, there are 
15 
significant mitigating factors that act to offset this benefit in any given year 
16 
such as extended cold periods which cause frost induced ground 
17 
movement.
