Q:What is your occupation?
A: New York State Assembly Member, District 85
Q. Please summarize your background.
A: I am a second-generation Bronxite. I know from experience the realities that face so many
members of my community, and this has inspired me to serve the residents of the 85th
Assembly District, and to address the health disparities, racial injustice, and socio-economic
issues.
I attended New York City public schools, graduating from the Bronx High School of Science and
earning a Bachelor’s Degree in Economics from the University at Albany. My  interest in public
service was sparked by my undergraduate studies, which gave me an understanding of the role
of government has in the lives of the people. After graduation, I held positions of Deputy Chief of
Staff and Budget Director on the New York City Council. In the City Council, I worked directly
with New York City government agencies, nonprofit organizations, and small businesses to
address these disparities and formulate partnerships aimed at improving the communities’ local
schools, libraries, and playgrounds. I also helped host immigration service events, domestic
violence aid, and programs tackling the food insecurity crisis.
It is my commitment to ensuring that the Bronx, New York City, and New York State are fairer,
more equitable places for all residents that has led me to provide testimony in this rate case.
Q: On whose behalf are you testifying in this proceeding?
A: I am testifying on behalf of Sane Energy Project, a party to the rate case, and to serve my
constituents.
Q: What is your purpose in submitting this testimony?
A:To discuss concerns with the Company’s overall filing and impacts of fracked gas projects on
NYS Assembly District 85 (AD85), and to address the energy affordability issues facing my
constituents.
Q: Please describe the demographics of AD 85.
A: A majority of my constituents are people of color: 65% are Latina/o/x, 33% are Black, 8% are
white, 2% are Native American, and 2% are Asian and Pacific Islanders. The median household
income in the district is under $36,000. 30% are immigrants.

Q: Please describe the sources of fossil fuel pollution in and around AD 85.
A: ConEdison’s massive transmission pipeline that brings fracked gas down from interstate
pipelines in Westchester cuts through AD85 and just to the southwest of the district, ConEdison
brings gas into its system through the Iroquois interstate pipeline.
At Hunts Point ConEdison has a compressor station.
Across the river at the ConEdison Astoria compound and neighboring facility there are currently
five fracked gas power plants (Astoria Generating Station, New York Power Authority, NRG
Astoria, Astoria Energy LLC 5 and Astoria Energy II).
Further southwest the New York Power Authority owns and operates two fracked gas power
plants (Harlem River Yard and Hellgate).
The web of fracked gas pipelines, power plants and gas storage creates a ring of pollution
around Rikers Island.
In addition, the Bruckner and Cross Bronx Expressways run through the district and these,
along with heavy surface traffic, contribute fossil fuel pollution from gasoline and Diesel vehicles.
The Bronx ranks 62nd–least–among New York’s 62 countries for health, and has the highest
asthma rates in the U.S. We know now that gas stoves and heaters emit pollutants indoors that
are harmful to the health of residents, and the gas distribution systems to which they are
connected contribute to outdoor air pollution. Improving the health of our residents will require
significant decreases in the burning of fossil fuels in the Bronx.

Q: How would the Company’s overall filing impact constituents of AD85?
A: Given the Company’s monopoly status, residents and businesses in AD35 have no choice
but to get their electricity and gas from ConEd. The current gas and electricity rates are already
too high and it is very difficult for many residents to pay their bills. In January, 411,000 ConEd
customers in NYC and Westchester had arrears over 60 days, for a total of $819 million in the
territory: those numbers are surely much higher now, as electricity and gas prices spike in the
winter. Raising rates another 18.2 % for gas and 11.2% for electricity will further increase the
brutal energy burden of these residents. Low-income households and businesses–our
communities–will be simply unable to manage these rate hikes.
In addition, investing in gas infrastructure, even for repairs, would mean that the lifetime of these
polluting systems would be extended, harming the health of the community and making climate
change worse.
AD 85 includes Rikers Island, itself a Potential Environmental Justice Area.

Q:Do you support those projects? Why or why not
A: No. Investments to extend the life of pipelines and LNG storage interferes with goals and
mandates of the Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act. They also continue a
history of environmental racism, treating Black and Brown communities like ours as sacrifice
zones. AD85 is a waterfront district extremely vulnerable to climate change. It goes against my
constituents’ own interest to be paying more for fracked gas pipelines and storage over the next
three years.
Q: Please describe the nature and volume of constituent cases your office received
concerning ConEd bills in 2021 vs 2022
A: While our office does not have quantifiable data at this moment, we have witnessed a
significant increase in constituents cases related to ConEd bills.
