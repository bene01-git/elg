Benny Woodard 
 
Statement of Opposition to 
Case 19-G-0309 – Proceeding on Motion of the Commission as to the Rates, Charges, Rules and 
Regulations of The Brooklyn Union Gas Company d/b/a National Grid NY for Gas Service Case 
19-G-0310 – Proceeding on Motion of the Commission as to the Rates, Charges, Rules and 
Regulations of KeySpan Gas East Corporation d/b/a National Grid for Gas Service 
 
 
June 1, 2021 
 
 
I write in opposition to the Joint Proposal filed May 14, 2021. I am a 24-year old 
resident of Brooklyn, a teacher, musician and a concerned community member doing his civic 
duty to hold those in power accountable.  
I am not an accountant, lawyer or industry professional. I have a degree in Music 
Performance. Upon entering this rate case I did not know what a “revenue requirement” was, 
nor did I have the slightest idea what the acronyms “EAM,” “RDM,” or “ROE” meant. 
It is essential for you to understand that the severity of the Climate Crisis in which we 
live and the extent to which our State regulatory bodies have failed to act with due urgency in 
response has created a situation in which a young musician is spending his evenings and 
holiday weekends (his free time, if you will) reading through hundreds of pages of dense and 
nearly indecipherable legal documents, attempting to synthesize them and compose an 
informed, well-substantiated rebuttal because he does not trust the aforementioned 
regulators (whose salaries are paid by his tax dollars) to put the wellbeing of millions of New 
Yorkers before corporate profit.  
It is essential that you understand that my voice is speaking not just for myself but for 
tens of thousands of similarly concerned New Yorkers who, for reasons including “I have a 
full-time job” and “I have a family to take care of” (gee, imagine that!), were unable to have a 
seat at the table during this appallingly arcane and exclusionary process. As an advocate for 
the health and safety of my community over the financial growth of a multibillion-
dollar international corporation, I am doing the work that my regulators are failing to 
do. And I am not getting paid for it. 
At the core of my opposition to this Joint Proposal is a simple and yet deeply unnerving 
fact: The system from which this Joint Proposal was created is designed and maintained 
to protect and enable Corporate Interests at the expense of the Public Interest. In the 
year 2021, as the Climate Crisis spirals us into an ever-darkening abyss of horrific pain and 
suffering, the Corporate Interest remains one of finance while the Public Interest has become 
one of survival. Any regulating body that prioritizes the former over the latter is committing 
an act of grave and tangible violence. Department of Public Service: you are failing us.

I. In reference to the ROE 
The Joint Proposal stipulates that the Companies shall receive a return on equity (ROE) 
of 8.8%. The implication of this figure underlies every investment the Companies make, every 
report they file, every demand forecast they produce, every piece of testimony they present 
and every argument they have against transitioning to renewable energy systems. Because of 
this figure, the expansion of fracked gas infrastructure is both incentivized and rewarded 
under our State-regulated corporate utility system. It is irrelevant to the Companies and to 
this System that the extraction process through which the fracked methane gas that they sell 
New Yorkers (the “commodity”, as the JP refers to it) is so dangerous and polluting that it is 
illegal in their very service territories. The cost in human life of the continued use of that 
“commodity” is irrelevant to the Companies because they profit off of it. It could be toxic, 
poisonous, explosive, cancer-causing, atmosphere-warming gas for all they care. Oh, wait…  It 
is!!! 
 The Companies do not actually make money selling the commodity, they make money 
building the gargantuan pipes and trucks and City Gates and Injector Stations and Vaporizers 
through which that commodity is transported from point A to point B and then by hiring 
lawyers to plead their case to a gullible (at best) and complicit (at worst) Regulatory 
commission, one which inevitably gives them the permission they seek to raise bills for 
millions of everyday New Yorkers and fill shareholder pockets with ROE. The result of this 
dystopian dance between Corporate Utility and Regulator is a public that is sick and dying. 
Department of Public Service: you are failing us. 
A favorite phrase of both the Regulators and the Companies is “safety and reliability.” 
In a System such as this, what does the phrase “safety and reliability” actually mean? Safety 
and reliability of what, for whom? Safety and reliability of “essential gas infrastructure?” No, 
no, quite the contrary. This infrastructure was unsafe from day one. It will never be safe, no 
matter how many hundreds of millions of dollars of LPP replacement investments the 
Companies make. Building $185 million worth of new, 30-inch diameter pipeline through 
Environmental Justice communities in Brooklyn will not make it safe. Sending explosive CNG 
and LNG trucks through city streets will not make it safe. Adding two new “vaporizers” (am I 
living in a sci-fi movie?) to a fracked gas depot along one of North America’s most polluted 
waterways will not make it safe. Department of Public Service: you are failing us. 
 
II. In reference to MRI Phases 1-5

Phases 1-5 shall be eligible for rate recovery. When over ten thousand public comments 
are filed against a project, when dozens of elected officials, including the Mayor of New York 
City, have made public statements against it, and when concerned citizens are so afraid of the 
harm it will bring to their communities that they lock their bodies to the construction site on

III. In reference to the Demand Capacity Surcharge Mechanism (DCSM) 
I recognize certain elements of the DCSM as concessions and even as genuine attempts 
by Staff to add some form of climate and emissions accountability to the JP. This is a step in 
the right direction. However, there are a few important changes that I recommend in order to 
ensure that the DCSM is, in fact, as the JP says, “intended to ensure that the Companies are 
taking aggressive actions to promote energy efficiency, demand response and electrification 
and seeking to offset the need for additional gas supply infrastructure through non-traditional 
solutions” (Section IV.5.3.2, pg. 48). If it is not actually this, the DCSM reads instead as a 
dishonest tactic to delay rate recovery for tens of millions of dollars worth of infrastructure 
projects to the end of the rate term, giving the deceptive appearance to the Commission and to 
the Public that Staff did a good job keeping rates low.  
1. Selection of the Independent Consultant 
The JP indicates that the independent consultant responsible for determining whether 
or not the Long Term Capital Capacity projects subject to the DCSM are needed is “to be 
selected by Staff” (IV.5.3, pg. 44). It goes on to say, somewhat confusingly, that “Staff and 
the Companies will commence the consultant selection process immediately upon the 
submission of this Joint Proposal in these proceedings” (IV.5.3, pg. 44). The former 
statement indicates that the selection of the independent consultant is up to the sole 
discretion of the Staff, whereas the latter suggests that the selection process will be 
collaborative between Staff and the Companies. 
In either case, the situation is problematic. In the nature of transparency, I request 
that other parties and members of the public be involved in the consultant selection 
process. I voice a particularly strong opposition if the second quoted statement above is 
true and the Companies are allowed to have a say in the selection process, for this would 
bring up an obvious conflict of interest. We know that it is in the Companies’ financial 
interest to build these projects and have rates recovered for them. As a result, they will 
have an inherent bias for choosing a consultant who is sympathetic to their interests. For 
this independent review process to truly be about accountability it is necessary for the 
consultant to be as nonbiased as possible. Removing the Companies from the process and 
adding the public will make sure that happens.

The timeline is also a serious issue. If the selection process had indeed commenced 
“immediately upon the submission of this Joint Proposal,” then Staff and the Companies 
are already hashing it out trying to agree on a consultant as I write this Statement. This is 
not ok. The Commission has not yet ruled on any part of this JP and the selection process 
for the independent consultant is an integral element of the agreement. Staff and the 
Companies wouldn’t attempt to begin putting new rates into effect before a Commission 
ruling so why do they think it’s ok to do this? The process of selecting the independent 
consultant must only begin after a Commission order adopting the terms of this 
Joint Proposal, it must involve the public, and the Companies must not have a say in 
the negotiation. 
The criteria set forth in the JP for selecting the consultant also requires amendment. It 
states that the consultant “shall be nationally or regionally recognized in the area of gas 
engineering” (IV.5.3, pg. 44). It is essential that the consultant must also be an expert 
in the engineering of alternative energy systems. Three of the major points of criteria 
that the assessor must consider when reviewing the Long Term Capital Capacity Projects 
are centered around alternative energy solutions (ie., “viable alternatives,” “All-In Cost of 
the project vs. All-In Cost of alternatives” and “GHG emissions attributable to the project 
vs. those of alternatives”). How could the assessor possibly be able to make adequate 
proposals and estimates for alternatives if they are not an expert in alternative energy 
fields as well as gas? 
I restate that the intention of the DCSM must be to genuinely hold the Companies 
accountable to our city and state Climate Laws (ie., Local Law 97 and the CLCPA) and to 
the overwhelming demands of the public to transition our state to 100% renewable 
energy infrastructure. Selecting an independent consultant who is well versed in 
renewable thermal energy systems such as geothermal heat pumps will provide the DPS 
with an opportunity to put this legally and morally mandated transition into practice. 
 
2. The Independent Consultant’s process for assessing Long Term Cap. Projects 
 
Section IV.5.3 of the JP lists the criteria that the independent consultant must use to 
make their determinations about need for Long Term Capital Capacity Projects. The first 
criterion they must consider is whether or not the Company’s existing infrastructure 
system requires the project in order to meet “reasonably forecast peak demand.”  It states 
that this peak demand forecast will be “based on the Companies’ most recent forecast 
available.” In order to unpack why this is problematic we must acknowledge that 
overblown demand forecasts are one of the Companies’ tactics for justifying new 
infrastructure buildouts on which they can receive an ROE. Because they have a direct 
financial interest in overestimating their supply/demand gap, their forecasts can never be 
fully trusted. For these reasons, gas demand forecasts made by third party

organizations without financial stake in the Companies (such as this 2020 report by 
Synapse Energy Economics) must also be considered by the consultant and by the 
Commission when determining a project’s need. 
In reference to the consultant’s timeline for completing their assessment, the JP states 
that they must “complete and file [their] review within 45 days of the filing of the 
Companies’ report” (IV.5.3, pg. 44). I request that the consultant’s deadline for filing 
their review be increased to 90 days to ensure adequate time for an exhaustive 
assessment of alternatives with sufficient public engagement. Also, in keeping with 
my above request to allow public and intervening party input into the consultant selection 
process, I recommend an adjustment to the language quoted from the JP directly above to 
express that, in the event that the consultant has not yet been selected at the time of the 
Companies’ filing of their first report, upon being selected, the consultant will be given 90 
days to complete and file their review. 
IV. In reference to Future of Heat 
 
Although the steps made toward Climate action and net-zero emissions in this 
section are important, they serve chiefly to disguise what is most disturbing about this 
Joint Proposal-  the billions of dollars in Capital Investments into new and existing 
fossil fuel infrastructure that the Companies are making and that Staff is allowing them 
to recover through the proposed rate hikes. How could these investments possibly be 
prudent when many of them will become stranded assets within the next few decades? How 
could these investments possibly be prudent while California is on fire, while the number of 
multi-billion dollar natural disasters increases every year, while hundreds of thousands of 
people live in a flood zone in New York? How could these investments possibly be safe, 
reliable and in the public interest while Brownsville and Greenpoint (two of the Brooklyn 
neighborhoods around which several of KEDNY’s fracked gas expansion projects are 
centered) have some of the worst air quality levels and highest rates of asthma and cancer in 
New York City? 
 
My fear is that the Companies’ will use the fact that they signed onto these Future of 
Heat concessions as justification for a vast and manipulative green-washing campaign. This 
campaign will be systematically designed to obscure the reality that they are raising customer 
rates through at least 2024 in order to pay for billions of dollars in dirty, polluting fracked gas 
investments. This is yet another example of how a failure on the part of regulators to demand 
true accountability from the corporate utility can have widespread negative implications. If 
people are manipulated into believing that National Grid is actually making significant steps 
toward a renewable energy transition then they will not feel inspired to fight back. As 
“business as usual” plods on, year after year, GHG emissions will not slow down. Climate 
Catastrophe waits for no one.

In conclusion, I oppose this Joint Proposal because the framework in which it was 
created is systematically flawed to favor the corporate utility at the expense of the wellbeing 
of the Public whom it is supposed to protect. As a result of the inclusion of billions of dollars of 
investments into new and existing fracked gas infrastructure, the overall affect of this JP is to 
preserve “business as usual” and to reward Company shareholders extravagantly through the 
ROE while not just ratepayers but all life forms on planet Earth suffer from continuingly 
increased GHG emissions. For it is exactly “business as usual” which has hurled us into this 
Climate Nightmare in the first place. The Department of Public Service’s choice to enable this 
is an act of immense violence, one whose consequences are felt in flesh, blood and acute 
human suffering. 
To the Staff who made this choice, to the Companies who are enriched by my 
community’s pain, and to the members of the Commission who have the power to rule on this 
Joint Proposal, I leave you with this. Maybe you do not have the benefit of coming of age 
during the Sixth Mass Extinction like I do. Perhaps you are not plagued with recurring 
nightmares of heat suffocation and dying of thirst like I am. Perhaps you do not have six-year 
old students like I do, with whom every interaction you have is tinged with an existential 
despair and fear for their future. Perhaps you do not have loved ones with asthma or cancer 
resulting from years of breathing polluted air. Perhaps you do not return to the forest you 
grew up playing in, the one in which you developed an indescribably profound sense of awe 
for and love of the interdependent systems of life of which we are a part, and imagine the 
trees withering and dying, the ground barren and dusty, and the streams choked with sickness 
and disease. Perhaps these images do not plague your daily existence on Planet Earth as they 
do mine. Your choices in this rate case matter and will have a profound effect on every one of 
our futures. It is your duty to begin to make things right. 
 
 
 
 
Respectfully submitted, 
Benny Woodard
