Before we get to the run of the show and how the session's going to work, I'd 
like to just give you some context and other important information. 
 
 
New York has seen dynamic economic growth in the downstate region 
expanding residential and non-residential building space and thousands of oil 
to gas conversions over the last ten years. 
 
 
These factors have resulted in a substantial increase in the demand for natural 
gas placing stress on our existing gas network and threatening National Grid's 
ability to meet our customer's demands when peak is at - when demand is at 
its peak. 
 
 
This leaves little room for error in the face of unplanned supply interruptions 
or other contingencies.  And as part of the November 2019 settlement 
agreement with New York State, that lifted that moratorium on new gas 
connections imposed in May of 2019.   
 
 
We're taking measures to ensure we have enough supply for the winters of 
2019 through 2020 and 2020 through 2021 including increasing reliance on 
compressed natural gas otherwise known as CNG Trucking when needed to 
meet peak demand. 
 
 
Beyond the next two winters, however, continued growth in the demand for 
natural gas creates a challenge that must be addressed.  The long-term natural 
gas capacity report presents ten options to solve for that challenge. 
 
 
It's important to note that the long-term solution options we're presenting and 
the various combinations of these options working together have different 
implications to how long we continue to rely on CNG Trucking to meet peak

Compared to other non-infrastructure options, demand response is more cost-
effective and can be ramped up more quickly. 
 
 
That's because demand response will leverage existing equipment at customer

number of eligible customers we have and the challenges that multi-family 
and commercial customers have in maintaining a backup source of heat 
typically fuel oil for use in temperature control or non-firm programs. 
 
 
Those who fall under the temperature control category include those multi-
family and some commercial and industrial customers who we ask to switch 
off of natural gas and move to another fuel source, typically fuel oil when the 
temperature drops below a certain level.

programs where we provide an incentive for residential customers to turn 
down their thermostats during cold weather events.
