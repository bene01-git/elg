Q. 
Please describe the “theoretical depreciation reserve.” 
5 
A. 
The theoretical depreciation reserve is the estimated amount of accumulated 
6 
depreciation that should exist at the date of the depreciation study, assuming the 
7 
mortality statistics and net salvage parameters reflected in the study always had 
8 
been in effect historically and always will be in effect prospectively.  
9 
 
10 
Q. 
How does the theoretical depreciation reserve compare to the actual 
11 
accumulated depreciation reserve? 
12 
A. 
The theoretical depreciation reserve is an estimate based on the depreciation study 
13 
technique, groups, and parameters.  The theoretical depreciation reserve is 
14 
calculated for each plant account/subaccount.  The theoretical depreciation reserve 
15 
is calculated only for depreciation study purposes and is not recorded in the 
16 
Companies’ accounting books and records.   
17 
 
 
In contrast, the actual accumulated depreciation reserve is the sum of the 
18 
actual depreciation expense, less actual gross plant retirements, plus actual salvage 
19 
in all prior years.  The actual accumulated depreciation reserve is recorded in the 
20 
Companies’ accounting books and records. 
21

Q. 
Are there generally differences between the theoretical reserve and the actual 
2 
accumulated depreciation reserve? 
3 
A. 
Yes.  These differences are referred to as “reserve variances.”  These differences 
4 
are due to the fact that the mortality statistics and net salvage parameters estimated 
5 
historically and reflected in prior depreciation rates and recoveries were different 
6 
in actual experience (reflected in the actual accumulated depreciation reserve) and 
7 
different than those proposed in the current depreciation study (reflected in the 
8 
theoretical depreciation reserve).   
9 
 
 
If the actual accumulated depreciation exceeds the theoretical reserve for an 
10 
account, then there is a reserve surplus.  That means the Company has recovered 
11 
more of its plant and net salvage costs to date than was necessary to provide a 
12 
systematic and rational allocation of those costs over the average service life of the 
13 
account.  In other words, there has been excessive capital recovery. 
14 
 
 
If the actual accumulated depreciation is less than the theoretical reserve for 
15 
an account, then there is a reserve deficiency.  That means the Company has 
16 
recovered less of its plant and net salvage costs to date than was necessary to 
17 
provide a systematic and rational allocation of those costs over the average service 
18 
life of the account.  In other words, there has been inadequate capital recovery. 
19
