II. 
Niagara Mohawk Load Modifiers and Ancillary Service Costs 
4 
Q. 
What is a load modifier? 
5 
A.  
In the NYISO’s Distributed Energy Resources Roadmap for New York’s 
6 
Wholesale Electricity Markets1 (“DER Roadmap” published in January 2017), 
7 
the NYISO describes load modifiers as follows:   
8 
Load modifiers are not wholesale market resources, but instead 
9 
are a tool that [load serving entities or “LSEs”] can use to 
10 
reduce their wholesale market load and capacity obligation.  
11 
Load modifiers can consist of many different technologies that 
12 
LSEs can use to manage its load. Load modifiers are primarily 
13 
used: (i) to avoid purchasing high-cost power during peak 
14 
hours, and (ii) to reduce the LSE’s seasonal capacity 
15 
obligations. Load modifiers are not dispatched by the NYISO 
16 
and therefore any load reduction is not based on an explicit bulk 
17 
power system or wholesale market need. 
18 
 
19 
 
For Niagara Mohawk, load modifiers include generation facilities that have 
20 
power purchase agreements with the Company (Legacy and SC-6 contracts), 
21 
as well as Distributed Energy Resources (“DERs”), including projects that 
22 
participate in the Company’s retail programs such as net-metering, community 
23 
distributed generation, and Value Stack.  These different generation facilities 
24

are types of load modifiers that reduce the Company’s NYISO wholesale load 
1 
obligations.  
2 
 
3 
Q.  
Why is the Company required to designate these generation facilities as 
4 
load modifiers?    
5 
A.  
The majority of the load modifier generating facilities are very small and do 
6 
not have the ability to offer power into the NYISO wholesale electric markets.  
7 
In the absence of being able to bid the facilities into the NYISO markets, the 
8 
Company utilizes the load modifier designation mechanism to realize the 
9 
market value of the energy exported to the system from these facilities.  By 
10 
providing the supply directly to the Company, these generating facilities 
11 
reduce the amount of electric purchases the Company needs to procure through 
12 
the NYISO markets.  If the Company were not able to utilize the load modifier 
13 
designation, the facilities would no longer be reducing the wholesale load 
14 
obligations for the Company.  Therefore, the Company would effectively be 
15 
paying twice for the energy supply that it receives from these facilities unless 
16 
and until other arrangements could be put in place to facilitate their 
17 
participation in NYISO markets.  These alternative arrangements would likely 
18 
be very costly.  The Company does not designate the generators as load 
19 
modifiers to avoid ancillary purchases intentionally; it is simply a consequence 
20 
of the NYISO market settlement mechanics. 
21

Q. 
What is Mr. Haff’s concern with regard to the Company’s use of load 
1 
modifiers to reduce its NYISO purchases? 
2 
A. 
Mr. Haff argues that the Company’s use of load modifiers to decrease costs for 
3 
its commodity customers simultaneously increases costs for other market 
4 
participants, including OGS (at 3).  Mr. Haff states that, because the total 
5 
amount of ancillary service costs assessed by the NYISO is fixed, a decrease 
6 
in Niagara Mohawk’s share of such ancillary costs unavoidably increases the 
7 
costs to all other load.  
8 
 
9 
Q. 
Does OGS have the ability to receive similar benefits by taking supply 
10 
directly from specific generation resources?     
11 
A. 
Yes.  OGS has previously acknowledged that it can procure electricity directly 
12 
from an electric generator in New York State (see Exhibit ___ (CLM-1R)).  
13 
OGS, however, has not entered into any such contracts.  OGS also has 
14 
confirmed that it has on-site, behind-the-meter resources at some of its 
15 
customer locations that can reduce NYISO purchase requirements.  Although 
16 
this energy is not exported to the electric grid, this energy does reduce the 
17 
amount of energy OGS needs to settle at the NYISO for its commodity load.   
18 
This is true for all LSEs who have customers with generation facilities installed 
19 
behind-the-meter or LSEs who receive supply directly from a generation 
20 
facility that is not participating in NYISO markets.  All of these facilities are 
21

3 
including OGS – have the ability to use load modifying resources to reduce 
4

Are NYISO ancillary service costs the only ancillary service costs that the

No.  Aside from the ancillary service costs incurred at the NYISO, the 
10 
Company also allocates ancillary service costs associated with exported energy 
11 
from net-metered facilities to commodity customers.  In addition, as agreed in 
12 
the Joint Proposal adopted by the Commission in Case 17-E-0238, in April 
13 
2018, the Company began allocating charges to commodity customers for its 
14 
estimate of monthly avoided ancillary costs for legacy and SC-6 contracts, with 
15 
a corresponding credit being applied to the Legacy Transition Charge 
16 
mechanism that is applicable to delivery customers.  Therefore, the commodity 
17 
customers incur ancillary service costs for customer load procured at the 
18 
NYISO and for all the customer load served directly on its system by load 
19 
modifiers except for facilities that receive Value Stack compensation, which is 
20 
further discussed below.

3 
A. 
No.  Although OGS’s response provides the requested information, it also 
4 
includes certain information that is misleading.  In particular, the column 
5 
labeled “National Grid Ancillary Service Rate” (see page 2 of Exhibit __ 
6 
(CLM-2R)) is used in the response to compare OGS’s ancillary service costs 
7 
against the Company’s ancillary service costs.  The National Grid Ancillary 
8 
Service Rate is a forecast rate that is calculated using a twelve-month rolling 
9 
average total of the Company’s NYISO ancillary costs.  The Company includes 
10 
this forecast rate in the Electric Supply Cost rate pursuant to Rule 46.1 of its 
11 
electric tariff, PSC No. 220.  Because this ancillary service rate is a forecast, it 
12 
must be reconciled to the actual NYISO ancillary service costs incurred.  This 
13 
reconciliation occurs through the Electric Supply Reconciliation Mechanism 
14 
(“ESRM”) pursuant to Rule 46.3 of the tariff.  Additionally, the National Grid 
15 
Ancillary Service Rate is not inclusive of all ancillary service costs allocated 
16 
to commodity customers as stated above.  The Company also allocates the 
17 
ancillary service costs associated with load modifiers to commodity customers 
18 
through the ESRM.  The Company therefore concludes that the comparison of 
19 
costs made by OGS in Exhibit __ (CLM-2R) is not a fully accurate comparison

Request: 
Is OGS able to procure electricity directly from producers of electric generation in New York 
State either through power purchase agreements (PPAs) or other arrangements to serve any 
portion of the OGS customer load?  Please describe all such arrangements used to provide 
electricity to OGS customers in Niagara Mohawk’s service territory. 
Response: 
OGS can procure electricity from an electric generator in New York State. However, OGS has 
not entered into contracts with generators. All OGS customer load is served through the NYISO 
day ahead and real time markets. If OGS did have a PPA with a generator, OGS would still not 
be able to use that PPA to under report kWh use, and OGS would still be required to procure 
ancillary services for all of its customer load. It is not possible for OGS to have load modifiers in 
the form that National Grid has. If OGS contracted with a generator all OGS load would still be 
reported to the NYISO for ancillary services charges. If OGS installed on site generation, that 
generation would all be at OGS’ expense, and would eliminate use of the grid, while National 
Grid load modifiers do not do that. National Grid customers continue to pull power from the 
grid, at the same time as load modifying generators are using the grid to sell power to National 
Grid. The fact that National Grid nets the two before reporting to the NYISO does not eliminate 
the usage of the grid.
