3. Heating Electrification Make-Ready Program
Q.
Please describe the Company’s proposed heating electrification make-ready
proposal.
A.
The Company’s Heating Electrification Make-Ready Program is intended to help offset
costs of behind-the-meter electrical upgrades required to electrify space and water
heating in buildings, preparing buildings for full electrification. The program will offer
customer incentives for electrical upgrades in residential and multifamily buildings,
including support for new electric panels or sub-panels, wiring upgrades, and other
behind-the-meter upgrades required for comprehensive electrification projects.
The program will offer customer incentives and education and marketing over a three
year period, for a total investment of $76.6 million. ConEd forecasts that the program
will address electrical upgrade needs in 8,500 residential (1-4 family) properties, 2,500
multifamily properties, and 1,300 commercial properties.
Program incentives will vary based on housing type, participant income, and project
specifics:

Market-rate residential customers (1-4 family homeowners) undertaking only space
and/or water heating will be eligible for an incentive of up to $2000 to support a larger
electrical panel. LMI customers will be eligible for an incentive of up to $2500.
Residential customers pursuing full electrification would be eligible for an additional
$1000 incentive to support new conduit and service entrance wires and additional labor
required due to project complexity.
Multifamily property owners would receive incentives based on total project cost of
behind-the-meter electrical upgrades. Commercial and market-rate customers would be
eligible for incentives covering up to 70% of total project costs. For LMI-qualifying
multifamily buildings, incentives could cover up to 100% of total electrical upgrade
project costs.
Q.
What is your overall impression of the Company’s building electrification
make-ready proposal?
A.
BlocPower generally supports the Company’s proposal and believes that it will make a
meaningful difference to customers who struggle with upfront costs of electrification
projects, particularly for multifamily buildings where electrical upgrades may be a larger
portion of overall project costs. The program will be additive to existing incentives in
ConEd’s territory. BlocPower suggests a few program modifications to ensure equitable
deployment of the incentive, but broadly speaking believes the program is prudent and
necessary.

Q.
Will the Company’s proposed program support the achievement of state climate
goals?
A.
The Company’s proposal is aligned with several important state goals. In 2019,
then-Governor Cuomo signed the Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act
(“CLCPA”), which set a goal to reduce emissions by 85% below 1990 levels by 2050. In
support of this goal, Governor Hochul recently announced a plan to achieve 2 million
electrified or electrification-ready homes by 2030. Achieving this goal will require
increasing the current rate of electrification statewide more than tenfold. ConEd is
currently surpassing its program targets under the Clean Heat Program, but will
nonetheless need to ramp up electrification efforts to meet the state’s ambitious goals.
That said, BlocPower recommends some modifications of the proposal to ensure
equitable distribution of program dollars, including greater participation of multifamily
properties and renters in residential (1-4 family) properties.
Q.
Is the Company’s proposal additive to the Clean Heat Program?
A.
Yes. As the Company notes, about 10% to 30% of existing 1-4 family homes in their
territory lack capacity on their existing electric panel to connect electrified end-uses.
These panel upgrades can represent significant additional costs for homeowners.
Research by Pecan Street has found that, nationwide, the need for panel upgrades
represents a nearly $100 billion impediment to residential electrification.1 In BlocPower’s
experience, electrification projects in multifamily properties nearly always require a
panel upgrade, often accounting for about $2500 per unit - a significant portion of overall

Commissions in other jurisdictions have found incentives that address panel upgrades to

Q.
Will the Company’s proposal support increased deployment of comprehensive
electrification projects in multifamily buildings?
A.
Yes. In BlocPower’s experience, the cost to upgrade electrical capacity and wiring in
multifamily properties can exceed the cost of electric appliances. While single-family
properties in ConEd’s territory have had high participation rates in the Clean Heat
Program, multifamily buildings have completed electrification projects at a much lower
rate. Electrifying multifamily buildings is essential to achieve state climate goals, and
also to offer residents additional benefits of electrification like improved health
outcomes. This program removes a major barrier to electrification by meaningfully
lowering upfront costs for multifamily program participants.

Q.
Does BlocPower suggest any modifications to the proposed building electrification
make-ready program?
A.
Yes. BlocPower recommends the following program modifications to ensure the program
is able to support a broader set of ConEd’s customer base and maximize equitable
program participation.
●
Increase the proportion of project funding targeted for multifamily buildings.
BlocPower believes the proposed Heating Electrification Program is particularly
critical for the electrification of multifamily buildings. However, as designed the
program targets a greater portion of single-family residences than multifamily
buildings. One- and two-family residences account for only about 30% of the housing
units in ConEd’s New York City service territory. Yet according to the New York
State Clean Heat Program 2021 Annual Report, 92% of ConEd-territory Clean Heat
program savings in 2021 were from residential customers. The Building
Electrification Make-Ready Program is meant to close a participation gap by
addressing significant remaining up-front cost barriers that prevent ConEd customers
from electrifying. Current participation trends in the Clean Heat program in the
Company’s territory suggest that the barriers are much greater for multifamily
building owners than owners of single-family properties. Accordingly, the Heating
Electrification Make-Ready Program should specifically target the under-represented
multifamily building category. ConEd projects that the program would provide
support for about 3,000 existing multifamily and commercial buildings and 8,000
