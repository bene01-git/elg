BACKGROUND 
Capital Capacity Project Review Process 
 
 
The Rate Order adopted the terms of the Joint Proposal 
filed on May 14, 2021, with modifications and established rate 
plans for KEDNY and its affiliate, KeySpan Gas East Corporation 
d/b/a National Grid (KEDLI) (collectively, National Grid or the 
Companies).  Among other things, the Joint Proposal set forth a 
process for additional review of certain identified Long-Term 
Capital Capacity Projects (Capacity Projects), including the 
Vaporizers 13 & 14 Project. 
 
 
The Rate Order provided that the Companies could seek 
recovery of the revenue requirement associated with the Capacity 
Projects through the DCSM.4  The Rate Order specifies that the 
Companies can recover the costs of a Capacity Project if the 
Commission finds that both meeting reasonably forecasted peak 
customer demand requires the implementation of the project and 
alternatives that could sufficiently meet, reduce, or eliminate 
the demand cannot reasonably be expected to be available and 
operational in the timeframes necessary for a lower all-in cost.5

Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act 
 
 
The Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act 
(CLCPA) went into effect on January 1, 2020.  Amongst its 
provisions, §7(2) requires that state agencies shall consider 
whether decisions, including “issuing permits, licenses, and 
other administrative approvals and decisions… are inconsistent 
with or will interfere with the attainment of the statewide 
greenhouse gas emissions limits established in article 75 of the 
environmental conservation law [ECL].”  Further, §7(2) requires 
that “[w]here such decisions are deemed to be inconsistent with 
or will interfere with the attainment of statewide greenhouse 
gas emissions limits, each agency … shall provide a detailed 
statement of justification as to why such limits/criteria may 
not be met and identify alternatives or greenhouse gas 
mitigation measures to be required where such project is 
located.”  Additionally, §7(3) requires that, in considering 
administrative approvals and decisions, state agencies “shall 
not disproportionately burden disadvantaged communities as 
identified pursuant to subdivision 5 of section 75-0101 of the 
[ECL].” 
Description of the Vaporizers 13 & 14 Project 
 
 
The Greenpoint LNG Plant is located at the Greenpoint 
Energy Center and has been in service since 1968.  The LNG plant 
occupies 50 acres including approximately 1/4 mile of waterfront 
along the Newtown Creek.  The plant has two LNG storage tanks 
with a total storage capacity of 1.6 billion standard cubic feet

(BCFs).18  LNG is a non-pressurized cryogenic liquid that can be 
warmed from a liquid state to a gaseous state through a 
vaporizer and then injected into KEDNY’s gas system when gas 
supply or pressure support is needed.  The plant liquifies and 
stores gas from the KEDNY system during periods of warm weather 
and low demand.  LNG is stored at the facility in two existing 
LNG storage tanks, which are not impacted by the Project.  The 
plant vaporizes LNG to return gas to the system when it is 
needed through vaporizers connected to either KEDNY’s 350 psig 
system – “high-pressure vaporizers” – or KEDNY’s 60 psig system 
– “low-pressure vaporizers.”  Presently, the LNG plant has three 
high-pressure vaporizers, two of which can operate at any one 
time, and three low-pressure vaporizers, all three of which can 
operate at any one time. 
 
 
The Project consists of two low-pressure vaporizers, 
designated as Vaporizers 13 & 14, and would bring the number of 
low-pressure vaporizers to five and the total number of 
vaporizers at the plant to eight.  After the installation of 
Vaporizers 13 & 14, up to four of the five low-pressure 
vaporizers could operate at any one time.19  The incremental 
vaporizer would increase the injection capacity from the 
facility into KEDNY’s gas system from approximately 290 million 
standard cubic feet per day to approximately 350 million 
standard cubic feet per day.  The Project would not add any 
additional LNG storage at the plant.  Thus, although the 
increased vaporization capacity would enable KEDNY to use the 
LNG plant to meet higher peak demand, it would not change the

DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION 
 
 
To ensure that utilities can continue to provide safe 
and adequate service to customers in New York State, the 
Commission reviews proposed capital projects to determine 
whether investments in those projects are prudent and whether a 
utility can recover the costs of such projects from customers.  
Generally, individual capital projects are reviewed in the 
context of a review of an overall capital expenditure budget and 
proposed additions to rate base in a rate case.  In that 
context, our paramount concern is ensuring that customers who 
rely on utility service can securely depend on it, particularly 
when needed most.  For gas service, relied upon by customers for 
heating above all else, the need is highest, and the 
consequences of a system failure are greatest when temperatures 
are the coldest.  Thus, the Commission requires gas corporations 
to plan their systems so that they can provide safe, adequate, 
and reliable service under design day conditions; i.e., the 
potential coldest day of the year.  For KEDNY’s service area, 
the design day is based on an average temperature of zero 
degrees Fahrenheit, the coldest average daily temperature 
actually experienced in KEDNY’s service territory.  Arctic 
temperatures can hit New York City, as they did in late December 
of 2022 when they dropped below 10˚F.49 
 
 
The Commission is mindful of, and has taken numerous 
actions to support, New York State’s transition from dependency

The Commission also wishes to address three claims

considered as satisfactorily accomplished unless certified by an 
inspector of the department.”  Department Staff certified that 
the tests were satisfactorily conducted by KEDNY prior to Phases 
1-4 of MRI being placed in service.
