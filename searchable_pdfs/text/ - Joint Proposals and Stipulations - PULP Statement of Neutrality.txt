Dear Judges Costello and Leary:  
Pursuant to your Ruling Revising the Procedural Schedule, issued May 24, 2021, in the 
above referenced proceedings, the Public Utility Law Project of New York, Inc. (“PULP”) 
submits this statement of Neutrality on the Joint Proposal (“JP”) filed on May 14, 2021.  PULP 
neither supports nor opposes the JP and instead files this Statement of Neutrality. 
 
I. Introduction 
Since the Brooklyn Union Gas Company d/b/a National Grid NY (“KEDNY”) and 
KeySpan Gas East Corp. d/b/a National Grid (“KEDLI”), (hereinafter referred to as the 
“Companies” or “KEDNY/KEDLI”), tariff filing on April 30, 2019, PULP has been a party in 
these cases.  PULP has filed both direct and rebuttal testimony, appeared at public hearings

across the service territory, engaged in discovery, participated in the Evidentiary Hearing, and 
engaged in rigorous and lengthy negotiations leading to this proposed settlement.1   
PULP’s organizational mission requires that it advocate, educate, and litigate on behalf of 
rural and urban low-income and fixed-income utility consumers. In rate cases, PULP therefore 
begins by reviewing the proposed rate increases of a utility and the affect any increase would 
have on affordability for the low-income and fixed-income consumers in that utility’s service 
territory.  PULP examines collections procedures including the obtaining of liens, replevins, 
service terminations, and other collections activity, and PULP generally examines all proposals 
that would affect the revenue requirement in a manner that might harm the interests of vulnerable 
consumers, and any other proposals in PULP’s opinion that could be contrary to the public 
interest.  
In reviewing the Company’s existing rates and rate structure, we have found an 
extraordinary increase in the negative impacts of the Company’s high rates on the low-, 
moderate-, and fixed-income residents in its service territory, especially considering the COVID-
19 health pandemic.  In fact, the increases that will occur in rate years two (April 1, 2021, to 
March 31, 2022) and three (April 1, 2022, to March 1, 2023), along with numerous issues 
concerning embedded cost of service (“ECOS”) methodology, Return on Equity (“ROE”), 
earnings sharing mechanisms, the “demand capacity surcharge mechanism,”2 and various 
“earnings incentive mechanisms,” when reviewed collectively could result in earnings 
substantially higher than those sought in the agreed upon revenue requirement, and do not 
comprise a JP that PULP can support. 
However, there are aspects of the JP that specifically recognize key concerns we raised in 
our testimony – see below -- and in previous KEDNY/KEDLI rate cases, which we believe could 
significantly enhance consumer protections throughout the rate plan.  As such, PULP is not 
opposing the JP, but instead, submits this Statement of Neutrality.

II. Specific Terms of the Joint Proposal 
A. Customer Initiatives3  
1. Arrears Resolution 
With a rapidly growing number of KEDNY/KEDLI customers falling behind on their 
utility bills due to the COVID-19 health pandemic and economic downturn, the JP includes an 
agreement to address the arrears crisis if the Public Service Commission does not Order a 
resolution by mid-September 2021.  This provision is the first of its kind in a joint proposal and a 
responsible and proactive plan that could serve as a model for any regulated energy utility 
operating in New York State. (JP at 120). 
2. KEDLI File Match with Nassau and Suffolk Counties4 
The JP specifically requires KEDLI to collaborate with the New York State Office of 
Temporary Disability Assistance (“OTDA”) and Nassau and Suffolk Counties to investigate an 
expanded file-matching mechanism for its Energy Assistance Program (“EAP”) customers.    
Increasing file-matching gives KEDLI the ability to find more eligible low-income utility 
customers that will benefit from EAP. PULP and AARP have collaborated in the past to try and 
push the Commissioners of the Nassau and Suffolk County DSS’ to create a file matching 
process with KEDLI, to no avail. It is our hope that provision 11.2.4 of Section IV of the JP and 
the anticipated meeting arranged by KEDLI with OTDA, DPS, the County Commissioners, 
AARP, PULP and other stakeholders may finally resolve this unnecessarily harmful and 
protracted failure to sign up income-eligible Long Island ratepayers for HEAP and the low-
income affordability program.  
3. Language Access  
The Joint Proposal requires KEDLI and KEDNY to deploy language access offerings 
sufficient to better serve its diverse customer base and ensure consumers are aware of the options 
and programs to help them.5 This will be additionally important in the arrears resolution process 
so that consumers will be able to read and understand documents they must sign to enter into a 
process to eliminate arrears. PULP anticipates advocating for such language access in every

B. Earnings Sharing Mechanism and Superfund Site Cleanup 
KEDNY and KEDLI have agreed to use 50% of their portion of shared earnings within the 
bands identified in Sections 4.3.2 and 4.3.3 of the JP, to reduce regulatory assets (i.e., deferred 
balances to be recovered from customers) related to Site Investigation and Remediation (“SIR”).6    
This agreement, thereby, provides an important step towards the Companies sharing in superfund 
related costs, which can lower the financial impact on ratepayers and, in the opinion of PULP, 
effectuates the Commission’s goals of case 11-M-0034.7  
C. E-DPAs and Ending the Requirement for in-person negotiations 
Prior to Year One of the COVID-19 pandemic and economic collapse, KEDNY required 
elderly, blind and disabled customers to travel to a customer service office (only 2 in Brooklyn, 
and 1 each in Staten Island and the Rockaways) between 8:15am and 5pm to negotiate an 
affordable deferred payment agreement (“DPA”).  Section 11.1 of this JP requires the 
Company(ies) to continue allowing consumers to negotiate their DPAs, and particularly 
“minimum DPAs” over the phone, which was begun as a public health concession during the 
State’s “shelter in place” requirements created by Executive Order 202 et seq. of 2020. 
 
III.  Conclusion 
As explained herein, PULP neither opposes nor supports the JP, but rather is remaining 
neutral due to a belief that such a position is consistent with the balancing of equities in this 
settlement. On the one hand, KEDNY & KEDLI engaged with PULP and AARP and DPS in 
seeking solutions to several discrete issues of key importance to vulnerable ratepayers. On the 
other hand, there are certain other issues relating to rate increases, the sui generis movement of 
capital expenditures on infrastructure to a process that arguably cannot be opposed by parties and 
non-parties,8 and certain other procedural decisions and matters that prevent PULP from
