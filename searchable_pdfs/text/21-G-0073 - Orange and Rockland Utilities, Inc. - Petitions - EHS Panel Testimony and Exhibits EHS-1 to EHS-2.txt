A. 
MGPs provided energy in the form of combustible gases 
1 
of varying composition to municipal street lighting 
2 
systems and to homes and businesses in cities and 
3 
towns across the more densely populated regions of the 
4 
United States. In the case of the areas served by 
5 
Orange and Rockland and its predecessor companies, 
6 
MGPs operated from the late 1850s through the early 
7 
1960s. The MGPs converted coal (oven gas), or a 
8 
combination of coke or coal, oil, and water in the 
9 
form of steam (carbureted water gas), into a gas 
10 
product that could be used for lighting, cooking, and 
11 
heating prior to the time when electricity and natural 
12 
gas came into use for these purposes. There were more 
13 
than 200 MGPs in New York State and an estimated 3,000 
14 
to 5,000 in the United States, mostly in the Northeast 
15 
and Midwest, prior to these plants becoming obsolete 
16 
due to the construction of natural gas pipelines and 
17 
large electric generating stations.
