PRELIMINARY STATEMENT 
 
In accordance with the April 11, 2024 Ruling Establishing Procedural Schedule, the City 
of New York (“City”) submits this Reply Statement in Support of the Joint Proposal filed with the 
New York State Public Service Commission (“Commission”) on April 9, 2024 in the above-
captioned gas rate cases for The Brooklyn Union Gas Company d/b/a National Grid NY 
(“KEDNY”) and KeySpan Gas East Corporation d/b/a National Grid (“KEDLI”) (together, 
“National Grid” or the “Companies”).   
Initially, the City appreciates the concerns raised by parties on continued capital 
investments in the gas system and the need to shrink and decarbonize this system.  Those concerns, 
however, should not be viewed solely in the vacuum of these rate cases and instead should be 
considered within the broader context of Commission proceedings designed to further the City’s 
and State’s decarbonization goals.  In particular, as noted in its Statement in Support, the City 
views this Joint Proposal as but one step in “an assertive and actionable plan to transition National 
Grid customers off of gas and onto non-pipes alternatives while also building reliable infrastructure

to provide zero emissions fuels generated from local sources to hard-to-decarbonize uses.”1  The 
City looks forward, in particular, to National Grid’s forthcoming Long Term Gas Plan, and expects 
that this Plan will provide much-needed detail on the actions that need to be taken in the near-, 
mid-, and long-term to implement many of the commitments embedded in the Joint Proposal. 
Specific to this Joint Proposal, several parties filed Statements in Opposition stating 
concerns with KEDNY’s Newtown Creek purification system and opposing the limited cost

Reply to specifically address those concerns and reiterate that, based on the record developed in 
these proceedings and the likely outcome had the cases been fully litigated, the Joint Proposal 
represents a better outcome for customers.3

REPLY STATEMENT 
 
POINT I 
 
THE JOINT PROPOSAL PROPERLY ADDRESSES 
CONCERNS ON THE NEWTOWN CREEK PROJECT 
 
Several parties raised concerns over the ongoing operational problems at KEDNY’s 
Newtown Creek purification system (“KEDNY System”), which operates in conjunction with the 
City’s Department of Environmental Protection’s (“NYC DEP”) digesters at the Newtown Creek 
Wastewater Resource Facility (“Newtown Creek WRRF”).  The City acknowledged these 
problems in its Initial Statement and recognizes that several parties are concerned that the problems 
will continue.  The Joint Proposal, however, properly addresses many of these concerns and the 
recommended solutions therein would not have been achieved had these cases been litigated. 
Most important, the Joint Proposal requires KEDNY and NYC DEP to negotiate a service 
level agreement to address specific issues raised by both the City and other intervening parties, 
including, but not limited to, notifications and prompt responses to outages at the KEDNY System.  
When operating properly, the KEDNY System allows for the capture and beneficial re-use of the 
on-site biomethane production that results from the operation of the Newtown Creek WRRF, 
thereby significantly reducing the flaring that would otherwise necessarily occur.  Prompt response 
by National Grid to outages at the KEDNY System is critical to addressing biomethane flaring at 
Newtown Creek, which NYC DEP must do when it cannot capture and re-use the biomethane 
produced by the on-site digesters.  The Joint Proposal provides the opportunity to address and 
rectify operational issues with the KEDNY System, and addressing the flaring concerns raised by 
parties to these proceedings would not be possible by litigating the case.

The Joint Proposal contains additional measures designed to improve performance and 
operational transparency.  It requires KEDNY to file an annual public report regarding the 
operation of the KEDNY system, as well as requiring additional efforts to ensure that any future 
environmental benefits associated with the production of renewable natural gas from the KEDNY 
System remain local.4  And, in response to concerns that the rate plan does not incentivize 
improved performance, the Joint Proposal contains a revenue reconciliation mechanism specific 
to the KEDNY System that places shareholder funds at risk if defined revenue targets are not met,5 
thereby creating a concrete financial incentive within the Joint Proposal for improved performance. 
Many of these proposed improvements were not raised in testimony.  Indeed, most 
references to the Newtown Creek WRRF in testimony were general statements of concern related 
to the use of biomethane as an alternative fuel source.  As such, the Newtown Creek provisions in 
the Joint Proposal create a better, more transparent result than what could have been achieved in a 
litigated case.  Together, the Newtown Creek provisions are intended to ensure better operation of 
the KEDNY System, so that the surrounding community will reap the full environmental benefits 
associated with those improved operations.

POINT II 
 
THE BIOMETHANE INTERCONNECTION PROVISIONS 
ARE CONSISTENT WITH COMMISSION PRECEDENT 
AND STATE AND CITY POLICIES 
 
 
Some parties opposed the Joint Proposal provisions that authorize recovery of limited 
interconnection costs for new biomethane production facilities.  These parties raised particular 
concerns with the use of biomethane as an alternative fuel source.6   
Biomethane has been recognized by the State as a viable option for transitioning off of the 
gas system.  The Climate Action Council, for example, noted in its Final Scoping Plan that 
renewable natural gas may be considered “to decarbonize the gas system as it transitions.”7  The 
Commission also previously acknowledged the role that renewable natural gas will have in 
decarbonizing the system.8  Recently, the Commission approved cost recovery for interconnection 
facilities for a new biomethane project in Consolidated Edison Company of New York, Inc.’s 
(“Con Edison”) gas rate case.9

The biomethane interconnections provisions in the Joint Proposal are directly in line with 
the requirements in the Con Edison Rate Order.  There, the Commission required that Con Edison: 
[A]t least 90 days prior to the construction of any interconnection project to receive 
renewable natural gas, file a report containing the following information: cost 
estimate of the interconnection project; summary of benefits to the reliability of the 
natural gas system in the vicinity of the interconnection project and in the service 
territory in general; a detailed description of the source materials being used at the 
interconnected facility to produce renewable natural gas; and a detailed accounting 
of the environmental impacts of any renewable natural gas that will be procured by 
Con Edison as a result of the interconnection.10 
 
The Joint Proposal contains near-identical requirements before National Grid can recover 
interconnection costs for new biomethane facilities.   
The only material difference between the Con Edison requirements and those in the Joint 
Proposal is that here, the Companies would include in the report “a detailed accounting of the 
upstream [greenhouse gas] emissions avoided by the biomethane that the Companies will procure 
as a result of the interconnection,”11 as opposed to the “environmental impacts” referenced in the 
Con Edison Rate Order.  This difference is not related to the interconnection itself, but in any event 
it is helpful to explicitly calculate the avoided greenhouse gas emissions from these proposed 
projects.  Limited cost recovery of interconnection facilities is also aligned with the Commission’s 
finding in the Con Edison rate case that the interconnection project can help determine whether 
using renewable natural gas can reduce emissions,12 as well as the Climate Action Council’s note

that additional analysis should be taken to examine the emissions impacts of renewable natural

Mitigating existing emissions is largely why the City is pursuing two of the biomethane 
projects covered by the Joint Proposal.  These projects are targeted to existing City wastewater 
resource recovery facilities that currently flare the excess biomethane produced at the facilities.  
To the extent that they are sited during the Rate Plan, the new biomethane projects will allow the 
City to capture and beneficially re-use this biomethane, in turn reducing the localized air impacts 
associated with flaring. 
 
Finally, the City specifically disagrees with NRDC’s contention that new biomethane 
projects should be treated analogous to electric distributed generation (“DG”) projects, with project 
developers responsible for interconnection upgrade costs.14  There are several critical differences 
between electric DG projects and biomethane projects that preclude an apples-to-apples 
comparison.  For example, unlike biomethane projects, electric DG projects have an established 
and predictable valuation structure for project output—the Value Stack—that compensates 
projects for electricity, capacity, environmental attributes, and avoided distribution value.15  
Electric DG projects also have access to a host of NYSERDA incentives, including base incentives 
and a variety of adders depending on project configuration, which biomethane projects cannot 
access.16

The limited cost recovery for biomethane interconnection facilities in the Joint Proposal

on cost recovery while supporting projects that align with State and City climate policies.  
CONCLUSION 
For all the reasons set forth herein and in the City’s Initial Statement in Support, the terms 
and conditions of the Joint Proposal are reasonable and the Joint Proposal was developed 
consistent with the Commission’s Settlement Guidelines.  The issues of concern raised by the 
opposing parties regarding Newtown Creek and new biomethane projects have been sufficiently 
addressed by provisions within the Joint Proposal that are in the public interest and provide for 
safe and adequate service.  Therefore, subject to the City’s limited concerns in its Initial Statement 
in Support, the City respectfully recommends that the Commission adopt the Joint Proposal.
