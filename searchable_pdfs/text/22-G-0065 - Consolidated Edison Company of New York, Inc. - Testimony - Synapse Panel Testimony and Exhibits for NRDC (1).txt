Q. 
Please summarize your conclusions. 
3 
A. 
Our conclusions are summarized as follows: 
4 
• Several of Con Edison’s gas proposals and programs may be inconsistent with the 
5 
CLCPA. Con Edison’s proposals with respect to the Main Replacement Program 
6 
(MRP), renewable natural gas (RNG), and certified natural gas (certified gas) as 
7 
CLCPA compliance strategies could extend reliance on and create large 
8 
undepreciated balances for the gas system, even as sales will need to decline to 
9 
meet CLCPA targets. Con Edison may set itself up for rapid defection from the 
10 
gas system as gas sales decline and gas rates rise.  
11 
• Continuing Con Edison’s proposed MRP approach at equivalent inflation-
12 
adjusted costs until all of its leak-prone pipe is replaced (in 2040) would result in 
13 
a very large undepreciated plant balance in 2050 of $5.1 billion. This approach 
14 
would result in $52 billion in total revenue requirement associated with MRP 
15 
program investments from 2023 to 2040, which would exacerbate the gas rate 
16 
increases expected as a result of declines in sales. 
17 
• MRP is not a cost-effective way to reduce GHG emissions, relative to 
18 
electrification and pipeline retirement. Specifically, we show that an approach 
19 
based on building retrofits, electrification, and pipeline retirement could reduce 
20 
emissions at a cost per ton that is  77 percent less expensive than the cost per ton

Q. 
Under standard regulatory practice, how do regulators determine whether

A.

9

11

12

19

20

21 
the investment to recover.

Q. 
What types of electric rate designs can be used to promote beneficial electrification? 
11 
A. 
Electric tariffs with lower volumetric charges can encourage beneficial electrification 
12

13

14

15

16

11 
• By shifting recovery of costs away from volumetric rates, higher fixed charges

and demand charges can encourage customers to consume more electricity. 
13

carbon-intensive fuels through beneficial electrification, but not when it is 
15 
merely a result of less efficient energy use. Lower volumetric rates also reduce 
16 
incentives for traditional energy efficiency and conservation measures,

potentially reducing the effectiveness of these programs. 
18 
• For residential customers, most of the distribution system is sized based on the 
19

20 
maximum demand. Thus, demand charges during off-peak periods may not be

3

4

5

6

9
