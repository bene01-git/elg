ORDER DENYING REHEARING  
 
(Issued and Effective July 18, 2017) 
 
BY THE COMMISSION: 
 
INTRODUCTION 
 
By petition filed on January 17, 2017, the Public 
Utility Law Project of New York, Inc. (PULP) seeks rehearing of 
a Commission order, issued on December 16, 2016 (the December 
Order), which adopted the terms of a Joint Proposal (or JP) 
filed in the above-referenced cases on September 7, 2016.1  The 
December Order established a three-year rate plan for the 
Brooklyn Union Gas Company d/b/a National Grid NY (KEDNY) and 
KeySpan Gas East Corp. d/b/a National Grid (KEDLI) 
(collectively, the Companies) for gas service for the period 
January 1, 2017 through December 31, 2019.  PULP argues that 
rehearing is warranted inasmuch as the December Order (1) 
permits the Companies to recover the majority of their site 
investigation and remediation (SIR) costs through base rates, 
(2) rejects PULP’s recommendation that SIR costs be shared 
between ratepayers and the Companies, and (3) approves rate 
increase percentages without considering the more than 50 
percent increase in the spot market price of natural gas at the 
Henry Hub during the process of the rate case.  For the reasons 
stated below, the petition for rehearing is denied.

BACKGROUND 
The December Order 
As reflected in the December Order, the Companies’ SIR 
expenses include the costs to assess, monitor, clean up and 
restore environmentally contaminated sites, formerly used for 
manufactured gas plant (MGP), for which the Companies were 
determined to be responsible, in whole or part, under the 
federal Superfund law and consent orders entered into with the 
New York State Department of Environmental Conservation (DEC).2  
The Companies each are responsible for more than two dozen MGP 
sites and had been recovering the bulk of their SIR costs 
through SIR recovery surcharges, with a substantially smaller 
portion of SIR costs included in base rates.  As of the December 
Order, KEDNY had been recovering approximately $6.0 million of 
SIR costs in base rates and about $62.5 million annually through 
its SIR Recovery Surcharge.  KEDLI had been recovering 
approximately $3.1 million of SIR costs in base rates and $40 
million annually through its SIR Recovery Surcharge.  However, 
both KEDNY and KEDLI had incurred SIR costs in excess of these 
amounts.  Consequently, the Companies accrued significant levels 
of deferred SIR costs from past periods that had not been 
recovered from ratepayers.   
As relevant here, the December Order adopted 
provisions of the Joint Proposal providing for recovery of SIR 
deferral balances and forecast costs through base rates.  The 
rate plans provide that the Companies will collect in base rates 
for each rate year not only their forecast SIR costs but also 
one-tenth of their SIR deferral balances as of December 31, 2016 
($18.52 million for KEDNY and $14.17 million for KEDLI).  Thus, 
the rate plan allows KEDNY to collect in base rates $72.3

The SIR Order

PULP’s Petition 
On January 17, 2017, PULP filed the petition seeking 
rehearing of the December Order, asserting three principal 
arguments.  First, PULP argues that the December Order 
erroneously allows the Companies to recover the majority of 
their SIR costs through base rates.  Second, it claims that the 
Commission erred in concluding that it was unnecessary to 
require SIR costs to be shared between ratepayers and the 
Companies’ shareholders.  Third, PULP asserts that the increase 
in natural gas prices during the pendency of the rate cases 
constitutes new circumstances showing that the Companies’ rates 
are not just and reasonable.

the Commission in the December Order.  They also assert that 
PULP’s efforts to introduce evidence of recent increases in the 
commodity cost of natural gas should be rejected as untimely.  
They further argue that PULP’s evidence that natural gas prices 
have increased in recent months does not require a change to the 
December Order because “the current price identified by PULP

DISCUSSION 
 
 
Pursuant to 16 NYCRR 3.7(b), rehearing may be sought 
only on the grounds that an error of law or fact was committed 
or that new circumstances warrant a different determination.   
As discussed in further detail below, PULP has identified no 
error of law or fact, nor any new circumstances that would 
warrant a different determination.  Rather, it simply disagrees 
with the legal, factual and policy-based conclusions that the 
Commission reached.  Accordingly, PULP’s petition for rehearing 
is denied. 
 
Recovery of SIR Costs in Base Rates 
PULP first argues that the December Order lacks any 
statutory or public policy basis for allowing the Companies to 
recover the majority of their SIR costs through base rates.  
PULP apparently asserts that SIR costs are not costs that the 
Companies are entitled to recover from ratepayers.  PULP 
maintains that the MGP sites were not “used and useful” for 
existing ratepayers, and that the purchase of those sites was 
not an investment of money reasonably expended to meet the 
utility’s legal obligation to provide adequate service.19 
PULP’s argument lacks merit.  As Staff points out, 
the SIR Order declined to impose SIR cost sharing under a “used 
and useful” standard.  Instead, the Commission determined that 
SIR expenses should be treated as “normal costs of doing 
business” because utilities are required by law to incur those

Spot Market Price of Natural Gas 
Finally, PULP argues that the rates set in the 
December Order are unjust and unreasonable because they failed 
to take into account that the spot market price of natural gas at 
the Henry Hub increased by more than 50 percent during the 
processing of these rate cases.40  PULP claims that the Companies 
and DPS Staff did not correct for the change in gas price as part 
of their cases and that, as a result, the Commission approved 
rate increase percentages that are much higher than the 
percentages indicated in the December Order.  According to PULP, 
the change in the price of gas is a new circumstance that 
warrants rehearing.  
We disagree that a change in the commodity price of 
natural gas warrants rehearing of the December Order.  In 
considering proposed revenue increases, the Commission may 
consider total bill impacts, which are most meaningful to 
ratepayers and which include not only proposed delivery rates 
but also forecasted commodity costs for natural gas.  However, 
the Commission regulates only the delivery rates, not the 
commodity cost of natural gas.  The December Order determined 
that the revenue increases proposed for the Companies resulted in 
just and reasonable delivery rates.  Therefore, rehearing is not 
required on this issue because fluctuations in the commodity cost 
of gas do not change the delivery rates in the December Order, 
which remain just and reasonable.
