8 
 
9 
Q.  
Please summarize the greenhouse gas reduction mandates required by the CLCPA. 
10 
A.  
The CLCPA requires economy-wide greenhouse gas emissions reductions of 40% from 
11 
1990 levels by 2030 and 85% reductions from 1990 levels by 2050.4 The law requires 
12 
electric power greenhouse gas emissions reductions of 100% by 2040.5 The law also 
13 
requires 70% renewable electricity by 2030 and statewide energy efficiency gains of 185 
14 
trillion British thermal units (“tBtu”) from the 2025 forecast.6 The greenhouse gases 
15 
covered by the law are not just carbon dioxide, but also methane and other greenhouse 
16 
gases. The emissions reduction requirements cover not just emissions coming from 
17 
electricity generation, but also combustion heating and transportation. The CLCPA does 
18 
not specify GHG reduction sub-targets for different sectors.  
19 
 
20 
Q.  
When you refer to “combustion heating” what exactly are you talking about? 
21 
A.  
I am talking about the burning of any carbon fuels directly in buildings to heat a space, to 
22 
heat water, to cook, to dry clothes, and to run a gas fireplace that results in GHG 
23 
emissions directly from the buildings. Combustion heating does not include electricity 
24 
used for space heating, hot water, stoves or clothes dryers. 
25

Q.  
Please characterize the greenhouse gases that are emitted by the combustion heating 
4 
sector in New York.  
5 
A.  
When fossil fuels used for heat are burned for thermal energy, those fuels emit carbon 
6 
dioxide (“CO2”), which is one of the two major contributors to global climate change.7 
7 
The other major contributor to global climate change is methane, which is a greenhouse 
8 
gas with a global warming potential 86 times higher than CO2 over a 20-year period.8 
9 
 
10 
Importantly, there are fugitive greenhouse gas emissions associated with the fossil fuels 
11 
used for combustion heating, which are released during their extraction, transportation, 
12 
and distribution to buildings before they are burned for energy.9 In the case of natural 
13 
gas, a main component of these fugitive emissions is methane.  The most recent Proposed 
14 
Part 496, Statewide Emissions Limits by the New York Department of Environmental 
15 
Conservation (“DEC”) Emissions Inventory identified 3.14% as the likely methane 
16 
leakage rate along the gas distribution system for New York.10 As shown in Exhibit 4, 
17 
this leakage rate represents a CO2 equivalent (CO2e) greenhouse gas emissions level that 
18 
is 98% higher than the CO2 level (see Exhibit 4 for calculations), thereby nearly doubling 
19 
the emissions footprint of the natural gas used in buildings. As an example, a natural gas 
20 
building that emits 5 metric tons of CO2 per year from burning the gas for heat has a 
21 
CO2e footprint of 9.9 metric tons per year (5 times 1.98) once fugitive methane is 
22 
accounted for.

Environmentally beneficial electrification is a term to describe the process of

11

12 
low efficiency, emissions intensive fossil fuel space and hot water heaters to high 
13

RECOMMENDATIONS 
4 
 
5 
Q.  
Based on all of your analysis of the CLCPA targets, the application of those targets 
6 
to National Grid’s Upstate service territory, and National Grid’s proposals, what 
7 
are your recommendations? 
8 
A.  
The Commission should require the Company to plan its investments around specifically 
9 
meeting the CLCPA greenhouse gas reduction mandates.   
10 
1. This will require the Company to invest in heat pumps and energy efficiency at a scale 
11 
that brings down emissions associated with the Company’s gas system by 40% by 2030 
12 
and 85% by 2050 and plan for the near total phase out of its gas infrastructure system by 
13 
2050.  
14

15 
cease marketing gas as a clean or beneficial technology.  
16

17 
infrastructure beyond what is absolutely necessary to maintain reliability and safety for 
18 
current gas customers.  
19 
4. The Company should be required to develop a plan for an orderly phase out and 
20 
retirement schedule for a minimum of 85% of its gas assets and their replacement with 
21 
individual or shared renewable thermal energy systems.  
22 
5. The Commission should require National Grid to adopt annual targets in CO2e emissions 
23 
reductions to be achieved with specific targets for energy efficiency and heat pumps, as 
24 
these provide the most effective methods for reducing emissions.

26 
subsidize oil-to-gas conversions, as they would continue to nearly double residential 
27 
housing unit CO2e emissions.
