Q. Please explain municipal interference and why Con Edison incurs costs 
related to municipal interference.  
 
A. Municipal interference occurs when Con Edison’s facilities are within, 
or very close to, the work area of a municipal project and depending on 
the project scope, Con Edison must relocate or protect its facilities. The 
majority of the Company’s costs to relocate and protect its facilities are 
not reimbursable by the municipalities. Common municipal projects that 
generate interference costs for Con Edison include, but are not limited to, 
installation and repair of sewers and drainage facilities, and the 
reconstruction of roadways, curbs, sidewalks, and highway bridges. 
 
Please see the initial testimony of the Company’s Municipal Infrastructure Support Panel on 
page 9-10: 
 
Q. Please explain the term “interference” as it pertains to the Company. 
 
A. Con Edison has an extensive system of gas and electric facilities under 
and above the streets in the service territory, including but not limited to 
gas mains, gas services, manholes, vaults, electric services, electric cables, 
conduits, poles, and appurtenances of various sizes and operating voltages.  
Under the streets, Con Edison gas and electric facilities share space with 
privately-owned facilities such as telephone and cable TV, and with 
municipally owned facilities such as water, sewer, transit and traffic 
facilities.  Above ground, Con Edison electric overhead facilities share 
space with telephone, cable TV, fire alarm, street lighting and traffic 
signals. 
 
The term “interference” describes the work Con Edison must perform 
whenever its overhead or underground facilities are “in the way” on a 
municipal project and therefore must be located, identified, relocated, 
replaced, protected, or otherwise supported to accommodate the municipal 
project. 
 
A detailed description of Con Edison’s program to address interference can be found starting on 
page 9 line 15 through page 18 line 7.
