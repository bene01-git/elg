PRELIMINARY STATEMENT 
 
The Public Utility Law Project of New York (“PULP”) hereby submits this Statement in 
Opposition of the Joint Proposal (“JP”) filed with the New York State Public Service Commission 
(“PSC” or the “Commission”) in these proceedings on February 16, 2023.1 PULP urges the 
Administrative Law Judges (“ALJs”) to recommend to the Commission that it reject the JP because 
it offers rates for Consolidated Edison (“Con Edison” or the “Company”) that are not “just and 
reasonable” and not in the public interest. These concepts are the fundamental responsibilities of 
the PSC and the utilities it regulates.2 The JP promotes bill increases and policies that fail to go far 
enough to accurately support the public interest, including low-fixed income residential 
households and disadvantaged communities (“DACs”). Especially concerning to PULP are:  
• The JP is not supported by a majority of parties;

twenty months), the settling parties’ recommendations would have the following average 
impacts on delivery charges for Con Edison’s residential customers:

A. The JP Fails To Comply With The Commission’s Settlement Guidelines 
 
 
The Commission has established the following criteria for evaluating a joint proposal: (a) 
the extent to which a joint proposal is supported by adverse parties; (b) whether the record for 
decision is adequate; and (c) whether the settlement is consistent with law and public policy, has 
a rational basis, balances the interests of customers and shareholders, and compares favorably with 
the probable outcome of litigation.

Company. For instance, the Company has agreed to modify its policy on service terminations 
during extreme heat events,7 as well as, “where feasible,” expanding its language access offerings

both the electric and gas side of the rate cases, which is evident when reviewing the number of 
signature pages filed on DMM.9

low- and fixed-income households in the JP, which is inconsistent with public policy and the law, 
including the CLCPA. Without appropriate, specific focus on assisting low-income households 
and DACs to manage the increase in cost and energy burden caused by the proposed rates, PULP

New York State regulation offers a myriad of benefits to the utilities it regulates. In 
addition to fully forecasted test years and the ability to earn their allowed return, New 
York regulation also allows for risk-reducing deferral and true-up mechanisms, a full 
pass through of commodity costs to ratepayers and an approach to setting ROEs that is 
very transparent and predictable. These attributes of New York State regulation reduce 
the risk borne by the owners of New York utility equity, thereby reducing the return 
required and the cost of equity. These risk-reducing regulatory mechanisms are not 
employed in all jurisdictions, and those jurisdictions that use them may not do so to the 
same extent as New York. 
 
Finally, PULP’s proposal can achieve net benefits for ratepayers in rising interest rate 
environments, such as the one since 2020. The GFM uses multiple factors to produce its 
recommended ROE, including expectations for economic growth, inflation, stock prices, 
dividends, interest rates and other variables. Changes in any one of the GFM’s inputs (such as 
interest rates) are not necessarily predictive indicators, either in direction or magnitude, for 
changes in the model’s recommended ROE. In fact, “risk-free” rates and Model ROEs moved in 
opposite directions more times than they moved together in the past twelve years.36   
 
PULP considers this proposed framework as able to capture a reasonable representation of 
updated macroeconomic and investment expectations affecting the Company’s cost of equity 
during the proposed rate plan, unlike the proposed “fixed” ROE of the settling parties, which PULP 
argues has no rational basis and therefore cannot be deemed to be likely to result in just and 
reasonable utility rates to be paid by Con Edison customers. To cite but one example, PULP’s 
proposal seeks to address “regulatory lag” through as timely a true-up as practicable; while the 
settling parties proposed framework fails entirely to update ROE, and provides only an inadequate,

E. The Failure of the JP To Freeze Fixed Charges Makes It Unaffordable And Against 
The Public Interest. 
 
PULP argues that freezing monthly fixed charges and allocating any rate increases to 
volumetric rates would lower bill impacts for low- and moderate-usage customers. Such a freeze 
would be especially helpful to low- and moderate-usage gas customers, since the proposed 
monthly fixed charge increases for gas are much higher than they are for electric. High fixed 
charges interfere with the ability of individual ratepayers to achieve bill savings by managing their 
usage, and therefore make it more difficult to achieve the goals of the CLCPA through 
conservation. The JP fails to address the fixed charges in a manner that would help residential 
customers achieve such savings through conservation, thereby providing another example of why 
the JP is unaffordable and against the public interest. Accordingly, PULP believes the settling 
parties’ proposal to increase such charges should be rejected, and any increased revenue 
requirements resulting from an order in these proceedings should be applied to volumetric rates.
