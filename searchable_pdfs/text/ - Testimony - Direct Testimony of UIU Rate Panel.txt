IV. REVENUE ALLOCATION 
15 
A. 
Background 
Q. 
Please explain the revenue allocation process in 
16 
a typical utility rate case.  
17 
A. 
Revenue allocation is the assignment of the 
18 
dollar net increase or decrease to each of the 
19 
Company’s rate classes in a base rates 
20 
proceeding. In contrast, rate design determines 
21 
how the allocated revenue is recovered from 
22 
individual ratepayers within each class. From a 
23 


--- Page 20 ---
Cases 25-E-0072 and 25-G-0073  
 Direct Testimony of the UIU 
Rate Panel 
 
 
19 
cost recovery standpoint, revenue allocation 
1 
addresses inter-class cross-subsidization 
2 
issues, while rate design addresses intra-class 
3 
cross-subsidization issues.  
4 
 
5 
Most utilities and regulators adopt a policy 
6 
that aims to move class revenues into line with 
7 
allocated costs by varying the magnitude of rate 
8 
increases for individual rate classes. The 
9 
results of one or more ECOS studies are used to 
10 
help guide decisions regarding how proposed rate 
11 
changes should be spread across the various rate 
12 
classes. A common metric to link cost allocation 
13 
to revenue allocation is the indexed (or 
14 
unitized) rate of return (ROR). The indexed ROR 
15 
metric is measured as the class ROR divided by 
16 
the system average ROR, at current and proposed 
17 
rates. If revenues are fully in line with 
18 
allocated costs, the class indexed ROR is unity 
19 
(1.0). Thus, if a class has an indexed ROR at 
20 
present rates that are higher than system 
21 
average, it is deemed to be over-recovering 
22 
costs, and conversely, where the indexed ROR is 
23 


--- Page 21 ---
Cases 25-E-0072 and 25-G-0073  
 Direct Testimony of the UIU 
Rate Panel 
 
 
20 
below unity, the class is under-recovering 
1 
allocated costs. If a class is over-recovering 
2 
costs at present rates, the utility should
