IV. 
IT Cost Allocation and Historic Test Year Costs 
1 
Q.  
How are the costs of IT projects and investments charged to Niagara 
2 
Mohawk? 
3 
A.  
IT capital projects and investments that are shared investments across 
4 
operating companies are implemented and owned by National Grid 
5 
Service Company.  The costs of these investments are allocated to Niagara 
6 
Mohawk (and the other operating companies that utilize and/or benefit 
7 
from the investments) in the form of rent expense.  Exhibit __ (ITP-2) 
8 
provides the allocation factors utilized to determine the forecast annual 
9 
rent expense for the IT projects planned for the Rate Year and Data Years.   
10 
 
11 
Q. 
How are the costs of IT capital projects recovered? 
12 
A. 
The costs of IT capital projects are amortized over a period of years.  The 
13 
amortization period is determined by the average service lives of the 
14 
assets.  In general, many of the IT-related assets on the books of the 
15 
National Grid Service Company are either software intangibles or portable 
16 
general plant equipment that is being depreciated/amortized and recovered 
17 
over a period equal to their planned usefulness or expected lives.  The 
18 
actual physical replacement of assets is determined by business decisions 
19 
to maintain, refresh, replace, and/or decommission assets, and that 
20 
evaluation is conducted based on variables such as operational risk, cost to 
21 


--- Page 21 ---
Testimony of the Information Technology Panel 
Page 19 of 74 
 
be incurred, and the availability of replacement options.  Recovery of 
1 
hardware and equipment and amortization of software intangible assets 
2 
will approximate the useful life of the new system, taking into 
3 
consideration technological and functional obsolescence.   
4

IT Operating Model 
The IT organization is structured to ensure accountability for achieving its goals.  The organization 
includes four CIO positions (US CIO, UK CIO, Group Functions CIO, and Ventures CIO) who 
report to the Group Chief Information and Digital Officer.  The US CIO is fully responsible for 
US IT operations including developing the current and future capabilities and skills of the teams 
that serve National Grid’s US businesses.  The US CIO is a member of the executive management 
team overseeing US business operations and is the face of IT for National Grid’s US business, as 
well as the conduit for presenting business requirements to the overall IT organization.

Reporting to the US CIO are five US IT Business Partners (i.e., Electric, Gas, Transmission and 
Capital Delivery, Customer, and Finance and Business Services).  The Business Partners sit on the 
management teams of their respective business segments and are responsible for the relationship 
with the business stakeholders.  They also are responsible for long-term planning, development of 
annual budgets, and ensuring IT programs and projects deliver the promised business benefits and 
enable the businesses to deliver their commitments to customers.  
The Business Partners work with National Grid’s IT Delivery Center leaders to ensure that 
resources with appropriate skill sets are available to meet the forecast demands of National Grid’s 
businesses.  Delivery Center leaders are accountable for providing services at agreed levels of cost, 
time, and quality by developing and implementing common ways of working across the US.  The 
IT organization contains the following Delivery Centers: Strategy and Innovation; Enterprise 
Architecture; Global Solution Development; Infrastructure and Operations; Security; Commercial 
and Vendor Management; and Data Management.  A simplified graphic depiction of National 
Grid’s IT organization is provided in Exhibit __ (ITP-1).  
IT Delivery Centers 
Strategy and Innovation - The Strategy and Innovation delivery center is accountable for the 
long-term IT strategy of National Grid and ensuring alignment with the regions and NG Ventures. 
This delivery center also incorporates ngDigital Labs to pilot emerging technologies and take a 
leading role in the digital transformation of National Grid. The Strategy and Innovation delivery 
center is focused on the longer term organizational and technology strategy for the IT function. 
The development of the IT strategy has a number of steps through the year. Annually, the NG US 
Exhibit __ (ITP-1)

business creates a strategic business plan. This plan lays out a bottom up Program level plan for 
the next five years laying out major investment areas, the expected benefit from these investments, 
the cost to achieve and the projected run the business costs. The Strategy and Innovation team 
work closely with the business partners and delivery center leaders to lay out the investment plans 
for the next five years. As part of the extended strategy team, Enterprise Architecture reviews these 
plans and identifies where we need to set new architecture policies, leverage emerging 
technologies and where we can find opportunities for reuse. These plans are then combined to give

delivery centers and supporting functions (HR, Procurement, etc.)  
 Enterprise Architecture – The Enterprise Architecture delivery center develops strategies and 
defines roadmaps that best achieve business and technology goals with security, compliance, and

responses to disruptive forces and business strategy demand by identifying, analyzing, and 
publishing architectural artifacts that serve as IT’s guide rails and architectural “north star” for 
delivering solutions to National Grid. Enterprise Architecture’s primary purpose is to guide 
National Grid towards optimal IT investment decisions. Enterprise Architecture ensures that the 
identification of options, trade-offs and the strategy for the future is neither narrow nor shallow. 
This includes:
