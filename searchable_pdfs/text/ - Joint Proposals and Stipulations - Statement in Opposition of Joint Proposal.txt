I. Preliminary Statement 
State Senator Robert Jackson (“Senator Jackson”) respectfully submits this Statement in 
Opposition to the Joint Proposal (“JP”) filed by Consolidated Edison Company of New York 
(“Con Edison”) on November 5, 2025. 
While acknowledging constructive progress in language access, reporting, Energy Affordability 
Program (EAP) funding, AMI deployment, and initial NWA/NPA frameworks, Senator Jackson 
opposes the Joint Proposal because overall residential affordability remains inadequate. The JP 
leaves households facing higher bills, complex reconciliations, and property tax volatility 
without sufficient relief. Neutrality requires that rate design and recovery structures stabilize or 
reduce the average household’s energy burden; this Joint Proposal fails that test. 
II. Statement of Interest 
Senator Jackson represents communities within Con Edison’s service territory that are especially 
vulnerable to bill impacts, housing instability, and cumulative environmental burdens. Over 30% 
of constituents are eligible for the Energy Affordability Program (EAP) and still struggle to pay 
bills even while stacking multiple programs and subsidies. 
Senator Jackson has been a party to these proceedings since their inception and has actively 
participated in the settlement negotiations. His continued involvement underscores a direct and 
substantial interest in Commission review and correction of any proposal that does not prioritize 
affordability, independent verification, and distributional fairness. 
III. Grounds for Opposition 
A. Affordability and Rate Impacts  
The JP proposes 2.8% annual increases for electric service and 2.0% annual increases for 
gas service, compounded across Rate Years 1–3. For many families, these increases cause major 
financial distress.

B. Return on Equity (ROE) The proposed ROE of 9.4% is excessive under current market 
conditions. A 9.0% ROE better balances investor needs with affordability obligations.

C. Reconciliations and Bill Volatility Frequent surcharges, overlapping reconciliations, and 
long amortizations increase bill complexity and volatility. Simplification is necessary to reduce 
consumer burden. 
D. Commodity Supply Procurement Discipline Con Edison must demonstrate best-efforts 
procurement practices to minimize commodity supply costs, even though it does not profit from 
supply. 
E. Property Tax Advocacy Property tax reconciliations pass volatility to customers. Con Edison 
should actively advocate for reforms to reduce burdens on its tax class and report annually on 
outcomes. 
F. Transparency and Verification Independent audits, machine-readable filings, and public 
disclosure windows are essential to ensure accountability and protect ratepayers. Rates and tariff 
calculations must be provided in machine-readable form, with full customer data pools 
accessible regardless of size. Customers should not need a rate engineer or advanced accounting 
expertise to understand how their bills are calculated. Bill transparency is a bipartisan issue in 
both chambers of the Legislature, and Con Edison should proactively provide this level of access 
before state law compels them to do so. 
IV. Acknowledged Improvements 
Senator Jackson recognizes and supports several constructive elements within the Joint Proposal, 
which should be preserved and strengthened regardless of the Commission’s ultimate 
disposition: 
• 
Residential Demand Calculation (SC 1 Rates IV and V, Rider Z) – Improves 
understandability of demand rates. 
• 
Coincident Demand – Extends coincident demand billing to all customers with multiple 
AMI meters. 
• 
Earnings Adjustment Mechanisms (EAMs): 
o Demand Response EAM promotes grid flexibility.

Sherman Creek Service Center (Project 21656717) reflects needed investment in 
infrastructure and community service reliability.

V. Conclusion 
For the reasons stated above, Senator Jackson respectfully urges the Commission to reject the 
Joint Proposal in its current form and require modifications that directly address affordability, 
bill stability, property tax reform, and transparency in rate calculations, while preserving the 
constructive improvements noted herein.
