BY THE COMMISSION: 
INTRODUCTION 
 
 
On May 26, 2022, New York State Electric & Gas 
Corporation (NYSEG) and Rochester Gas and Electric Corporation 
(RG&E) (together, the Companies) filed amendments to their 
tariff schedules, P.S.C. Nos. 119, 120, and 121 – Electricity, 
P.S.C. Nos. 87, 88, and 90 – Gas, and P.S.C. Nos. 18 and 19 – 
Electricity, and P.S.C. No. 16 – Gas, respectively, to increase 
their annual electric and gas delivery revenues for the Rate 
Year, which is the twelve months ending April 30, 2024. 
 
 
NYSEG is requesting an increase in annual electric 
delivery revenues of approximately $274 million (31.2 percent 
increase in delivery revenues, or 16.8 percent increase in total 
revenues) for the Rate Year.  NYSEG projects that the requested 
increase would result in a total monthly bill increase of about 
$18.31, or 22.2 percent, for a residential customer using 
600 kWh/month. 
 
 
For its gas business, NYSEG is requesting an increase 
in annual gas delivery revenues of approximately $43.4 million 
(20.7 percent increase in delivery revenues, or 9.8 percent 
increase in total revenues).  NYSEG projects that the requested 
increase would result in a total monthly bill increase of about 
$14.94, or 15.6 percent, for a residential heating customer 
using 83 therms per month. 
 
 
RG&E is requesting an annual increase in electric 
delivery revenues of approximately $93.8 million (19.0 percent 
increase in delivery revenues, or 11.3 percent increase in total 
revenues).  RG&E projects that the requested increase would 
result in a total monthly bill increase of about $12.95, or 
15.0 percent, for a residential customer using 600 kWh/month. 
 
 
For its gas business, RG&E is requesting an increase 
in annual gas delivery revenues of approximately $37.7 million

DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION 
 
 
On April 11, 2023, the Companies filed their consent 
to extend the maximum suspension period of these proceedings 
through October 18, 2023, in order to provide additional time to 
continue settlement discussions.  However, the Companies 
conditioned their consent on receiving a “make whole” provision 
that would keep the Companies in the same financial position as 
they would be absent the extension.  The Companies’ letter 
stated they understand “make whole” to mean that the Companies 
would recover or refund any revenue under-collections or over-
collections in sales revenue resulting from the extended 
suspension period.  The Companies would calculate any revenue 
adjustments as the difference between sales revenues the 
Companies would have billed at new rates compared to sales 
revenues at current rates during the period for which the 
suspension was extended.  The revenue adjustments would include

 In response to the Companies’ April consent, Multiple 
Intervenors filed a letter with the Secretary expressing 
agreement with an extension and the continuation of settlement 
discussions including granting a make whole for the first 
30 days beyond the statutory 11-month suspension period allowed 
by section 66 of the Public Service Law, but objecting to any 
make whole beyond those 30 days.  Several other participating 
parties filed a joint response, objecting to the request to 
extend the maximum suspension period as well as the request to 
be made whole.  In making their objections, the parties’ 
responses cited concerns regarding the size of the requested 
rate increases and the potential effect on bills associated with 
compressing a large annual rate increase into a shorter period 
depending on the date of a final Commission rate decision. 
 
 
In the April Extension Order we noted that we are 
cognizant of the size of the increases requested in the 
underlying case, but that we do not see that foregoing 
settlement discussions might provide outcomes superior to those 
that may be achieved through a multi-year agreement.  As for the 
make-whole, there is no indication that the Companies have acted 
in bad-faith or otherwise are responsible for undue delay.  
Accordingly, we grant the make whole.  However, we request that 
the parties to any proposed settlement consider terms to 
mitigate the impact of the rate year compression resulting from 
the make whole and will consider all options available should a 
proposal fail to include such terms.  To facilitate the orderly 
conduct of these proceedings, the effective date of the tariff 
leaves filed by the Companies is further extended through and 
including October 18, 2023, unless otherwise ordered by the 
Commission, to facilitate the orderly conduct of these

The Commission orders: 
1. The operation of the matters suspended by Order 
issued April 20, 2023, in these proceedings, is further 
suspended and the use of the rates, charges, rules and 
regulations stated in said Notices be further extended through 
October 18, 2023, unless otherwise ordered by the Commission. 
2. New York State Electric & Gas Corporation and 
Rochester Gas & Electric Corporation are directed to file with 
the Commission, on not less than one day’s notice, on or before 
June 28, 2023, consecutively numbered supplements announcing the 
extension of the suspension period of suspended amendments. 
3. New York State Electric & Gas Corporation and 
Rochester Gas & Electric Corporation shall be made whole, as 
defined in these Orders, for the period between June 30, 2023, 
and the date the Commission sets new rates, charges, rules and 
regulations in the final decision in these proceedings. 
4. These proceedings are continued.
