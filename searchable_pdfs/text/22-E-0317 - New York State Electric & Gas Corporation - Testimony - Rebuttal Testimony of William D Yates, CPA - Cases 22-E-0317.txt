45

46

47 
instead of the five-year cycle proposed by the Company. Staff believes this will lessen the

Q. 
Do you agree with Staff’s recommendation for a six-year trim cycle? 
51 
A. 
No. As our climate continues to change, ratepayers will be increasingly reliant on utility 
52

53

54 
quality is achieved for the NYSEG service area that is comparable with the State’s other 
55

58

approach has the best chance of contributing to a solution to the problems of degraded 
63 
service and extremely high deferred storm costs faced by NYSEG customers as identified 
64

Energy Manager Platform 
77 
Q. 
Please summarize the Companies’ energy manager platform proposal and Staff’s 
78 
response to the proposal.  
79 
A. 
As described by Staff, the Companies propose to implement an Energy Manager platform 
80 
concurrent with the deployment of smart meters over the next three years. The Rate Year 
81 
costs associated with this project, as shown in Exhibit__(CSP-8), are $500,000 for NYSEG 
82 
and $700,000 for RG&E. The total costs projected as shown in Exhibit__(CSP-8) are 
83 
$800,000 for NYSEG and $1.7 million for RG&E. Staff agrees with the Companies’ 
84 
proposal to implement the Energy Manager Platform, indicating that the costs of the 
85 
platform are nominal compared to potential benefits to customers. (Staff Consumer 
86 
Services Panel, Page 72-74).  
87 
Q. 
Do you agree with Staff that the Energy Manager Platform should be approved in 
88 
this case? 
89 
A. 
No. Rollout of the Energy Manager Platform should be delayed to the next rate case, when 
90 
AMI meter deployment is completed. Unlike expenditures for vegetation management, 
91 
spending on an energy management platform is not necessary at this time to ensure 
92 
reliability for NYSEG and RG&E customers. Given the trying times alluded to by the Staff 
93 
Policy Panel, reducing spending to only what is necessary to provide safe and reliable 
94 
service across the Companies’ service territories is imperative in order to mitigate the 
95 
additional energy burdens the Companies’ proposals in these proceedings would impose 
96 
on its low-income customers. (Staff Policy Panel Testimony, Page 23).

EAP Budget Caps 
98 
Q. 
Please summarize the Companies’ proposal regarding EAP budgets and Staff’s 
99 
response to the proposal. 
100 


--- Page 7 ---
A. 
The Companies project increases in EAP rate allowances by 28.5% for NYSEG Electric, 
101 
42.3% for NYSEG Gas, 28% for RG&E Electric, and 44% for RG&E Gas. These 
102 
projections contemplated continuing the Companies’ current arrears forgiveness programs 
103 
and continuing reconnection fee waivers for EAP participants. As Staff indicates, these 
104 
projected rate allowances, excluding that of NYSEG Electric, result in budgets surpassing 
105 
the 2% budget cap established in the May 2016 Low Income Order (Staff Consumer 
106 
Services Panel, 82-83).  
107 
In order to bring these projections down below the 2% budget caps, Staff recommends that 
108 
the Companies recalculate bill discounts, phase out their arrears forgiveness programs, and 
109 
reevaluate reconnection fee waivers. (Staff Consumer Services Panel, Page 83-84).  
110 
Q. 
Do you agree with Staff’s recommendation? 
111 
A. 
No. I believe that the Companies should continue reconnection fee waivers for EAP 
112 
participants. When accounts are disconnected, customers typically must make a payment 
113 
toward the balance in order to have their service reconnected. As outlined in the 
114 
Companies’ Human Resources Training Document “RECONNECTION AFTER SONP” 
115 
some customers, including customers who have defaulted on a minimum agreement, have 
116 
no choice but to pay the entire past due amount or to request assistance from the 
117 
Department of Social Services in order to have service reconnected. (NYRC-0372-PULP-
118 
8, Attachment 6, 1-2). I believe that requiring a reconnection fee in addition to the payment 
119 
that must be made in order to restore service will prevent low-income customers from 
120 
having service restored as quickly as possible which presents both economic and safety 
121 
concerns for low-income customers. Given EIA forecasts for sustained, additional supply 
122 
costs increases this winter and the Rate Year, we need to ensure that there are as few 
123 
financial barriers as possible faced by EAP participants seeking to reconnect their service.  
124 
Regarding the Companies’ arrears forgiveness programs, while I understand that Staff 
125 
recommends the discontinuation of the Companies’ current arrears forgiveness programs 
126 
as a means to bring projected EAP rate allowances below 2%, I do not think the period that 
127 
Staff analyzed to arrive at its determination that the benefit of the program is outweighed 
128 
by its administrative costs is appropriate. Staff outlines that from 2018 to 2022, program 
129 
enrollment decreased by 77% at NYSEG and 67% at RG&E. (Staff Consumer Services 
130 


--- Page 8 ---
Panel, Page 85). While this is a steep decline, it is important to note that the Companies 
131 
discontinued residential service terminations from March 2020 to September 2022. During 
132 
this period, customers who would normally make use of such a program no longer had a 
133 
reason to do so.  
134 
The historic relief many low-income customers are seeing as a result of the Commission’s 
135 
Order Authorizing Phase 1 Arrears Reduction has, for the time being, lessened but not 
136 
eliminated the arrears burden for the Companies’ low-income customers. Considering this 
137 
fact, I believe that arrears management programs like those of the Companies remain useful 
138 
and important. Thus, in balancing the need to bring down projected EAP costs while also 
139 
recognizing the importance of arrears management programs, I recommend that if the 
140 
Companies’ arrears forgiveness programs are eliminated for the Rate Year, analyses be 
141 
undertaken assessing their viability as part of NYSEG/RG&E’s filing for its next rate plan.
