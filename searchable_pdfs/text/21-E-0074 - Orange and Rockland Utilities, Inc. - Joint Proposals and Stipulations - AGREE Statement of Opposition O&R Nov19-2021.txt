1. Introduction
Alliance for a Green Economy (“AGREE”) opposes the Joint Proposal filed on October 29, 2021
in the above referenced proceeding.
AGREE works for safe, affordable energy and the development of a just, green economy in New
York State. We educate the public and our policymakers about renewable alternatives that can
both revitalize the economy and safeguard human health and the environment. AGREE is the
only environmental nonprofit to participate in every major rate case across the state in order to
promote a collaborative transition to a renewable energy future.
AGREE participated thoroughly in the process of this rate case, including submitting expert
testimony, conducting discovery, and attending settlement negotiations. Our main objective in
this case was to ensure the utility would align its gas investments and plans with the Climate


--- Page 4 ---
Leadership and Community Protection Act and to protect consumers from the hardship of rising
energy costs and debt.
The agreement developed through settlement is an improvement over the terms initially filed by
Orange & Rockland (“the Company”), but on balance the improvements do not go far enough to
satisfy the public interest. We urge the Commission not to approve this Joint Proposal without
further modifications.
2. Overview of the Joint Proposal
A. Benefits and Downsides of the Joint Proposal
1. CLCPA Compliance
From our perspective, the positive aspects of the Joint Proposal include, briefly:
●
Commitment by the Company to quantify and report its greenhouse gas emissions
●
Commitment by the Company to pursue a gas sales decrease of 1.5% over three years,
making the Company only the second utility in the state to work toward reducing gas
sales.
●
Changes to the Company’s website to eliminate outdated promotion of defunct oil-to-gas
conversion incentives.
●
Commitment by the Company to consider what would become the state’s first
utility-funded induction stove rebate as a strategy to meet its energy efficiency targets.
●
Commitment by the Company to consider and possibly implement refrigerant
management as an energy efficiency measure.
●
Plan by the Company to submit a utility-owned geothermal district pilot proposal to the
Public Service Commission.
The Company’s commitment to reduce billed gas sales over the term of the agreement is a
significant public interest provision, and we recognize the Company’s willingness to take a
leadership position in New York in that regard.


--- Page 5 ---
We also appreciate the Company’s willingness to pilot geothermal district systems and develop
new energy efficiency programs based on efficient induction cooking and refrigerant
management. These new programs, if successfully developed, can become models for other
utilities, and they will have many public interest benefits beyond energy savings. For instance,
New York leads the nation in premature deaths resulting from burning gas in buildings.1 Helping
people replace their gas stoves with induction cooking will improve indoor air quality and public
health. Meanwhile, refrigerants represent a significant portion of New York’s greenhouse gas
emissions, and the provision of incentives for better management will have climate benefits
beyond the simultaneous energy consumption reduction.
That said, the proposal lacks the specificity and breadth of CLCPA-compliance provisions that
other utilities have committed to in recent rate cases. For example, other utilities have agreed to
either detailed non-pipe alternative eligibility criteria (NYSEG/RG&E and Con-Ed) or have
committed to pursuing a certain number of non-pipe alternatives during the term of the rate plan
(KEDNY/KEDLI, Nimo, Central Hudson). The pursuit of non-pipe alternatives in the near term is
of utmost importance. These projects can avoid costly infrastructure investments and avoid the
creation of additional stranded assets. Further, it is critical that utilities begin the learning
process now for how to develop non-pipe alternatives because these alternatives will become
increasingly important as New York works to scale electrification in order to meet the Climate
Leadership and Community Protection Act-mandated 2030 greenhouse gas emissions targets.
We are very concerned that the Company will not gain important experience and insights if it
does not practice non-pipe alternatives during the next three years, which will put it behind other
utilities in the state.
Other utilities have also agreed to specific language inhibiting their continued marketing of gas,
while such a provision is missing from the O&R agreement. Individuals in various service
territories frequently forward utility gas marketing materials to us with notes about how shocking
it is that New York has passed the nation’s most ambitious climate law and yet gas utilities are
still marketing fossil fuels. The message being sent to customers is confusing. On the one hand,
our elected leaders are committing to climate action, while on the other hand, companies

2. Bill Impacts
The mixed-bag of CLCPA compliance provisions is compounded by the Joint Proposal’s bill
impacts and high Return on Equity (ROE) of 9.2%. To our knowledge this is the highest ROE
seen in New York rate cases going back at least to 2017.3 The bill impacts are also the highest
we have seen since the COVID-19 pandemic took hold in 2020. At a time when the winter
commodity prices for gas are dramatically higher than last year, and, as of September 2021,
17,424 residential and 1,129 non-residential Orange & Rockland customers are already behind
on their energy bills, owing collectively $27,422,560 to the utility,4 we do not think the ROE and
bill impacts appropriately balance the interests of ratepayers and shareholders. If the
Commission approves this case without requiring further bill decreases, they will send a
message to the public that customers alone are meant to bear the economic hardship of the
pandemic while shareholders will be spared paying their fair share of the costs and risks
associated with providing an essential service.

Procedural soundness, of course, is necessary but not sufficient, and the factors to be
considered in the ensuing substantive review do not lend themselves to codification. As
the comments suggest, they include (1) the settlement's consistency with law and with the
regulatory, economic, social, and environmental policies of the Commission and the
State; (2) whether the result compares favorably with the likely result of full litigation
and is within the range of reasonable outcomes; (3) whether the settlement strikes a fair
balance among the interests of ratepayers and investors and the long-term soundness of
the utility; (4) the existence of a rational basis for decision; (5) the completeness of the
record; and (6) whether the settlement is contested.
The first four of the foregoing factors are themselves elements of the public interest
standard. The fifth and sixth, in contrast, simply guide us in our assessment. We would
give weight, for example, to the fact that a settlement has been agreed to by all parties,
including normally adversarial ones, but the absence of objection would not relieve us of
our obligation to form an independent judgment that the settlement is in the public
interest. Similarly, the less developed the record, the greater the burden on the
settlement's proponents to show that the result compares favorably to the likely result of
full litigation.
C. The Joint Proposal Does Not Comport with the Commission’s
Settlement Guidelines
1. The Joint Proposal does not comply with the CLCPA.
New York has precious little time to comply with ambitious greenhouse gas emissions
reductions by 2030. Recent analysis presented to the Climate Action Council by the integration
analysis team demonstrates the scale needed to meet the mandate and states that “energy
efficiency and end-use electrification will be essential parts of any Pathway that hits NYS
Emissions Limits.” 6 In particular:
●
In all scenarios modeled, zero emission vehicles and heat pumps become the majority of
new purchases by the late 2020s, and fossil-emitting cars and appliances are no longer
sold after 2035
●
1 - 2 million efficient homes electrified with heat pumps by 2030
●
Consumer decision-making plays a large role, especially important for the purchase of
new passenger vehicles and heating systems, for homes and businesses through the
next decade

Despite some provisions that put Orange & Rockland in a leadership position among utilities on
billed gas sales reductions and piloting new programs, the Orange & Rockland Joint Proposal
does not inspire any confidence that the CLCPA mandates will be met in its service territory. The
Company has committed to quantifying its emissions, but it has not done so in this case, so the
Commission has no record upon which to determine how the Company’s commitments will align
with the CLCPA. More is needed to ensure the Company is able to scale beyond the committed
1.5% greenhouse gas reduction target to transform energy use in its territory to align with the
law.
2. The Joint Proposal does not strike a fair balance between the interests of
ratepayers and shareholders
The COVID-19 pandemic challenges us to transform our understanding of fair burdens in
society and to reconsider many aspects of the status quo. In these extraordinary times when
every sector of society has been asked to make sacrifices to protect public health and the public
good, the Commission must carefully weigh the costs of this rate case and the appropriate
distribution of these costs. The arrears numbers show that more and more customers are at a
breaking point, and with winter descending, fuel prices rising, and the economy still struggling,
this situation is only going to get more dire. It is unjust to try to squeeze more value from
ratepayers at this time in order to increase shareholder profits.
3. The Joint Proposal does represent a coming together of all of the parties in the
case
While the Joint Proposal is supported by a handful of parties, we call the Commission’s attention
to the lack of support by the major environmental and consumer protection organizations who
are parties to this case. Though we have participated in good faith negotiations and the Joint
Proposal does reflect some compromises toward some of our positions, this case does not go
far enough to balance the public interest positions of all of the parties.

3. Conclusion
As a state agency, the Commission is required to enforce the CLCPA and to ensure that its
decisions are consistent with the greenhouse gas emissions reductions mandated by the law. In
August, 2021, the Commission expressly acknowledged this requirement extends to rate case
decisions.7 Yet, the Commission has yet to provide clear guidance to DPS Staff and the utilities
on CLCPA compliance. We urge the Commission to set an initial policy for gas utility compliance
with the CLCPA before the next round of rate cases is filed. In the absence of such a policy,
parties will continue to be forced to litigate the meaning of the CLCPA through rate cases, a
process that will become increasingly contentious as we get closer to the 2030 deadline and as
climate disasters continue to unfold.
We similarly urge the Commission to take immediate action in the Proceeding on Motion of the
Commission Regarding the Effects of COVID-19 on Utility Service (Case 20-M-0266). It has
now been well over a year since the Commission issued the initial order directing Department of
Public Service Staff (“Staff”) to study the effects of the pandemic on ratepayers, and over 1.1
million residential ratepayers are now mired in debt.8 The average household debt exceeds
$1,350, and we are dismayed to see a lack of recognition from the Commission of this level of
crisis.
Despite this lack of guidance from the Commission, state law and settlement guidelines still
apply. Taken as a whole, the Joint Proposal is an improvement over the Company’s original
proposal and includes many concessions worth supporting, but it falls short of the standard of
review. The Commission can remedy these deficiencies by lowering the ROE and bill impacts,
by ordering the Company to include some of the non-pipe alternatives and gas marketing
provisions from other more forward looking rate cases, and by removing the unvetted
combustion-based fuels from counting toward CLCPA compliance.
