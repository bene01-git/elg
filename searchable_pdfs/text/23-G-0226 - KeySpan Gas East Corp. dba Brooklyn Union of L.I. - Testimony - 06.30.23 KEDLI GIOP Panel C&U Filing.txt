i.
Northwest Nassau Project Phase 3
7 
Q.
Please describe the updates to the Northwest Nassau Project. 
8 
A.

9

10 
course of this multi-year project.  As originally requested, the project scope in

12

13

14 
budget from FY 2025 to FY 2026 and FY 2027 to ensure the work can be 
15 
effectively managed and delivered.  This adjustment will not affect the project’s 
16 
in-service date of November 2026.  The overall impact of these modifications 
17 
is a reduction of $2.650 million in the project’s capital forecast.  The revised

8 
Project forecast. 
9 
A. 
The in-service date for this transmission station integrity project was 
10

11

12 
rate than originally anticipated.  The revised in-service date results in 
13 
corresponding changes to the project timeline and costs in the Rate Year, Data 
14 
Year 1, and Data Year 2; however, at this time, additional capital funding is not 
15 
required to accelerate the project’s in-service date.  The project changes are 
16 
included in Exhibit __ (GIOP-1CU) and Exhibit __ (GIOP-5CU).

iii.      Reliability Project Updates 
1 
Q. 
Please describe the updates to the Company’s proposed projects to support 
2 
safe and reliable operation of the Holtsville LNG Facility. 
3 
A. 
As explained in this Panel’s direct testimony, the Company proposes several 
4 
capital investments in its Reliability capital category to support the continued 
5 
safe and reliable operation of its Holtsville LNG Plant.  Included within these 
6 
investments were projects to enhance cyber security capabilities at the plant and 
7 
upgrade the vapor suppression system.  Supporting details for these investments 
8 
were provided in Exhibit __ (GIOP-5).  The Company updated the estimate for 
9 
the Cyber Security Upgrade Project due to new Transportation Security 
10 
Administration requirements and a recently completed security assessment.  
11 
The updated estimate shifts project costs from the Rate Year to Data Year 1, 
12 
resulting in an overall project increase of $0.057 million.  The updated in-
13 
service date of March 2026 is reflected in Exhibit __ (GIOP-5CU).  With 
14 
respect to the Vapor Suppression System Project, the project estimate was 
15 
revised based on updates to the design requirements, which increased the 
16 
project’s scope and the required material and construction costs.  The updated 
17 
estimate requires an additional $2.33 million of capital funding in the Rate Year 
18 
and Data Year 1.  Changes to the capital funding requested for these projects 
19 
are included in Exhibit __ (GIOP-1CU).

LNG - Cyber Security Upgrade 
Type: ☒ Project ☐ Program
Category: ☒ Capital  ☐ Both Capital & O&M

Work Description: 
Through this project, National Grid’s Information Technology (NG IT) organization will remediate cybersecurity vulnerabilities 
and start supporting aspects of Operational Technology (OT) in LNG plants with external vendors continuing to provide some 
level of OT support due to their expertise and knowledge of the vaporization and liquefaction control systems in NG LNG 
plants.  This project will be funded under the KEDLI LNG portfolio while managed by NG IT. 
This project has two phases: 
1.) Remediation - In FY23, third-party vendor, Network + Security Technologies (NST), is assessing each US LNG plant to 
identify vulnerabilities and recommend mitigations. Based on NST's assessments in FY23, this phase will remediate 
vulnerabilities in all US LNG plants by implementing operational policy improvements, hardening OT networks, and 
completing other work.  All LNG plants will be addressed in this phase with TSA-critical plants remediated first. 
2.) Support - NG IT will provide ongoing support to all US LNG plants to ensure the remediations implemented during phase 
one remain intact.  NG IT support will start supporting a given plant after remediations have been implemented in that plant. 
Justification Summary: 
Cybersecurity vulnerabilities have been identified in LNG plant operational procedures and OT that will be addressed by this 
project.  This project will also enable LNG plants to comply with the new US federal regulations under Transportation Security 
Administration's (TSA) Security Directive Pipeline-2021-02C for Pipeline Cybersecurity Mitigation Actions, Contingency 
Planning, and Testing. 
Customer Benefits: 
The primary customer benefit is the continuous, safe, and reliable supply of natural gas without unplanned outages due to 
facility shutdowns.  Serving as the principal source of “Peak Shaving” for New York customers, the plant also plays a strategic 
role in the Company’s gas supply portfolio.  The facility has an LNG storage tank with a total design capacity of 600 MMSCF. 
Holtsville LNG facility has a design output of 100 MMSCFD of natural gas.  LNG peak shaving facilities operates intermittently, 
supplying natural gas pipelines during periods of peak need as required. 
This project will add further benefits by remediating cybersecurity vulnerabilities and ensuring NG's ability to safely and 
reliably operate the LNG plant to meet gas demand for customers in New York. Securing OT in all US LNG plants will prevent 
restoration costs from a cyber-attack that renders a single LNG plant inoperable during winter heating season, reducing 
customer bill impacts.

Transmission Station Integrity - PHMSA - Rockville 
Centre Take Station Overhaul 
 
Type: ☒ Project ☐ Program 
Category: ☒ Capital  ☐ Both Capital & O&M 
 
Investment Code: 5230101599 
Region: ☐ KEDNY ☒ KEDLI

Business Contact: Adesina, Risikat  
 
Work Description:  
Rockville Centre Gate Station is in Lynbrook, New York and consists of three (3) regulating stations being QL-18, QL-19, and 
QL-20.  QL-18, 19, and 20 are 1969 vintage regulating stations consisting of a primary regulator vault, a secondary regulator 
vault, and a metering vault.  The inlet pressure to all three regulator stations is 450 psi with QL-18, 19 outlet pressure of 99 
psi and QL-20 outlet pressure of 60 psi.  QL-18 feeds the Southshore 99# system, QL-19 feeds the Lakeview 99# system and 
QL-20 feeds the Inwood 60# system.  Due to missing DOT pipe records, a full replacement of all DOT piping within the station 
is required.  Additionally, due to the current condition of the station, a full station rebuild is called for consisting of the 
following work scope: 
  
Work Scope:    
• 
Installation of a new station inlet valve, station outlet valve, strainer, primary regulator, secondary regulator, and OPP 
• 
Installation of new station piping and control lines 
• 
Installation of new vent poles  
• 
Installation of new access manholes and ladder systems 
• 
Installation of pipe supports within each vault 
 
Justification Summary:  
Due to recent PHMSA rule changes, are record review was performed at all regulating stations that fall under the new 
guidelines.  The record review was performed to ensure both material and hydrostatic test records were accurate and 
available for the station.  When adequate records are not available, National Grid has decided it is most prudent to replace 
pipes and equipment that are missing required documentation. Under the current PHMSA ruling, this work must be 
completed by July 1, 2035. 
 
Customer Benefits: 
The current max flow through QL-18 is 960.37 MCFH. The current max flow through QL-19 is 940.91 MCFH. The current max 
flow through QL-20 is 1,609.72 MCFH.  The completion of this project will bring National Grid into compliance with PHMSA 
ruling and improve system reliability.
