Q. 
What is RG&E’s BSS? 
1 
A. 
In 2018, as a demonstration project, RG&E installed a 2 MW, 10 MWh BSS that is 
2 
interconnected to a 12.5kV bus at RG&E’s Station 127 34.5kV/12.5kV substation in 
3 
Farmington, New York.  Prior to the BSS installation, Station 127 served approximately 
4 
5,000 customers and was forecasted to exceed its distribution load-serving capacity by 
5 
0.2 MWs in 2020 with a forecasted additional 2% increase in load each year thereafter.  
6 
Station 127 is physically adjacent to a 115 kV/35 kV sub-transmission substation.  As a 
7 
result of this proximity, the distribution substation experienced occasional power quality 
8 
issues, mainly associated with transmission switching operations and transmission level 
9 
fault events, which impacted the distribution system.  In addition, a large number of 
10 
commercial customers are connected to Station 127 and such customers’ equipment 
11 
suffered reliability issues during these power quality events.   
12 
The BSS: 1) mitigates an expected capacity deficiency; 2) manages system 
13 
reliability through low load periods; 3) improves power quality; and 4) demonstrates the 
14 
value of additional battery technology capabilities.  Since the BSS was installed in 2018, 
15 
RG&E has experienced significant load growth in the area, greater than initially 
16 
forecasted, requiring increased reliability and redundancy upgrades to RG&E’s Station 
17 
127.  The BSS has been out of service during the construction effort at Station 127 and is 
18 
expected to be available in the third quarter of 2022.  RG&E is evaluating the opportunity 
19 
to optimize the value of the battery storage system through the wholesale markets once 
20 
the BSS is returned to service.

Q. 
How do the Companies forecast the physical load requirements from the NYISO? 
9 
A.

13

14

15 
NYISO.

18 
A. 
In that situation, the Companies sell into the NYISO real-time market.

B. Resource Cost Recovery 
9 
Q. 
Please explain how the Companies utilize their three electric supply options. 
10 
A. 
Each of the three electric supply options (company-owned generation, contractual 
11 
purchases and NYISO purchases) is utilized differently.  The Companies bid their 
12

13

16

17 
allocated to the Companies’ delivery customers through the NBC.  Finally, the 
18 
Companies use NYISO purchases or sales to balance the remainder of their resources and 
19 
load.

C. Market Price Volatility Mitigation 
1 
1. Allocation of Fixed Price Resources 
2 
Q. 
How do the Companies mitigate market price volatility for customers? 
3 
A. 
The Companies’ customers are allocated a load ratio share of fixed price resources, 
4 
company-owned generation and contractual purchases.  For all of the Test Year, 4.6% of 
5 
NYSEG’s residential delivery load and 2.5% of its non-residential delivery load were 
6 
hedged by these resources.  For all of the Test Year, about 0.8% of RG&E’s residential 
7 
and 0.1% of its non-residential delivery load were hedged by fixed price resources, 
8 
RG&E-owned generation and contractual purchases.  The difference between the 
9 
residential and non-residential percentages is the Companies’ NYPA hydro allocation, 
10 
which is solely for the benefit of residential customers.  The allocation of these fixed 
11 
price hedges mitigates market price volatility.  In addition to the physical hedges, the 
12 
Companies also utilize financial hedges to mitigate market price volatility. 
13 
2. Owned Generation 
14 
Q. 
How does the generation owned by the Companies hedge customer market risk?

X. ELECTRIC WHOLESALE LOAD SETTLEMENTS 
1 
Q. 
Please describe the existing process for electric wholesale settlement. 
2 
A. 
The Companies’ current settlement system uses regularly-scheduled readings from the 
3 
billing kWh meters applied to static load profiles and for the hourly-priced customers 
4 
with interval meters, the customer’s actual hourly usage.  This data is used to determine 
5 
the load serving entities’ (“LSE”) hourly loads in accordance with NYISO’s billing 
6 
schedule.  The difference between the net system load (by subzone) and the aggregated 
7 
LSE loads grossed up for transmission and distribution losses, results in unaccounted for 
8 
energy (“UFE”).  The UFE is calculated on an hourly basis and prorated based on load-
9 
share to the non-hourly priced customers.  For the 2,300 hourly priced customers, losses 
10 
are applied, but UFE is not.  The current settlement is performed in the Companies’ 
11 
customer care system energy data management module (“SAP-EDM”).    
12 
Q. 
Are the Companies proposing any changes to the current process? 
13 
A. 
Yes.  Because the SAP-EDM module is not capable of processing the number of hourly 
14 
reads that will be available once AMI is fully deployed within the Companies’ service 
15 
territories, the Companies propose to use AMI hourly data for settlement purposes.  
16 
Q. 
Why are the Companies proposing to use AMI hourly data for settlement? 
17 
A. 
Because load profiles are static, use of the SAP-EDM module process can result in 
18 
significant deviations in each LSE’s hourly NYISO reported load relative to what those 
19 
loads would actually be based on AMI data.  As a result, an LSE could be paying the 
20 
NYISO for load that is greater or lesser than the load their customers actually consumed.  
21 
The ability to use AMI data for wholesale load reporting would increase the accuracy of
