Gas corporations including Consolidated Edison Co. of New York, Inc. (“Con Edison” or 
the “Company”) supply natural gas to customers through a system of underground pipes and 
customer meters.  Larger pipes, called mains, carry gas through the utility’s service territory, 
while smaller pipes, called service lines, deliver gas from mains to meters at customer buildings.  
Meters may be located inside or outside of customer buildings and measure the volume of gas a 
customer consumes.  Service lines and meters are thus necessary components of each gas 
corporation’s distribution system.   
The Public Service Law vests the New York State Public Service Commission 
(“Commission”) with broad regulatory authority and jurisdiction over each gas corporation 
(“utility”) for the protection of consumers, and to ensure safe and adequate service at just and 
reasonable rates.1  The Commission’s jurisdiction includes gas service lines and meters, in part

C. The Commission’s Exclusive Gas Service Line Jurisdiction Does Not Change 
While an Indoor Meter is in the Process of Being Relocated Outdoors Pursuant 
to a Commission-Approved Program 
 
A meter relocation under the Relocation Program is a Commission-approved process that 
is complete only when Con Edison restores gas service after installing the outdoor meter and 
reconnecting the service line.  Shutting off service, removing and relocating the meter, and 
restoring service including limited inside pipework are interrelated parts of one continuous action 
subject to Commission jurisdiction.  It is unreasonable and impractical to parse a meter 
relocation into discrete jurisdictional steps that deprive the Commission of authority over the 
step most important to customers – the work required to restore their gas service. 
As discussed above, the location of the meter generally determines the endpoint of each 
gas service line and the extent of the Commission’s regulatory jurisdiction.  At indoor meter 
locations, the Commission’s gas service line jurisdiction ends at the outlet of the meter.  If there 
is no meter, the Commission’s service line jurisdiction ends at the connection to the customer’s 
piping.  In contrast, at outdoor meter locations, the Commission’s service line jurisdiction ends at 
the exterior of the building foundation wall.  The Commission addressed this important
