The Joint Proposal (JP) is flawed and cannot be approved. Companies and Staff 
failed to establish that the JP is in the public interest either in substance or in fulfillment of 
the mission of the DPS. Further, the proposal fails to meet the mission of DPS to provide 
rates that are “just and reasonable” and to “protect the natural environment.” 
(https://dps.ny.gov/about-us, State Public Service Law, Article 1, Section 4) 
A. BURDEN OF PROOF NOT MET IN SUBSTANCE REGARDING PROTECTION OF 
THE NATURAL ENVIRONMENT 
Burden of proof related to CLCPA has not been met. Ratepayer and Community Intervenors 
(RCI) emphasizes points by other Joint Parties regarding requirements of the CLCPA. RCI also 
emphasizes the concerns of AGREE that no evidence has been provided to justify the use of 
Certified Natural Gas (CNG) and therefore that section must be stricken from the JP.  
In addition, both staff and companies stated that they haven’t even attempted to perform 
measurement of greenhouse gas emissions. They did answer, however, that performing 
measurements would not have been disallowed; such measurements could have been included 
should the parties have agreed to do so. It is highly contrary to the public interest to delay clear, 
measurable progress towards reduction of GHG emissions. In spite of written and verbal requests 
to address measurement, the JP fails to include them. Further, proposed capital expenditures do 
not meet the standards of the CLCPA, indeed, the "Avangrid Capital Project Prioritization & 
Governance Review Process" used to prioritize the proposed projects doesn't include anything 
about preparing the grid for renewables or addressing climate change. (Exhibit #583).  
In other sections of the JP established Commission orders are appropriately considered to 
be a floor, rather than a ceiling, in negotiated compromise, for example as discussed during the 
hearing related to ROE (Page 169, lines 9-16). It is unreasonable and arbitrary to declare that 
GHG emissions should be exempt from similar negotiation.  
Failure to update programs in Appendix M displays disregard for the public interest:  
7/17/23, Page 54, answers to questions regarding the ending of Appendix M provisions 
regarding marketing of gas showed that not only was there no further progress to rein in gas 
promotion, but that this JP will take a step backwards from the 2020 JP. This, again, is counter to 
the public interest.

C. THERE ARE ONLY A FEW, NARROW SIGNATORIES TO THE JP WHILE THE 
BROADER, OPPOSING CONSUMER AND CLIMATE/ENVIRONMENTAL PARTIES 
REPRESENT DIVERSE INTERESTS 
Contrary to repeated company and staff assertions, signatories to the full JP are 
comprised of only companies, staff, the union that represents company workers, Walmart and 
Nucor, each of which have been awarded lower per-unit rates for their service class.  
The JP states inaccurately: “The signatory parties represent a wide range of interests and 
demonstrate the broad support of the Joint Proposal.” (Page 10, also references to a “diverse 
spectrum” on page 9) or by Staff in their Statement of Support (on pages 11 and 13) of “broad 
support by diverse stakeholders, as well as business, industry and governmental interests.” 
In contrast, the parties opposing the JP represent wide ranging interests, professional 
expertise, size of group, geographic range, and organizational purpose. Missions range from low-

income representation to regional economic interests, interests of elderly, and that of climate. 
Several are legal advocates. One consists of engineers, researchers and elected local officials; 
one primarily does public education, one describes itself as state-level advocates, one says it has 
local advocates and organizers, one is a legal rights organization, one has hundreds of thousands 
of members. They each describe different geographic regions. These can be found in Intervenor 
Status request forms and in the concise summary included in Joint Letter Requesting Evidentiary 
Hearing and Opposing Make Whole (DMM #7212-22-22).  
The JP falls apart as a working system if only small pieces are agreed to work and should 
be denied as not having been agreed. Multiple Intervenors and others who claim to be in “partial 
support” are in support of only the small pieces that provide preferential treatment to their 
constituencies. They fail to prove that the JP works with only their small selection. The concept 
of partial support makes no sense (other than where parties are involved only with one broad 
realm such as electric but not gas or vice versa).   
D. CONCLUSION 
While one can quibble with interpretation of any of the specific examples cited above, the 
Commission has not met its standard to ensure equity among parties. The process has been 
tainted by unequal treatment - most notably evidenced in "cool kids" but also in multiple 
instances that affected the outcome of the JP, especially in the outcome of (and adherence to) 
Appendix M. These are just a few examples not veiled by confidentiality.  But it raises questions 
about the fairness of the process, especially when so few of the parties have signed the JP. For 
these reasons and for the reasons presented in the respective statements of the opponents, 
Ratepayer and Community Intervenors urges the Commission to reject the JP and not endorse 
unreasonable double digit delivery rate increases without concomitant progress above the 
minimum floor established for a transition away from natural gas.
