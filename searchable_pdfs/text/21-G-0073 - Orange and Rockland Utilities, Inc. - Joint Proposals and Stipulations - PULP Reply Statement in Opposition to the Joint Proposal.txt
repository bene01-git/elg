III C. Cost of Capital 
Return on Equity (“ROE”) 
PULP rejects Staff’s assertion that: 
“The Joint Proposal’s recommended allowed ROE of 9.20% is a fair compromise 
between the Company’s position in its original filing and Staff’s position in its direct 
testimony. Further, although the 9.20% is slightly higher than the ROE included in the 
Central Hudson joint proposal, the Joint Proposal does not include the thicker common 
equity ratios that were included in that agreement. In addition, it reflects increased 
equity return requirements in recent months, as shown in Staff’s testimony recently 
filed in the Corning rate case.” (Staff at 11-12) 
The signatory parties’ proposed 9.2% is not “fair”. It requires Orange and Rockland 
customers—already reeling from the economic disruption caused by the COVID-19 pandemic—


--- Page 3 ---
to suffer the undue burden of rates based on an unjustified and unnecessarily generous ROE. When 
PULP asked Staff and the Company in I/R PULP 64a to provide all workpapers, analyses, and 
other documentation that the signatory parties used to determine that the return on common equity 
(“ROE”) for each of the rate years in this proceeding should be 9.2%, they demurred, offering only 
their testimonial positions with no documentary support for their agreed-upon 9.2% ROE. 
Thankfully, Staff provides the above reference to their recently filed testimony in Corning Gas 
Case 21-G-0394, which “reflects increased equity return requirements in recent months”. Page 3 
of Staff Finance Panel Exhibit ___(FP-6) in the Corning case shows an ROE cost of capital 
calculation of 9.0% as of September 2021.1 This result, which is the product of the Commission’s 
long-standing generic financing methodology, is both fair (in terms of the public interest), and 
consistent with the ROEs of cases decided since the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic. For this 
reason, PULP rejects Staff’s characterization of 9.2% as a justified, compromise ROE and 
reiterates its call for the Commission to reject any ROE over 9.0% in these proceedings.

which is important particularly in light of the COVID-19 pandemic.”2

Amortization of Unprotected Excess Deferred Income Taxes (EDFIT) 
PULP also rejects the Signatory Parties’ argument that it is in ratepayers’ interest to delay 
the return of EDFIT by requiring a six-year, instead of three-year, amortization period for these 
funds. Specifically, PULP objects to O&R’s apparent suggestion that the six-year period is a 
sufficient response to the need to provide customers relief in light of the financial burdens they 
have faced (and are continuing to experience) due to the COVID-19 pandemic.7 The staggering 
increase in O&R residential arrears since February 2020 provides clear and objective evidence 
otherwise. These funds are needed by customers during the proposed three years of this rate plan. 
A six year wait to receive one’s money back is not sufficiently timely and ignores the urgency 
faced by ratepayers needing to pay for food, medicine, rent/mortgages and many other vital 
expenditures during the COVID-19 financial crisis. 
In its statement of support, Staff doesn’t even raise the issue of the impact of the COVID-
19 pandemic as having played a part in its determination that a six-year amortization of EDFIT 
was necessary. Instead, Staff utters conclusory reasoning without any reference to the public 
interest, by stating: 
“The Joint Proposal’s resolution of this issue is reasonable as it strikes a balance 
between mitigating the instant rate increase and passing the benefits back over a long 
enough period of time to provide stability for future customers’ bills. This is a fair and 
reasonable resolution of the issue and, as such, this provision should be approved.”8 
As PULP argued in its statement of opposition, O&R’s customers are experiencing rate 
instability now. They have been for the past 21 months, a fact that has been apparent for all to see.
