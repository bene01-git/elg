A. The Joint Proposal Will Facilitate Careful Assessment of the Validity of 
Certified Natural Gas Products  
“Differentiated” natural gas is purported to have been produced and transported via 
methods that meet certain environmental, social, and methane emission best practices,8 and 
“according to the supplier, has undergone assessment by an independent third–party to determine 
that the gas is produced under specified best practices.”9 The Company refers to this product as 
“certified” natural gas,10 though EDF believes this terminology--which suggests that improved 
performance standards have been implemented and verified—is premature given the variability 
and subjectivity of existing programs.11 Certifying entities vary in everything from the subjects 
of their certifications (methane emissions, water protections, community protections, etc.), to the 
methodologies and technologies on which they rely, to the level of operations required to be 
evaluated in order to secure a certification.12 Much greater scrutiny is needed to assess whether 
voluntarily certified gas products can drive genuine reductions in methane emissions, but the 
commitments to tracking and reporting on the Certified Natural Gas Pilot program in the Joint 
Proposal represent a significant improvement from the Company’s original open-ended proposal.

1. Differentiated Gas is Gaining Popularity and Interest to Demonstrate 
Methane Emission Reductions 
Methane, the primary component of natural gas, is a potent GHG with a warming 
potential 84 times greater than CO2 over a 20-year period, and 30 times greater over 100 years.13 
Rapid implementation of all methane mitigation measures could slow the rate of near-term 
warming by 30% and avoid 0.25°C of additional warming by midcentury.14 As oil and gas 
companies face public, investor, and regulator pressure to reduce methane emissions, 
differentiated or certified gas is becoming an increasingly popular product. An Enverus 
Intelligence report estimated that producer-certified gas would grow to make up about 18% of 
the North American natural gas market by the end of 2022, and notes that 90% of expected 2022 
differentiated gas production was just announced in 2021.15  
In New York, the CLCPA requires measures to reduce statewide GHG emissions by 40% 
by 2030 and 85% by 2050, including GHGs “produced outside of the state that are associated 
with” the extraction and transportation of imported fossil fuels and imported electricity 
generation.16 The CLCPA acknowledges the particular importance of addressing GHGs with

2. Differentiated Gas Raises Concerns and Requires Scrutiny 
EDF has identified three major concerns with certification programs:23 (1) Lack of 
Measurement, Reporting and Verification (MRV) standards: Without comprehensive direct 
measurement of methane emissions, independent verification, and transparency around methane 
intensity calculations, it is inappropriate for producers or certifiers to label gas as responsibly 
sourced or to claim that gas is achieving a certain methane intensity.

C. The Existing Rate Case Paradigm is Inadequate to Ensure Consistency with the 
CLCPA 
“Reducing carbon emissions is a critical priority and a significant portion of the 
Commission’s responsibility,”35 and utility rate cases—the forum where utilities seek approval to 
raise rates and recover costs for major investments—are an important forum where the 
Commission must ensure that investments and priorities are aligned with state climate policy. 
The Commission must establish clear standards for utility rate cases, that will facilitate 
decarbonization of the energy system and ensure consistency with New York’s climate goals.

expenditure, program, and initiative included in their rate filings.”47 The Commission also

the current direct and indirect GHG emissions, including upstream emissions from imported

III. 
CONCLUSION  
EDF maintains a position of neutrality regarding the Joint Proposal. The Joint Proposal 
provisions regarding the Certified Gas Pilot will enhance transparency and accountability, and 
the deployment of advanced leak detection will reduce methane emissions on the distribution 
system; but the Commission must establish a clear standard for its CLCPA Section 7 review of 
rate case orders to bring further clarity to the rate case process.
