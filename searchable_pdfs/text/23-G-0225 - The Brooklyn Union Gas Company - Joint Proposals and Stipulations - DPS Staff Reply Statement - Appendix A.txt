Response: 
As explained in the response to PULP-61 (Amended), upon further review of the KEDNY low-
income discount calculation included in response to PULP-061, the Company identified an 
inadvertent discrepancy between the allocation of the capped discounts among the four tiers and 
the calculation methodology set forth in the Energy Affordability Program (“EAP”) workbook. 
The Company submits this amended response specific to KEDNY only to provide amended bill 
impacts.  The attachments provided in the response to PULP-62 for KEDLI do not require update.  
Attachment 1 provides the KEDNY bill impacts for current vs. proposed Rate Year 1.  
Attachment 2 provides the KEDNY bill impacts for Rate Year 1 vs. Rate Year 2.  Attachment 
3 provides the KEDNY bill impacts for Rate Year 2 vs. Rate Year 3.  Attachment 4 is an 
updated version of Appendix 1 that was included in the Companies’ Statement of Support.
As explained in the Company’s response to PULP-061, the bill impacts depict a snapshot of 
average usage for an EAP customer.  It is important to note, however, that these impacts are not 
necessarily reflective of the average bill increase a typical EAP customer may experience for the 
following reasons: 

The average bill assumes, for KEDLI, as an example, 79 therms per month.  Over the 
course of the Rate Year, more than half of the months have usage lower than 79 therms.   


--- Page 4 ---
Form 103

The calculation of annual EAP discounts is based on a hybrid average low-income bill 
that combines an historical three-year weighted monthly average commodity bill with a 
forecast year delivery bill and assumes three-year historic average usage.  As a result, 
there will be differences when comparing EAP discounts that are intended to keep the 
average bill at the 6% energy burden and what customers may actually experience 
throughout that Rate Year depending on actual usage each month. 

Specific to KEDNY, the calculation of Heating EAP discounts for Rate Years One 
through Three was limited to the 2% budget cap in accordance with the EAP Order.  As a 
result, the 6% energy burden was not able to be maintained which is why we are seeing 
higher monthly bill impacts for KEDNY Heating EAP customers.   

For KEDLI, the 2% budget cap was not a factor.
