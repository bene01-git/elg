III.  Reducing Methane Emissions from the Oil and Gas Supply Chain is Critical to 
3 
Address Climate Change  
4 
Q. 
Why is it important to reduce methane emissions?  
5 
A. 
Even though methane only remains in the atmosphere for about a decade, its warming 
6 
impact during that time is significant. Compared with carbon dioxide (CO2), methane’s 
7 
warming potential is 84 times higher over a twenty-year period and 30 times higher over 
8 
a 100-year period.4 Given the urgent global need to reduce greenhouse gas emissions to 
9 
mitigate the climate crisis, cutting methane emissions is an important strategy to limit the 
10 
rate of current warming. Moreover, many mitigation measures exist already and could 
11 
significantly limit methane emissions from human activities.5 Recent peer-reviewed 
12 
research demonstrates that rapid implementation of all methane mitigation measures 
13 
could slow the rate of near-term warming by 30 percent and avoid a quarter of a degree 
14 
centigrade of additional warming by midcentury.6 
15

What is advanced leak detection?  
6 
A. 
Advanced leak detection technology and data analytics (“ALD+”) uses high-sensitivity 
7 
methane analyzers (with detection limits on the order of parts per billion), GPS, and wind 
8 
sensors deployed on survey vehicles to identify where natural gas is leaking from 
9 
distribution systems. In contrast to traditional survey technologies, the sensors in ALD+ 
10

11 
find more leaks, and ALD+ uses advanced data analytics that examine the overall 
12

13 
are elevated. Survey vehicles drive selected routes to collect emissions data, and then the 
14 
data are analyzed using algorithms to draw out key leak information such as location, 
15

Q. 
How can ALD+ reduce methane emissions from a gas distribution system? 
1 
A. 
ALD+ can report not just the presence of a leak, but can also estimate leak size. Utilities 
2 
can use leak size information to prioritize their leak repair and pipe replacement efforts of 
3 
non-hazardous leaks to address the largest leaks fastest. By remediating large, super-
4 
emitting leaks earlier, a utility can reduce near-term methane emissions from the system. 
5 
Peer-reviewed research estimates that on average, fugitive methane emissions on a 
6 
distribution system could be cut in half by repairing the largest 20% of leaks.31 
7

12

13
