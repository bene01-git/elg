STAFF REPLY BRIEF 
I. 
INTRODUCTION 
 
 
In a ruling at the evidentiary hearing held on May 20, 2024, Administrative Law 
Judges (ALJs) Maureen Leary and James Costello authorized parties to file post-hearing briefs 
regarding the specific issues raised at the hearing.  In this Post-Hearing Reply Brief, Staff will 
address: (1) the role of Biomethane Interconnections within sections 7(2) and 7(3) of the Climate 
Leadership and Community Protection Act (CLCPA) and the cost recovery restrictions in the 
Joint Proposal; (2) We Act’s argument regarding the Companies’ marketing; and (3) the Joint 
Proposal’s vast improvements to the Companies’ Non-Pipeline Alternatives (NPAs) framework.  
In our own Initial Statement and Reply Statements, and our Post-Hearing Initial Brief, Staff has 
already addressed many of the arguments made by We Act for Environmental Justice (We Act) 
in its Post-Hearing Initial Brief.  To that extent, Staff will not repeat those same arguments in 
this Reply Brief. 
 
 
The Joint Proposal is a balanced resolution to the issues in these cases; the terms 
of which provide meaningful solutions and benefits regarding a myriad of issues.  Specifically, 
the provisions of the Joint Proposal related to the Companies’ Biomethane Interconnection 
projects are reasonable.  The Joint Proposal protects customers from funding these 
interconnections should the developer-led projects never materialize.  Further, the record in this 
proceeding clearly enables the Commission to render its decision in light of CLCPA sections 
7(2) and 7(3), contrary to We Act’s argument.

II. 
BACKGROUND 
 
 
On April 28, 2023, the Companies filed tariff leaves, direct testimony, and 
exhibits in support of their request to increase gas delivery rates for the rate year ending March 
31, 2025, referred to in the context of the proposed three-year rate plans as “RY1.”  Over the 
course of these proceedings, Staff, the Companies, and other parties have pursued a resolution of 
these cases on both litigated and settlement tracks.1  On April 9, 2024, the Brooklyn Union Gas 
Company d/b/a National Grid NY (KEDNY) and KeySpan Gas East Corporation d/b/a National 
Grid (KEDLI) (collectively, the Companies) filed on behalf of Staff of the New York State 
Department of Public Service (Staff); Environmental Defense Fund (EDF); the City of New 
York (CNY); and NRG Energy (NRG) collectively the “Signatory Parties,” a Joint Proposal 
recommending a comprehensive resolution of all issues raised in these proceedings.2  
 
 
After the Signatory Parties filed the Joint Proposal, numerous parties filed initial 
statements on May 1, 2024, and reply statements on May 10, 2024, expressing their support or 
opposition to the Joint Proposal.

III. 
ARGUMENT 
A. We Act’s Argument is Patently Flawed and the Record is Sufficient for the 
Commission to Approve the Joint Proposal. 
In its Post-Hearing Brief, We Act contends that the Companies’ four proposed 
biomethane interconnection projects are inconsistent with CLCPA §7(2) and §7(3).8  We Act’s 
argument is patently flawed as the Commission’s determinations pursuant to CLCPA §7(2) and 
§7(3) in this proceeding address the Joint Proposal in its entirety, not determinations specific to 
individual capital projects nor future or potential projects separate from the Companies’ capital 
plan(s).

We Act also conflates the Companies’ interconnection projects with developer led 
Biomethane facilities, which are not proposed by nor included in the Companies’ capital plan.  
Despite numerous examples, Staff and the signatory parties have previously addressed the 
emissions and reliability benefits of these interconnection projects and reiterates that RNG is a 
meaningful contribution towards the State’s goals specifically acknowledged by the State’s 
Climate Action Council.13   
We Act is correct that the net impacts of these potential or future projects is 
currently unknown, however, these projects are separate and apart from the interconnections 
themselves, may not come to fruition and if they do, will require their own permitting.  It is 
premature for any party or the Commission to surmise that these projects are inconsistent with 
the CLCPA.  Anticipating the technologies and specific actions of these developers is nothing 
more than a hypothetical exercise, therefore, We Act’s argument that the Companies’ 
interconnection projects are inconsistent with the CLCPA is misplaced.  The Joint Proposal also 
includes reporting requirements which the Companies must adhere to, prior to these facilities 
becoming operational, which should answer the net impacts of these facilities.  It is, however, 
reasonable to assume that should these projects reach the point that they can be operationalized 
they are consistent with the CLCPA having undergone significant permitting and design work.   
For these reasons, We Act’s arguments should be rejected, and the Commission 
should adopt the terms of the Joint Proposal as proposed.

on a matter of public controversy [and] necessarily reflects the political views or self-interest of 
managements or shareholders and should not be borne by ratepayers.”14

C. The Joint Proposal Restricts New Gas Marketing and Includes Vastly Improved 
NPA Processes  
 
 
We Act contends that the Companies will fail to implement the Joint Proposal’s 
NPA provisions, and the Commission should take aggressive action and institute a penalty if the 
Companies fail to meet NPA benchmarks.22  Further, We Act argues that the Joint Proposal’s 
NPA provisions are aspirational due to the lack of targets or benchmarks.23  Although We Act 
expressed these concerns regarding the Joint Proposal’s NPA provisions, We Act did support the 
“Companies’ plan to leverage regional outreach and engagements and proactively communicate 
with and educate customers.”24 
The Joint Proposal includes the Companies’ agreement to restrict marketing new 
gas connections and to pursue referrals for electrification.25  Further, the current NPA framework 
was established in the 2021 Rate Order.26  Additionally, the Commission is considering 
proposals submitted by the Companies, and other gas utilities, regarding a new NPA framework
