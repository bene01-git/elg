RE:  Cases 19-G-0309 and 19-G-0310 - KEDNY and KEDLI - Rates 
 
Dear Secretary Phillips and Judges Leary and Costello: 
AARP appreciates this opportunity to comment on the Joint Proposal (“JP”) filed on May 14, 
2021.  We are pleased that KEDNY and KEDLI (“the Companies”), Department of Public 
Service Staff (“DPS Staff”) and the other signatory parties agreed to include in Section IV (11) 
of the JP several new or enhanced customer-facing initiatives proposed by AARP and the Public 
Utility Law Project (“PULP”).  We oppose, however, the proposed abdication of Commission 
authority and degraded due process pertaining to an independent consultant’s determinations 
about whether or not the Companies may implement certain long-term capacity projects as 
described on page 47 of the JP. 
AARP New York has 2.5 million members, more than 700,000 of whom reside in the service 
territories of the Companies.  AARP members are 50 years old and older and many are elderly.  
Others are on fixed incomes or work at businesses experiencing a marked drop off in sales and 
were already struggling to make ends meet prior to the onset of the pandemic.  The new or 
enhanced customer-facing initiatives contained in Section IV (11)  have the potential to

ameliorate some of the challenges facing our Long Island, Staten Island, Queens, and Brooklyn 
members.  We will review these initiatives in the order they appear in the JP.   
Prior to the onset of the pandemic, KEDNY (unlike KEDLI) did not allow residential customers 
to negotiate electronic deferred payment agreements over the telephone.  Instead, KEDNY 
required time-strapped and physically challenged customers to travel to one of the utility’s few 
customer service offices (two in Brooklyn, one each in Staten Island and Queens) open only on 
weekdays from 8:15 am to 5:00 pm.  Provision 11.1 commits KEDNY to continue the practice, 
forced upon it by the pandemic, of allowing customers to negotiate DPAs over the telephone.  
AARP supports this change. 
Provision 11.1.1 requires the Companies to provide a variety of granular data pertaining to the 
active, reinstated, defaulted and satisfied DPAs.  This information would allow customer 
advocates to analyze the effectiveness and impact of the Companies’ implementation of the DPA 
process and develop recommendations for improvement if needed.  AARP supports this 
provision. 
PULP estimates that approximately 70% of eligible KEDNY customers and approximately 30% 
of eligible KEDLI customers are enrolled in the respective utility’s low-income program.  AARP 
is pleased that the Signatory Parties agreed to several measures designed to increase enrollment.  
The Companies will add one FTE resource to support KEDLI and KEDNY in continuation of the 
Energy Affordability Program (“EAP”) (11.2.2).  The Companies agreed to increase the 
frequency of its file match process with the City of New York’s Human Resources 
Administration (“HRA”) from twice per year to four times per year.  These two measures 
address situations where customers are not aware of the low-income programs or experience 
changes in income and become newly eligible for low-income programs.  The measures are 
important components of a meaningful low-income program. 
Provision 11.2.4 is especially important to AARP.  A key reason that so few eligible KEDLI 
customers are enrolled in KEDLI’s low-income program, in contrast to the KEDNY experience,  
is that its file match is limited by Nassau and Suffolk Counties to HEAP recipients.  Despite 
numerous attempts and outreach efforts, AARP has not been able to convince the counties to

improve the lives of their low-income residents by replicating the robust file match conducted by 
HRA for KEDNY and Consolidated Edison and by Westchester County for Consolidated 
Edison.  The robust file match for KEDNY includes other benefit programs such SNAP and

Pursuant to provision 11.3, KEDNY will join KEDLI in waiving all reconnection fees for 
customers who are EAP participants.  This would remove a major impediment to a low-income 
customer’s financial ability to restore utility service.  AARP supports this change. 
AARP and PULP have long been concerned about the huge amount of arrears that KEDLI and 
KEDY customers have accumulated.  The Commission’s generic COVID proceeding is 
supposed to develop a plan for addressing arrearages but the proceeding is moving slowly.  
Provision 11.6 obligates the Companies to commence a collaborative by October 1, 2021 to 
discuss arrears resolution-related issues in the event the Commission does not address residential 
and small business arrears arising from the pandemic by September 15, 2021.  The Companies 
would file a report describing its plans and seek authorization as needed by November 15, 2021. 
AARP supports this initiative. 
Customers of the Companies speak dozens of languages; for many customers English is not their 
first language.  Pursuant to provision 11.7, the Companies have agreed to work with DPS Staff 
and other interested parties to assess the efficacy of their program to communicate in different 
languages. AARP supports this initiative. 
As noted above, AARP is troubled by the large increase in arrears resulting from the economic 
impacts of the pandemic.  Recently enacted federal and state programs have earmarked hundreds 
of millions of dollars to help utility customers pay down their arrears.  We asserted that some of 
the customers are in arrears for the first time and may not be aware of the assistance available to 
them.  AARP also asserted and, to our pleasure, the Companies agreed, that their Consumer 
Advocates would benefit from additional training focusing on these customers and the

new programs.  Accordingly, provision 11.8 reflects the agreement that the Companies will 
develop new training for its Consumer Advocates based on implementing a checklist of steps 
similar to the checklist provided in Appendix 12.   
This checklist should not be rigidly applied.  Rather, new or revised assistance programs and best 
practices derived from experience should be recognized.  For example, the 2021 state budget 
includes funding for the New York Assistance Program, which is to be administered by the State 
Office of Temporary Disability Assistance in coordination with the Commission.  Under this 
program, qualifying renters may have up to twelve months of their utility arrearages waived, 
provided that the arrears were incurred on or after March 13, 2020.  In addition, the federal 
government has established a $10 billion Homeowner Assistance Fund as part of the American 
Rescue Plan.  AARP urges the Companies to add the emergency rental assistance program 
(“ERAP”) and the emergency homeowner’s assistance program (“EHAP”) to the checklist. 
In contrast to these excellent initiatives is this provision on page 47 of the JP: 
 
If, upon consideration of the Companies’ reports and any public comments 
received, the independent consultant’s assessment finds that Long-Term Capital 
Capacity Project is not needed at the time proposed by the Companies, the 
Companies shall not be permitted to recover the costs of the project through the 
Demand Capacity Surcharge Mechanism. The Companies may seek to have the 
Commission reconsider the independent consultant’s assessment that a project is 
not necessary in a future rate proceeding on the basis of changed circumstances or 
any other basis permitted under the Public Service Law. Otherwise, the 
independent consultant’s assessment of need will not be subject to review during 
the term of the Companies’ rate plans.  
This provision would improperly and unlawfully remove the Commission from the decision-
making process regarding whether or not a capacity project is needed.  The provision would 
allow the Companies to contest the independent consultant’s assessment that a project is not 
necessary but would not allow DPS Staff or other parties or even the Commission to challenge 
an assessment.  Due process issues arise as does the question whether the Commission has the 
authority to remove itself from the decision-making process that may result in surcharges on 
customers’ bills.  This process includes the SAPA notice and comment period and the ability of 
parties to appeal decisions.
