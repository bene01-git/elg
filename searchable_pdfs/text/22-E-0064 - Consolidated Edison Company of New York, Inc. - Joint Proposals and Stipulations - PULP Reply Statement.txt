PRELIMINARY STATEMENT 
 
In accordance with the March 6, 2023, Ruling on Schedule, the Public Utility Law 
Project (“PULP”) hereby submits this Statement in Reply to the Initial Statements on the Joint 
Proposal (“JP”) filed on March 29, 2023, in the above captioned proceedings. 
  
PULP argues that Consolidated Edison (“Con Edison” or the “Company”) and the 
Signatory Parties to the JP have not met their burden of proving that the JP is in the public 
interest.1 Upon reading comments submitted by the parties in these proceedings, PULP believes 
that approving the JP “as is” will result in significant financial harm to residential low-income 
and disadvantaged customers, especially those low-income customers not currently identified by 
the Company through their Energy Assistance Program (“EAP”).  
 
In PULP’s Statement in Opposition to the JP,  we described our concerns as they related 
to unaffordability under the proposed rates: the unaffordability created by the 9.25% Return on 
Equity (“ROE”), unnecessary Earnings Adjustment Mechanisms (“EAMs”) in light of the 
requirement for action under the Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act 
(“CLCPA”), the financial harm caused by not freezing fixed charges, the failure to provide 
adequate consideration to disadvantaged communities (“DACs”) who will experience a 
disproportional economic impact from the proposed rate increase, the increase in energy burden 
that will impact low-income households not enrolled in EAP, and the lack of specificity in 
relation to the Company’s plans to apply for federal funds that could offset customer costs.2 For 
all of the reasons discussed herein, PULP once again urges the Public Service Commission 
(“PSC” or “Commission”) to reject the JP. The cases should be remanded for the purpose of 
establishing temporary rates pending litigation. The temporary rate order should contain the 
modified programs and practices included in PULP’s Statement in Opposition to the JP.3

1. SUPPORT FOR THE ELECTRIC AND GAS JPS IS NARROW AND NOT 
INDICATIVE OF BROAD BACKING 
 
During PULP’s review of the Statements in Support of the JP, a number of questions and 
concerns were raised about “who” actually supports the entirety of the JP. These concerns are 
detailed below. To begin, PULP does not believe that the JP is supported by adverse parties. The

“[s]upport for the Joint Proposal comes from a variety of entities with varying interests,

2. ROE   
 
Con Edison’s Statement of Support for the JP seeks to portray the Signatory Parties’ 
proposed 9.25% ROE and 48% equity ratio as a significant compromise with the assertion that: 
 
“The Joint Proposal’s 9.25 percent ROE and 48 percent common equity ratio represent 
significant compromise that accepts an ROE and capital structure that meets the bare 
minimum to establish just and reasonable rates.”7 
 
In fact, as PULP showed in its Statement of Opposition to the JP, the ROE chosen by the 
Parties is not likely to be just and reasonable because it does not have a rational basis, is not subject 
to update or “true-up” to reflect changes in market conditions impacting Con Edison’s cost of 
equity during the rate plan, and is falsely justified, in part, by the inclusion of an “earnings sharing 
mechanism” (“ESM”) which the past four Con Edison rate cases have shown to be grossly 
inequitable in favor of the Company. Based on the history of Con Edison rate plans since 2009, 
which PULP argues have cost its ratepayers up to $1.8 billion in excessive delivery charges, it is 
more likely than not that ratepayers would, under the proposed JP, incur delivery charges far higher 
than what they would have paid if the Commission were to approve an ROE correcting these 
deficiencies, as described in PULP’s Statement of Opposition.8 
 
To help support its claim that the 9.25% ROE and 48% equity ratio are significant 
compromises, Con Edison makes misleading comparisons to average nationally authorized ROEs

6. THE NEED FOR MORE TRANSPARENCY AND COMMITMENT BY THE 
COMPANY WITH APPLYING FOR FEDERAL FUNDS 
 
In PULP’s Statement of Opposition to the JP, we noted our concern with the lack of 
transparency and commitment surrounding the Company’s application for federal funding under 
the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (“IIJA”) and the Inflation Reduction Act (“IRA”).48  
AARP New York raised similar concerns in its Statement of Opposition. Even the City of New 
York, as a Signatory to the Joint Proposal, stated that it, “is aware that Con Edison is pursuing 
federal funding, and perhaps [the Company] should expand the scope of its request,”49 in light of 
rising energy costs. 
 
Despite these concerns, Staff nevertheless argues in its Statement in Support of the Joint 
Proposal that the Company’s plans regarding its application for federal funding is “reasonable” 
because, “customers will receive the revenue requirement impact of the decrease in program or 
project costs if the Company receives funding from the DOE.”50 In its own Statement in Support 
of the Joint Proposal, the Company makes no claim as to the reasonableness of its plan regarding 
federal funding, or whether its efforts are in the public interest. Instead, the Company merely 
reiterates a commitment to meet with interested parties regarding the IIJA/IRA-related projects by 
September 30, 2023.51

As PULP stated in its Statement of Opposition, the public interest is furthered by increased 
transparency and accountability in all aspects of utility ratemaking.52 The JP provides the general 
public with an opportunity to understand the Company’s intentions for the coming Rate Years. By 
not disclosing the nature of the projects it has submitted for consideration under IIJA or IRA, the 
Company has denied the public an opportunity to fully appraise the reasonableness of the proposal. 
Moreover, the public has no indication of how much the revenue requirement impact potentially 
will be if the Company receives funding under IIJA or IRA. Considering the above issues and 
concerns, the Commission should direct the Company to provide greater transparency pertaining 
to Con Edison’s applications under IIJA or IRA and the total amount of savings that rate payers 
could expect to receive through the resulting grants. 
 
CONCLUSION 
 
PULP submits this Reply Statement for the purpose of PULP recommending that the PSC 
reject the JP on the grounds that it is not in the public interest and is inconsistent with the

adequate. PULP urges the PSC to remand the cases for the establishment of temporary rates 
pending litigation. The temporary rate order should contain the programs and practices discussed 
in the sections above.
