Commission generally adopts SAIFI and CAIDI metrics as part of

included in the rate plans usually require a utility to meet 
certain targets of reliability for each metric or incur an NRA. 
 
 
SAIFI is a measure of the average number of times that 
a customer is interrupted.3  Lower SAIFI values reflect fewer 
interruptions and better service reliability.  SAIFI is 
calculated by dividing the total number of customer 
interruptions in a year by the average number of customers 
served during the year.4  For example, a single event causing an 
interruption of service for 20 customers would count as 20 
“customers interrupted” for purposes of SAIFI.  To be considered 
a customer interruption for purposes of SAIFI, the customer’s 
electric service has to be interrupted for at least five 
minutes.5 
 
 
CAIDI is a measure of the average interruption 
duration time, in hours, for customers that experience an 
interruption during the year.6  The lower the CAIDI hours the 
faster service was restored to electric customers.  CAIDI is 
calculated by dividing the sum of the duration of all customer

LEGAL AUTHORITY 
 
 
Pursuant to Public Service Law (PSL) §§5 and 66, the 
Commission has the legal authority to review the proposed 
requests for waiver or exemptions to the terms of the rate plan

prescribed in this Order. 
 
DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION 
 
 
Pursuant to the 2020 and 2023 Rate Orders the 
Companies’ SAIFI and CAIDI performance is measured on an annual 
system-wide basis.  If the Companies’ performance fails to meet 
the identified thresholds, the Companies incur the prescribed 
NRAs.  In evaluating the Petitions, before addressing the merits 
of the requested exemptions, we first assess whether granting 
the exemptions would change the outcome of the Companies’ 
respective SAIFI and CAIDI metrics for 2022 and 2023.  As 
explained below, even if all the identified outages are excluded 
as the Companies’ request, the results of their SAIFI and CAIDI

In 2022, NYSEG incurred a $7.0 million NRA as a result 
of its SAIFI performance of 1.45, which failed to meet the 
maximum threshold of 1.26 for NYSEG’s SAIFI metric set in the

its SAIFI and CAIDI targets.  Accordingly, RG&E did not incur an 
NRA for its performance in 2022.  NYSEG and RG&E requested 
exemptions for 942 and 976 outages, respectively, in 2022.  
Based on their respective performance, excluding any outages 
could only impact the NRA NYSEG incurred for its SAIFI 
performance.  However, even excluding the outages as requested 
by NYSEG, its SAIFI result would be 1.35.20  With this level of 
SAIFI performance, NYSEG would still incur a $7.0 million NRA.

did not incur an NRA for its CAIDI performance.  RG&E met both
