17 
A. 
Generally, CCS involves capturing carbon dioxide (CO2) from an industrial point source, 
18 
compressing the CO2 into a fluid, transporting it to an appropriate storage site via

20

Q.  
Are there challenges associated with CCUS technology that have impeded its 
8 
adoption?  
9 
A. 
Yes. CCS project economics are a leading barrier to CCS projects, primarily due to the 
10 
high cost of carbon capture5 and a lack of a market mechanism or incentive that is 
11 
sufficiently large and long-term enough to reward an entity for its carbon reduction.6 
12 
Plants that are equipped with CCS technology impose additional capital costs associated 
13 
with the CO2 separation and capture equipment, as well as higher operating costs and 
14 
energy consumption associated with operating it.7 For power plant applications, CCS is 
15 
an energy intensive process that lowers the overall efficiency of energy and power 
16 
generating systems, leading to increased fuel requirements, solid wastes, and other 
17 
environmental impacts relative to the same type of plant without capture.8   
18 
 
19 
The other significant challenge concerns securing subsurface storage space. The 
20 
subsurface geology must be demonstrated to be capable of containing the CO2 safely,
