DISCUSSION 
 
 
It is long-standing practice and policy for New York 
State utilities to offer economic development grants and rate 
incentives, and the Commission has regularly approved ratepayer-
funded economic development programs in recognition of the key 
role that utilities play in attracting and retaining businesses 
within their service territories.  Well-designed business 
retention and attraction programs offered by utilities can 
benefit all customers in the service territory by stabilizing 
energy sales and moderating volumetric rates, and they help 
ensure that utilities are engaged with other economic 
development authorities in identifying and pursuing economic 
development leads.  These programs also enhance job 
opportunities for utility customers within the service 
territory. 
 
 
As described above, RG&E’s Economic Development 
Programs have leveraged significant capital investment, job 
creation and retention and other economic benefits to its 
service territory.  The Company’s Petition and Supplemental 
Filing provided details of a wide variety of benefits expected 
to be achieved in the service territory to support its claim 
that Fairlife’s new facility is consistent with the focus of 
RG&E’s economic development programs.  Fairlife’s new facility 
is projected to create jobs, attract significant capital 
investment, and trigger infrastructure enhancements that will 
benefit other expanding businesses in Webster New York. 
 
 
Focusing upon the threshold issues the Commission 
considered in this instance, the Company’s submissions to Staff

hospitals/healthcare facilities, and certain projects that are 
endorsed by one of the Empire State Development Corporation’s 
Regional Economic Development Councils and/or the Governors’ 
Office.  The applicant/customer must also demonstrate the 
intention to retain existing jobs and/or attract new employment.  
Further, the applicant/customer must obtain complementary 
financial support from other sources, such as state or local 
economic development agencies and that such financial support 
shall not amount to less than 50 percent of the Company’s TFA to 
the applicant/customer.

CLCPA Analysis 
 
 
New York’s recently enacted CLCPA reflects an 
ambitious commitment to reduce GHG emissions and achieve net-
zero emissions economywide, increase renewable energy usage, and 
prioritize climate justice.  Among its provisions, CLCPA §§7(2) 
and 7(3) require state agencies to conduct analyses regarding 
certain types of decisions.  CLCPA §7(2) requires all state 
agencies to consider whether certain final agency actions are 
inconsistent with or will interfere with the attainment of the 
statewide GHG emission limits established under Environmental 
Conservation Law (ECL) Article 75.  CLCPA §7(2) further states 
that, if a decision is deemed to be inconsistent with, or to 
interfere with, the attainment of the statewide GHG emissions 
limits, the responsible agency, office, authority, or division 
must provide a detailed statement of justification as to why 
such limits may not be met and identify alternatives or GHG 
mitigation measures to be required.  As regulations to implement

Turning to CLCPA §7(3), that section provides that, in 
considering and issuing permits, licenses, and other 
administrative approvals and decisions, state agencies, 
including the Commission, shall not disproportionately burden 
disadvantaged communities, as such are defined under state law.  
CLCPA §7(3) also requires that all state agencies prioritize 
reductions in GHG emissions and co-pollutants in disadvantaged 
communities.  The Fairlife CLCPA Analysis represents that the 
project is not located within a disadvantaged community.34  The 
Commission reviewed the location of the facility and has 
confirmed that it is neither in nor adjacent to a disadvantaged 
community.35  Thus, the Commission’s action in this Order will 
not disproportionately burden disadvantaged communities. 
 
CONCLUSION 
 
 
Considering the financial inputs of Fairlife and 
stated economic benefits discussed in this Order, it is 
appropriate and reasonable to grant a one-time exemption to the 
cap on RG&E’s TFA program allowing RG&E to award an economic 
development grant of up to $5.0 million to Fairlife for the 
electric infrastructure upgrades necessary to expand its 
business in New York.  This exception applies only for the 
single instance described in this Order and neither changes any 
of the existing guidelines or eligibility requirements for the 
TFA Program, nor sets any precedent concerning any potential 
future requests for waivers.
