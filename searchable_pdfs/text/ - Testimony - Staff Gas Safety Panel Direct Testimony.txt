INFRASTRUCTURE REPLACEMENT OR REDUCTION 
10 
Q. 
What type of pipe is considered leak prone? 
11 
A. 
Leak prone pipe (LPP) generally includes 
12 
unprotected steel, cast iron, wrought iron, and 
13 
some earlier vintages of plastic pipe, such as 
14 
aldyl.  According to its response to DPS-213, 
15 
contained in Exhibit__(SGSP-1), Con Edison’s LPP 
16 
inventory consists of unprotected bare steel, 
17 
small diameter cast iron that is 12-inches and 
18 
smaller, and wrought iron. 
19 
Q. 
What is meant by the term “unprotected”? 
20 
A. 
Unprotected means that the pipe lacks adequate 
21 
cathodic protection, thus rendering it 
22 
susceptible to corrosion.  Unprotected steel 
23 
often has inadequate or no coating, which makes 
24 


--- Page 14 ---
Case 25-G-0073 
 
 
 
    SGSP 
 
13 
efforts to cathodically protect such pipe 
1 
ineffective, uneconomical, or both. 
2 
Q. 
What is meant by the term “cathodic protection”? 
3 
A. 
Cathodic protection means the prevention of 
4 
electrolytic corrosion of a metallic structure, 
5 
such as a pipeline, by causing it to act as the 
6 
cathode rather than the anode of an 
7 
electrochemical cell. 
8 
Q. 
Explain the importance of replacing or reducing 
9 
unprotected steel pipe. 
10 
A. 
Data collected by the United States Department 
11 
of Transportation, Pipeline and Hazardous 
12 
Materials Safety Administration (PHMSA) shows 
13 
that corrosion is a leading cause of pipeline 
14 
failures, and of all LPPs unprotected steel is 
15 
one of the materials that is more susceptible to 
16 
corrosion.  This information is publicly 
17 
available at: 
18 
https://primis.phmsa.dot.gov/comm/FactSheets/FSC
19 
orrosion.htm. 
20 
Q. 
Explain the importance of replacing or reducing 
21 
cast iron pipe. 
22 
A. 
In general, cast-iron pipe is brittle, 
23 
susceptible to graphitization, which is a form 
24 


--- Page 15 ---
Case 25-G-0073 
 
 
 
    SGSP 
 
14 
of corrosion, and has low beam strength.  Beam 
1 
strength refers to the amount of load a 
2 
structure can withstand before it fails.  Low 
3 
beam strength means that cast iron pipe can fail 
4 
if it is subjected to increased loading from an 
5 
external force, or from a loss of ground 
6 
support.  This characteristic makes the material 
7 
particularly susceptible to stresses from 
8 
underground forces or disturbances.  Such 
9 
disturbances include, but are not limited to, 
10 
ground settlement, freeze-thaw cycles, soil 
11 
erosion, or nearby excavation activities.  The 
12 
physical characteristics of cast iron make it 
13 
more prone to catastrophic failure than 
14 
cathodically protected steel or plastic.  Also, 
15 
the smaller the diameter, the more susceptible 
16 
the cast iron piping is to breakage.  In 
17 
addition, segments of cast iron are joined by 
18 
hub joints, also known as bell and spigot 
19 
joints.  Packing material, such as jute, was 
20 
used in the joints to prevent leakage when cast 
21 
iron pipelines were first installed many decades 
22 
ago.  Unlike steel welded joints, cast iron 
23 
joints can develop leaks with time.  
24

Q. 
How does the replacement or reduction of cast 
1 
iron contribute to the safety of the gas system? 
2 
A. 
Cast iron tends to be located in densely 
3 
populated areas where there are many enclosed 
4 
structures, subsurface structures, and 
5 
continuously paved areas.  In the event of a 
6 
major leak or pipeline failure, these 
7 
environmental circumstances may lead to greater 
8 
volumes of below-ground gas migration, 
9 
particularly under paved areas and through 
10 
connected subsurface structures, which creates 
11 
an increased risk for fires or explosions.  
12 
Replacing or reducing cast iron pipe decreases 
13 
the potential for incidents resulting from leaks 
14 
and failures, thereby improving public safety. 
15 
Q. 
How does the infrastructure replacement or 
16 
reduction program improve the safety of the gas 
17 
system? 
18 
A. 
Underground piping leaks can create safety risks 
19 
to the public which can potentially lead to 
20 
serious incidents, resulting in fatalities or 
21 
injuries to persons or property damage. 
22 
Therefore, replacing or reducing LPP decreases 
23 
these safety concerns and risks. 
24 


--- Page 17 ---
Case 25-G-0073

16 
Q. 
Are there other benefits associated with 
1 
replacing or reducing LPP? 
2 
A. 
Yes, replacing or reducing LPP decreases the 
3 
number of leaks, leads to a decline in leakage 
4 
rates, reduces methane emissions, and reduces 
5 
the operating, maintenance, and overtime costs 
6 
associated with surveying leaks and responding 
7 
to emergency reports. 
8 
Q. 
Is water infiltration also a concern for low 
9 
pressure LPP? 
10 
A. 
Yes.  During periods of high rain, extreme 
11 
weather conditions, or when the water table 
12 
rises, low pressure LPP is susceptible to water 
13 
infiltration.  When water infiltration into low 
14 
pressure LPP occurs, any resulting outages, in 
15 
general, tend to be longer in duration and can 
16 
persist.  By removing low pressure LPP, system 
17 
reliability improves in addition to reducing the 
18 
number of service interruptions. 
19

Q. 
How does the Company prioritize pipeline 
11 
segments when replacing or reducing LPP? 
12 
A. 
The pipe to be removed from service is 
13 
identified and ranked using a risk assessment 
14 
model-based approach. 
15 
Q. 
What is a risk assessment model? 
16 
A. 
A risk assessment model prioritizes all segments 
17 
of LPP according to attributes that pose the 
18 
highest associated risk.  The model uses several 
19 
weighted factors including, but not limited to, 
20 
material type, diameter, pressure, date of 
21 
installation, and leakage history, to determine 
22 
a ranking.  This allows the Company to provide a 
23 
higher level of public safety by focusing more 
24

    SGSP 
 
18 
of its efforts and resources on pipeline 
1 
segments with higher risk.  Although there are 
2 
commonalities between the State’s distribution 
3 
companies, it is important to note that the 
4 
model is tailored to the Company’s unique mix of 
5 
assets and may not be appropriate for other 
6 
operators.  A uniform approach cannot 
7 
accommodate the characteristics that must be 
8 
considered for each system.
