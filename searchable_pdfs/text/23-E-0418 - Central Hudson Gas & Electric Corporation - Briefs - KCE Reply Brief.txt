INTRODUCTION 
As stated in Key Capture Energy’s Post-Evidentiary Hearing Brief, the rates proposed in 
this case as they are applied to bulk storage are unjust and unreasonable, discriminatory, and 
contrary to the State’s policy goals. While it is true that Central Hudson Gas and Electric 
(“Central Hudson” or the “Company”) has now filed a Wholesale Distribution Service (“WDS”) 
tariff, according to Central Hudson (as described below), the Federal Energy Regulatory 
Commission (“FERC”) defers to the states on matters of state rate design and policy. This case is 
the State’s opportunity to set that rate policy. Further, Central Hudson’s application of 
distribution rates to all facilities at 69, 115, and 345 kV obstruct the State’s policy goals, as set 
forth in the New York Energy Storage Roadmap (Roadmap), the Community Leadership and 
Climate Protection Act (“CLCPA”) and Governor Hochul’s enunciated goal of 6 gigawatts of 
storage capacity statewide by 2030 to support the State’s renewable energy needs. KCE Prefiled 
Test., Tr. p. 3744, ln. 10-19, and Tr. p. 3745, ln.1-4. Failing to establish rate policy here will set 
back the State’s ability to meet these goals, reducing the number of economic projects that may 
be available to competitively deliver storage benefits to New York ratepayers.

CONCLUSION 
KCE’s testimony in this case regarding the substance of its claims remains unopposed. 
Central Hudson appears to apply state-jurisdictional rates to all battery storage in its service 
territory. This runs counter to state law and policy. Key Capture Energy Post-Hearing 
Evidentiary Brief, p. 2. In the instant proceeding, the NY Commission should reject Central 
Hudson’s proposed SC 13 rate as it applies to bulk storage and should require Central Hudson to: 
(a) apply Order 841 by analogy to bulk storage under SC 13 and (b) resubmit its WDS tariff so 
that it exempts bulk storage from paying retail distribution rates, or derivations thereof, when 
providing a service to the wholesale electricity market, consistent with the rate treatment that 
Order 841 accords federally-jurisdictional transmission-interconnected bulk storage.
