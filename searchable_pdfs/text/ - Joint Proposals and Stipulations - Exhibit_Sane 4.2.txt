In that agreement, National Grid committed to researching and publishing a 
report that offers potential long-term solution options to address downstate 
New York's recognized natural gas constraint issue.  National Grid issued this 
report in February 2020 and scheduled a series of public meetings to hear 
from our customers and other stakeholders and understand their expectations 
and opinions on how we together can solve the energy challenges that face our 
region. 
 
 
Because of the outbreak of the coronavirus, and in consideration of the 
wellbeing of the public and our employees, we've had to cancel the public 
face-to-face meetings, except for our first one on March 9 that took place in 
Hicksville, Long Island.  
 
 
To provide an alternative way, to help you understand the report and our 
findings, we scheduled a series of virtual meetings to achieve the same 
objective, to share the information and answer any questions you may have on 
each of the options as well as hear from you directly through an oral comment 
period. 
 
 
Before we get to the run of show and how this session will work and I can 
provide you some context and other important information.  New York has 
seen dynamic economic growth in the downstate region, expanding residential 
and nonresidential building space and thousands of oil-to-gas conversions 
over the last ten years.   
 
 
These factors have resulted in a substantial increase in the demand for natural 
gas, placing stress on our existing gas network and threatening National Grid's 
ability to meet our customers' needs when the demand is at its peak.

This leaves little room for error in the face of an unplanned supply 
interruption or other contingencies.  As part of the November 2019 settlement 
agreement with New York State that lifted the moratorium on new gas 
connections imposed in May 2019, we're taking measures to ensure we have 
enough supply for the winters of 2019 through 2020 and 2020 through 2021 
including increasing reliance on compressed natural gas, or otherwise known 
as CNG trucking, when needed to meet peak demand. 
 
 
Beyond the next two winters, however, continued growth in the demand for 
natural gas creates a challenge that must be addressed.  The long-term natural 
gas capacity (unintelligible) presents ten options to solve that challenge.  It's 
important to note that the long-term solution options we're presenting and the 
various combinations of these options working together have different 
implications for how long we continue to rely on CNG trucking to meet peak 
demand and whether we might have future restrictions on new gas 
connections. 
 
 
Also of note, low common solutions will be an important part of reaching the 
longer-term clean energy goals.  While formerly not part of tonight's 
presentation, National Grid is partnering with third parties to develop 
renewable natural gas solutions, exploring hydrogen blending and palleted gas 
to help carbonize the gas system.  We're also piloting geothermal heat pump 
solutions with partners like NYSERDA, and we continue to implement clean 
energy solutions throughout our entire service territory in the US with solar, 
wind, battery storage and EV stations.

Long-term options to solve the natural gas capacity constraint can be grouped
