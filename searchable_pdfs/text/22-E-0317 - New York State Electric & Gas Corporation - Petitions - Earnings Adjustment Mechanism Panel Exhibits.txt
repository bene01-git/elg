compliance filing to the Commission showing the calculation of incentives earned under

EAM 1:  Electric Share the Savings (“ESTS”) 
The ESTS EAM was designed “to reduce unit costs for each Company’s electric energy 
efficiency portfolio by reducing the unit cost of lifetime energy savings (on a dollar per 
lifetime Megawatt-hour basis) below unit cost levels as approved in the Commission’s 
Order Adopting Accelerated Energy Efficiency Targets, issued December 13, 2018 in 
case 18-M-0084 and the January 16, 2020 Order, while increasing the overall 
achievement level of energy savings.”3 
Simply, the metric was designed to incent the utility to reduce the overall cost per 
lifetime MWH for the Energy Efficiency programs.  If the utility’s cost per lifetime 
MWH came in below the pre-set target, then the savings would be calculated as the 
difference between the actual cost per lifetime MWH and the pre-set target multiplied by 
the number of MWH of savings, times the effective use life (10 years).   The utility 
would then get to keep 30% of the calculated lifetime savings.

EAM 5:  Electric Peak Reduction 
The Electric Peak Reduction EAM was designed to incent NYSEG and RG&E to reduce 
the coincident peak in their respective control areas.10  The targets were derived using 
historical data from the NYISO Installed Capacity forecast Gold Book.11   
To the extent that the coincident peak load could be reduced (on a weather normalized 
basis) below the thresholds, then the companies could realize an EAM. 
For Rate Year 1, neither NYSEG nor RG&E saw a weather normalized reduction in the 
coincident peak.  While the company saw reduced commercial and industrial peak loads 
during the pandemic summer of 2020, this reduction was offset by increased residential 
electric peak loads. More residential customers, who traditionally are low load factor 
customers, were at home during the summer of 2020. As a result, there was greater air-
conditioning load used by this class, which had upward pressure on the coincident peak 
and offset any reductions by the commercial and industrial classes.

portfolio and reduce the unit costs of lifetime energy savings (on a dollar per lifetime 
million British Thermal Unit [MMBTU] basis) below targeted unit cost levels.12 
Similar to the ESTS EAM, the metric was designed to incent the utility to reduce the 
overall cost per lifetime MMBTU for the gas Energy Efficiency programs.  If the utility’s 
cost per lifetime MMBTU came in below the pre-set target, then the savings would be 
calculated as the difference between the actual cost per lifetime MMBTU and the pre-set 
target multiplied by the number of MMBTU of savings, times the effective use life (10 
years).   The utility would then receive 30% of the calculated lifetime savings. 
Neither NYSEG nor RG&E met the necessary thresholds for cost per MMBTU 
reductions, and thus no EAM was earned.

1.1. Electric Share the Savings excluding LMI EAM 
1.1.1. Description 
The ESTS excluding LMI EAM is designed to reduce unit costs for each Company’s 
electric energy efficiency (“EE”) portfolio, not including low-to-moderate income 
programs, by reducing the unit cost of lifetime energy savings (on a dollar per lifetime 
Megawatt-hour (“MWh”) basis) below unit cost levels as approved in the Commission’s 
Order Adopting Accelerated Energy Efficiency Targets, issued December 13, 2018 in 
Case 18-M-0084 and the January 16, 2020 Order, while increasing the overall 
achievement level of energy savings.  Under the ESTS excluding LMI EAM, each 
Company will be awarded 30% of unit cost savings realized from the respective 
Company’s acquired electric EE savings once the Company has met minimum electric 
EE lifetime savings targets, as provided in the metric described here. 
1.1.2. Description 
The EAM performance and associated Company incentive will be calculated by 
determining: (i) the electric EE unit cost savings relative to the baseline unit cost; (ii) 
applying that to the acquired EE savings; and (iii) applying a percent share to the result, 
in this case 30%. The following formula represents this calculation: 
[Actual EE Unit Cost without LMI – Baseline EE Unit Cost Less LMI] x EUL x MWH Savings x 30% 
1.1.3. Measurement 
The applicable Total Resource Measure (“TRM”), at the time savings are acquired, will 
be used for each EE measure in the RY. 
1.1.4. Achievement 
Achievement for this EAM is based on the formula detailed in section 1.1.2.  A Company 
must achieve the base eligible installed MWh target levels to be eligible to receive a 
share of the savings under this EAM. 
1.1.5. Targets 
The following table provides the target levels for ESTS EAM. Savings can be achieved 
by either: (i) lowering unit cost (i.e., lowering costs relative to the budget); (ii) increasing 
MWh savings; or (iii) a combination of (i) and (ii).

1.3. Heat Pump Share the Savings 
1.3.1. Description 
The HPSTS EAM is designed to reduce unit costs for each Company’s heat pump 
portfolio by reducing the unit cost of lifetime energy savings (on a dollar per lifetime 
million British thermal units (“MMBtu”) basis), below unit cost levels as approved in the 
Commission’s January 16, 2020 Order, while increasing the overall achievement level of 
energy savings. Under the HPSTS EAM, the Company will be awarded 30% of unit cost 
savings realized from the Company’s acquired heat pump savings once the Company has 
met minimum heat pump MMBtu savings targets, as provided in the metric described 
below. In the event NYSEG or RG&E undertake non-pipes alternatives which include 
heat pumps as part of the solution, the Companies will not include those heat pumps 
associated with any such project in the calculation of the HPSTS EAM. 
1.3.2. Metric 
The EAM performance and associated Company incentive will be calculated by 
determining: (i) the heat pump unit cost savings relative to the baseline unit cost; (ii) 
applying that to the acquired heat pump savings; and (iii) applying a percent share to the 
result, in this case 30%.  The following formula represents this calculation: 
[Actual EE Unit Cost - Baseline EE Unit Cost] x EUL X MWH Savings x 30% 
1.3.3. Measurement 
At the time a customer is awarded an incentive for a heat pump, the heat pump will be 
considered as installed for the purposes of calculating this EAM.  Typical residential 
installations will be counted based on 1) whether they are air-source heat pumps 
(“ASHPs”) or ground source heat pumps (“GSHPs”) and 2) whether the heat pump can 
provide water heating in addition to space heating, and then awarded an assumed level of 
lifetime MMBtu savings.  If a heat pump can provide both water heating and space

3. Peak Load Reduction EAMs 
3.1. Electric System Load Factor EAM 
3.1.1. Description 
The goal of the Electric System Load Factor EAM is to increase the system load factor by 
either (1) decreasing the daily peak MW OR (2) increasing the minimum MW.  Ideally 
this would be accomplished by shifting load from the more expensive day-time hours to 
the night-time, or off-peak, hours.  This would provide direct benefits to the distribution 
circuit as any reduction in peak decreases the pressure on needs to upgrade substations in 
the near term.  Additionally improving the load factor reduces load fluctuations and 
makes the overall system more efficient. 
3.1.2. Metric 
The Companies’ performance under this EAM will be determined by calculating the ratio 
of highest electric hourly demand to the lowest hourly electric demand to approximate 
the load factor for the Rate Year.

4.1.1. Description

water heating and space heating, it will be awarded the sum of the appropriate values 
found in the appropriate table in Section 4.1.5.
