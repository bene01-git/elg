 Q. How do Con Edison’s requested returns compare 
1 
with industry peers? 
2 
A. 
Regulated utilities, given their predictable and 
3 
stable nature, normally earn returns in the 9%–
4 
10% range. Historical data confirm that  
5 
companies like Con Edison that operate in a 
6 
quasi-monopolistic market have rarely been 
7 
allowed returns above that level. Con Edison’s 
8 
request for an elevated ROE exceeds these 
9 
established norms, implying an excessive profit 
10 
margin that would result in higher customer 
11 
rates. 
12 
 
13 
Q. 
What is the effect of an elevated ROE on 
14 
customer rates? 
15 
A. 
A higher allowed ROE increases a utility’s 
16 
revenue requirement, and these additional 
17 
revenues are in the end recovered through higher 
18 
customer rates. Such increases tend to 
19 
disproportionately affect low income and 
20 
disadvantaged consumers, thereby compromising 
21 
affordability and the recovery of residential 
22 
arrears.

Q.

4 
ratepayers?

A. 
They help ensure that ratepayers are not 
6 
overcharged for poor performance, while still 
7 
rewarding shareholders. When a utility fails to

returns that aren’t backed by true ratepayer

success.
