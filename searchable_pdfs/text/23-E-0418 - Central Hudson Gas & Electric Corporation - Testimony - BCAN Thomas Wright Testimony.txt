II. PURPOSE 
7 
Q-4.  What is the purpose of your testimony in this case? 
8 
A-4.  The purpose of my testimony is to use the narrative of my experience with BCAN 
9 
and as a commercial property owner and landlord to highlight the gap between the necessary 
10 
work required by the climate crisis and Central Hudson’s apparent unwillingness to engage in 
11 
that work on the one hand, and their efforts to actively undermine it on the other. 
12 
 
13 
Q-5.   In your opinion, what is the “necessary work required by the climate 
14 
crisis?”  
15 
A-5.  The necessary work required is the rapid phaseout of fossil fuels from the global 
16 
economy. 
17 
 
18 
Q-6.   In your opinion, what is the justification for calling this work “necessary”? 
19 
A-6.  The justification comes from the work of climate scientists across the world, 
20 
specifically those convened by the United Nations as members of the Intergovernmental Panel 
21 
on Climate Change (IPCC).  
22 


--- Page 5 ---
4 
 
 
Recognizing the urgent need to keep global average temperatures as close as possible to 
1 
1.5 degrees C above preindustrial levels, and to that end, the urgent need to lower greenhouse 
2 
gas emissions that result from the combustion of fossil fuels, governing bodies at all scales of 
3 
leadership have set policy based on the recommendations of these scientists. 
4 
Furthermore, the reports of the IPCC have shown a crystal clear link between the 
5 
combustion of fossil fuels, the greenhouse gas emissions resulting from the combustion of fossil 
6 
fuels, to rising global average temperatures, and the connection of these rising temperatures to 
7 
extreme weather events. These weather events, and other climactic realities such as loss of access 
8 
to water and arable land due to drought conditions, result in direct, and dire consequences for 
9 
humans across the globe. As a result, leaders around the world have taken to referring to the 
10 
climate crisis as “an existential threat” and a “threat to humanity.” 
11 
It is therefore my opinion that it is appropriate to describe the work of phasing out fossil 
12 
fuels required to meet and prevent these existential threats, as necessary. 
13

IV. BCAN CAMPAIGN FOR FOSSIL FUEL-FREE BUILDINGS 
1 
Q-14.   How does the work of BCAN relate to the issues being considered in the rate 
2 
case? 
3 
A-14.  BCAN was formed in May of 2022 with the following vision: “In the face of the 
4 
climate crisis and the related crisis of care, our vision is to create systems of democracy, 
5 
resilience, and regeneration, resulting in a hopeful, joyful future of collective flourishing.” Its 
6 
very first campaign was a community-based effort to encourage city leaders to craft and pass 
7 
legislation that would prevent the use of fossil fuels in new construction and major renovations. 
8 
 
9 
Q-15.  What was your reason for engaging in this campaign? 
10 
A-15.  I engaged with this campaign as a way to achieve local action in the urgent work 
11 
required to mitigate the climate crisis. Specifically, I saw the campaign as a way to lower 
12 
greenhouse gas emissions by prohibiting the use of equipment that relies on the combustion of 
13 
fossil fuels.  
14 
It can be frustrating to match the urgency of the climate crisis with the scale of the 
15 
climate crisis. In spite of this, through my reading and research, I became aware that there were 
16 
ways to find action in our everyday lives that do in fact, make a difference in mitigation at the 
17 
global scale. 
18 
Electrification of buildings is a great example of action that individuals can take at the 
19 
scale of their home, or workplace that has impacts at larger scales. Replacing fossil-fuel 
20 
combusting machines with electric machines is by definition a rapid phaseout of fossil fuels.  
21 


--- Page 9 ---
8 
 
 
Given the way that energy is supplied to our buildings, this phaseout has a broader, 
1 
systemic impact by reducing, albeit in a small way, the network of fossil fuel delivery. By 
2 
eliminating demand, the system of delivery is impacted. 
3 
At the same time, installing efficient, all-electric equipment is a way to encourage the 
4 
adoption of renewables, which deliver energy in the form of electricity. The world is seeing a 
5 
rapid growth in renewable energy on the supply side. This is due both to rapidly falling costs of 
6 
renewables, and policies put in place to accelerate the adoption of renewables. 
7 
In these ways, an individual act can lead to systemic impacts. 
8 
Targeting the emissions generated by buildings was a logical place to apply local efforts 
9 
since 30% of greenhouse gas emissions in NY State come from buildings.  
10 
In addition to targeting greenhouse gas emissions that result in the combustion of fossil 
11 
fuels, the campaign sought to reduce well-documented health risks stemming from the use of gas 
12 
for residential cooking. 
13

A-16.  We saw a gap between the policies of leadership regarding the climate crisis, and

action regarding the mitigation of the climate crisis. In fact, there seemed to be an inverse 
19 
relationship between the scale of leadership, and the ability to achieve action on mitigation 
20 
policies at that scale.  
21 
We saw that local legislation was something that we could achieve action on at the pace 
22 
required by the climate crisis.

20

legislation becomes more commonplace, there will be fewer users of fossil fuels. These users

6

favor of such behavior are not only myopic but ignorant of widely available facts.

VI. SUPPORTING THE GOALS OF THE CLCPA 
7 
Q-26.  Given the CLCPA’s statement of commitment to a phaseout of fossil fuels, 
8 
what is your opinion regarding the role of a utility company in these efforts? 
9 
A-26.  A utility company can and should play a key role in the phaseout of fossil fuels.  
10 
It is my understanding that a public utility exists to serve customers safe, reliable, 
11 
affordable, and increasingly clean power by building and managing infrastructure, and 
12 
monetizing this infrastructure by charging ratepayers for its use.  
13 
The question for the PSC, the agency that regulates the activities of a for-profit, 
14 
monopoly is: is this activity serving to phase out the delivery of fossil fuels by ending the 
15 
construction of fossil-fuel delivery infrastructure, or is it serving to encourage the use of fossil 
16 
fuels by expanding fossil-fuel infrastructure? 
17 
If the answer to this question is the latter, then the utility company is not playing a 
18 
supporting role in the fossil fuel phaseout strategies outlined by the CLCPA.
