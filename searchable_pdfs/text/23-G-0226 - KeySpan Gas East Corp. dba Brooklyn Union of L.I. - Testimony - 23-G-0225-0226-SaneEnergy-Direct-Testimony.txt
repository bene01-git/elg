II. GAS ISSUES 
135 
Introduction And Background: 
136 
 
137 
In light of the pressing challenges posed by climate change and the imperative of adhering to the 
138 
Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act (CLCPA), as well as evaluating proposals 
139 
related to fracked gas infrastructure, it is essential to scrutinize National Grid's request for $3.8 
140 
billion in billing to New Yorkers over the next four years. This proposal, intended to facilitate the 
141 
expansion and extension of the gas network, raises serious concerns about its environmental and 
142 
ethical responsibility.  
143 
We urge the New York State Department of Public Service (NYSDPS or “Staff”), The City of 
144 
New York (“NYC”), and The Public Service Commissioners (“PSC”) to uphold both the letter and 
145 
spirit of our state's laws. In the mission of the PSC, we find a commitment to "stimulate effective 
146 
competitive markets for clean, renewable, and distributed energy resources that benefit New York 
147 
consumers, as well as product and service innovations." 
148 
National Grid's plan for gas network expansion and extension is at odds with the CLCPA, failing 
149 
to align with the mandated goals of transitioning away from fossil fuels by 2050, while directing 
150 
40% of state climate and energy funding toward historically disadvantaged communities. 
151 
The proposal's core flaw lies in its attempt to expand and prolong aging and leak-prone 
152 
infrastructure over the next four years, falsely portraying this action as emissions reduction, only 
153 
to witness these same structures continue to deteriorate and emit past 2050. Such an approach is 
154 
incompatible with the legally binding emissions reduction standards and the overarching objective 
155 
of eliminating New York's dependence on fossil fuels by mid-century. 
156 
Moreover, National Grid's endorsement of fossil fuel expansion demonstrates a stark disregard for 
157 


--- Page 8 ---
7 
the profound implications of climate change and the injustices experienced by New Yorkers in 
158 
recent times. In the year 2023, we confronted a summer marked by unprecedented temperatures, 
159 
officially recorded as the hottest year on a global scale, as corroborated by data from authoritative 
160 
sources such as NOAA and NASA1. During this period, the infiltration of Canadian wildfire 
161 
smoke, driven by climatic shifts, enveloped National Grid's service territory, resulting in the 
162 
closure of educational institutions and a surge in emergency room visits, particularly among 
163 
hundreds of individuals afflicted by asthma. 
164 
Notably, a significant portion of National Grid's service territory constitutes an Urban Heat Island 
165 
(UHI), typified by a densely paved urban environment that retains heat. Among 44 U.S. cities 
166 
assessed, New York ranks the highest in the UHI Index2. This phenomenon disproportionately 
167 
affects low-income communities of color, whose residents face elevated risks of mortality during 
168 
and immediately following extreme heat events. An empirical analysis conducted by NYC 
169 
employing the Heat Vulnerability Index (HVI) reveals that certain neighborhoods are particularly 
170 
vulnerable to heat-related mortality, and it is crucial to underscore that these disparities are rooted 
171 
in historical and contemporary racism. 
172 
Given that one of National Grid's most substantial investment requests pertains to an area 
173 
proximate to 2,000 predominantly Black, low-income residents, we assert the compelling case for 
174 
the retirement of the 287 Maspeth facility, or the “Greenpoint Energy Center”.
