PRELIMINARY STATEMENT 
The City of New York (“City”) submits this Statement in Support of the Joint Proposal 
filed with the New York State Public Service Commission (“Commission”) on November 5, 2025 
in the Consolidated Edison Company of New York, Inc. (“Con Edison”) electric and gas rate cases.  
As demonstrated by the pre-filed testimony of the City and other parties in these proceedings, the 
City’s (and many of the other parties’) priority has been to ensure that electric and gas rates remain 
affordable as New Yorkers shoulder compounding increases in the cost of living.  However, as 
buildings and transportation electrify and system-wide electricity demand increases, and because 
of current economic conditions and the critical need to maintain safe and reliable infrastructure, a 
rate freeze was not possible.  The City appreciates that agreement was reached on a Joint Proposal 
that recommends rate increases over the next three years that are limited to approximately the rate 
of inflation, while advancing safety, reliability, and climate goals.  For this reason, and because of 
the other customer benefits included in the Joint Proposal, the City submits that the rate plans set

forth within the Joint Proposal satisfy the requirements of Public Service Law § 65(1) and should 
be adopted by the Commission. 
When viewed in totality and compared to the parties’ collective pre-filed testimony, the 
Joint Proposal is a better outcome for customers than if this matter had been fully litigated.  The 
foremost reason is that litigation would have produced rates for a single year only, whereas the 
Joint Proposal provides certainty and minimal volatility for a three-year period.  Importantly, the 
rate increases are comparatively small, much lower than the increases initially requested by Con 
Edison.  Additionally, customers will experience benefits that may not have been achieved in 
litigation, including expanded language offerings for outreach and education materials, 
continuation of enhanced customer protections from terminations during extreme hot and cold 
weather, greater transparency, and emphasis on investments and provision of service to 
Disadvantaged Communities.   
In New York City, electric service is a basic human need.  For this reason, Con Edison’s 
provision of safe, adequate, reliable, and resilient electricity is of paramount importance and 
essential to health, safety, and livability.  The recommended electric rate plan provides sufficient 
funding to ensure that Con Edison is able to continue to provide such service and address important 
public policy goals including, in particular, reducing greenhouse gas emissions.  At present, gas 
service also is critical to preserving the health and welfare of New York City residents and therefore 
existing gas service must remain safe and reliable.  The recommended gas rate plan maintains Con 
Edison’s focus on gas safety, provides sufficient funding to allow Con Edison to continue to 
provide safe and reliable service, and is structured to limit the expansion of, and continue to curtail,

adopt, without modifications, the Joint Proposal as the Company’s electric and gas rate plans for 
the next three years. 
PROCEDURAL HISTORY 
Con Edison filed requests for increases in its electric and gas revenue requirements and for 
new electric and gas rates on January 31, 2025 and supported its requests with extensive pre-filed 
direct testimony (“Initial Filing”).  The Company requested a $1.6 billion increase in its electric 
revenue requirement and a $440 million increase in its gas revenue requirement, both to take effect 
on January 1, 2026.  Those increases were estimated to result in an 11.40% overall bill increase 
for electric customers and a 13.3% bill increase for gas customers.  On April 10, 2025, the 
Company filed updates to the Initial Filing.   
On May 30, 2025, the City and 18 other parties submitted direct testimony in response to 
the Company’s Initial Filing and updates.  Notably, the parties’ direct testimony recommended 
significant reductions in requested revenue requirements, and Department of Public Service Staff 
(“Staff”) recommended that the Company’s gas revenue requirement be reduced.  Con Edison, the 
City, and seven other parties submitted rebuttal testimony on June 24, 2025. 
On June 24, 2025, the Company also filed a Notice of Impending Settlement Negotiations 
with the Secretary to the Commission.  On June 26, 2025, parties to these proceedings commenced 
settlement discussions regarding the potential to agree upon terms for multi-year rate plans.  After 
numerous negotiating sessions, several parties, including the City, were able to reach agreement

on compromise positions, as reflected in the Joint Proposal.  On November 5, 2025, the Joint 
Proposal was filed with the Commission. 
All settlement negotiations were conducted in accordance with the Commission’s 
Settlement Rules, 16 NYCRR § 3.9, and all parties received appropriate notice of all negotiating 
sessions.  The City’s understanding is that all interested parties were given an opportunity to 
participate in the negotiations.  This Statement in Support sets forth the basis and rationale for the 
City’s agreement with the Joint Proposal and recommendation to the Commission that it adopt the 
Joint Proposal’s terms and conditions, without modifications, as the Company’s electric and gas 
plans for the next three years.

the extent to which a joint proposal is supported by adverse parties; (b) whether the record for the

has a rational basis, balances the interests of customers and shareholders, and compares favorably 
with the probable outcome of litigation.1  The Commission should adopt the Joint Proposal because

1.

Presently, Con Edison’s electric and gas rates are barely or not affordable for many New 
York City residents and small businesses.  As noted above, and demonstrated by the pre-filed 
testimony of the City, Staff, and several other parties, minimizing as much as possible utility rate 
increases to reduce their contribution to the pressures that are driving up the cost of living and 
doing business in New York was the paramount goal in these rate cases.  With this goal in mind 
and, as a result of the pre-filed testimony and settlement negotiations, the increases initially 
proposed by Con Edison were substantially reduced.  These reductions will help prevent customers 
from being unduly burdened.

Low-Income Customers 
The Commission has long recognized the burdens energy costs can place on low-income 
customers, and it appropriately has adopted robust programs to provide aid to customers to attempt 
to keep their energy cost burdens at or below six percent of their household income.12   The Joint 
Proposal appropriately continues to ensure that there are no limitations on participation levels in 
the Company’s Energy Affordability Program, and the monthly discount levels will not be reduced 
as more customers join the Program.   
In order to facilitate enrollments in Con Edison’s Energy Affordability Program, and in 
furtherance of achieving the Commission’s six percent target, the City has worked closely with 
Con Edison and performed periodic matches of its Human Resources Administration records to 
Con Edison customer rolls.  These matches identify eligible customers, and after providing the 
customers notice and an opportunity to opt out, the customers are automatically enrolled in Con 
Edison’s Program.  Recently, the City has worked with Con Edison, DPS Staff, and others to 
simplify enrollment processes for eligible New Yorkers who are not automatically enrolled.  The 
Commission should continue to support these efforts and help ensure that all eligible customers 
are able to easily and quickly access the benefits provided by the Energy Affordability Program.  
Additionally, the Commission should support and encourage the utilities and other stakeholders to 
develop methods to identify and reduce energy cost burdens for households whose incomes are 
just above the eligibility threshold of the Energy Affordability Program.  For all of these efforts, 
the City submits that targeted approaches are superior to blunt approaches.  That is, providing

3. 
Customer Protections and Accessible Outreach 
At the urging of the City, the Joint Proposal contains several provisions regarding 
improvements to customer protections and the Company’s service to its customers.  First, New 
York City is very diverse, and many New York City residents/Con Edison customers do not use 
English as their primary language.  New York State has recognized the need to provide information 
to New Yorkers in multiple languages via the enactment of the Language Access Law, Executive 
Law § 202-a.  The State has identified the top 12 languages commonly spoken in New York, and 
State agencies must translate documents into all of those languages; they also must offer 
interpretation services for people who speak other languages.15  The City has recognized the same 
needs and enacted Local Law 30 of 2017, which requires city agencies to, among other things, 
develop language access implementation plans and translate their most commonly distributed 
documents into Spanish, Chinese, Russian, Bengali, Haitian, Korean, Arabic, Urdu, French, and 
Polish.16 
In the previous set of rate cases, the City was successful in getting Con Edison to provide 
certain materials in several languages.  In these proceedings, the City expanded on its prior 
successes.  The Joint Proposal requires Con Edison to provide outreach materials and other 
common documents in the same 12 languages used by State agencies.17  The Company also will 
ask prospective customers about their language preferences and create a new Language Assistance

6. Shareholder Incentives 
As discussed above, in setting rates, the Commission must balance customer and 
shareholder interests.  The primary value shareholders receive is a fair, market-based return on 
their investments.  For many years, this return was considered to be sufficient to satisfy 
shareholders’ interests.  More recently, the Commission decided to provide enhanced earnings 
opportunities and incentives to shareholders to influence their behavior. 
The City has long opposed many, but not all, of these incentives because they create extra 
burdens on customers who already have difficulty affording utility service.  Many of the incentives 
are designed in a manner that customer benefits are mostly conceptual, whereas shareholders 
recover actual dollars from customers.  That is, customers do not realize actual reductions in their 
utility bills, but they do experience higher bills because they must pay the shareholder incentives.  
Moreover, the targets associated with the shareholder incentives are sometimes set at very low 
levels of incremental performance, making them easily achievable. 


--- Page 21 ---
21 
 
As a general principle, positive shareholder incentives should be available, if at all, only 
for achieving superior levels of performance or stretch targets that require extra efforts by the 
Company.  Moreover, there should not be any costs imposed on or recovered from customers 
associated with achieving the targets.  If the incremental costs are fully recoverable, then 
shareholders bear no risks and should not be entitled to any rewards.27   
In the Joint Proposal, the shareholder incentives have been significantly curtailed.  This is 
a positive development for customers, and the reduced scope of incentives should not adversely 
impact Con Edison’s provision of safe and adequate service or hamper achievement of the State’s 
policy goals.  For example, there no longer is any need to reward Con Edison’s shareholders 
because its customers are buying energy efficient light bulbs.  With respect to managed charging 
for light duty electric vehicles, Con Edison was unable to demonstrate that its shareholder 
incentives contributed to any meaningful difference in customers’ behavior.  Therefore, there is no 
justification to continue those incentives. 
For gas safety, the existing positive shareholder incentives are proposed to be discontinued, 
but the negative revenue adjustments are continued to prevent backsliding and ensure the Company 
maintains a focus on gas safety.  Maintaining a safe gas system is a fundamental statutory 
obligation for Con Edison, and the City has always disagreed with the notion of rewarding 
shareholders for gas safety measures.  Therefore, the elimination of the positive incentives is a 
good development.
