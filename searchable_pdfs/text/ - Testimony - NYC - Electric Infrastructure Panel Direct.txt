Q. 
WHAT IS THE PURPOSE OF YOUR TESTIMONY? 
2 
A. 
Our testimony will cover three areas pertaining to Con Edison’s electric rate 
3 
filing. We comment on the testimony and associated exhibits, supplements, and 
4 
updates for Con Edison’s Electric Infrastructure and Operations Panel, or EIOP, 
5 
the Company’s Information Technology Panel, or IT Panel, and the Company’s 
6 
Shared Services Panel, or SSP. Our technical review of Con Edison’s filings is 
7 
limited to determining if the proposed projects and costs and allocations per 
8 
year, in our opinion, are prudent and properly documented.  
9 
While we recognize that the Company is not proposing a multi-year rate 
10 
plan, our testimony will discuss the Company’s Rate Year (“RY1”) capital plant 
11 
additions in the context of the Company’s recent historical investments, and its 
12 
proposed investments in the two subsequent years (“RY2” and “RY3”) 
13 
following the Rate Year. To consider these investments solely in the context of 
14 
the Rate Year is not meaningful because many of the Company’s investments 
15 
have been underway for several years and are forecasted to span multiple years 
16 
or, in some cases, decades.

A. 
The City fully appreciates the reality that Con Edison’s electric infrastructure is 
6 
aging; and that over the next 10 years, there will be a need for capital investment 
7 
throughout New York City and the Company’s service territory. However, 
8 
while Con Edison appears to be fulfilling its obligation to provide reliability and 
9 
resiliency, there appears to be inconsistencies with some programs and their 
10 
budgeting. A fair number of programs included in Con Edison’s capital 
11 
forecasts are ongoing, yet in this case the Company is requesting larger capital 
12 
budgets for such projects as compared to forecasts and actual project spending. 
13 
While year-over-year increases are expected, in some cases, the amount of 
14 
increased capital is significant. The projects and specific concerns highlighted 
15 
in this testimony represent the most significant examples of where this is 
16 
occurring without appropriate justification. 
17 
Furthermore, we believe that in some instances, the Company’s timing 
18 
for implementation of capital and staffing projects may be too accelerated and 
19 
would better serve ratepayers if postponed or delayed. In other instances, some 
20 
of the Company’s approaches to capital projects assume scenarios that do not 
21 
properly represent what is the most likely and the worst-case scenarios. 
22 
Generally, Con Edison's analyses are based on a future scenario that do not fully
