Q.
What obligation is the Company under to provide service and 
communications in languages other than English? 
A. 
New York State recognizes the importance of language accessibility.  
Executive Order 26, signed by New York Governor Cuomo on 
October 6, 2011, directed executive state agencies “that provide direct 
public services,” including the Department of Public Service, to offer 
document translation and interpretation services in the six most 
commonly spoken languages in New York other than English. The 
Executive Order recognizes that millions of New Yorkers do not speak 
English as their primary language and that “the public safety, health, 
economic prosperity, and general welfare of all New York residents is 
furthered by increasing language access to State programs and 
services.”​11  
 
Though the Company is not a state agency, it does provide an essential 
service, without which residents’ safety, health, economic prosperity, 
and general welfare are compromised.

Further, the Courts have recognized that utilities must not discriminate 
between customers.  Guy R. Brewer v. Brooklyn Union Gas Co. 
established that utility service must be rendered in a 
non-discriminatory manner: 
“… by its franchise …, its principal obligation is to serve the public, 
and as such, the [utility] is not permitted to choose its customers or to 
discriminate between customers by extending unreasonable conditions 
and preferences to some but must serve all on the basis of fairness and 
equality.​12

New York State's utility consumer rights, which are established by Article 2 
of the Public Service Law, called the Home Energy Fair Practices Act 
("HEFPA"), also recognizes this importance of language accessibility. The 
law requires that ”each utility bill to a residential customer shall provide, in 
clear and understandable form and language, the charges for service.” Further, 
HEFPA requires “every utility providing service to a county where, according 
to the most recent Federal census, at least 20 percent of the population 
regularly speaks a language other than English, shall, at the request of a 
customer residing in such county, send its messages on bills and notices in 
both English and such other language to such customer. At least once a year, 
every utility shall supply, to all residential customers in such county, a notice 
in such other language spoken regularly by at least 20 percent of the 
population in such county of the right to request messages on bills and notices 
in such other language.”​13
