Leonard Silverstein, being duly sworn, deposes and says: 
 
1. 
I am employed as a Utility Consumer Assistance 
Specialist for the New York State Department of Public Service.  
I have worked for the Department of Public Service since 2001.  
I work in the Office of Consumer Policy where my 
responsibilities include advocating positions on behalf of 
residential customers in utility rate proceedings, oversight of 
utility customer service operations, developing utility service 
quality incentive programs and evaluating utility low-income 
programs. 
2. 
I have testified before the Public Service Commission 
numerous times, in cases concerning National Fuel Gas in Cases 
04-G-1047 and 07-G-0141; St. Lawrence Gas in Cases 05-G-1635 and 
08-G-1392; Central Hudson in Cases 05-E-0934 & 05-G-0935, 08-E-
0887 & 08-G-0888, and 09-E-0588 & 09-G-0589; KeySpan in Cases 
06-G-1185 and 06-G-1186; Niagara Mohawk in Cases 08-G-0609 and 
10-E-0050; NYSEG and RG&E in Case 07-M-0906; Long Island Water 
in Case 07-W-0508; and New York Water in Case 09-W-0237.  The 
subjects of my previous testimony have included customer

service, service quality, low-income programs, and outreach and 
education. 
3. 
I offer this affidavit in support of a Joint Proposal 
filed with the Commission in the above captioned matter on 
February 22, 2013. 
4. 
Upon information and belief, the Brooklyn Union Gas 
Company d/b/a National Grid NY (KEDNY) has been operating under 
a rate plan established via various Commission Orders since 
2007.  See Joint Proposal at p. 2 (2007 Rate Plan).  The Joint 
Proposal was entered into to modify certain continuing 
provisions of the rate plan for an additional two years.  See 
Joint Proposal at p. 1. 
5. 
I have reviewed KEDNY’s existing customer service 
metrics and low income program that were part of the 2007 Rate 
Plan. 
6. 
The Joint Proposal contains some modifications to 
KEDNY’s customer service metrics to account for recent 
performance and prevent backsliding, and to KEDNY’s low income 
program to bring its spending level on such program closer to 
that of other New York utilities.  See Joint Proposal, §§ 5.1 
and 5.2. 
7. 
Upon information and belief, these changes would not 
have been made absent the Joint Proposal in that the Company had 
not filed for new rates or policies to go into effect on January 
1, 2013. 
Complaint Rate Threshold 
8. 
 The first modification reduces KEDNY’s Complaint Rate 
threshold from 1.7 to 1.1.  The Complaint Rate threshold is 
measured against the number of complaints the utility receives 
per 100,000 customers. 


--- Page 3 ---
9. 
The reduction from 1.7 to 1.1 is based on KEDNY’s 
recent historic performance with a cushion built in to allow for 
anomalous situations.  I calculated a three year average based 
on KEDNY’s filings for the years 2009 through 2011.  In each of 
those years, KEDNY reported its recent performance consistently 
under 1.0 complaints per 100,000 customers. 
10. 
Based on my experience, a 1.1 complaint rate threshold 
is reasonable in that it tightens the limit by which KEDNY would 
be exposed to a rate adjustment for inadequate performance but 
remains above the level at which KEDNY has performed such that 
any rate adjustment would result from declining performance when 
compared to current levels. 
Call Answer Rate Target 
11. 
The second modification to KEDNY’s customer service 
metrics increases the Call Answer Rate target from 52.9 percent 
to 59.0 percent.  The Call Answer Rate target is measured 
against the number of calls KEDNY’s customer service personnel 
answer within 30 seconds. 
12. 
The increase in the call answer rate target is based 
on the KEDNY’s recent historic performance.  I calculated a 
three year average based on KEDNY’s filings for the years 2009 
through 2011.  Based on those filings, KEDNY’s reported recent 
performance resulted in a three year average in excess of 60 
percent of calls answered within 30 seconds. 
13. 
Based on my experience, the call answer rate target 
increase to 59.0 percent of calls answered within 30 seconds is 
reasonable in that it tightens the limit by which KEDNY would be 
exposed to a rate adjustment for inadequate performance while 
allowing for some variability in its performance such that any 
rate adjustment would result from declining performance when 
compared to current levels.

Low Income Program 
14. 
Finally, KEDNY’s annual spending on its low income 
program is being increased from $7.4 million to $10.4 million.  
For both rate year six and rate year seven, the $10.4 million is 
to be budgeted as $10.2 million for low income discount credits, 
and $200,000 for one-time waivers of the reconnection fee for 
low income customers who have had service disconnected for non-
payment. 
15. 
One of the factors that Staff examines when 
determining what a New York utility should spend on low income 
assistance programs is the amount of dollars in the program as a 
percentage of the utility’s total revenues. 
16. 
KEDNY’s annual spend of $7.4 million on its low income 
program consisting of approximately 0.45 percent of total annual 
revenues was below the statewide average for gas programs.  The 
increase to $10.4 million, approximately 0.60 percent of total 
annual revenues, brings KEDNY’s annual spend closer to the 
statewide average for gas utility low income programs. 
17. 
Finally, section 5.3 of the Joint Proposal provides 
for an ongoing dialogue between Staff, KEDNY and any other 
interested parties as to the status of the company’s customer 
billing system.  This section results from a plan reflected 
KEDNY’s last Joint Proposal that the company was to replace or 
convert its billing system by December 31, 2012.  Staff expects 
that the discussion will focus on the historic context of why 
those plans existed, what part of the plans were completed or 
not and why, and an examination as to whether any issues 
presently exist that might result in either Staff 
recommendations or any KEDNY future action.
