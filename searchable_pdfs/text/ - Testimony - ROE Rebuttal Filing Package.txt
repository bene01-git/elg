Response: 
 
1.
Ms. Bulkley is not aware of the Commission making an explicit determination as to 
which Excel functions should be applied to the DCF model. 
2.
Attachment 1 contains an Excel spreadsheet that uses the IRR function instead of the 
XIRR function.  While the IRR function and XIRR function perform similar functions, 
the XIRR allows additional inputs as to the timing of cash flows.  Due to the reduced 
functionality of the IRR function relative to the XIRR function, the logic that pertains to 
the timing of cash flows cannot be used with the IRR function and therefore the IRR-
derived results in Attachment 1 are oversimplified and imprecise. 
3.
As described in response to part 1 above, Ms. Bulkley is unaware whether the Generic 
Finance Case RD relied on the IRR or XIRR formula.  As described in Ms. Bulkley’s 
direct testimony, page 11, lines 12-15, the models used in Ms. Bulkley’s analysis “extend 
the principles advanced in the RD in the GFP to best practices in financial analysis and 
current capital market conditions, as was contemplated in the RD.”  As described in 
response to part 4 below, the XIRR function, which reflects a more nuanced set of 
assumptions than the IRR function, represents best practices in financial analysis. 
4.
Ms. Bulkley is not aware of any regulatory commission making an explicit determination 
as to which Excel functions should be applied to the DCF model.  Many public utility 
commissions nationwide have accepted the practice of escalating the dividend yield 
within the Constant Growth Discounted Cash Flow model by one-half of the projected 
growth rate.1  This adjustment is intended to reflect the fact that utility companies tend to 
increase their dividend payments at different times throughout the year, so a mid-year 
convention is accepted.  The XIRR formula is intended to reflect a similar phenomenon, 
which assumes a mid-year convention for cash flow payments.   
The XIRR formula allows the analysis to better account for the timing of when cash 
flows (i.e., dividend payments) are available to investors in utility stock.  Utility stocks 
typically pay dividends on a quarterly basis throughout the year.  As constructed on 
Exhibit___(AEB-1), the XIRR formula reflects the assumption that dividends are paid, 
on average, halfway through the year (i.e., two dividends are paid in the first half of the 
year, and two dividends are paid in the second half of the year).  The IRR formula, in 
contrast, assumes that cash flows are only paid at the end of the period.  As shown in 
Attachment 1, that assumption within the IRR formula lowers the results of the DCF 
analysis by 11 to 21 basis points.
