II. 
Scope of Testimony 
14 
Q. 
What is the purpose of your testimony in this proceeding? 
15 
A. 
MAC was engaged by KeySpan Gas East Corporation d/b/a National Grid (“KEDLI” or 
16 
the “Company”) to conduct a depreciation analysis of its leak prone pipe (“LPP”) plant 
17 
assets.  The purpose of my testimony is to sponsor and describe the LPP depreciation 
18 
analysis conducted by MAC and present the necessary support for cost recovery 
19 
recommendations related to these assets.  
20 
 
21 
Q. 
Please describe the depreciation analysis that MAC performed. 
22 
A. 
The analysis involved reviewing the most recent seven years of historical data (2012-2018) 
23 
for certain plant accounts relating to the Company’s LPP.  KEDLI’s LPP assets consist of 
24 


--- Page 4 ---
Testimony of Paul M. Normand

3,458 miles of unprotected steel, cast or wrought iron, and pre-1985 vintage Alydl-A 
1 
plastic mains as of December 2018, as well as 4,129 copper services, 75,601 unprotected 
2 
steel or wrought iron services, and 51,940 Alydl-A and high density polyethylene 
3 
(“HDPE”) plastic services.  Those assets were installed by the Company some time ago 
4 
and are currently the subject of an accelerated replacement program to replace/retire all 
5 
LPP on the Company’s system over the next 20 years.  
6 
 
7 
Q. 
What were your responsibilities in connection with the depreciation study being 
8 
presented in this case? 
9 
A. 
I was responsible for planning the study, delineating and coordinating data collection, 
10 
ensuring the accuracy of the data, and properly reflecting any accounting adjustments.  
11 
Beyond data collection, I am also responsible for the performance and interpretation of 
12 
statistical analyses and the preparation of appropriate exhibits and schedules to reflect the 
13 
results of the studies.

16 
approved by the New York State Public Service Commission (“Commission”)?

The current depreciation accrual rates for these plant accounts, as shown on Exhibit ___ 
18

19 
depreciation rates established in Case 16-G-0058 were derived from a depreciation study 
20 
that used Company plant data through December 31, 2014.

Q. 
Why is the Company providing only a limited study of its LPP assets in this 
1 
proceeding? 
2 
A. 
As mentioned above, the Company’s depreciation rates were last reviewed and approved 
3 
only three years ago.  For most of the Company’s assets, there have been no meaningful 
4 
changes in the circumstances that affect the establishment of depreciation rates.  At the 
5 
same time, the fact that the Company has accelerated and will continue to accelerate the 
6 
replacement of LPP assets justifies an examination of whether the depreciation rates for 
7 
those assets should be adjusted at this time. 
8 
 
9 
Q. 
What Company facilities are considered LPP? 
10 
A. 
LPP facilities consist of all mains of 12-inches or less in diameter that are (i) cast and 
11 
wrought iron, (ii) unprotected (i.e., non-cathodically protected) steel pipe whether bare or 
12 
coated, and (iii) Alydl-A plastic.  LPP facilities also include bare steel, wrought iron, 
13 
copper, and Alydl-A and HDPE plastic services. 
14 
 
15 
Q. 
Please describe the Company’s program for accelerating the replacement and 
16 
removal of LPP facilities. 
17 
A. 
As described in more detail in the testimony of the Gas Infrastructure and Operations Panel, 
18 
the Company is undertaking an ongoing 25 year program to replace and retire all LPP 
19 
facilities on its gas system.  The program began in 2015; thus, the remaining term of the 
20 
program is approximately 20 years – at which time the Company anticipates that it will 
21 
have replaced all of its LPP facilities. 
22

A. 
The purpose of the depreciation analysis is to derive appropriate accrual rates reflective of 
1 
engineering judgment and current projections for the next 20 years, relative to the LPP 
2 
assets under study.  Because the Company is proceeding with a program through which 
3 
these LPP assets will be replaced and retired on an accelerated schedule, this analysis is 
4 
intended to produce depreciation rates that will reflect an annual amortization and recovery 
5 
of these LPP assets along with their retirement costs, commonly called net salvage, over 
6 
the proposed amortization period. 
7 
 
8 
Q. 
What procedures did you employ in compiling your depreciation studies? 
9 
A. 
First, MAC created the depreciation study databases that utilized the Company’s vintage 
10 
survivors by LPP account as of December 31, 2018.  KEDLI also provided the cost of 
11 
removal, annual retirements, annual mileage of retirements, and annual number of services 
12 
history for the most recent seven years (2012-2018) to complete MAC’s analysis of net 
13 
salvage for the LPP accounts.  See Exhibit ___ (PMN-4). 
14 
 
15 
Q. 
Why have you chosen the seven-year period for your analysis of LPP? 
16 
A. 
The Company began its program of retiring/replacing LPP on an accelerated basis in 2015 
17 
and has ramped up its annual retirement miles for these specific assets to an expected level 
18 
going into the future.  Because the primary costs incurred in connection with the program 
19 
are labor-related, the most recent period was deemed to be most representative of the costs 
20 
that are likely to be incurred in connection with future retirement efforts for these plant 
21 
assets.  Exhibit ___ (PMN-4) sets forth the three-year period of historical annual costs used 
22 
to derive our proposed recommendations.  MAC also examined data from the most recent 
23 
four and seven year periods.  MAC’s conclusion was that the three-year analysis was the 
24

most representative period.  It is important to recognize that this analysis is only applicable 
1 
to a portion of a few accounts associated with LPP as identified in the exhibits.  Exhibit

(PMN-5) sets forth the plant dollar amount for each LPP-related account and the dollar 
3 
portion related to LPP.

Q. 
What approach did you use in developing your proposed accrual rates? 
6 
A. 
The depreciation analysis utilized the period over which the LPP replacement program 
7 
would be in effect to determine the period of time over which these assets would be retired, 
8 
their cost of removal would be incurred, and their full cost recovery would be calculated 
9 
and effectuated.  This depreciation approach is necessary to fully recover all appropriate 
10 
costs for these facilities within the retirement period proposed. 
11

13 
A. 
No, we did not analyze each account with respect to rolling bands (10 years) and shrinking 
14 
bands (one year) for each polynomial (first, second, and third degree) as this was 
15 
unnecessary because we are proposing a 20 year amortization of all LPP plant accounts to 
16 
align with the Company’s replacement program.

Q. 
Do you believe it is reasonable for KEDLI to assess the increased depreciation rates 
2 
for its LPP plant accounts that you are recommending? 
3 
A. 
Yes.  If KEDLI does not assess the depreciation rates that MAC is recommending in these 
4 
proceedings, it will be unable to recover the full costs of its LPP facilities from the 
5 
customers who have been served by those facilities.  Instead, KEDLI will be required to 
6 
recover the costs of those LPP facilities from future customers.  It is more equitable to 
7 
assess cost responsibility for the LPP facilities to customers that are currently benefitting 
8 
from the operation of those LPP facilities.

The following is an explanation of each column of the Whole Life Schedule with Reserve Variance: 
 
 
 1. 
Column (1) presents the book balance for each account at the indicated date.  
 
 2.  
Column (2) labeled "DISP TYPE" is an SQ (Square survivor curve or straight line 
where the actual life is equal the average life) dispersion for each Leak Prone Pipe 
account.  
 
  
 3. 
Column (3) indicates the average dollar service life in years which in this study is a 
20-year amortization period. 
 
4. 
Column (4) is the unadjusted whole life accrual rate developed by dividing unity by 
Column (3), and expressing the quotient as a percentage.  
 
5. 
Column (5) is the whole life accrual with no salvage adjustment, based upon the 
average service life associated with each investment group.  These accruals are 
developed by multiplying Column (1) by Column (4).  
 
6. 
Column (6) is the percent net salvage expectation; net salvage equals gross   salvage 
minus removal cost.  
 
7. 
Column (7) is the salvage factor, derived by subtracting the (signed) net salvage ratio 
from unity; e.g., a salvage factor of 1.10 is the result of 1.00 minus an expected net 
salvage ratio of minus 0.10; i.e., 1.00 - (-0.10) = 1.10.  
 
8. 
Column (8) is the whole life accrual rate, reflecting adjustment for net salvage 
expectations; it is developed by multiplying Column (4) by Column (7), and 
expressing the product as a percentage.  
 
  
 9. 
Column (9) is the whole life accrual, adjusted for net salvage expectations.  It is 
developed by multiplying Column (8) by Column (1).  
 
10. 
Column (10) shows indicated depreciation reserves, unadjusted for net salvage 
expectations.  
 
11. 
Column (11) is the indicated depreciation reserve, adjusted for net salvage   
expectations by multiplying Column (10) by Column (7).
