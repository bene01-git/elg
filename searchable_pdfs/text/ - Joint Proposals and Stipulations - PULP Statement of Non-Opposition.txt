PRELIMINARY STATEMENT 
 
The Public Utility Law Project of New York, Inc. (“PULP”) hereby submits this 
Statement of Non-Opposition of the Joint Proposal (“JP”) filed with the New York Public 
Service Commission (“PSC” or “Commission”) by Department of Public Service (“DPS”) Staff 
(“Staff”) on November 5, 2025. 
 
PULP has taken on a position of non-opposition on the JP. The settlement secures 
important enhancements and improvements aligned with PULP’s core priorities, including 
stronger customer service protections during extreme weather, expanded language access, 
protections for Limited English Proficient (“LEP”) customers, protections for electric heat 
customers, protections for tenants residing in multiple dwellings, and more. PULP does 
recognize that cost impacts, such as the 9.4% Return on Equity (“ROE”) and the three-year 
delivery rate increases, will be difficult for customers to afford, especially during these 
unprecedented times where funding for vital federal low-income assistance programs, such as the 
Home Energy Assistance Program (“HEAP”) and the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance 
Program (“SNAP”), are uncertain. Therefore, PULP’s non-opposition reflects both the 
meaningful progress achieved and the challenges that remain.

STANDARD OF REVIEW 
 
According to the Commission’s Settlement Guidelines, “[a]ll Commission decisions, 
including those pertaining to proposed settlements must be, and appear to be just, reasonable, 
and in the public interest.”1 The elements considered in determining the “public interest” include: 
1. Consistency with the law and regulatory economic, social and environmental State and 
Commission policies; 2. Whether the terms of the joint proposal compare favorably with the 
likely result of a fully litigated case and produce a result within the range of reasonable 
outcomes; 3. Whether the joint proposal fairly balances the interests of ratepayers, investors, and 
the long-term soundness of the utility; and 4. Whether the joint proposal provides a rational basis 
for the Commission’s decision. Additionally, Commissioners are guided in their assessment by 
two additional factors. First, the completeness of the record, and second, whether the settlement 
is contested.2

I. 
The Joint Proposal Represents Monthly Bill Impacts and an ROE that are lower 
than the Company’s Original Proposal. 
 
In the Company’s original filing, dated January 31, 2025, the Company requested a 
delivery revenue increase of $1.6 billion on the electric side and $440 million on the gas side3, 
with bill impacts of 11.4% total bill increase on the electric and 13.3% total bill increase on the 
gas for a one-year rate plan.4 
a. Monthly Bill Impacts 
In the JP, the delivery revenue increases for the electric5 and gas6 are included for a 
three-year rate plan, with impacts presented on both a “unshaped” and “shaped” basis. 
According to the JP, “shaped rate changes” are designed to produce consistent bill impacts for 
each rate year (as opposed to “levelized” rate changes which produce consistent dollar amount 
rate changes each year).7 Based on this “shaped” design, electric revenue increases will be 
$234 million in Rate Year 1, $409.7 million in Rate Year 2 and $421.1 million in Rate Year 3, 
producing 4.3% – 4.4% delivery bill impacts each year.8 For gas, revenue increases will 
amount to $27.5 million in Rate Year 1, $68.8 million in Rate Year 2 and $70.3 million in Rate 
Year 3, producing 2.8% delivery bill impacts each year.9 Annual total bill impacts will be 2.8% 
on the electric side, and 2.0% for gas.10 
Residential bill impacts will be incrementally offset to the extent that overall enrollment 
in the Company’s energy affordability program (“EAP”) increases from current levels. That 
being said, Rate Year 1 percentage electric delivery bill impacts for customers already enrolled

b. ROE 
In the Company’s original filing, a 10.1% ROE was requested.13 The authorized ROE

II. 
The Joint Proposal Includes Several Meaningful Customer-Focused Provisions 
that Align with PULP’s Core Priorities in this Rate Case. 
a. The JP Includes Additional Provisions Related to Language Access 
 
In PULP’s direct testimony, we raised many concerns involving language access at the 
Company.15 PULP wants to ensure that Limited English Proficient (“LEP”) customers are on 
more even ground with customers who speak English. To that end, the JP has an array of 
provisions that will assist the Company’s communication with their LEP population. First, the 
Company has agreed to expand language offerings in various forms (i.e. brochures, direct mail, 
flyers etc.) in 12 languages other than English.16 These languages will include Spanish, Russian, 
Chinese, Korean, Polish, Bangali (Bangla), Haitian-Creole, Urdu, Yiddish, Arabic, French, and 
Italian.17

b. The JP Includes Additional Provisions Related to Customer Terminations 
 
The JP includes provisions that will support customers who are facing termination of 
services as well as provisions to assist in situations where an entire building is at risk of 
termination due to the landlord’s non-payment. PULP flagged these issues in our direct 
testimony, where we discuss the confusion and fear tenants have when they are in a situation 
where their landlord has not paid the utility bill and the entire building may be facing shut off.25 
Essentially, in these situations, the tenants are caught in the middle with limited options for 
preventing the loss of service.26 
In light of these issues, the Company has agreed to add a QR code to the “Urgent Notice” 
public posting for multiple dwellings that will take a tenant to a website with resources that can 
support tenants.27 The website will include information on who to contact for assistance as well 
as links to Housing Preservation and Development’s (“HPD”) and the Office of Temporary and 
Disability Assistance’s (“OTDA”) websites for information on emergency housing services.28 
The Company has also agreed to conduct annual outreach to associations and 
organizations representing the interests of landlords to discuss any problems with landlord 
arrears, service terminations, and solutions.29 PULP considers this provision to be a necessary 
step in working with landlords and building owners on the issue of service termination before 
tenants become stuck in the middle of any situation that could lead to their loss of utility service.

c. The JP Includes Provisions to Assist Customers with Electric Heat Re-Coding 
 
In PULP’s direct testimony, we flagged an issue with the coding of accounts to electric 
heat.32 Ultimately, PULP was, and is, concerned that low-income customers who would 
otherwise qualify for the Low-Income Home Energy Assistance Program (“LIHEAP”) full 
benefit were only receiving the “renters LIHEAP”, considered a “non-heating” grant which is 
significantly lower than the full regular benefit under LIHEAP and does not go to the utility 
company.33 We are grateful that the Company, and other parties, also considered this to be an 
important issue and that they have worked to finalize provisions ultimately helping low-income 
customers in the service territory.

d. The JP Includes Weather-Related Customer Protections 
 
Under the JP, the Company will continue to not perform service terminations for non-
payment for residential customers on days where the heat index is forecasted by the National 
Weather service to reach 90 degrees or higher, as well as one calendar day before and two 
calendar days after the extreme heat event.42 The Company has also agreed to use the highest 
heat index from the National Weather Stations at Central Park, LaGuardia Airport and JFK 
Airport.43 Adding additional weather stations to measure the heat index will help protect 
customers from extreme heat days. 
e. The JP Includes Additional Provisions Related to the Energy Affordability 
Program (“EAP”) 
 
The JP includes provisions related to the EAP, including provisions on outreach. First, the 
Company has agreed to amend its electric and gas tariffs to eliminate both the EAP electric and 
gas reconnection fee waiver program and to no longer subject those enrolled in EAP to meter
