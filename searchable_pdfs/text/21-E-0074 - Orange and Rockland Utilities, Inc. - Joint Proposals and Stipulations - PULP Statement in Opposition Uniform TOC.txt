II. Overview of the Joint Proposal 
A. Benefits of the Joint Proposal 
 
1. CLCPA Related Benefits 
The Company has agreed to institute several environmentally-focused efforts set forth in 
Appendix 20, some of which break new ground for the utility and are laudable. Other measures 
promised in Appendix 20 are either somewhat vague (e.g., lack of Non-Pipe Alternatives 
(“NPA”) specificity), lag behind measures taken by their sister utilities in 2020 and 2021, are 
tied to two-year-old Con Edison measures or use ratepayer funds to pay above-market rates for 
non-proven theoretically “greener” commodities (e.g., “certified natural gas”). Consequently, 
while the Company has made steps forward in providing CLCPA-related benefits, PULP 
believes there is room for the Company to stretch its goals by, for example, agreeing to match 
and apply any measures agreed to by its parent, Con Edison, in its 2022 rate case and settlement. 
 
B. Standard of Review 
 
The standard of review used by the Commission in reviewing settlement agreements is 
found in the Settlement Guidelines.5 The Settlement Guidelines require that all decisions must be 
just and reasonable, and in the public interest. Determining whether the terms of the Joint 
Proposal are in the public interest involves substantive consideration of the following:  
 
1. consistency with the law and regulatory, economic, social and environmental State and 
Commission policies;  
2. whether the terms of the Joint Proposal compare favorably with the likely result of a fully 
litigated case and produce a result within the range of reasonable outcomes;  
3. whether the Joint Proposal fairly balances the interests of ratepayers, investors, and the 
long-term soundness of the utility; and,  
4. whether the Joint Proposal provides a rational basis for the Commission’s decision.6  
 
The Commission must also give weight to the fact of whether a settlement reflects  
an agreement between normally adversarial parties.7

L. Miscellaneous Provisions 
 
1. Language Access 
 
Since its enactment, the Home Energy Fair Practices Act (“HEFPA”) has protected 
consumers. That protection has been made possible by the ability of utility consumers to read the 
documents provided to them by the utilities, and to understand offers or negotiations between them 
and the utilities’ employees, or DPS staff. Acknowledging the importance of clear communications 
with consumers in New York State where more than 100 languages are spoken, 16 NYCRR 
11.17(b) provides the following: 
 
"(b) Billing information in non-English language. Every utility providing service to a 
county where, according to the most recent Federal census, at least 20 percent of the population 
regularly speaks a language other than English, shall, at the request of a customer residing in 
such county, send its messages on bills and notices in both English and such other language to 
such customer. At least once a year, every utility shall supply to all residential customers in 
such county, a notice in such other language spoken regularly by at least 20 percent of the 
population in such county of the right to request messages on bills and notices in such 
other language."47 
 
In this rate case, PULP provided testimony based on U.S. Census Data that showed that 
17% of the population of Rockland County speak Haitian Creole as their first language.

IV. Conclusion 
 
As noted above in several areas, PULP opposes the Joint Proposal generally and 
specifically in several areas including agreement upon an unsupported and excessive ROE and 
insufficient use of EDFIT to moderate ratepayer impacts, failure to properly use funds collected 
pursuant to the 2016 Order in Case 14-M-0565, and language access.  
PULP does wish to acknowledge the Company’s flexibility and attempts to move forward 
on CLCPA/greenhouse gas reduction consistent with State policy. Overall though, we find the 
Joint Proposal does not sufficiently acknowledge the economic harms wrought in the Company’s 
service territory during Years 1 and 2 of the COVID-19 pandemic, nor does it sufficiently account 
for the economic impact of the selected rate increases, ROE and other added costs of the case upon 
small businesses, its residential customers, and particularly seniors and low-income households. 
 
Consequently, we respectfully request the Commission to reject the JP as submitted and to 
modify it to lower ratepayer impacts, expand language access during this vital period where arrears 
must be resolved equitably and without undue delay, and to instruct the Company to reform its 
low-income affordability program into consistency with Commission orders in Case 14-M-0565.
