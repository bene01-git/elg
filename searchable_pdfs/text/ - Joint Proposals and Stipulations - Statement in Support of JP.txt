In accordance with the Presiding Administrative Law Judge’s “Ruling On 
Schedule” issued February 6, 2013 in these proceedings, The Brooklyn Union Gas 
Company d/b/a National Grid NY (“KEDNY” or the “Company”) hereby submits this 
Statement in Support of the Joint Proposal (“JP”) by and among the Company, the New 
York State Department of Public Service Staff (“Staff”), and Queens Gas Consumers, 
dated February 22, 2013.  The JP is the product of negotiations among the Company, 
Staff and other parties and resolves all aspects of an investigation undertaken by Staff of 
the earnings computation and other aspects of KEDNY’s existing rate plan.  The JP 
provides significant benefits to KEDNY and its customers by continuing for at least 
another two years a period of long term base rate stability,1 and enabling the Company to 
provide customers enhanced benefits, including, inter alia, increased investment in gas 
infrastructure, greater low income customer discounts and credits, enhanced gas safety 
and customer service performance targets, and modifications to the Company’s current 
earnings sharing mechanisms.  For the reasons discussed more fully below, the Company 
requests that the Commission adopt the JP without modification.

A. 
The Standard Of Review 
 
The JP is the result of complex and difficult negotiations among normally 
adversarial parties.  The negotiations fully complied with the Commission’s settlement 
rules34 and provided all parties the opportunity to participate.  The Commission’s 
“Procedural Guidelines for Settlements”35 set forth the following criteria for determining 
whether a joint proposal is in the public interest: 
 
(1) 
A desirable settlement should strive for a balance among (i) protection of 
the ratepayers, (ii) fairness to investors, and (iii) the long term viability of 
the utility; should be consistent with sound environmental, social and 
economic policies of the Agency and the State; and should produce results 
that were within the range of reasonable results that would likely have 
arisen from a Commission decision in a litigated proceeding. 
 
 
(2) 
In judging a settlement, the Commission shall give weight to the fact that a 
settlement reflects the agreement by normally adversarial parties. 
 
 
As discussed herein, based on these guidelines, the JP meets the public interest 
standard.  The JP is the result of comprehensive negotiations among parties with diverse 
interests, including the Company, Staff, Queens Gas Consumers (representing large 
multi-family customers) and other interested parties.  The Commission should give 
considerable weight to the fact that the JP is supported by parties having normally 
adverse interests.  In addition, as demonstrated below, the compromises memorialized in 
the JP produce results that are both objectively reasonable and comparable to results 
approved by the Commission in recent base rate proceedings involving other utilities.

B. 
The JP Is The Product Of Negotiations Between Adversarial 
Parties And Produces A Result That Is Grounded In 
Commission Precedent And Objectively Reasonable 
 
The modifications to the Rate Plan set forth in the JP are the product of 
negotiations among the parties to this proceeding.  Notably, the Company has agreed to: 
(i) 
significantly increase capital spending above the levels reflected in the 
previous Rate Years and eliminate its City/State Construction deferral mechanism, 
without a corresponding increase in base delivery rates;  
(ii) 
the use for ratemaking purposes of a 9.4 percent return on common equity; 
and 
(iii) 
modifications to the current Rate Plan’s revenue sharing mechanism that 
significantly reduce the threshold at which the Company begins sharing revenue for the 
benefit of customers. 
These concessions, which have been achieved without the need for litigation, 
result in a Rate Plan that is consistent with Commission precedent.  For example, the 9.4 
percent return on equity is objectively reasonable and close to the returns recently 
adopted by the Commission as part of multi-year rate plans for similar utilities.36  
Similarly, the use of a ratemaking capital structure that reflects a 48 percent equity 
component for ratemaking purposes is consistent with numerous recent Commission 
decisions.37
