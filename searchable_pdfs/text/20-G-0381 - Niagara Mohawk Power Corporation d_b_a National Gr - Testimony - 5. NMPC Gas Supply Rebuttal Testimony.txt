III.
Non-Core Daily Balancing Program
1 
Q.
Why is the Company proposing to close its non-core daily balancing
2 
program to new customers and offer a new core daily balancing
3 
service?
4 
A.
The primary goal of the Company’s proposal is to achieve better long-term
5 
supply planning and ensure system reliability.  Non-core daily balanced
6 
customers are not assigned a portion of the Company’s capacity either
7 
through their ESCO or as a direct customer.  This means that the
8 
Company’s gas supply portfolio, which is developed to meet the
9 
requirements of all firm core customers, does not account for the gas
10 
supply needs of customers who (i) have an alternate fuel source or (ii) are
11 
daily balanced even though they have firm delivery service from the
12 
Company and may not have an alternate fuel source.  As the SGRP
13 
acknowledges, there are capacity constraints affecting parts of the
14 
Company’s service territory.  Therefore, if these customers or their ESCOs
15 
fail to deliver adequate supplies to meet the demands of these customers,
16 
the Company needs the ability to interrupt critical service to customers that
17 
have failed to supply the Company with sufficient quantities of gas to
18 
ensure reliability on its gas system.  In practice, however, if an ESCO fails
19 
to deliver sufficient gas to the Company’s city gate on behalf of its non-
20 
core daily balanced customers, the Company cannot identify the non-core
21

customer/customers to which the shortfall should be attributed.  Therefore, 
1 
the Company cannot terminate any non-core customer in such a pool even 
2 
if the Company may be entitled to do so, and the Company must utilize its 
3 
own capacity to safeguard its system reliability.  In essence, the Company 
4 
is at risk for supply shortfalls by non-core customers, which could 
5 
jeopardize reliability during periods of peak demand.  The Company’s 
6 
proposal seeks to mitigate this risk going forward.

Q.
Please address the SGRP’s first concern (at 11) that the Company’s
9 
proposal does not comply with existing Commission policy.
10 
A.
The SGRP argues that the Company’s proposal “tacitly redefines non-core
11 
customers as core and does not comply with the existing Commission
12 
policy established in Case 07-G-0299, in the “Order on Capacity Release
13 
Programs (Capacity Release Order), issued on August 30, 2007.”  As noted
14 
by the SGRP, the Capacity Release Order required gas utilities to establish
15 
a mandatory capacity assignment program for core customers only.  The
16 
stated goal of the mandatory capacity assignment was to “ensure the
17 
continued reliability of the natural gas system in New York State, while
18 
preventing the inefficiencies that result when duplicative capacity assets
19 
are held by the LDCs to backstop marketer supplied capacity.”  (Capacity
20 
Release Order at 5).  As noted above, failure of non-core customers

1 
jeopardize system reliability.  The Capacity Release Order acknowledges 
2 
that the Company, as system operator, must assure proper operation of its 
3 
system.  (Id., at 3).  Because the Company cannot identify which non-core 
4 
customer’s usage creates the shortfall, the Company cannot in practice

alternatives to traditional infrastructure are still under development.  The

Panel’s proposal, if approved, will allow the Company to identify the 
16 
needs of all of its firm customers and efficiently develop solutions that 
17 
meet these criteria.

customer gas supply requirements can be met for the upcoming winter, it
