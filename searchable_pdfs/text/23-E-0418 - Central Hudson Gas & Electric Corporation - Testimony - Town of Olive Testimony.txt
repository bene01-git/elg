Commission deny the proposed electric rate increase until the forensic audit of CH’s billing 
14 
system implementation is completed.

21 
faith to keep the lights on.  As a municipality the Town of Olive has a legal and ethical 
22 
obligation to its residents to provide accurate accounting of its paid bills. The Town staff

including a gas delivery charge increase of 29.2%.

Q. What is the purpose of the Town of Olive's testimony as regards the proposed gas rate 
4

5

The purpose of the Town of Olive’s testimony is to request that the Public Service 
6 
Commission deny the proposed gas rate increase, to reduce CH’s financial return on its 
7

10 
Q. 
What climate related concerns does the Town of Olive have when it comes to Central 
11 
Hudson and the rate case? 
12 
A. 
 
Despite the clear scientific evidence in favor of terminating the supply and 
13 
burning of fossil fuels, CH continues to impact our communities in the Hudson Valley 
14 
with their continuing operation of natural gas supply.  The Town of Olive has 
15 
encountered significant direct impacts of climate change, including severe flooding in the 
16 
hamlet of Boiceville which had serious adverse consequences for our only local 
17 
supermarket – it was closed for several months -- and for other flooded businesses, and 
18 
for housing in the hamlet, causing hardship to the residents of the Town.  In addition 
19 
there has been significant stream bed and bank erosion requiring extensive and expensive 
20 
remediation.  Indirect consequences of climate change in the Town of Olive include 
21 
releases by New York City-DEP of turbid water from the Ashokan Reservoir causing 
22 
impacts as far away as those towns that take their drinking water from the Hudson River.  
23 
These direct and indirect impacts of climate change are exacerbated by carbon dioxide 
24

emissions from the burning of fossil fuels by Central Hudson’s customers and by leaks of 
1 
methane from the natural gas system operated by Central Hudson.

Q. Does the Town of Olive have any concerns with whether Central Hudson is incentivized 
4 
to continue and even expand its Gas Operations? 
5 
A.  
 
Yes.  As indicated by CH’s filings with the Public Service Commission on July 31, 
6 
2023, CH’s Electric Operations are less profitable than its Gas Operations.  See Exhibit 
7 
___ (SSS-01) which shows that for the year ended March 31, 2023 CH’s Electric 
8 
Operations generated Regulatory Operating Income of $74.5 million on Revenues of 
9 
$436.5 million (an Operating Margin of 17.1%).  By contrast, Exhibit ___ (SSS-02) shows 
10 
that for the year ended March 31, 2023 CH’s Gas Operations generated Regulatory 
11 
Operating Income of $33.0 million on Revenues of $139.8 million (an Operating Margin 
12 
of 23.6%).  And, despite commitments in the 2020 Rate case not to continue advertising 
13 
its Gas Operations, CH continues to do so.  (See CH Gas Operations advertising on 
14 
Facebook from September 15, 2023 – Exhibits ___ and ___ (SSS-03 and SSS-04)) 
15 
 
16 
Q. Does the Town of Olive have any proposals to disincentivize CH’s Gas Operations?  
17 
A.  
 
Yes.    The Town of Olive proposes that from and including this rate case 
18 
forward: 
19 
a. 
CH be permitted to no more than break-even at the Operating Income line on its gas 
20 
revenues. 
21 
b. 
CH pay for out of its shareholder funds for any gas capital expenditures beyond 
22 
immediate repairs (for example replacement of leak-prone pipe). 
23

c. 
CH agree not to spend any more marketing, promotions or advertising for gas sales 
1 
and instead commit an agreed-upon amount each year through 2030 for such 
2 
marketing for beneficial electrification. 
3 
d. 
For any PSC-approved expenditures by CH on beneficial electrification (heat pump 
4 
subsidies, EV and EV charging subsidies, non-fossil fuel-based Thermal Energy 
5 
Networks, induction stove and cooking utensil subsidies etc.) CH be permitted a 
6
