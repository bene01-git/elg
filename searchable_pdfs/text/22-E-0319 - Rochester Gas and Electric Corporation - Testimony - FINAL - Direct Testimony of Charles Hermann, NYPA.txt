BACKGROUND 
18 
Q.  
What enables municipalities to purchase their street light assets from utility 
19 
companies? 
20 
A.  
In November 2015, New York’s Governor signed into law Chapter 495 of the 2015 Laws 
21 
of New York, which added Section 70-a to the Public Service Law and established 
22

procedures for the transfer of ownership of street lighting systems to municipalities or 
1 
other government entities. In an October 2016 order in Cases 15-E-0745, et al. (“2016 
2 
Street Light Transfer Order”) the New York Public Service Commission (“PSC”) 
3 
approved tariff changes for all of New York State’s investor-owned utilities, including 
4 
the Companies, that implemented the new street lighting ownership transfer procedures. 
5 
This regulatory change opened new opportunities for municipalities to achieve both 
6 
energy and cost reductions that did not exist previously. 
7 
Q. 
Why do municipalities opt to purchase street light systems from utility companies? 
8 
A. 
Municipalities mainly choose to buy the street light systems in their jurisdictions because 
9 
they can achieve savings from both the reduced energy usage associated with conversion 
10 
to LED luminaires and reduced facilities charges associated with maintaining and 
11 
operating the street light system.  
12 
Q. 
Are there other factors driving street light asset sales?  
13 
A.  
Yes. Purchasing their street lights allows municipalities to have control over their 
14 
required maintenance and ensure more streamlined communications with constituents 
15 
regarding street light maintenance issues. Furthermore, the municipality can achieve cost 
16 
savings by competitively bidding the maintenance work to reduce charges that otherwise 
17 
would be paid to the utility. Some municipalities also are interested in maintaining a 
18 
uniform or unique appearance. Purchasing the street lights gives them the flexibility to 
19 
install various styles of street lights that would not be offered by the utility. 
20 
Q.  
What role does NYPA play in the conversion of customer-owned street light assets 
21 
to LED?

A.  
The Governor’s 2018 State of the State Address directed NYPA to lead the Smart Street 
1 
Lighting NY Program with a goal to reduce energy consumption from street lights across 
2 
the state and expand the use of beneficial smart street lighting technologies. NYPA 
3 
implements the Smart Street Lighting NY Program as a one-stop-shop for customers to 
4

5 
NYPA’s technical expertise in design, procurement, and construction to purchase and 
6

7

8 
also offers upfront financing for street light projects, where financing costs are offset by 
9 
the cost savings created by the lower maintenance costs and reduced energy use of LED 
10 
street lights. In the large majority of cases, the cost savings generated by the project 
11 
exceed the maintenance and energy costs prior to project implementation, resulting in 
12 
lower costs to the municipality.  
13 
Q.  
Are there additional benefits to the Smart Street Lighting NY Program?  
14 
A.  
Yes. As the Commission recognized in the 2016 Street Light Transfer Order, the 
15 
conversion of street lights from high-intensity discharge luminaires to LED luminaires 
16 
can assist New York in achieving its long-term clean energy objectives through reduced 
17 
energy usage and the corresponding greenhouse gas (“GHG”) emission reductions. 
18 
Therefore, achieving the Smart Street Lighting NY Program goals will support the 40 
19 
percent reduction of GHG emissions by 2030, based on 1990 levels, that is mandated in 
20 
the Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act.

III. 
SMART STREET LIGHTING TECHNOLOGY 
1 
Q.  
In addition to LED conversions that create energy savings, what services can be 
2 
provided to customers through street lighting modifications?  
3 
A.  
Smart street lighting, or “smart city” technologies, provide numerous other benefits and 
4 
services beyond the direct energy savings that arise from converting traditional 
5 
luminaires to LED. One example of these technologies is the network lighting control 
6 
(“NLC”) node.  
7 
Q.  
What is an NLC node? 
8 
A.  
NLC nodes are modules that take the place of photosensors installed in the street light 
9 
fixture. They allow the street lighting customer to control lighting levels—including 
10 
dimming capabilities—and reduce energy consumption.  
11 
Q. 
What benefits are associated with the adaptive dimming control capabilities of the 
12 
NLC nodes?  
13 
A.  
NLC nodes allow municipalities to reduce energy consumption through dimming. 
14 
Without network lighting controls, street lights can only be turned off or operated at full 
15 
brightness. The Seattle Study estimated that street light dimming strategies could enhance 
16 
the energy savings achieved through LED conversion by as much as 25% compared to 
17 
pre-LED energy usage levels, depending on the dimming strategy utilized, reducing costs 
18 
for municipal taxpayers. Exhibit__(CH-1) at page i. The Dallas Study, meanwhile, 
19 
showed energy savings of 30-40% from LEDs and an additional 20-30% in operational 
20 
savings with smart controls like NLC nodes. Exhibit__(CH-2) at page 15. Importantly, 
21 
dimming strategies enable energy savings without impacting overall lighting 
22 


--- Page 8 ---
Direct Testimony of Charles Hermann 
New York Power Authority 
 Cases 22-E-0317, et al. 
Page 7 of 14 
 
 
 
performance. The Seattle Study demonstrated that, based on field tests with new LED 
1 
lamps, dimming does not significantly impact object detection by the naked eye, and 
2 
even dimming by 50 percent did not significantly reduce object detection in dry 
3 
pavement conditions. Exhibit__(CH-1) at pp. ii, 33-42, 59, 69. Dimming also extends the 
4 
useful life of the LEDs without sacrificing lighting quality or public safety by reducing 
5 
the heat generated by the electrical components used to control the LED. These 
6 
components typically fail first, so dimming extends the life of these components.  
7 
Larger scale dimming strategies to reduce street light energy use also benefit 
8 
customers. For example, additional energy savings can be realized by reducing lighting to 
9 
a greater extent during low-traffic, early morning hours, or by reducing lights in parks 
10 
when they are not in use. Strategic dimming of high wattage luminaires can also be 
11 
utilized for public safety purposes, for example by brightening lights during police or first 
12 
responder activities or blinking or flashing lights to warn the public of natural disasters 
13 
and/or direct residents away from danger.  
14 
Q. 
Can NLC nodes be used to measure energy usage when dimming strategies are 
15 
implemented?  
16 
A.  
Yes, NLC nodes can serve a dual purpose. In addition to allowing municipalities to 
17 
monitor and control their energy usage by controlling the light, they also are high-quality 
18 
electric meters that measure energy usage and record data in real-time. Ultimately, NLC 
19 
nodes can be used as a cost-effective way to accurately measure energy savings from 
20 
dimming. 
21

Q.  
Do you have any recommendations on a street light dimming offering?  
5 
A.  
Yes. The most accurate way to reflect the energy savings associated with street light 
6 
dimming strategies is to utilize the NLC node to meter street lights, with associated 
7 
metered rates in the tariff. However, currently, no NLC nodes have been approved for 
8 
metering purposes in New York State. The Pilots undertaken with the two other 
9 
jurisdictional utilities will provide data to support utilizing the NLC nodes for metering 
10 
purposes.  
11 
As an interim step while the Pilots are ongoing, I recommend that the Companies 
12 
establish adaptive operating schedules that reflect street light dimming capabilities and 
13 
enable customers to achieve energy cost savings from these capabilities. These schedules 
14 
function by identifying equivalent hours of operation for certain levels of street light 
15 
dimming (e.g., 80%, 50%, 30%). The equivalent hours of operation can then be used with 
16 
the total device wattage to estimate overall energy use for billing purposes. The NLC 
17 
node software can be utilized to help validate the schedules.  
18 
A schedule-based approach is an appropriate interim step that would allow 
19 
customers to realize savings associated with street light dimming without the use of a 
20 
meter while the necessary metering testing and certification of the NLC nodes takes 
21 
place. National Grid has utilized this approach in Massachusetts and Rhode Island and

Q.  
What are the benefits of establishing a tariff that takes street light dimming into 
5 
account? 
6 
A.  
A dimming tariff will enable customers to be billed for energy usage that reflects the 
7 
savings associated with their dimming strategies. Creating a dimming tariff or schedule 
8 
also will allow dimming to occur in a predictable fashion that utility schedulers can rely 
9 
on and use; the alternative would be an administratively burdensome process with each 
10 
customer adopting their own strategies and schedules. Established tariffs would also 
11 
encourage the adoption and proliferation of dimming by municipalities, which would 
12 
allow them to reduce costs and be good environmental stewards. 
13 
Q. 
Do you have specific recommendations on the adaptive operating schedules that 
14 
should be implemented? 
15

Yes. The Companies should establish operating schedules based on set levels of street 
16

street lighting energy usage between dusk and dawn. After one year of operating 
18 
experience the Companies should initiate a collaborative effort with municipalities, 
19 
NYPA, and other interested parties to determine if the adaptive operating schedules 
20 
should be modified based on the municipalities’ experiences over the year with different 
21 
dimming strategies. I recommend maximum flexibility in setting the schedules, including
