6 
Q.

7 
requirements. 
8 
A. 
As stated in the Panel’s direct testimony at pages 24 to 25, the Company did 
9 
not include in the April 30, 2019 rate filing costs to implement Staff’s

proposed new requirements for gas company operator qualification programs

(see “Notice of Proposed Rulemaking,” issued March 27, 2018 in Cases 14-

13 
the rulemaking proceeding and due to the timing of the proposal that did not

allow the Company to complete its analysis and develop implementation costs 
15 
in time for inclusion in the April 30 filing.   
16 
 
17 
 
While the Commission has yet to issue an Order, the Company has developed 
18 
a plan to proactively begin implementation of enhanced operator qualification 
19 
best practices that are recommended by Staff’s proposal and are likely to 
20 
become compliance requirements during the term of the rate plan.  Proactive 
21

Response: 
 
1.
a.
The “CSC/Public Works – Non Reimbursable” forecast was developed using 
historical data and ratios that correlate the Company’s public works spending to 
the City of New York’s (“NYC”) total capital spending plan, as NYC does not 
publish a project work plan in advance of any given fiscal year (“FY”).  As such, 
specific municipal work projects for the Rate Year are not known at this time.  
The Company’s analysis yielded a 0.2:1 (20%) of KEDNY’s CSC work to NYC 
spending on infrastructure projects.  NYC’s capital spending plan for the Rate 
Year is approximately $1.275 billion.  The Company applied the spending ratio, 
added expected incremental paving and restoration costs, and then allocated 90% 
of the total forecast to Non-Reimbursable CSC spending based on NYC’s 
reimbursable and non-reimbursable projection that was showing a 90/10 split 
between reimbursable and non-reimbursable to forecast the Rate Year amount.  
As is typical each Spring and early Summer, NYC recently provided additional 
information regarding its capital spending plans, which revises the reimbursable

a.
Capital project SE-853 is a major water/storm sewer project along Flatlands 
Avenue that was initiated by the DDC on behalf of the New York City 
Department of Environmental Protection (“DEP”) for construction of 1,400 liner 
feet (“LF”) of 132” x 48” Storm Sewer, 800 LF of 66” x 30” Storm Sewer, 800

to be in direct conflict with certain portions of KEDNY’s gas infrastructure. 
DDC commenced this work in July of/2018 with anticipated completion in July of 
2021.

approximately 150 LF at Louisiana Ave. with 11.5 feet of cover and off-setting

groundwater is approximately 8 feet.

In previous years, the NYC Department of Design and Construction (DDC) would audit 
the invoices including requesting additional information, disputing charges and going 
back and forth with the Company until a final agreement was reached for payment of 
invoices.  Once the DDC issued their approval letter, the NYC Department of 
Environmental Protection (DEP) would then do a high-level, cursory review with 
payment generally following several months later.   
Beginning in early 2019, the DEP added an additional step whereby it will also audit the 
CSC invoices after the DDC audit is complete.  The DEP is now issuing formal memos 
requesting additional information and disputing charges.  With the benefit of several 
months working under this revised process, the Company now appreciates the extent the 
DEP’s additional auditing will impact the timing of reimbursements.  Indeed, this 
additional audit process is expected to increase the reimbursement lag, as is evidenced by 
the increase in the oldest invoice date from 20 months in December 2018 to 23 months in 
May 2019 (see Attachment 1).  Company personnel are regularly communicating with 
both the DDC and the DEP regarding these revised invoice review procedures and are 
facilitating the review process by providing higher quantity invoices in smaller 
increments, as well as providing additional documentation to describe the work 
performed and justify the associated cost.  Based on the payment history, as well as the 
additional DEP audit process, the Company believes increasing the billing lag assumption 
to 21 months is reasonable and still a conservative estimate.  The Company will be 
updating the billing lag assumption in its Corrections and Update filing.

updated CSC/Public Works - Reimbursements capex forecast that relates to the revised 
shift of capex from the CSC/Public Works - Reimbursable category to the CSC/Pubic 
Works – Non-Reimbursable category, described in the response to DPS-352.
