I.  INTRODUCTION 
 
 
On May 14, 2021, The Brooklyn Union Gas Company d/b/a National Grid NY 
(KEDNY) and KeySpan Gas East Corporation d/b/a National Grid (KEDLI) filed, on behalf of 
the Signatory Parties, a Joint Proposal in the above captioned proceedings.  On June 2, 2021, 
parties to the above captioned proceedings filed Statements in Support of or Opposition to the 
Joint Proposal (Initial Statements).  In this Reply Statement, Staff responds to a number of the 
claims raised in parties’ Initial Statements. 
II.  OVERALL RESPONSE TO STATEMENTS IN OPPOSITION 
1.  Overall Summary 
 
 
The Joint Proposal recommends a reasonable and balanced resolution of all the 
issues raised in Cases 19-G-0309, 19-G-0310, and 18-M-0270.  Staff recognizes that it does not 
please all the parties to these proceedings, but it does present a resolution that includes 
provisions addressing, though not necessarily adopting, many parties positions, whether or not 
those parties support or oppose the Joint Proposal.  Staff’s Initial Statement described many of 
these benefits of the Joint Proposal, and so this Reply statement will not rehash them. 
 
 
Despite the balanced resolution presented in the Joint Proposal, a number of 
parties do oppose it.  Some oppose specific provisions of the Joint Proposal.1  This Reply

3.  Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act 
 
 
Certain parties assert that the in considering the Joint Proposal, the Commission 
must make findings identified in CLPCA §§7(2) and 7(3).13  In addressing these positions, it is 
important to reflect on what action the Commission would be taking if it adopts the terms of the 
Joint Proposal.  The Commission would be authorizing rate recovery for certain of the 
Companies’ capital expenditures, but it would not be issuing any permits or otherwise actually 
authorizing construction.  Further, the Commission would be adopting provisions that increase 
the Companies’ energy efficiency, demand response, and electrification efforts.  The 
Commission is not, and cannot take any action requiring that end users of the gas delivered by 
the Companies curtail their usage.  Such actions are not within the scope of these rate 
proceedings. 
 
 
The CLCPA is not explicit regarding whether the Commission’s adoption of a 
rate order falls within the covered actions of CLCPA §§7(2) and 7(3).14  Both of those provisions 
refer to actions where state agencies are “considering and issuing permits, licenses, and other 
administrative approvals and decisions, including but not limited to the execution of grants, 
loans, and contracts….”  A rate order merely sets rates, authorizing the recovery of a utility’s 
prudently incurred expenses necessary to provide safe and adequate service.  With regard to 
capital expenditure projects, other entities, such as DEC issue any permits required for 
construction and operation of the projects.  Accordingly, the Commission’s adoption of a rate 
order may not fall within the actions identified by CLCPA §§7(2) and 7(3).

12  Filing for New Rates 
12.2  Following the Term of the Rate Plan 
 
 
The Joint Proposal identifies a Stayout Period, the period after the end of RY3 
until the earlier of March 31, 2024 or the date new Commission-approved rates become 
effective.  The Companies can file for new rates to be effective during this period, however, the 
Joint Proposal provides certain provisions that apply should the Companies refrain from 
requesting new rates to be effective prior to April 1, 2024.  Primarily, the Joint Proposal 
identifies the rates that customers will be charged during the Stayout Period that reflect the 
termination of the deferral credits used to moderate the impact of the revenue requirements 
during RY1, RY2, and RY3. 
 
 
The rates to be charged during the Stayout Period and the bill impacts are 
included in Schedule 4.3 of Appendices 3 and 4 for KEDNY and KEDLI, respectively.  The 
Joint Proposal recommends no changes to revenue requirements for the Stayout Period.  
However, the Joint Proposal recognizes that the deferral credits being used for rate moderation 
during the term of the rate plans will not be available for further rate moderation after the end of 
RY3.  Accordingly, the Joint Proposal specifies different base delivery rates for the Stayout 
Period reflecting RY3 revenue requirements without using deferral credits to offset those 
revenue requirements. 
 
 
Additionally, Section IV.12.2 of the Joint Proposal includes two additional 
provisions that would take effect only during the Stayout Period.  First, the Joint Proposal allows 
the Companies to reconcile net utility plant and depreciation expense, up to forecast capital 
expenditure level caps specified in Schedule 5 of Appendices 1 and 2 for KEDNY and KEDLI, 
respectively.  The Companies could recover reconciled amounts through the Delivery Rate 
Adjustment.  Second, the Joint Proposal provides for treatment of any Commission-approved 
energy efficiency spending, including, but not limited to, their authorized NE:NY budgets, not 
otherwise recovered in rates.  The Joint Proposal allows the Companies to surcharge customers 
for the costs of Commission-approved energy efficiency programs necessary to meet 
Commission-specified energy efficiency targets for the Stayout Period.  Importantly, these 
provisions preclude the Companies from collecting any money through the two surcharges, or 
deferring the amounts for future recovery, to the extent the recoveries would cause the 
Companies’ actual returns on common equity to exceed 8.8% during the Stayout Period.
