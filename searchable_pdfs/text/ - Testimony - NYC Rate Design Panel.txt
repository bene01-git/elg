Q. 
WHY IS AN ECOSS IMPORTANT? 
4 
A. 
An ECOSS illustrates the costs a utility incurs to serve each customer class.  It is widely 
5 
accepted that costs should be allocated among customer classes on the basis of cost 
6 
causation.  That principle is perhaps the most universally accepted tenet of allocating costs 
7 
that cannot be directly assigned to a particular customer class.  As such, costs should be 
8 
allocated to those classes on the basis of how or why those costs are incurred by the utility.  
9 
The results of such studies are normally used in assigning cost and revenue responsibilities 
10 
to various customer classes. 
11 
 Q. 
DO YOU SUPPORT THE PREMISE THAT COST CAUSATION PRINCIPLES 
12 
SHOULD GUIDE THE ALLOCATION OF COSTS TO THE CUSTOMER 
13 
CLASSES? 
14 
A. 
Yes.  Rates that are based on consistently applied cost causation principles are fair and 
15 
reasonable and further the cause of stability, conservation and economic efficiency.  Other 
16 
factors such as simplicity, gradualism, economic development and ease of administration 
17 
may also be taken into consideration when determining the final allocation of the revenue 
18 
requirement among classes, but the fundamental starting point and guideline should be the 
19 
cost of serving each customer class produced by the ECOSS. 
20 
  Q. 
PLEASE EXPLAIN THE PURPOSE AND DEVELOPMENT OF A CLASS COST 
21 
OF SERVICE STUDY. 
22 


--- Page 8 ---

7 
A. 
A class cost of service study allocates and assigns the total cost of providing utility service 
1 
to the classes of customers receiving that service.  The development of a class cost of 
2 
service study consists of three steps: functionalization, classification, and allocation.  
3 
Functionalization refers to the process by which the Company’s investments and expenses 
4 
are identified and segregated into different cost categories.  For KEDNY and KEDLI, these 
5 
functional categories include supply and storage, transmission, distribution, metering and 
6 
billing, collections, sales promotions and procurement. 
7 
 
 
Once functionalization is complete, the utility’s costs are classified into categories 
8 
of demand-related, energy related and customer-related costs in order to facilitate the 
9 
allocation of costs by applying cost causation principles.  In general, all distribution costs 
10 
are either responsive to increases in natural gas demand or to the number of customers.  
11 
Demand-related costs are those that are needed to serve the winter peak demands of  
12 
distribution customers.  Demand-related costs are fixed and do not vary with the monthly 
13 
and annual commodity consumption of the utility’s customers.  Customer-related costs are 
14 
associated with the number of customers and are costs that are incurred to connect 
15 
customers to the system independent of the customer’s demand and energy requirements.  
16 
Primary examples of customer-related costs are investments in meters, services, and a 
17 
portion of main investment incurred to extend the distribution system to the customer’s 
18 
premises and conform to local and national codes and standards.  In addition, such 
19 
accounting functions as meter reading, bill preparation and revenue accounting are 
20 
generally considered customer-related costs. 
21 


--- Page 9 ---
Cases: 19-G-0309 
NYC RATE DESIGN PANEL 
19-G-0310 
 
 
 
8 
   
 
Lastly, the functionalized and classified costs are allocated to the various customer 
1 
classes based on each class’ contribution to the respective cost classifications.  In general, 
2 
demand-related costs are allocated based on each class’ contribution to the maximum 
3 
demand placed on the system by the classes or by the customers within the classes.  
4 
Customer related costs are allocated based upon the number of customers in each class, 
5 
weighted to account for the complexity of servicing the needs of the different classes of 
6 
customers.

Q. 
HOW DID THE COMPANIES’ ECOSS CLASSIFY DISTRIBUTION MAINS? 
8 
A. 
The RDP explained on pages 17 and 18 of its Direct Testimony that it used a minimum-
9 
system study to classify distribution mains into customer and demand related costs.  Based 
10 
on this study, 47.08% of the distribution system was classified as customer-related and

12 
allocated to customer classes based on the number of customers and the demand component 
13 
was allocated based on class contribution to design-day demand.
