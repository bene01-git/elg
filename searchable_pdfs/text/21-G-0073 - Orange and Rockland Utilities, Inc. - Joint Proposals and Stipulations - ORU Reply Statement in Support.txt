I. 
Preliminary Statement 
Pursuant to a Ruling Revising Procedural Schedule, issued on September 20, 2021, the 
following parties submitted Statements in Support of the Joint Proposal (“Proposal”) filed in the 
above-referenced proceedings: Orange and Rockland Utilities, Inc. (“Orange and Rockland” or 
the “Company”), New York State Department of Public Service Staff (“Staff”), the New York 
Power Authority (“NYPA”), New York Geothermal Energy Organization (“NYGEO”) and New 
Yorkers For Cool Refrigerant Management (collectively, the “Signatory Parties”).  The Public 
Utility Law Project of New York, Inc. (“PULP”), the Alliance for a Green Economy 
(“AGREE”), and Bruce Levine1 submitted Statements in Opposition to the Proposal.  The other 
parties to these proceedings, such as the Utility Intervention Unit, Division of Consumer 
Protection, New York State Department of State (“UIU”), chose not to oppose the Proposal.

Overall, the parties supporting the Proposal demonstrated that the Proposal is just and

for its adoption.   
Although both PULP and AGREE support various aspects of the Proposal, their 
opposition focuses on two aspects of the Proposal.  First, they both contend that the Proposal 
should do more to advance the goals of the Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act 
(“CLCPA”).2  Second, both PULP and AGREE oppose the Proposal’s bill impacts and its 9.2% 
Return on Equity (“ROE”).  As discussed below, PULP’s and AGREE’s positions are not 
persuasive and they should not prevent the Commission from adopting the Proposal without 
modification.

5. 
Revenue Allocation/Rate Design and Other Tariff Changes 
In its Statement of Opposition (pp. 14-15), PULP opposes both the increase in the fixed 
electric residential customer charge and the increase in the fixed gas residential charge.  The 
Proposal provides for: (1) increases in the fixed electric residential customer charges of $1.00 in 
RY1 and RY2 and $0.50 in RY3; and (2) increases in the fixed gas residential customer charges 
of $0.50 in RY1 and $1.00 in RY2 and RY3.  PULP states that such increases run contrary to 
both the Commission’s historic objectives regarding the promotion of energy efficiency in New 
York and the objectives of the State’s CLCPA because these increases result in higher bill 
impacts for conservation-minded low and moderate usage customers, as compared to the typical 
residential customer.  PULP’s position should be rejected for two reasons.  First, PULP ignores 
the fundamental purpose of customer charges.  The Company’s customer charge is used to 
recover costs that do not vary with customer energy usage.  Thus, there is no valid reason for 
conservation-minded customers (or any other customers for that matter) to expect to reduce these 
charges because the underlying costs are not reduced.  Second, because the current residential 
customer charge is below the customer costs indicated in the Company’s embedded cost of 
service study for both electric and gas, the shortfall in the revenue recovered through the 
customer charge increases the revenue to be recovered through the per kWh or per Ccf usage 
charges, resulting in an improper subsidy for lower usage customers by higher usage customers.   


--- Page 14 ---

A critical objective of rate design is related to cost causation, whereby rates should 
provide accurate and transparent price signals to customers that reflect the costs of serving them.  
Rates paid by customers for delivery service should reflect the costs of providing such service. 
Thus, the pricing of the Company’s delivery service should be based on the Company’s delivery 
system costs.  The Proposal includes a modest and gradual increase in the customer charge that 
makes progress in reducing this subsidy, while PULP’s proposal to keep the customer charges at 
their current level would maintain that subsidy.  In summary, PULP has failed to offer any 
evidence that the proposed cost-based rates contained in the Proposal are at odds with the 
Commission’s energy efficiency objectives or CLCPA goals.

Arrears Management
