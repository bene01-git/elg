Dear Secretary Phillips and Judges Moreno and Costello: 
 
AARP New York appreciates this opportunity to comment on evidence presented at the 
evidentiary hearing held on April 18, 2023.  Testimony given by Department of Public Service 
(DPS) Staff, the City of New York (City), Consolidated Edison (Con Ed or Company), Public 
Utility Law Project (PULP), and AARP New York, as well as the exhibits proffered by several 
of those parties, not only confirm but actually strengthen the analyses and conclusions about the 
Joint Proposal’s (JP) serious flaws we presented in our initial and reply statements.  The 
proposed increase is too high, and these flaws only serve to compound the harm to middle-, 
moderate- and low-income households that would result were the Commission to approve the 
JP’s recommended residential delivery and fixed charge increases:  electric delivery and fixed 
charges totaling approximately 25 percent, gas heating delivery and fixed charges totaling

The JP IS Flawed Because It Fails To Address EAP Program Under Enrollment  
There is no question that Con Ed’s low-income discount programs are under enrolled, although it 
is not known by how much. Inasmuch as the Settling Parties recommend substantially increasing 
the delivery rates of residential customers, it is inexplicable that the JP is silent about reaching 
out to the potentially hundreds of thousands of low-income customers who are not enrolled in the 
Company’s EAP discount program.   
As presented in Exhibit 604, Office of Temporary and Disability Assistance (OTDA) data shows 
that, during the federal fiscal year of October 1, 2021 – September 30, 2022, 934,913 New York 
City households were authorized to receive Non-Emergency HEAP benefits (843,913 “Autopay” 
and 91,452 “Regular Non-Autopay”) and 34,873 Westchester County households were 
authorized to receive Non-Emergency HEAP benefits (33,186 “Autopay” and 1,687 “Regular 
Non-Autopay”).2  Added together, the OTDA data indicates 970,238 households in New York 
City and Westchester County were authorized to receive Non-Emergency HEAP benefits during 
the federal fiscal year ended September 30, 2022.  In contrast, Con Edison’s December 2022 
Energy Affordability Policy Report indicates EAP low-income program enrollment of 470,363 
(326,111 electric only; 3,632 gas-only; and 140,620 combination).3  
 
AARP New York appreciates that it is likely that a large portion of the 460,000 difference 
between the number of HEAP recipients and the number of EAP program enrollees is due to the 
category of “renter’s HEAP.”  That is, people receive a small stipend to offset a portion of their 
rent if heat is included in the rent.  It is equally likely, however, as PULP testified regarding its 
analysis of United States Census American Community Survey data, some number of these 
HEAP recipients are Con Ed electric customers who are not enrolled in the Company’s EAP

enrollment.  In the process of preparing its testimony in the instant cases, PULP, not DPS Staff, 
noticed anomalies in program enrollment while reviewing Con Ed’s quarterly EAP reports. The 
response to a PULP information request revealed that Con Edison failed to inform DPS Staff or 
the Commission that Westchester County Department of Social Services stopped data matching

with the Company 18 months earlier, on September 28, 2020.11  The Consumer Protection Board 
(CPB) uncovered a perhaps more serious incident while preparing its testimony in the 
Company’s 2009 electric rate case (09-E-0428).  The CPB learned through discovery that in 
2006 Con Ed, without informing the Commission or DPS Staff, unilaterally stopped using the

were immediately enrolled.12  
Arguing now that AARP New York and PULP do not know the exact amount of under 
enrollment is a red herring.  The point is that under enrollment exists, potentially in the hundreds 
of thousands.  The JP’s failure to tackle EAP program under enrollment is more than 
disappointing and puzzling; it is not reasonable and is not within the range of a likely litigated 
outcome.  It cannot be concluded that the proposed rates are just and reasonable for the 
thousands of low-income customers not enrolled in the EAP programs.

ROE Was Not “Negotiated,” As That Word Is Commonly Understood 
The Settling Parties’ claims that the JP’s 9.25% return on equity was the result of actual 
negotiations is disingenuous. Rather, the JP’s ROE is based on the Commission and DPS Staff’s 
multi-decades long approach to establishing ROEs in multi-year rate plans. It is undisputed that 
DPS Staff uses the Generic Finance Methodology (GFM) to determine the appropriate ROE for a 
one-year litigated case and then recommends in testimony adding a risk or stay out premium of 
20 to 50 basis points to “protect” the utility in the event a mult-year rate plan is negotiated.  For 
example, DPS Staff stated in its pre-filed testimony in Cases 13-E-0030 et al.: “Additionally, as 
is the case in nearly all New York multi-year rate plans, each of the ROEs also reflects a

Conclusion 
For these reasons and for the reasons presented in the respective statements of the opponents, 
AARP New York urges the Commission to reject the JP and not endorse unreasonable double-
digit delivery rate increases that will harm low- and moderate-income residential customers, 
especially those who are not enrolled in Con Ed’s EAP programs and people living in 
disadvantaged communities.
