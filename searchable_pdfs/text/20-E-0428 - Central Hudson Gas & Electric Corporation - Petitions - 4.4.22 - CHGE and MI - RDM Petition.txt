Preliminary Statement 
Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corporation (“Central Hudson”) and Multiple Intervenors 
(collectively the “Joint Petitioners”) hereby petition the New York State Public Service 
Commission (“Commission”) to modify/adjust the mechanics of certain Revenue Decoupling 
Mechanisms (“RDMs”) applicable to non-residential customers that were adopted as part of the 
Commission’s Order Adopting Terms of Joint Proposal and Establishing Electric and Gas Rate 
Plan issued on November 18, 2021 in the above-captioned electric and gas rate proceedings.1  
The Proposed Modifications (hereinafter defined) are minor in scope but important as they adjust 
for and address an unintended consequence resulting from the application of the RDMs.  
Specifically, as discussed in greater detail below, the Proposed Modifications relate to 
(a) application of the Electric RDM to S.C. 13 customers, and (b) application of the Gas RDM to
S.C. 11 customers.  The Proposed Modifications are characterized as “modifications/adjustment”

Relevant Background 
 
As part of its direct testimony filed in support of these rate cases, Central Hudson 
proposed to expand: (a) the Electric RDM to include S.C. 13; and (b) the Gas RDM to include 
S.C. 11.3  Both classes, each of which contains very few customers, have previously been exempt 
from RDMs.  Central Hudson’s proposals were opposed by New York State Department of 
Public Service Staff (“Staff”) and Multiple Intervenors.4 
Following settlement negotiations, various parties entered into a Joint Proposal under 
which the Electric RDM was expanded to include S.C. 13 customers and the Gas RDM was 
expanded to include S.C. 11 customers, subject to numerous conditions intended to protect those 
customers from excessive RDM surcharges and, conversely, preclude them from excessive RDM 
credits.  The Commission subsequently issued the 2021 Rate Order which approved the Joint 
Proposal.5

Some of the Joint Proposal’s conditions applicable to the expansion of the Electric RDM 
to S.C. 13 customers include: 
 Combining S.C. 13 (which has an extremely small number of customers) with 
S.C. 3 for RDM purposes;6 
 Excluding from RDM reconciliations the loss or gain of revenues due to 
loss/gain of one or more S.C. 13 customers; 
 Excluding from RDM reconciliations the loss or gain of revenues due to the 
significant decline/increase in electric demand by one or more S.C. 13 
customers (with “significant decline/increase” referring to a variance of 40% 
or more compared to the customer’s forecasted electric demand); 
 Utilizing a 5% “deadband” around S.C. 13 revenue reconciliations within 
which no monthly reconciliations would be implemented; and 
 Limiting S.C. 13 RDM revenue excesses/shortfalls outside of the deadband to 
plus or minus 2.5%.7 
The negotiated conditions applicable to the expansion of the Gas RDM to S.C. 11 customers – 
which are even fewer in number than S.C. 13 electric customers – are essentially identical in 
substance to those listed above.8

customers (a) previously were exempt from RDMs, (b) are very few in number (and, therefore,

The Proposed Modifications are necessitated by the initial operation of the Electric RDM, 
which produced unanticipated – and, to Multiple Intervenors, unacceptable – impacts.  
Specifically, in applying the initial Electric RDM, Central Hudson appropriately utilized a 5% 
deadband to limit S.C. 13 revenue reconciliations and also limited the amount of S.C. 13 revenue 
excesses/shortfalls to 2.5%.  Significantly, however, when S.C. 13 subsequently was combined 
with S.C. 3, which had experienced much-greater revenue shortfalls during the initial six-month 
period, the resulting RDM surcharge that was applied to S.C. 3 and S.C. 13 customers was well 
in excess of the intended 2.5% cap on surcharges/credits that was negotiated for S.C. 13 
customers as shown in Exhibit A attached hereto.  In fact, certain S.C. 13 customers experienced 
RDM surcharges in excess of 5%, contrary to the Joint Petitioners’ intent in negotiating the 2.5% 
cap.  
The unintended consequence requiring modification/adjustment is that the 2.5% 
limitation on S.C. 13 surcharges/credits currently is being applied prior to the combination of 
S.C. 3 and S.C. 13 for RDM purposes.9  Consequently, if S.C. 3 experiences a substantial 
revenue shortfall (or excess) that is shared with S.C. 13, combining those two classes without 
restriction may result in S.C. 13 customers incurring RDM-related surcharges (or credits) 
substantially greater than the 2.5% limitation that was intended when the Joint Proposal was 
negotiated – which is precisely what occurred during the first Electric RDM calculation as

Requested Relief 
 
In order to address the unintended outcome discussed above, the Joint Petitioners herein 
propose that the RDMs as set forth in the Joint Proposal be modified as follows: (a) for the 
Electric RDM, to make explicit that while revenue excesses/shortfalls for S.C. 3 may be shared 
with S.C. 13 for RDM purposes, such sharing is limited such that the total S.C. 13 
credit/surcharge is capped at 2.5% of the adjusted S.C. 13 target; and (b) for the Gas RDM, to 
make explicit that while revenue excesses/shortfalls for S.C. 2, S.C. 6, and S.C. 13 may be 
shared with S.C. 11 for RDM purposes, such sharing is limited such that the total S.C. 11 
credit/surcharge is capped at 2.5% of the adjusted S.C. 11 target (together, the “Proposed 
Modifications”).  A redline of the proposed revisions to Appendix O to the Joint Proposal needed 
to effectuate the Proposed Modifications is set forth in Exhibit B hereto.  An example of how the 
Electric and Gas RDMs would be calculated pursuant to the Proposed Modifications is also set 
forth in Exhibit B.  Additionally, a redline of the very minor proposed tariff changes needed to 
effectuate the Proposed Modifications are set forth in Exhibit C hereto. 
Importantly, the Proposed Modifications are: (a) the same for the Electric RDM and the 
Gas RDM in order to preserve consistency between the two mechanisms; (b) symmetrical, in that 
the 2.5% limitation on RDM surcharges to S.C. 13 electric and S.C. 11 gas customers would

The Joint Petitioners hereto maintain that the Proposed Modifications are absolutely 
essential to preserving their intent with respect to applying the Electric RDM to S.C. 13 
customers and the Gas RDM to S.C. 11 customers.  As discussed, supra, the conditions that were 
negotiated in order to achieve agreement on expansion of the Electric RDM to include S.C. 13 
customers and expansion of the Gas RDM to include S.C. 11 customers were intended to cap 
RDM surcharges/credits to those two classes such that they would have no greater than a 2.5% 
delivery rate impact (positive or negative) on customers.  That intent was not realized in the 
initial RDM reconciliation as certain S.C. 13 customers experienced RDM-related rate impacts 
that were more than double that amount.  Working together, Central Hudson and Multiple 
Intervenors determined that the cause of the unanticipated Electric RDM impacts was that the 
2.5% limitation on S.C. 13 excesses/shortfalls is being applied prior to those excesses/shortfalls 
being combined with S.C. 3 excesses/shortfalls, but not also after such combination.  
Indeed, as already has happened, the goal of capping RDM surcharges/credits to S.C. 13 
customers to 2.5% is frustrated – and rendered ineffectual – by the limitation not also being applied 
after the excesses/shortfalls of S.C. 13 and S.C. 3 have been combined.  Additionally, the same 
potential for unintended, excessive rate impacts to S.C. 11 gas customers has been identified and 
can be resolved successfully with similar modification to the Gas RDM.

Conclusion 
For all the foregoing reasons, Central Hudson and Multiple Intervenors jointly petition 
the Commission for authorization to implement the Proposed Modifications to the Electric RDM 
and the Gas RDM.  Such modifications are: (a) symmetrical in nature and would cap 
RDM-related credits as well as surcharges; (b) prospective only in nature; and (c) absolutely 
necessary to be consistent with the intent to cap RDM credits and surcharges to S.C. 13 electric 
and S.C. 11 gas customers to no more than a 2.5% impact.

Electric SC 13 RDM Mechanics 
Effective July 1, 2021 the RDM Adjustment will be expanded to include Service 
Classification No. 13. Delivery revenue targets will be determined for S.C. 13 Substation 
and Transmission combined with monthly revenue excess/shortfalls determined as 
follows:  
o
The RDM would exclude from reconciliations revenue losses/gains
associated with the loss/gain of any S.C. 13 customers (including
intermittent billing of electric generators not included in forecast);
o
The RDM would exclude from reconciliations revenue losses/gains
associated with the significant decline/increase in electric demand of any
S.C. 13 customers.  For purposes hereof, “significant decline/increase”
would be defined as a reduction/increase of 40% or more in a customer’s
billed demand as compared to the forecast presented in Appendix [ ].  A
customer would be excluded from RDM reconciliation if the demand varied
by 40% or more over the course of the 6-month RDM reconciliation period;
o
A 5% deadband will be established around adjusted monthly S.C. 13 RDM
targets within which no monthly reconciliations will be made;
o
Monthly S.C. 13 RDM revenue excess/shortfalls outside the 5% deadband
shall be limited to 2.5% of the adjusted S.C. 13 target. Monthly amounts in
excess of the 2.5% cap for S.C. 13 are eligible to be carried over to a
subsequent month during the term of the rate plan to the extent the carry
over does not exceed the 2.5% cap in that month; and
o
Actual and target S.C. 13 delivery revenue subject to the RDM shall be
exclusive of EE credits.
For purposes of determining semi-annual RDM Adjustment factors, revenue 
excess/shortfalls for S.C. Nos. 3 may be shared with S.C. 13 but will be limited so that 
the total S.C. 13 excess/shortfall including any portion of S.C. 3 excess/shortfall is 
limited to 2.5% of the adjusted S.C. 13 target. Separate and 13 will be combined to 
determine a single RDM Adjustment factors will be developed for S.C. 3 and S.C. 13, 
both of which will be applicable to both classes assessed on a per-kW basis. The per-
kW rate will appear on the RDM statement but for billing purposes will be assessed 
through the MISC II rate and as such will not be separately identified as RDM on 
customers’ bills and will be further subject to the following: 
o
The existing RDM line item on SC 3 customers’ bills will be eliminated as
the recovery/passback of RDM amounts moves to the MISC II;
o
For SC 3 customers billed mid-month (i.e. 1/16 – 2/15), the RDM
recovery/passback in the first month of the new combined RDM factors
(i.e., February 2022) will appear prorated in both the existing RDM line
item (assessed on a per-kWh basis for periods prior to February 1) and


--- Page 12 ---
the MISC II line item (assessed on a per-kW basis for periods on and after 
February 1). The prorated amounts will represent the period of time 
covered by the billing period (i.e. 0.5 months) multiplied by the total kWh 
or kW billed during the time period; and, 
o
Given that a single factor would be developed for S.C. Nos. 3 & 13, any
prior period unreconciled S. C. 3 balance will be reflected in a subsequent
combined SC 3/13 RDM factor.

Gas SC 11 RDM Mechanics 
Effective July 1, 2021 the RDM Adjustment will be expanded to include Service 
Classification No. 11 (Transmission, Distribution and Distribution Large Mains). S.C. 11 
delivery revenue targets and actual delivery revenue as further described below will be 
combined with S.C. Nos. 2, 6 & 13 for purposes of determining a single non-residential 
class for purposes of determining RDM reconciliations: 
o
The RDM will exclude from reconciliations revenue losses/gains associated
with the loss/gain of any S.C. 11 customers;
o
The RDM will exclude from reconciliations revenue losses/gains associated
with the significant decline/increase in gas demand of any S.C. 11
customers.  For purposes hereof, “significant decline/increase” would be
defined as a reduction/increase of 40% or more in a customer’s MDQ as
compared to the forecast presented in Appendix [ ].  A customer would be
excluded from RDM reconciliation if the MDQ varied by 40% or more over
the course of the 6-month RDM reconciliation period;
o
A 5% deadband will be established around adjusted monthly S.C. 11 RDM
targets within which no monthly reconciliations will be made;
o
Monthly S.C. 11 RDM revenue excess shortfalls outside the 5% deadband
shall be limited to 2.5% of the adjusted S.C. 11 target. Monthly amounts in
excess of the 2.5% cap for S.C. 11 are eligible to be carried over to a
subsequent month during the term of the rate plan to the extent the carry
over does not exceed the 2.5% cap in that month.
For purposes of determining semi-annual RDM Adjustment factors, revenue excess 
shortfalls for S.C. Nos. 2, 6, 11 and 13 may be shared with S.C. 11 but will be limited so 
that the total S.C. 11 excess/shortfall including any portion of S.C. 2, 6 and 13 
excess/shortfall is limited to 2.5% of the adjusted S.C. 11 target. Separate will be 
combined to determine a single RDM Adjustment factors will be developed for S.C. Nos. 
2, 6 and 13, combined, and S.C. 11, both of which will be applicable to all classes 
assessed on a per-Ccf basis.

42. OTHER CHARGES AND ADJUSTMENTS (Cont’d)
E. Revenue Decoupling Mechanism (RDM) Adjustment
Pursuant to the Commission’s Order in Case 08-G-0888 issued and effective June 22, 2009, as amended and 
superseded by the Order in Case 20-G-0429 issued and effective November 18, 2021, actual gas deliveries 
for certain customer classes are subject to reconciliation through an RDM Adjustment. The RDM Adjustment 
is applicable to Service Classification Nos. 1, 2, 6, 12 and 13. Effective July 1, 2021 the RDM Adjustment 
will be expanded to include Service Classification No. 11 (Transmission, Distribution and Distribution Large 
Mains).  The RDM is not applicable to Service Classification Nos. 8, 9, 11 (electric generators), 14, 15 and 
16. 
1.
Definitions
a.
Delivery Revenue Targets  by month for each service classification will be based on delivery
revenue targets for the Rate Years ending June 30, 2022, June 30, 2023, and June 30, 2024 as
set forth in Case 20-G-0429 except that delivery revenue targets for the months of July through
November 2021 will be adjusted by the amounts so determined to be refunded or recovered
through the Make Whole provision as described in General Information Section 42.  Delivery
revenue target for Service Classification Nos. 1 and 12 will be combined and Service
Classification Nos. 2, 6, 11 and 13 combined. Rate Year ending June 30, 2024 delivery revenue
targets so determined in the aforementioned Case, shall remain in effect until otherwise changed
by the Commission.  Delivery revenue targets will be adjusted monthly to account for certain
changes in demand associated with SC 11 customers as described below.
b.
Actual Delivery Revenue is defined as the sum of monthly total billed revenue derived from
customer charges, base rate volumetric charges, Maximum Daily Quantity (MDQ) charges and
Gas Bill Credit adjusted by the Weather Normalization Adjustment described in General
Information Section 27. The Actual Delivery Revenue in the first month of each rate year will
be adjusted upward to reverse the effect of proration between old and new rates in actual
revenue. For purposes of reversing the effect of proration between old and new rates, the first
month of the rate year for the Rate Year ending June 30, 2022 shall be December 2021.  Actual
delivery revenue will be adjusted monthly to account for certain changes in demand associated
with SC 11 customers as described below.
c.
Semi-Annual RDM Periods are defined as the six months ending December 31, 2015 and June
30, 2016 and each succeeding six-month period thereafter.
d.
RDM Adjustment Period is defined as the six months beginning February 1 or the six months
beginning August 1 immediately following each Semi-Annual RDM Period.
2.
Determination of RDM Adjustment
Except as described further below for Service Classification No. 11, for each service classification 
group, the Company will compare, on a monthly basis, the difference between Actual Delivery 
Revenue and corresponding Delivery Revenue Target.  If the monthly Actual Delivery Revenue 
exceeds the Delivery Revenue Target, the delivery revenue excess will be accrued for refund to 
customers at the end of the Semi-Annual RDM Period.  Likewise, if the monthly Actual Delivery 
Revenue is less than the Delivery Revenue Target, the delivery revenue shortfall will be accrued for 
recovery from customers at the end of the Semi-Annual RDM Period. For purposes of determining 
semi-annual RDM Adjustment factors, effective February 1, 2022 revenue excess/ shortfalls for 
S.C. Nos. 2, 6, 11 and 13 will be combined to determine a single RDM Adjustment factor applicable
to all classes assessed on a per-Ccf basis and effective August 1, 2022 revenue excess/shortfalls for
S.C. Nos. 2, 6, 11 and 13 may be combined for purposes of determining the RDM Adjustment
factors with the S.C. 11 factors limited to 2.5% of the adjusted S.C. 11 target for the preceding 6 
months.

36. OTHER CHARGES AND ADJUSTMENTS (Cont’d)
G.
Revenue Decoupling Mechanism (RDM) Adjustment (Cont’d)
c.
Semi-Annual RDM Periods are defined as the six months ending December 31, 2018
and June 30, 2019 and each succeeding six-month period thereafter.
d.
RDM Adjustment Period is defined as the six months beginning February 1 or the six
months beginning August 1 immediately following each Semi-Annual RDM Period.
2.
Determination of RDM Adjustment
Except as described further below for Service Classification No. 13, for each service classification
or sub classification subject to the RDM Adjustment, the Company will, on a monthly basis,
compare Actual Delivery Revenue to a Delivery Revenue Target. If the monthly Actual Delivery
Revenue exceeds the Delivery Revenue Target, the delivery revenue excess will be accrued for
refund to customers at the end of the Semi-Annual RDM Period. Likewise, if the monthly Actual
Delivery Revenue is less than the Delivery Revenue Target, the delivery revenue shortfall will be
accrued for recovery from customers at the end of the Semi-Annual RDM Period.
On a monthly basis, interest at the Commission’s rate for other customer provided capital will be
calculated on the average of the current and prior month’s cumulative delivery revenue
excess/shortfall (net of state and federal income tax benefits).
At the end of a Semi-Annual RDM Period total delivery revenue excess/shortfalls, and associated
interest, for each service classification or sub classification will be refunded/surcharged to
customers through service classification or sub classification-specific RDM Adjustments
applicable during a corresponding RDM Adjustment Period. The Delivery Revenue Targets
utilized in the determination of any such revenue excess/shortfalls will be adjusted, as applicable,
to exclude credits applied to customer accounts pursuant to General Information Section 16.B.1.
Effective November 1, 2011, Service Classification Nos. 1 and 6 will be combined for purposes of
determining the RDM Adjustment. Effective February 1, 2022, Service Classification Nos. 3 and
13 will be combined for purposes of determining the RDM Adjustment factors which will be
developed on a kW basis and effective August 1, 2022 Service Classification Nos. 3 and 13 may
be combined for purposes of determining the RDM Adjustment factors with the S.C. 13 factors
limited to 2.5% of the adjusted S.C. 13 target for the preceding 6 months. The RDM Adjustment
for each applicable service classification or sub classification shall be determined by dividing the
amount to be refunded/surcharged to customers in that service classification or sub classification
by estimated kWh and/or kW deliveries to customers in that service classification or sub
classification over the RDM Adjustment Period. RDM Adjustments shall be rounded to the
nearest $0.00001 per kWh or $0.01 per kW.
Following each RDM Adjustment Period, any difference between amounts required to be charged
or credited to customers in each service classification or sub classification and amounts actually
charged or credited will be charged or credited to customers in that service classification or sub
classification, with interest, over a subsequent RDM Adjustment period, or as determined by the
Commission if no RDM is in effect.
If for any reason, a service classification or sub classification included in the RDM no longer has
customers, the Company will consult with Commission Staff.

1.
Proposed Action
The New York State Public Service Commission (the “Commission”) is considering a joint 
petition filed by Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corporation (“Central Hudson”) and Multiple 
Intervenors (“MI”) to modify the mechanics of certain Revenue Decoupling Mechanisms 
(“RDMs”). 
2.
Substance of Proposed Rule
The Commission is considering a petition filed by Central Hudson and MI to modify the 
mechanics of certain RDMs that were adopted as part of the Commission’s Order Adopting 
Terms of Joint Proposal and Establishing Electric and Gas Rate Plan issued in Cases 20-E-0428 
et al. on November 18, 2021. 
Specifically, Central Hudson and MI propose that the RDMs be modified as follows: (1) for the 
Electric RDM, to make explicit that while revenue excesses/shortfalls for S.C. 3 may be shared 
with S.C. 13 for RDM purposes, such sharing is limited such that the total S.C. 13 
credit/surcharge is capped at 2.5% of the adjusted S.C. 13 target; and (2) for the Gas RDM, to 
make explicit that while revenue excesses/shortfalls for S.C. 2, S.C. 6, and S.C. 13 may be 
shared with S.C. 11 for RDM purposes, such sharing is limited such that the total S.C. 11 
credit/surcharge is capped at 2.5% of the adjusted S.C. 11 target.
