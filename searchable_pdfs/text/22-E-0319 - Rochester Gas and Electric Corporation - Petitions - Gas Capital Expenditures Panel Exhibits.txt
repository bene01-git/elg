I.
Main Target
NYSEG’s Olean gas distribution system is located in the City of Olean and Town of Allegany, New York.  The 
system is fed by one supplier, TC Energy through the Flat Iron gate station into Olean and Allegany and direct 
into NYSEG’s Limestone system serving the Limestone district regulator station.     
The Flat Iron gate station is the sole gate station providing natural gas to Olean through Line J and Line E. 
The Line J pipeline feeds the north and Line E pipeline feeds the southern side of Olean.  Both maintain a 
250-psig MAOP.  Line J feeds the Hebron district regulation station (Hebron station), reducing pressure to 85-
psig MAOP then serves the medium pressure system at 59-psig MAOP.  Hebron station accounts for nearly
half of the natural gas load to the Olean distribution system to approximately 2,900 customers.  Line E feeds
the McCann Hollow regulation station (McCann Hollow station) which reduces pressure to 124-psig MAOP.
The 124-psig feeder main from McCann Hollow station to the 20th Street regulation station (20th Street station)
is the primary feed from the south for the 59-psig MAOP distribution system in the City of Olean to
approximately 3,600 customers.  Refer to Map 1 for a general overview of the Olean gas system.
Hebron station was constructed in the 1940’s and is at the end of its useful life.  Line J from the south near 
Flat Iron to Hebron station is approximately 6.4 miles, constructed in the 1940’s and is at the end of useful 
service life also.  Both are critical to the safety and reliability to NYSEG’s customers in Olean.  The majority of 
Line J runs “back-lots”, crosses the Allegheny River, installed along an abandoned railroad bed right-of-way 
(ROW).  It consists of 6-inch and 8-inch steel pipe which was installed in 1940’s and is classified as leak 
prone.   
The overall scope of the project includes the construction of a second source of supply, from Empire/National 
Fuel, into the Olean system.  This point of delivery station would be located south of the Dresser Rand Group 
Inc. facility.  NYSEG would construct a gate station and pipeline to deliver the gas to the 85--psig system at 
the inlet of the existing Pepsi Regulator station (Pepsi station).  Upon the completion of the project, the 
following assets would be retired: Hebron station; approximately 6.4 miles of Line J; approximately 1.8 miles of 
aging 85-psig MAOP main from the existing Hebron station to Pepsi station.

PENDLETON, NY 
I.
Main Target
NYSEG’s Lockport gas distribution system is located mainly in Niagara County, New York. Tennessee (Kinder 
Morgan) supplies Lockport from the west through the Robinson Rd gate station and Empire supplies Lockport 
from the east/central through the Ertman/Royalton gate station.   
The Robinson Rd gate station is the main gate station providing natural gas to the Town of Pendleton through 
a pipeline that operates at 51-psig maximum allowable operating pressure (MAOP).   
The overall scope of the project includes the installation of a new District Regulator Station and approximately 
13,000 LF of polyethylene (PE) main at 51-psig MAOP.  This project will improve system pressures and 
reliability in the southwest quadrant of the Lockport medium pressure system. 
II.
Description
The scope of work includes: 
•
Acquire an easement and/or property for the new District Regulator Station.
•
Construct a new District Regulator Station that will reduce pressure from 249-psig to 51-psig on the
west side of Erie Canal and RR tracks.
•
Install approximately 13,000 LF of 8-inch PE gas distribution main along the RR tracks from Hinman
Rd south towards Mapleton Rd them west towards Campbell Blvd to tie into the existing 51-psig
medium pressure system.
III.
Driver
The Design Day for the Lockport system is 75 heating degree days (HDD).  The hydraulic model predicts 
Design Day load for Lockport through the Robinson Rd and Ertman/Royalton gate stations for a total of 2,900 
Mcfh (thousand cubic feet per hour).  The southwest area of the Town of Pendleton accounts for 
approximately 175 Mcfh of the total load and serves about 2,300 customers. 
The project will improve the pressure throughout the 51-psig system and specifically at the endpoints of the 
system along Townline Rd south of Mapleton Rd and Townline Rd north of Beach Ridge Rd in the Town of 
Pendleton, from approximately 23.5-psig to 40.5-psig and 27.0-psig to 39.5-psig, respectively.  The project will 
increase system resiliency and reliability, and allow the system to maintain safe delivery to the existing 
customers. 
The project will also support potential IDA economic development interest located in the northwest area of the 
Lockport system near Upper Mountain Rd and Lockport Junction Rd.

I.
Main Target
The Town of Canandaigua (including the City of Canandaigua) has experienced significant residential, 
commercial and industrial growth over the last 20 years.  There is one primary feed, an 8-inch steel 124-psig 
main from the Hopewell Tap, which provides natural gas to the Canandaigua area through two district 
regulator stations (DRS): RS420075 Canandaigua City Gate and RS420079 Brickyard Road (refer to Map 1).

Allowable Operating Pressure (MAOP).  The Canandaigua Feeder Main (124-psig) Reinforcement Project will 
shed the load across the feeder main, reduce the pressure drop across the feeder main, increase inlet

MF120 Eastern Monroe System Improvements (State Road) 
I.
Main Target
The MF120 Eastern Monroe Pressure System (“MF120EM”) is a critical feeder main to the easterly and central 
portion of the RGE-Rochester franchise.  At Design Day conditions, the endpoint pressure of the MF120EM 
(also being the inlet to regulator stations RS468-Browncroft and RS470-Blossom) is at 74-psig, approximately 
61% MAOP.  This project will increase feeder main and distribution main pressures, capacity and reliability for 
existing customers. 
II.
Description
The MF120 Eastern Monroe Pressure System is located in the northeast quadrant of Monroe County and 
serves multiple municipalities, including: the towns of Henrietta, Pittsford, Perinton, Penfield, Webster and the 
City of Rochester. 
The MF120EM System Improvements include the replacement of approximately 22,000 linear feet of 8-inch 
feeder main (FM-3) with 12-inch wrapped steel main on the MF120 Eastern Monroe System (120-psig MAOP) 
along State Road from Five Mile Line Road/Plank Road to Salt Road.  The project also includes de-rating 
approximately 7,000 linear feet of the existing MF120EM system and tying it over to the MF60 Northeast 
System (60-psig MAOP). 
III.
Driver
The two terminal points of the MF120EM pressure system are regulator stations RS468-Browncroft and 
RS470-Blossom. These two regulator stations directly feed, and account for approximately 25% of all the gas 
that travels through the MF60 Northwest Pressure System.  The current 12-inch, 16-inch and 20-inch MF120 
Eastern Monroe mains located along Whalen Road, Browncroft Road and Blossom Road are operationally at 
their capacity. Additionally, the current 14-psig pressure differential is the lowest recommended value for 
adequate regulator equipment performance at RS468 and RS470.  This project, in conjunction with the 
proposed CM3D improvements, will increase the MF120 Eastern Monroe endpoints to above 76% MAOP on 
Design Day conditions as shown in Table 1. 
In addition to reinforcing the MF120 Eastern Monroe and MF60 Northeast pressure systems, the project also 
addresses asset condition, as the proposed 8-inch main to be cut-dead was installed prior to 1961 and 
qualifies as Leak Prone Main.

MF60 SOUTHEAST SYSTEM IMPROVEMENTS, PHASE I-III 
I.
Main Target
The MF60 Southeast System Improvement (Phase I-III) is a multi-phased system reliability project intended to 
increase pressures, capacity and reliability to an area of the system that has grown in recent years.  
II.
Description
The MF60 Southeast Pressure System extends over two counties (Monroe and Ontario), and primarily serves 
the municipalities of Pittsford, Perinton, Rush, Mendon, Victor and Farmington.  Farmington is the 
southeasterly bounds of the RGE-Rochester franchise and represents the low pressure point of the system.  At 
Design Day conditions, the endpoint pressures are approximately 36 psig (60% MAOP).  The system is 
adjacent to NYSEG-Geneva at the Town of Canandaigua/ Town of Farmington town line.  
The MF60 Southeast System Infrastructure Improvements include the following Phases: 
A. Phase 1 (Mendon Gate - Rte 66), Install Gas Main
Install approximately 14,800 LF of 12-inch WRST main, from the outlet of Regulator Station 428 (at the
Mendon Gate Station), crossing west and continuing south along NYS Rte. 66 to the intersection of NYS
Rte. 251.  The project will include the rebuild of Regulator Station 428 to accommodate the additional
capacity.  The project will also include crossing NYS Thruway (I-90).
B. Phase II (Willis Hill Road), Install Gas Main
(Note: this phase was completed in 2020 due to a municipal highway project)
Install approximately 8,300 linear feet of 8-inch MDPE main along Willis Hill Road, from NYS Rte. 251 to
Dryer Road.
C. Phase III (Malone Road), Install Gas Main
Install approximately 19,000 linear feet of 6-inch MDPE main along Dryer Road, Malone Road and
Boughton Hill Road, from Willis Hill Road to NYS Rte. 441.
III.
Driver
The MF60 Southeast System has been an area of active growth, particularly in the towns of Victor and 
Farmington.  According to the US Census Bureau, from 1990 to 2010, the population of the Town of Victor and 
Farmington has increased 98.5% and 13.9%, respectively.  Annualized over twenty years, these are 4.9% and

CM1 PIPELINE: SECTION 4, Chili GS to Ballantyne Rd, REPLACE GAS MAINS, 
ROCHESTER 
 
I. 
Main Target 
 
This asset condition and reliability risk project will replace the CM-1 20-inch 1951 gas transmission main with a 
new 16-inch wrapped steel gas main from Chili Gate Station (GS), northeast to Ballantyne Rd. With the recent 
construction of the CM-5 transmission pipeline in 2017, this section of the CM-1 is no longer needed to support 
the RG&E transmission system that operates at 250-psig. 
 
The new pipeline will be constructed for an MAOP of 120psig. It is valved off from the remaining CM-1 sections 
north of Ballantyne Rd, and will be connected to the MF120 Western Monroe pressure system.   This project 
will improve the reliability of the MF120 Western Monroe pressure system. District Regulator station 500 is at 
the west end of this section of the CM-1 and moves distribution gas from the regulator outlet northeast toward 
Ballantyne Rd. This project decreases reliance on the Buffalo Rd stations to support the MF120, by 
approximately 350 mcfh (the flow provided by district regulator station 500). 
 
The Buffalo Road Regulator Station is a major distribution hub located in the Rochester District feeding four 
systems: SF115 Mt Read Blvd, SF180 CM1B, MF120 Western Monroe, and the MF99 East Station systems. 
70% MAOP at the inlet to Buffalo Road is insufficient to feed these 4 systems for the design hour. The area 
served by this station has been experiencing significant load growth due to customer requests for CNG fleet 
fueling stations, coal conversion, and other residential and commercial growth. 
 
II. 
Description 
 
The existing 20-inch steel gas transmission main from the Chili gate station to Ballantyne Rd will be replaced 
with 16-inch steel gas pipe to operate at a maximum allowable operating pressure of 120-psig. The 16-inch 
pipeline will be designed for less than 20% SMYS. 
 
The new 16-inch pipeline will tie into the Chili GS and CM5 at Humphrey Rd. The entire project will be 
completed in the Towns of Chili and Gates. 
 
III. Driver 
 
The main replacement of the CM-1 between the Chili GS and Ballantyne Rd would best serve to support the 
existing MF120 Western Monroe pressure system, as the CM-5 is now the major feed from the Caledonia East 
and Chili GS toward the Buffalo Rd regulator hub. The location of this project helps to balance the pressure 
system regulators due to changes in customer needs on the MF120 system. The project also improves overall 
transmission and feeder system reliability and system pressures at major distribution regulating stations in 
accordance with Avangrid’s Gas System Planning Manual criteria, since it adds an extra source of pressure to 
existing regulators 461 and 462. Inlet pressures would be supported at the Buffalo Rd Regulator Station, RS 
320 Whalen Rd, RS 319 Salt Rd, RS 468 Browncroft Blvd, RS 470 Blossom Rd and RS 369 East Station.

V. 
Other Alternatives Considered 
 
Alternate locations and routes for the new 16-inch pipeline were considered and rejected due to the location of 
the new Empire West Chili GS and available space in the new CM-5 corridor. Other routes would increase the 
overall pipeline length and have higher construction costs to tie-in to the Chili gate station. 
 
CM-1 pipeline repair and MAOP increase is not considered a viable alternative to replacement. The basis for 
not repairing and uprating the CM-1 pipeline to 330psig is due to the need for more system support for 
customers on the 120psig, 60psig, and 48psig pressure systems. 
 
VI. Customer Benefits 
 
The installation of 16-inch wrapped steel gas transmission pipeline better accommodates evolving customer 
load demand, and allows RG&E to maintain adequate system pressure at the Buffalo Rd regulator station. 
Regulator 500 supplements the support for the MF120 Western Monroe system supplied by RS 461 and RS 
462, thereby increasing the pressure at the inlet to the Buffalo Rd station hub, the termination point of the 
CM1, CM4 and CM5 pipelines. Safety is also improved by the replacement of the existing pipeline. 
 
VII. Project Costs 
 
The estimated cost for the CM-1 replacement project is $18,200,000.

SF115 MT READ, REPLACE GAS MAINS, ROCHESTER 
I.
Main Target
This project involves the replacement of approximately 3,000 LF of 20-inch and 24-inch steel gas mains on 
the SF115 R343 MT READ gas pressure system along Mt. Read Blvd and Buffalo Rd, in the City of 
Rochester and the Town of Gates. The project will improve system reliability through the 115-psig pressure 
system as well as maintain safe and adequate operation of 60-psig and 14-psig regulators fed by this 
pressure system. 
II.
Description
The existing 20-inch steel gas mains along Buffalo Rd from the outlet of Regulator 343 to Mt Read Blvd, 
continuing north along Mt Read Blvd to the inlet of Regulator 341 will be replaced with 20-inch and 24-inch 
steel mains, with the requirement of providing enough gas to areas supported by the SF115 R343 Mt Read 
pressure system. This new pipe will increase the available inlet pressure at the north end of the existing 
pressure system and allow regulator station 463 to optimally manage downstream customer demand. These 
new mains will be designed and tested to be able to operate at 120-psig in the future, allowing for the 
interconnection with the MF120 Western Monroe pressure system, to provide more reliability to that system 
as well. The entire project will be completed in the town of Gates and City of Rochester, NY. 
III.
Driver
This project is categorized as asset condition, as related to prioritization purposes. RG&E will replace and 
relocate the existing 20-inch steel gas mains with new 20-inch and 24-inch steel pipe to accommodate current 
system loads. This project will also help in maintaining safe operation of 60-psig and 14-psig pressure 
systems connected to the 115-psig pressure system currently. The mains replaced as part of this project will 
also be uprated to 120-psig, and will be tied in to the MF120 Western Monroe pressure system, extending the 
120-psig pipe network, and improving reliability for many systems and customers. The scope and estimated
costs for this project have been identified (see system pressure maps at the conclusion of this report). Based
on hydraulic network analysis, the improvement project increases the pressure at the north end of the project
by approximately 12-psig (from 96-psig to 108-psig) at design day conditions.
IV.
NPA Screening
Please see attached NPA Suitability Screening Form. 
V.
Other Alternatives Considered
Avoidance of the proposed gas main replacement within this project scope would eventually compromise 
system reliability to multiple downstream pressure systems, as well as existing large commercial customers

VI.
Customer Benefits
The installation of 20-inch and 24-inch steel gas mains will better accommodate system reliability 
requirements to downstream pressure systems, as well as allow RG&E to maintain adequate system 
pressure through the entire SF115 R343 MT READ system. The new mains installed will improve the 
distribution of gas through the SF115 R343 MT READ pressure system enough so that 70% of system MAOP 
can be maintained on RG&E’s peak gas load design day, in accordance with Avangrid’s Gas System 
Planning Manual criteria. It will also improve gas pressure at the inlet of downstream regulators that control 
60-psig pressure systems (thus improving the performance of the regulator equipment due to the acceptable
range of pressure in which the regulator will operate without issue). This project will benefit approximately
60,000 residential, commercial, and industrial customers.

The estimated cost for the SF115 R343 Mt Read project is $2,750,000. Although asset condition projects 
typically have negative net present values, they allow the company to continue to meet its obligation to serve 
customers in the franchise while maintaining system pressures on the design day that are adequate for safe 
and reliable service at the customer meter.
