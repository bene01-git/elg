INTRODUCTION
This reply to the Company’s and Staff’s Statements in Support of the Joint Proposal follows on
themes first raised in Staff’s testimony for Gas, Electric and Shared Services CapEx projects
regarding the lack of justification for expenditures proposed in the Companies’ initial filings.
Fossil Free Tompkins (FFT) subsequently highlighted these concerns in our Rebuttal Testimony1,
in which we suggested that in light of the deficiencies noted in Staff’s testimony, the Companies’
proposal should be withdrawn and revised or rejected. Our concerns were subsequently detailed
in the Motion To Dismiss2 signed by seven consumer and environmental advocacy parties, in
which the advocates argued that the Companies’ filings did not meet the burden of proof required
in 16 NYCRR § 61 to show that their proposed rates are just and reasonable. To date there has
been no ruling on that motion.
Now, in our reply to the Companies’ and Staff’s Statements in Support for the JP, we
demonstrate that the deficiencies identified in Staff’s original CapEx testimony have not been
cured in the JP, nor in the Companies’ or Staff’s Statements of Support, nor in DPS replies to
recent IR’s regarding justification of CapEx project expenditures. Thus we submit that once
again, the record is incomplete and the Companies’ burden of proof has not been met. Without
such evidence, there is no rational basis for supporting a significant portion of the JP. Instead we
urge that the JP be rejected in favor of a one-year litigated case.
STANDARD OF REVIEW
The Procedural Guidelines for Settlements states:
In the case of proposals which could affect materially a utility’s rates or charges, the
proposals shall be supported by documentation of the quality and detail required for
major rate case filings. This supporting data is required even if the filing is, in the first
instance, a proposal for a rate settlement but not in the form of a traditional rate case
filing. In all rate proceedings, the supporting documentation required shall include the
relevant information discussed in Part 61 of the Commission’s Rules of Procedure, 16
NYCRR.3
As noted in the previously filed Motion to Dismiss:

DISCUSSION
The Commission Rules of Procedure 16 NYCRR § 61 make clear that the burden of proof rests
with the Utility, and the PSC’s Settlement Guidelines make clear those same standards apply to
negotiated agreements. As noted in each CapEx section above, the deficiencies noted in Staff’s
original capex testimony and highlighted in the Motion to Dismiss remain. Neither the JP nor
the Companies’ or Staff’s Statements in Support of the JP have fortified the record to provide
evidence required under 16 NYCRR § 61 to justify the proposed capital expenditures.

That the record was deficient initially was troubling enough when considering the cost of the
one-year rate increases being proposed. Now, with no further evidence to remedy the
deficiencies, it is incomprehensible that a 3-year negotiated agreement that obligates ratepayers
to increase far greater than contemplated before will serve ratepayers better than the outcome of
a one-year litigated proceeding. There can literally be no rational basis for a decision supporting
this Joint Proposal.
We urge that the Joint Proposal be denied and that, after allowing adequate time for preparation,
litigation should commence. Further, in light of the extraordinary delays that have resulted from
the Companies’ failure to file a complete record, we ask that any make-whole be denied.
