Abstract 
Naturally occurring radioactive material (NORM), which is present in the oil and 
gas industry, can pose a risk to the workers engaged in the activities in this field. 
Nevertheless, to our knowledge, there is no study on NORM presence and NORM 
measurement in liquefied natural gas (LNG) fuel systems and installations. 
Therefore, the question on whether NORM, particularly radon and radon progeny, 
can pose a risk to the health of employees, is still open. This study provides the 
initial base for further investigation and real NORM measurement. In this study, all 
stages of radon and radon decay products movement and deposition starting from 
natural gas production till the final destination are analyzed and reviewed. Several 
techniques for NORM detection and measurement are proposed. In the case if radon 
levels exceed the reference level, several actions towards radon reduction are 
proposed. 
Key words 
LNG, TENORM, NORM, Detection, Contamination, Radon, Systems Engineering.

1 Introduction 
1.1 Background and problem description 
Liquefied Natural Gas (LNG) fuel, which is an oil and gas industry product, has 
captured particular attention recently. The reason behind increased attention to LNG 
fuel systems is current environmental issues resulting in demand for less greenhouse 
effect polluting fuels in the maritime and land-based industries. Compared to other 
fuels, LNG-based combustion processes produce fewer sulfur emissions; therefore, 
LNG is considered as a possible fuel option [1]. However, according to the life-
cycle study of different marine fuels [2], where global warming potential (GWP) of 
fuels over 20-year and 100-year time frame was evaluated, it was found that LNG 
positive impact on climate is either questionable or LNG releases even more 
emissions than other types of marine fuel. Nevertheless, over the past two decades, 
LNG technologies have been significantly developed resulting in an increase of 
LNG technologies efficiency [3]. Therefore, LNG is considered as a fuel of the 
future, and there is a huge potential of LNG technologies to be improved further. 
Various ferry and shipping companies mentioned by [4] such as Destination 
Gotland, Furetank, and Viking Line have already adopted LNG-fueled engines. 
Besides, a new LNG tank terminal is now projected in the port of Oxelösund, 
Sweden [5]. However, as it was mentioned before, LNG is the oil and gas industry 
product which implies the possibility of naturally occurring radioactive material 
(NORM) contamination of LNG fuel systems. NORM is the term referred to 
material containing radionuclides which can be found in the environment. The 
radionuclides of interest are uranium-238 (238U), uranium-235 (235U) and thorium-
232 (232Th), and their radioactive decay products such as radium and radon. NORM 
can present in various industries such as the coal industry and metal mining, 
including the oil and gas industry [6]. LNG fuel systems onboard vessels may 
contain radon and radon decay products which may have an adverse effect on 
human health. There is no short-term effect caused by NORM presenting in LNG 
fuel systems; nevertheless, exposure to radiation increases the risk of long-term 
effects such as cancer [7].  
 
Figure 1 Typical LNG refinery diagram [51]. 
The reason behind the presence of NORM in final LNG product, particularly radon 
and radon decay products, is due to radon presence in sedimentary rocks from where

oil and gas is usually extracted. After extraction, numerous treatment steps take 
place, that can be seen in Figure 1, such as absorption via amine solvents in order to 
remove particular gases such as carbon dioxide and hydrogen sulfide, elimination of 
water vapor with the help of dehydration process where molecular sieve is used, and 
removal of mercury commonly via charcoal columns [8]. During treatment steps, 
radon and radon decay products, particularly Pb-210 [9], can be accumulated on 
molecular sieve or any other used material. Radon and radon progeny, particularly 
Po-210 and Pb-210 [8], can also be captured by charcoal columns. In addition, some 
amount of radon tends to accumulate in liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) [10], which 
is a derivative of natural gas as it can be seen in Figure 1. Nevertheless, despite 
purification processes, certain concentrations of radon decay products in LNG were 
recorded [11]. The range of radon concentration varied a lot starting from less than 
1 pCi/L and reaching up to 23 pCi/L. Therefore, there is a possibility of radon and 
radon progeny presence in LNG. Moreover, taking into consideration the 
accumulative nature of radon decay products, namely the accumulation of radon 
daughters on the inner parts of LNG equipment, there is a risk of health-threatening 
levels of radiation to which workers can be exposed.  
 
According to the Swedish law, the employer shall examine the working conditions 
and evaluate possible risks of any person being affected by ill-health or accidents at 
work on a regular basis [12]; and is responsible for implementing the measures 
necessary to prevent ill-health and accidents at work [13]. Taking into consideration 
all the aforementioned facts, the importance of possessing information regarding 
NORM presence in LNG fuel systems is apparent. However, according to [4], “the 
Swedish government has little, if any, knowledge about whether NORM is present in 
LNG fuel systems”. This statement is further supported by the e-mail 
correspondence between the Swedish national agencies: Linnaeus University, 
Swedish Radiation Safety Authority, Swedish Work Environment Authority, 
Swedish Transport Agency, and Swedish Energy Agency [14] – [31]. Therefore, a 
thorough investigation is required in order to address gaps in knowledge regarding 
the existence of NORM in LNG fuel systems and provide a safe work environment. 
1.2 Hypothesis 
Taking into consideration the aforementioned facts, the author postulates the 
following hypothesis: 
 
There is a risk of NORM presence and contamination of LNG systems, and there 
are certain locations in LNG systems where radon and radon progeny are more 
likely to be observed. Therefore, there is a risk of radiation levels posing a threat to 
a safe work environment.

2 Theory 
2.1 NORM, radon and radiation types 
2.1.1 
NORM and TENORM 
According to the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) Safety Glossary 
[41], naturally occurring radioactive material is defined as "a radioactive material 
containing no significant amounts of radionuclides other than naturally occurring 
radionuclides". NORM, among which are uranium-238 (238U), uranium-235 (235U) 
and thorium-232 (232Th) which have been mentioned before, have been present in 
the Earth’s crust since the Earth formation. Radionuclides are not stable and tend to 
undergo radioactive decay process after which nuclides become stable and stop 
emitting radiation. Nevertheless, due to the fact that some radioactive elements 
decay slowly, i.e. these elements have a long half-life, they are still present in rock 
and soil. Due to certain human activities such as oil and gas extraction, naturally 
occurring radioactive materials tend to concentrate or, in other words, tend to 
become “technologically enhanced” and are exposed to the human environment. 
This phenomenon is known as Technologically Enhanced Naturally Occurring 
Radioactive Material (TENORM), and the National Academy of Sciences defines 
TENORM as “any naturally occurring radioactive materials not subject to 
regulation under the Atomic Energy Act whose radionuclide concentrations or 
potential for human exposure have been increased above levels encountered in the 
natural state by human activities”. According to [42], total amounts of produced 
TENORM in the United States per year may exceed 1 billion; thus, TENORM 
constitutes a challenge for particular industries.  
2.1.2 
Radon and radiation types 
There are several different isotopes of radon; however, the one which poses a threat 
to human health is radon-222. Radon-222 (222Rn), which is a result of decay 
processes chain starting from uranium-238 (238U), is a radionuclide with a half-life 
of approximately 3.8 days [6]. The decay chain can be seen in Figure 3. 
The decay rate or activity of any radioactive element can be measured in becquerels 
(Bq), where 1 Bq is one radioactive disintegration per second, or in curie (Ci), or to 
be more precise picocuries (pCi) - 2.2 disintegrations per minute. Thus, in order to 
measure the activity concentration of radon, the unit of Bq/m3 or pCi/L is used [43]. 
Radon is a colorless inert gas which is soluble in water and organic solvents. 
Despite the fact that radon is gas, its decay products are solid particles that can be 
inhaled and deposited in the lung increasing the potential for lung cancer. Radon 
daughters being accumulated on the lung emit a different type of radiation, namely 
alpha and beta radiations as well as weak gamma radiation [6].

Different types of radiation have different penetrating power. Alpha particles can 
travel only a few centimeters and do not constitute a danger since alpha radiation 
cannot penetrate the skin. However, alpha radiation can be harmful when 
radioactive material emitting alpha particles is inhaled, swallowed, or absorbed 
through open wounds. Beta particles can be stopped by a piece of clothing. 
However, compared to alpha particles, beta particles can penetrate human skin to 
some extent and cause skin injuries if the skin is exposed to beta radiation for a long 
period of time. Gamma radiation can travel large distances and has the strongest 
penetration power [45]. Among all aforementioned types of radiation, alpha and 
beta radiation are the ones of interest since alpha and beta particles can be inhaled 
during the maintenance process.  
2.2 Natural gas exploration and treatment  
2.2.1 
Natural gas exploration process 
In order to find out whether natural gas reserves are present in rock, geologists use 
several methods. First of all, there is an observation of surface rocks called 
geological examination or geological survey giving an idea about underground 
formations. In the mid 1800s, it was understood that certain slopes called anticlinal 
slopes where the earth has folded up on itself have a greater likelihood of containing 
natural gas compared to other areas. Natural gas can move toward the surface of the 
earth thus a large portion of natural gas, more specifically methane, will dissipate 


--- Page 15 ---
 
9(68) 
 
into the air. However, a significant amount of natural gas will also be trapped under 
impermeable formation of rock through which natural gas will not be able to escape. 
There are various types of such formations; however, the most common one is dome 
shape impermeable rock layers which look like an umbrella capturing the natural 
gas trying to reach the surface, and which is illustrated in Figure 4. Therefore, a 
geological survey is an essential step which helps to explore possible natural gas 
deposits. Once a location that has a probability to contain natural gas or petroleum 
reserves is set, further tests can be carried in order to obtain more data regarding 
area with natural gas potential [46].  
 
Figure 4 Rock formation [46]. 
Seismic survey is one of the further steps which should be taken to get more 
detailed data. In old days, explosive materials were used such as dynamite in order 
to set small explosions and create seismic waves which further were caught by a 
special device called geophones, and received data was interpreted by geophysicists, 
geologists, and petroleum engineers. Nevertheless, nowadays, due to technology 
improvement, non-explosive seismic technology is used which also helps to avoid 
any environmental damage. Special vehicle is carrying equipment which creates 
vibrations, and, in turn, these vibrations create seismic waves which help to receive 
useful data. The same approach is applied for offshore exploration where instead of 
the truck there is a ship, and instead of the geophone, there is hydrophone [46]. 
 
In addition to the seismic survey, there are other means of obtaining data such as the 
use of magnetometers. The earth’s magnetic field is not uniform and differs 
depending on location. The difference in magnetism can be caused by several 
reasons one of which is the differing nature of rock formations. Therefore, oil and 
gas deposits may affect the magnetic field, and variance can be observed via 
magnetometer – a device measuring even the small variance in the magnetic field 
[46].  
 
When obtained data shows a high probability of petroleum and gas reserves in the 
formation rocks, exploratory well can be drilled in order to get more precise data on 


--- Page 16 ---
 
10(68) 
 
deposits. Afterward, the logging procedure, which is an ongoing record of the 
physical characteristics of rock and fluid with the help of the logging tool, is carried.  
 
There are over a hundred types of logging, and some of the most common are 
standard logging and electric logging. During standard logging, the physical 
properties of a well can be evaluated. For instance, the drill cuttings which are 
pieces of rock that are generated when a well is drilled, allow geologists to analyze 
the material. In the case of electric logging, electric current is run through the rock 
layers measuring the electrical resistance. The electrical logging helps geologists to 
obtain data regarding fluid content and properties. Recently, a novel method which 
is logging while drilling (LWD), also called measurement while drilling (MWD), 
provides the same data about the well as previously mentioned techniques. 
However, instead of lowering the logging tool into the well at the end of wireline 
cable, the logging tool is incorporated into the drill string [46]. In the case if logging 
provides positive data regarding oil and natural gas deposits, production process 
takes place.  
2.2.2 
Production of natural gas and radon escape mechanism 
Oil and gas can be found in any porous rock but in most of the cases it is discovered 
in sedimentary rocks such as sandstone or limestone. Usually, the oil and gas 
reservoirs are located on the top of a layer of interstitial water. Uranium, which is a 
radioactive element, is present in the sedimentary rock [6]. One of the reasons 
behind the presence of uranium, to be more precise uranyl ion, is the physical 
adsorption of uranyl ion on the surface of organic compounds which are also present 
in sedimentary rock [47]. Two of uranium decay products, namely radium and 
radon, can escape from the rock and be dissolved in the interstitial fluids. One of the 
main reasons behind radon emanation, i.e. release of radon from the mineral grain to 
the pore space where radon can diffuse further, is based on recoil phenomenon. 
During radium decay, alpha particle, which is helium nucleus, is ejected. When the 
alpha particle is ejected, radon atom which has been formed recoils in the opposite 
direction which can be observed in Figure 5 [48].  
 
Figure 5 Radium nuclide decay mechanism [48]. 
The process resembles a recoil of the rifle after the shot. The release of radon to the 
pore space depends on several factors: the location of radium atom, i.e. whether it is 
close or far from the surface of a mineral sample, and direction of recoiled radon 
atom, i.e. whether it recoils in direction to the surface of the grain or against the 
surface. When all conditions are favorable, i.e. radium atom is located near the 


--- Page 17 ---
 
11(68) 
 
surface of the mineral grain, and radon recoils toward the surface, radon atom can 
escape to the pore. However, frequently, due to the fact that recoiled radon atom has 
substantial energy, it can travel all the way through the pore space, and enter in 
nearby mineral grains. Nevertheless, in the case if pore space is filled with water, 
radon tends to remain in pore space. Due to the higher density compared to air, 
water slows down the speed of radon thus preventing radon atom to travel further 
and eventually captures it [48]. The process is illustrated in Figure 6.

It is also worth mentioning that radon is gas, in contrast to uranium and radium 
which are solids, implying that radon more easily escapes mineral grain and then 
moves further with natural gas in pore space. In order to retrieve the gas trapped in 
rock formations, well should be drilled, and certain equipment is installed in the 
well such as well casing, wellhead, and installation of lifting equipment. The 
equipment plays a crucial role for further steps: for instance, well casing contributes 
to the strengthening of the wellbore sides, and natural gas can be brought to the 
surface without being lost by entering to rock layers on the way up. After all the 
necessary procedures are completed, well may start to produce natural gas. Natural 
gas located in pressurized formations will naturally rise up through established well, 
especially in the case when a deposit consists of only natural gas or together with a 
light condensate. This case is called a primary recovery. However, sometimes 
special treatment is needed in order to provide an effective flow of hydrocarbons 
through the well. Such treatment may include injection of water, gases such as CO2, 
or an acid such as hydrochloric, into the well to open up the formation and allow 
hydrocarbons to flow through [46]. One of the most common technologies is 
hydraulic fracturing: the pressure of injected fluid, which is usually the mixture of 
water (99.5%), sand and chemical additives (0.5%) “cracks” the fractures which are 
already present in the formation ensuring the release of trapped natural gas. Apart 
from water, as it has been mentioned before, carbon dioxide can also be used to 
crack the formation which has even more advantages compared to water as injected 


--- Page 18 ---
 
12(68) 
 
fluid. Injected CO2 and sand mixture fractures the formation through which desired 
hydrocarbons can flow through. Afterward, CO2 is vaporized, and only sand 
remains in the formation, holding the cracks, which have been enlarged and created 
by fracturing, open. Such a technique does not generate underground wastes, and 
also ensures protection of groundwater resources [49]. Another common technique 
is horizontal drilling: with the help of horizontal drilling, the area of a well can be 
increased without creating several vertical wells. One of the reasons behind the use 
of horizontal drilling is that the significant part of oil and gas deposits is 
concentrated in a horizontal dimension rather than in a vertical dimension. 
Therefore, with the help of horizontal drilling more natural gas can be extracted 
[50]. The extraction of natural gas is known as the production process or upstream. 
After extraction, natural gas transportation, or midstream, takes place. In the case of 
LNG, natural gas is first converted to LNG in the production plant which is near to 
the extraction area, and afterward is transported to the market, and all stages will be 
deeply examined in the following chapters. 
2.2.3 
Natural gas treatment  
After extraction, raw natural gas cannot be transported directly and should undergo 
some treatment processes such as removal of water vapor and solids, as well as 
natural gas liquids such as ethane, propane, and butane. After treatment, there are 
several ways to transport natural gas: pipelines, liquefied natural gas (LNG), 
compressed natural gas (CNG), gas to solids (GTS), gas to power (GTP), gas to 
liquids (GTL), and other ways [46]. Nevertheless, among all the aforementioned 
methods, only LNG and pipelines are fully commercial and mature technologies. 
GTL is commercial but requires certain improvements in order to enhance 
efficiency, and CNG together with GTS have not been put on a commercial scale 
yet. Therefore, natural gas is usually transported either by pipelines or by LNG 
cargo ships or trucks. The main criteria of choosing a particular method are based 
on distance and volume of extracted natural gas. LNG becomes a reasonable 
solution when natural gas should be transported for long distances, particularly for 
the routes which should be crossed via ships since undersea pipeline construction is 
not cost-efficient. In addition, in order to transfer a large amount of natural gas for 
long distances via pipelines, large diameter pipes and gas recompression facilities 
are needed which is also costly [51]. Nowadays, LNG is a fast-growing market, and, 
according to BP Statistical Review of World Energy 2019, the amount of natural gas 
transferred via LNG cargo ships and trucks in 2018 was 431 billion cubic meters 
whereas in 2008 it was only 234.9 billion cubic meters, which shows the substantial 
increase of LNG trade. The data is show in Table 1. Compared to 2017, LNG 
supplies increased by almost 10% (37 bcm) in 2018 due to the fact that new 
liquefaction plants in Australia, the United States and Russia were either started or 
the number of plants increased [52].

The traditional LNG plant is illustrated in Figure 1. Typically, first, the liquids from 
natural gas should be removed; therefore, the raw natural gas is sent to a slug 
catcher. Afterward, gas is sent to a high pressure (HP) separator whereas the liquids 
are fed to a medium pressure separator, where the hydrocarbon liquids are further 
separated and fed to the condensate stabilizer. The liquids which were fed to the 
condensate stabilizer are separated, and formed condensate comprises of C5 and 
other heavy hydrocarbons. As it can be seen, vapors from the medium separator and 
stabilizer column overhead are compressed and recycled back to the HP separator.  
 
The vapors then are fed to the Gas Sweetening Unit, GSU which is also called Acid 
Gas Removal Unit (AGRU), where hydrogen sulfide (H2S) and carbon dioxide 
(CO2) are removed. The hydrogen sulfide is removed with the help of an amine 
solvent in order to meet sulfur product requirement which is typically below 4 ppmv 
[51]. It is important to remove H2S since it contributes to general corrosion, which is 
also called uniform corrosion; pitting corrosion, which causes the formation of 
cavities in the material; and acid corrosion, which results in crack or blisters. In 
addition, H2S can be emitted through crack having a detrimental impact on people’s 
health and damaging to the environment [53]. Another compound, which is CO2, 
should be decreased to 50 ppmv in order to avoid CO2 freezing which will cause 
heat exchanger plugging and other operational problems [54].  
 
The acid gas is fed to the Sulfur Recovery Unit (SRU) which is usually comprised 
of a Claus unit and a Tail Gas Treating Unit (TGTU), and the off-gas from the 
TGTU is sent to the atmosphere. The sweet gas from GSU is sent to the Gas 


--- Page 20 ---
 
14(68) 
 
Dehydration Unit (GDU) in order to decrease the amount of water vapor to below 
0.1 ppmv [51]. Water should be removed since water vapor freezes and may 
damage the main liquefaction exchanger [55]. For GDU process, molecular sieves 
are utilized. In hot climate operation, the water content in the sweet gas can be 
substantial; therefore, the sweet gas can be chilled first in order to remove most of 
the water prior to the molecular sieve unit. This procedure, i.e. the sweet gas 
chilling, is more energy-efficient and cost-effective rather than direct feeding of 
saturated sweet gas to the molecular sieve unit. As it can be seen from the scheme, 
there is also Mercaptans Removal Unit where mercaptans from the dried gas can be 
removed via molecular sieve in order to meet the sulfur standards [51].  
 
Afterward, the gas is sent to the Mercury Removal Unit (MRU), where mercury 
(Hg) must be reduced to less than 10 nanograms per cubic meter. The presence of 
mercury has a detrimental effect on brazed-aluminum heat exchangers that are used 
in cryogenic systems causing equipment corrosion used. On top of that, mercury can 
be a threat to the environment and safety. There are numerous methods of mercury 
removal, and one of the most common methods is the use of the sulfur which reacts 
with mercury and form a stable compound on the sorbent surface. The support 
material is usually activated carbon, and sulfur-impregnated activated carbon is an 
established commercial process in natural gas refineries [51]. Nevertheless, other 
trapping agents such as metal sulfides, silver and gold, and other support materials 
or carrier agents such as alumina and zeolite can be used [56].  
 
After MRU, treated gas is fed to Natural Gas Liquid (NGL) Recovery Unit where 
C2+ or C3+ are removed and recovered, and a lean gas, i.e. a gas which contains 
mainly methane, is further liquefied. The use of NGL Recovery Unit excludes the 
need in the scrubber column where aromatics and heavy hydrocarbons are removed 
in order to avoid waxing phenomena in the main exchanger. There can be also 
ethane recovery where ethane is recovered and further supplied to the petrochemical 
refinery. The heavy hydrocarbons, namely C3-C5+ liquids, are also separated: 
propane and butane can be exported as separate products, or as liquefied petroleum 
gas (LPG), and C5+ and heavier components can be used for gasoline blending [51]. 
 
After NGL Recovery Unit, methane gas is fed to the Liquefaction Unit where a 
refrigeration process occurs. During liquefaction process, a refrigerant takes the heat 
from the gas: the process is based on the principle of a refrigeration cycle where a 
refrigerant undergoes cyclical expansion and compression, and turns the gas into 
liquid by decreasing its temperature to cryogenic one [51]. The lighter 
hydrocarbons, or lean gas, is fed to the main cryogenic heat exchanger (MCHE) in 
order to convert lean gas to LNG. Nevertheless, not only lean gas but undesired 
heavy compounds may also enter MCHE which may constitute a substantial 
operational problem. These heavy compounds can freeze and accumulate in the 
MCHE, leading to the decrease of the heat transfer efficiency and increase of 
operational costs. In the case of the continued build-up of solids in the MCHE, a 
blockage and a shutdown of the plant may occur [57]. In addition, there is a case 
study on MCHE failure which was caused by mercury embrittlement leading to the 
shutdown of refrigeration compressors for 6 weeks which, in turn, led to the loss of 
LNG production [58]. 
 


--- Page 21 ---
 
15(68) 
 
After the lean gas is liquefied, a nitrogen removal is required in the case if the 
nitrogen content exceeds the commercial specification of LNG which is typically 
below 1 mole per cent. There are several reasons behind the need for nitrogen 
removal: the presence of nitrogen in LNG does not allow to meet a higher heating 
value (HHV) requirement set for LNG product; and may cause so-called “rollover” 
phenomenon. Nitrogen has no positive impact on HHV and needs to be removed so 
LNG product specification can be met. In addition, due to the fact that natural gas 
trade is based on a heat content rather than on weight, nitrogen fills up valuable 
space, and thus should be eliminated. Another problem that appears when nitrogen 
content is higher than 1 mole per cent is called a rollover. The floor and walls of the 
tank are heated, and the heat is absorbed by the tank is transmitted, or more 
specifically, convected to the liquid. At the top layer, light gases such as nitrogen 
start to evaporate, and the liquid in the tank becomes denser. The evaporation taking 
place in LNG tanks is known as boil-off gas (BOG). When boil-off occurs, 
convection takes place where a bottom layer of warm and less dense liquid goes up 
to the interface. However, evaporation does not occur since the top layer exerts 
hydrostatic pressure on the bottom layer. Since top layer, or lighter layer, gets 
denser, and bottom layer, or heavier layer, gets lighter, at some point the density of 
two layers approach each other, and rollover occurs [51]. The process is illustrated 
in Figure 8. According to [59], even a little difference in temperature (less or around 
1 K) causes a sudden revolution of density layers in the tank. Due to the fact that the 
bottom layer is superheated, after rollover a substantial amount of vapor is released. 
One of the major risks of rollover is the over-pressurization of the tank. There is 
also a possibility of the failure of tank relief system due to the high evaporation rate 
which will eventually lead to LNG discharge into the atmosphere [51].

After all treatment processes, LNG is loaded either to trucks or to ships which are 
equipped with special cryogenic tanks and transported to the demand point.  
2.3 Radon presence in LNG refinery and LNG product 
2.3.1 
Radon in LNG refinery 
According to the review study [60], where records on radon and radon decay 
products were gathered, the concentration of radon in natural gas varies between 5 
Bq/m3 and 200,000 Bq/m3. Prior to entering refinery, according to [8], a certain 


--- Page 22 ---
 
16(68) 
 
amount of radon decay products, which were formed during transportation via 
pipeline since it takes time for natural gas to reach LNG plant, accumulates either 
on inner part of pipeline or at plant inlet filters. Radon, due to the fact that it is in 
gaseous state, is able to path through filter and enter to LNG refinery [8]. During 
certain treatment processes, radon and radon progeny can also be removed from 
natural gas. For instance, Pb-210 which is radon daughter, can be captured via 
molecular sieve during Gas Dehydration Unit process. According to the study [9], 
where the concentration of Pb-210 deposition on molecular sieve was estimated 
taking into consideration the adsorption equilibrium coefficients (0.037 for 
molecular sieve), the concentration of radon in the natural gas (100 to 20,000 
Bq/Nm3), the typical operating conditions in LNG refinery (5.5 MPaA) and the 
operation period of the adsorbents from its installation to exchange, the following 
data was obtained. In this research, two different standards were taken into account: 
IAEA NORM regulation with 1.0 Bq/g exemption level, and the International 
Association of Oil & Gas Producers (IOGP) NORM regulation with 0.2 Bq/g 
exemption level. The amount of accumulated Pb-210 has a positive correlation both 
with radon concentration in natural gas and the number of sieve operating years. In 
the case if radon concentration equals to 400 Bq/Nm3, even after 10 years of 
operation the level of deposited Pb-210 will not exceed 0.2 Bq/g. Nevertheless, in 
the case of 2,000 Bq/Nm3 Rn-222 concentration, the level of Pb-210 accumulation 
is below 1.0 Bq/g after 10 years of operation; however, at this radon concentration, 
it exceeds 0.2 Bq/g after 3 years. In case if radon concentration is 10,000 Bq/Nm3, it 
is over 1.0 Bq/g after 3 years. Experiment was also conducted on silica gel, and in 
the case of Rn-222 concentration of 10,000 Bq/Nm3, the level of Pb-210 
accumulation exceeds 1.0 Bq/g after one year [9]. Conducted experiment illustrates 
that sufficient amount of radon progeny can be found in treatment units.  
 
Another treatment unit where radon daughters tend to concentrate is Mercury 
Removal Unit. Radon progeny can be accumulated on activated charcoal bed, 
particularly Po-210 and Pb-210, due to the fact that the chemical properties of Po-
210 and Pb-210 are similar to those of mercury. Radon itself will also be trapped by 
charcoal [8]. Radon can also be captured by zeolite [61], one of the materials which 
is used in MRU. Nevertheless, compared to many types of materials such as 
molecular sieve, silica gel and alumina, charcoal possesses greater adsorption 
properties and capable of capturing greater amount of radon [62].  
 
Afterward, remained radon and radon decay products move to NGL unit. According 
to numerous data from natural gas refineries, severe NORM contamination of inner 
parts of pumps was observed in this unit, particularly in propane/propene fraction. 
Other types of equipment were affected as well, and thin invisible films of Pb-210 
were detected and reported [60]. Apart from invisible Pb-210 films, dark grey or 
black films can be observed in natural gas refineries due to contamination of short-
lived decay products of radon, namely Po-218, Pb-214, Bi-214 and Po-214, on inner 
parts of equipment. Some of these short-lived daughters are the reason of high-
energy gamma radiation emission and, consequently, the reason of external gamma 
radiation dose rates in the area of contaminated equipment [63]. In addition, low 
specific activity (LSA) sludge, which is a term for radioactive deposit, can be 
observed in different places such as pipeline bottom; nevertheless, LSA sludge tends 
to deposit in voluminous equipment such as NGL storage tanks, separator and

dehydration vessels, LPG delivery facilities. The deposition occurs mostly due to 
the long retention time [60].

The case of shutdown in natural gas refineries should also be taken into 
consideration. After the shutdown of a natural gas plant, the continuous flow of 
natural gas stops; therefore, short-lived radon daughters decay since their half-life 
varies between 160 microseconds and 27 minutes [8]. Thus, there is no gamma 
radiation emission; however, the long-lived decay products of radon such as Pb-210, 
Bi-210 and Po-210, remain on the inner part of the equipment. These radionuclides 
pose no threat due to weak gamma radiation but the alpha particles emitted by Po-
210 and Pb-210 pose a potential hazard if they are ingested or inhaled during the 
maintenance process [63]. Experiment conducted by [67] showed that during 
maintenance of LPG equipment an employee can be exposed to a significant amount 
of radiation. It was observed that a disassembled pump impeller from LPG refinery 
emitted a significant amount of combined gamma and beta radiation, and even after 
a year of storage impeller emitted a considerable number of alpha particles. The 
concentration of 30 pCi/L was detected during twelve minutes of maintenance 
whereas the limit for constant occupational exposure to Pb-210/Po-210 is 0.2 pCi/L 
for 40 hours per week.  
 
It is also worth mentioning that Pb-210 concentration was discovered in LPG rail 
tanks [69]. This study indicates the fact that NORM contamination can be deposited 
not only on natural gas facilities but also further on transportation equipment. 
Taking into consideration all aforementioned facts such as radon concentration in 
natural gas feed which can be substantially high, radon decay products deposition in 
treatment units and transportation facilities, it is apparent that radon concentration 
level in LNG should be investigated.  
2.3.2 
Final LNG product and radon concentration 
LNG mostly consists of methane which is between 87 mole per cent to 99 mole per 
cent. Other compounds such as ethane, propane, butane, as well as nitrogen and 
trace amounts of sulfur (less than 4 ppmv), and CO2 (around 50 ppmv) are also 
present in LNG. The content of LNG depends on the content of the initial gas source 
and the treatment processes to which gas was subjected. In general, LNG is an 
odorless, colorless, nontoxic and also noncorrosive cryogenic liquid (-169°C) at 1 
atm pressure. LNG produces very low particle emissions, i.e. emissions contain 
almost no sulfur oxides and low level of nitrogen oxides. In addition, compared to 
other hydrocarbon fuels, LNG releases substantially lower carbon emissions. 
Despite the fact that LNG is non-toxic, it may lead to asphyxiation in the case of an 
unventilated area where there is an absence of oxygen. In addition, LNG can be 
ignited in the presence of the right concentrations of air [51].  
 
To our knowledge, there has been only one record of radon and radon decay 
products presence in LNG [11]. According to the conducted study, the range of 
radon concentration varied a lot starting from less than 1 pCi/L and reaching up to 
23 pCi/L. The concentration of radon and radon decay products in LNG is based on 
the following assumptions: 


--- Page 25 ---
 
19(68) 
 
 
I. 
Radon concentration in the initial feed. It is assumed that there is a direct 
relation between initial radon concentration and radon concentration in the 
final LNG product. 
II. 
Natural gas flow rate in LNG plant. In the case of turbulent flow, some of 
lead atoms may stick to the inner surface of transportation system [70]. 
Therefore, it is assumed that the type and speed of flow in natural gas 
facility is directly related to the NORM concentration in final LNG product.  
III. 
The type of material used in treatment units. Due to the fact that 
different materials have different adsorption power, the concentration of 
radon in the final LNG product depends on how much of radon can be 
adsorbed during treatment processes. 
 
It is also assumed that even with insignificant radon and radon progeny 
concentration in LNG, over time radon and radon decay products concentration in 
LNG systems will increase due to the depositary nature of radon and radon 
daughters. Taking into consideration all facts which have been mentioned, it is 
extremely important to possess information regarding radon and radon decay 
products concentration deposited on the inner parts of equipment to ensure proper 
provision of safe work environment. In the following chapters, LNG systems such 
as LNG carrier, as well as maintenance procedure, together with information 
regarding where radon is more likely to be accumulated, will be examined in depth. 
2.4 LNG systems and maintenance 
2.4.1 
LNG carrier and NORM contamination 
The capacity of LNG ships may range between less than 30,000 m3 to around 
265,000 m3. However, the common capacity varies between 125,000 m3 and 
140,000 m3 which can contain from 58,000 to 65,000 tonnes of LNG. In order to 
transport LNG, special tank design is required which keeps LNG at cryogenic 
temperature and pressure which is just above atmospheric pressure. LNG ships are 
used in the case when a consumer is located in the coastal area. In the case of the 
inland location, LNG road tankers with double-skinned, vacuum insulated tanks are 
used [51].  
 
To our knowledge, there is no study conducted on NORM measurement and no 
records on NORM contamination of LNG carriers and tanks. Nevertheless, there is a 
study on deposition of Pb-210 in LPG rail cars, and author noted that “each batch of 
LPG will add new daughter activity to the dust except for that which remains in the 
gas itself and is discharged from the tank when it reaches its destination”, 
highlighting the depository nature of radon daughters [69]. It is assumed that radon 
and radon decay products tend to deposit more in LNG land-based storage tanks 
rather than in LNG ship tanks. The assumptions are based on the following facts: 
 
I. 
Radon density. LNG storage temperatures are much lower (-169°C) 
compared to LPG temperatures. LPG storage temperatures and pressures 
vary; nevertheless, LPG temperature does not go below -43°C [71]. At 


--- Page 26 ---
 
20(68) 
 
LPG temperatures, radon is either in the gaseous or the liquid state whereas 
in LNG radon is in the solid state; thus, the density of radon is much higher 
in LNG compared to LPG. Therefore, radon and radon decay products are 
more easily settled in LNG tank.  
II. 
Sea motion. It is assumed that radon and radon decay products tend to 
deposit more in LNG land-based storage tanks rather than in LNG ship 
tank. The reason behind the assumption is a motion of cargo during 
transportation, to be more precise the motion of sea. The motion of sea 
definitely affects the processes going on in LNG tank since the 
phenomenon of rollover which has been mentioned before does not 
constitute a problem for LNG cargo due to mixture of layers by sea motion 
[51]. Therefore, it can be assumed that it is more difficult for radon and 
radon decay solid particles to settle in LNG carrier tanks at sea, and part of 
them remain “floating” in LNG.  
III. 
Truck motion. The same assumption regarding the mixture of layers in 
LNG can be applied to LNG truck. During straight road and constant 
movement, it is assumed that LNG does not experience any shake and stays 
relatively stable. Nevertheless, when truck stops, starts movement or takes 
a turn, due to inertia phenomenon, LNG experiences certain movement. 
Therefore, particles tend to float in LNG; nevertheless, some portion of 
radon and radon decay daughters deposit on equipment both in LNG cargo 
and LNG truck. 
Despite the fact that rollover is not considered as a problem for LNG carrier, there is 
a case study on Moss Type LNG carrier which experienced the rollover phenomena. 
Nevertheless, there were several reasons behind occurred BOG such as loading of 
tanks with heavy LNG whereas a heel inside was of lighter density [72]. Under 
normal conditions, rollover does not pose an issue both for LNG cargo and LNG 
road trailer [51].  
 
When LNG cargo/LNG truck is unloaded, as it has been mentioned by [69], some 
amount of radon and radon daughters is discharged as well. One of the reasons 
behind is high flow rate where methane and other particles “kick out” deposited 
radon and radon progeny, and radon together with radon decay products follow 
LNG stream and appear in LNG storage tanks or directly in LNG fueled ship 
depending on whether it is shore-to-ship (STS)/port-to-ship (PTS) or truck-to-ship 
(TTS) bunkering method.  
2.4.2 
LNG systems contamination 
In order to identify the locations with a high probability of radon and radon decay 
products, it is important to possess information on the properties of radon and radon 
progeny and LNG. The behavior of solid particles in fluid depends on numerous 
parameters such as solid properties, such as size and density; on the properties of the 
fluid, such as density and viscosity; on the operation parameters of pipe flow such 
as flow velocity, flow direction and diameter of pipe [73]. According to study [74], 
where authors measured the size of radon decay particles in different environments 
and different modes, namely unattached and attached, diameter of radon and radon 


--- Page 27 ---
 
21(68) 
 
daughter particles did not exceed 1 µm. Therefore, radon and radon decay products 
are considered as submicron particles or nanoparticles.  
 
There are various factors that affect particle deposition such as gravitation, 
electrostatic interactions, gravitational forces, turbulence, and Brownian diffusion. 
However, Brownian diffusion is more significant for nanoparticles than for larger 
particles, leading to higher deposition rates. Thermophoresis is also more important 
for nanoparticles compared to other factors, and the smallest particles are affected 
the most due to their low inertia [75]. Gravitational forces are not dominant for 
nanoparticles: as it has been mentioned before, Brownian and diffusional forces 
together with thermophoresis have more effect on the deposition rate of 
nanoparticles. However, there is a study on radon and radon progeny deposition [76] 
where it is stated that the deposition rate of radon and radon progeny particles which 
are smaller than 100 nm was dominated by diffusion. For particles larger than 500 
nm, the main force that affected sedimentation rate was a gravitational force. There 
is another study where it is stated that in the case if particles have high density, 
gravity can become a significant driving force even if the size of particles is less 
than 100 nm [77]. Taking into consideration the fact that the density of radon and 
radon progeny particles is significantly high, and the diameter of particles varies 
greatly starting from 1nm in the case if radon and radon decay products are 
unattached, it is extremely challenging to predict the exact behavior of radon and 
radon daughters in LNG. Nevertheless, certain assumptions can be made based on 
the physics of flow and behavior of particles in the regions where both gravity and 
Brownian motion have a significant contribution to the deposition rate. Apart from 
radon and radon progeny physical properties, it is important to possess information 
on LNG flow properties since the behavior of particles also depends on the fluid. It 
is assumed that LNG is a Newtonian fluid, i.e. the relation between the shear stress 
and the shear rate is linear, and viscosity (µ) is independent of strain rate. The same 
assumption was stated in several studies such as [78] – [79]. In this case, the

regardless of the state of LNG, radon and radon progeny will have almost similar

for instance, in LNG regasification terminal, it is assumed that thermophoresis will 
facilitate the accumulation of radon and radon decay products on inner parts of 
equipment whereas in systems where LNG is mainly in the liquid state, 
thermophoresis will hinder the deposition process on the inner surface. The 
assumption will be discussed in detail in the following chapter.  
2.4.2.1 
LNG piping systems 
Piping system is one of the systems where radon and radon decay products can be 
observed. As it has been mentioned, when it comes to submicron or nanoparticles, 
Brownian diffusion and thermophoresis are the main factors affecting the deposition 


--- Page 29 ---
 
23(68) 
 
rate of the particles. Most of the studies are conducted on the thermophoresis 
phenomenon in hot gases in cold pipes, and there is a lack of knowledge on solid 
particle behavior in the cryogenic liquid. Nevertheless, some assumptions can be 
made based on the working principle of the thermophoresis and Brownian 
phenomenon and processes which are going on in LNG systems. 
 
The main principle of the thermophoresis phenomenon is the following: the 
thermophoresis depends on the temperature gradient in the surrounding gas 
molecules. The gas molecules coming from the hot side have a greater velocity and 
energy compared to gas molecules coming from the cold side. Therefore, the greater 
gas molecules from the hot side will have greater momentum and push the particle 
in the opposite direction, i.e. to the direction of the cool side [86]. Such 
thermophoresis is called “positive”. Nevertheless, there is also “negative 
thermophoresis” when solid particle moves from cold towards hot region. The 
mechanism behind negative thermophoresis is sophisticated and hasn’t been studied 
in-depth yet. However, there is a study where radon daughters were collected via 
thermophoresis and electrostatic attraction in personal dosimetry in uranium mines 
[87]. In the study, positive thermophoresis is used in order to collect radon 
daughters. Therefore, it is assumed that radon and radon progeny tend to move from 
hot side to cold one. For radon and radon progeny in LNG flow the following 
scenario is assumed: even though LNG piping system is insulated, there is still heat 
leakage from ambient environment [88]. Therefore, the temperature closer to the 
inner walls of pipes is higher compared to the temperature further from the walls 
towards to middle of the pipe. The particle near the wall with higher kinetic energy 
will “kick” radon and radon decay products away from the walls of the pipeline. 
However, one should keep in mind that thermophoresis is especially applicable to 
the cases when the temperature gradient is high, i.e. when the temperature 
difference between the wall of equipment and fluid is large, like in furnace. For the 
pieces of equipment where LNG is in the liquid state, it is assumed that the 
temperature difference is not really high, and there is a possibility for radon and 
radon decay products to be attached to the inner walls of pipe due to the strong 
turbulent nature of LNG flow and Brownian motion. It is also assumed that 
thermophoresis plays much more important role in LNG systems where LNG is in 
gaseous state (NG) due to the fact that turbulence is lower compared to LNG, and a 
temperature difference between walls and flow of NG is higher. Therefore, in 
straight pipes with NG flow, it is assumed that there will be higher deposition of 
radon and radon progeny. However, the concentration in straight pipe should be less 
both in NG and LNG systems compared to other locations. It is assumed that there 
are certain locations along the piping system where radon and radon progeny can be 
observed with a greater probability. The locations with greater probability are 
elbows, valves, t-bends or t-joints, sudden expansion and sudden contraction 
regions. The main reason behind assumption is a change in numerous parameters 
such as pressure, velocity, vortices and other parameters in these locations, and 
aforementioned changes can facilitate the accumulation of solid particles.  
 
The elbow of the pipe is considered as one of the locations where there is a 
probability of accumulation of radon and radon decay products. In the elbow, solid 
particles tend to separate from gas flow due to centrifugal forces. These particles 
tend to form a relatively dense phase structure in the elbow known as a rope. In the

contribute to deposition rate and Brownian diffusion plays the major role, there can 
be different scenarios which should be examined. 
 
The nanoparticle deposition in pipe or bend regions in turbulent flow has not been 
fully studied yet. There is a lack of experimental studies due to the fact that particle 
deposition behavior is complex and can be affected by numerous factors even in 
straight pipe, channels or ducts [95]. Nevertheless, according to the study [96], the 
minimum of deposition enhancement factor for nanoparticles occurs near the inside 
edge, and the maximum occurs near outside edge implying that outside edge will 
have more deposited radon and radon decay products. This actually corresponds to

Other areas in piping system where it is assumed that the accumulation of solid 
particles can be observed are locations around valves. In the study where 
computational fluid dynamics (CFD) modelling was used in order to investigate 
particle deposition in natural gas pipelines, correlation between ball valve opening 
and solid particles deposition ability was observed [97]. As the ball valve opening 
angle decreases, more energy loss or pressure drop can be observed due to increased 
vortices, which, in turn, leads to more solid particles deposition. Due to the pressure 
drop, the temperature of fluid decreases leading to retrograde condensation and 
formation of nuclei particles. Due to the turbulent flow, these particles are brought 
together and coagulation phenomenon occurs. Since radon and radon decay particles 
are already in solid state, condensation phenomenon is not applicable to radon and 
radon progeny in LNG. Nevertheless, it can be assumed that due to pressure drop 
and energy loss, as well as turbulent flow which makes radon decay products stick 
to the inner wall of the pipe as it has been mentioned before, downstream locations 
of valves are of particular interest.  
 
Sudden expansion or contraction are also regions where accumulation of radon and 
radon decay products may occur. In the location of sudden expansion, recirculating 
flow occurs where submicron particles can be captured and deposited [98]. 
According to study [74], which has been already mentioned, radon and radon 
progeny are submicron particles. Therefore, it is assumed that both unattached and 
attached radon decay products can be captured by recirculating flow. The same 
approach is applied to sudden contraction with contraction angle more than 60° 
since at angles less than 60° recirculation zone does not occur [99]. Nevertheless, 
there are various parameters which affect deposition efficiency in sudden expansion 
and contraction areas such as Stoke number, contraction ratio, contraction angle and 
other associated parameters.  
In general, for piping system, any region where LNG flow changes direction, 
including t-joints [100] or bifurcating tubes [101], is considered as region where 
there is a high probability of radon deposition on the inner part of walls. Apart from 
locations which have been noted, it is also assumed that radiation can be detected 
near pumps since, as it has been already noted in previous chapters, significant 
NORM contamination of inner parts of pumps was observed in certain gas 
processing units.  
 
When it comes to LNG regasification terminal which is another type of land-based 
system, LNG vaporizer or ambient air vaporizers (AAV) is assumed to contain 
some amount of radon decay products. LNG flows into the AAV fin tube and is 
converted to natural gas, i.e. regasification process occurs. At the same time, the 
temperature of the fin tube of AAV and the ambient air is decreased, and this results 
in frost formation as the operation time lasts. Temperature and partial pressure of 
water vapor are the main factor causing the frost formation. Water vapor is either 
condensed first and then freezes into frost crystal, or water vapor is converted into 
frost crystal directly [102]. As LNG is converted to natural gas in AAV, the 
temperature rises from around 114 K to the temperature which is slightly lower than

AAV inlet is thicker compared to the outlet [103]. Based on the provided 
information, several assumptions can be made: 
 
I. 
Radon and radon decay products can be observed in frost formation. 
Radon and radon decay products tend to attach to different substances 
including water vapor [104]. Therefore, it is assumed that radon and radon 
decay products can deposit on inner parts of AAV and constitute a part of 
frost formation. Nevertheless, it is assumed that there is a higher possibility 
to observe radon decay products on inner parts of AAV rather than radon 
due to the fact that the state of radon changes to gaseous one whereas radon 
daughters stay in solid state.  
II. 
Radon tends to concentrate in the inlet of AAV. Due to the fact that 
temperature is rising from inlet to outlet of AAV, radon tends to vaporize 
together with LNG due to its boiling point which is around -62°C [65]. 
Therefore, it is assumed that radon tends to accumulate near inlet locations 
where it is still in solid state rather than in outlet.  
Other pieces of equipment of LNG regasification installation such as BOG 
recondenser and compressors can also contain radon and radon progeny. 
 
LNG storage tank is assumed to be another location of radon and radon decay 
products accumulation. It is assumed that in LNG storage tanks sedimentation or 
settling process takes place: in still environments, under gravity, heavy solid 
particles tend to deposit on the bottom of the tank. Nevertheless, due to the external 
heat leakage to the tank, there is convective circulation of fluid close to LNG walls 
[105]. It is assumed that this recirculating flow causes small and light radon and 
radon progeny particles to be stuck to the wall, and relatively larger particles are 
captured by the flow and remain in the layers near walls of LNG fluid. Apart from 
recirculation flow, rollover phenomenon which occurs in land-based LNG storage 
tanks may lift radon and radon decay particles which were deposited via 
gravitational force from the bottom due to the motion of fluid layers. Thus, it is 
assumed that there is always radon and radon decay products presenting in LNG. 
There is a record on NORM deposition in NGL storage tanks, where it is stated that 
NORM is deposited due to long hold up times [60]. The same logic can be applied 
to LNG storage tanks. Nevertheless, even though radon decay products 
concentration will increase, substantial amount of radon can be decreased due to the 
half-life of radon which is 3.8 days [10]. Apart from storage duration which affects 
radon concentration, transportation to desired site should also be taken into 
consideration. Currently, there are two LNG receiving terminals in Sweden: one in 
Nynäshamn, and one in Lysekil. LNG for both terminals comes mainly from Gasum 
LNG plant located in Risavika, Norway. Sweden also receives LNG from Gate

Belgium. LNG containers from Gate and Zeebrugge LNG terminals reach 
Gothenburg in five days. However, both Gate and Zeebrugge LNG terminals 
receive LNG from different sources such as Qatar, US or Russia; therefore, initial 
LNG is transported for more than five days before reaching Gothenburg [106] – 
[107]. It implies that radon concentration will actually decrease before reaching

There is a very low probability of radon and radon progeny deposition in engine 
room. Due to the fact that alpha and beta particles cannot penetrate the equipment, it 
is assumed that there is a very low probability of detecting alpha and beta radiation 
in air in the engine room. Level of gamma radiation can be present since gamma 
radiation can penetrate. However, since many parts of LNG systems are made of 
nickel steel, and certain thickness of steel is able to block gamma radiation to some 
extent, it is assumed that gamma radiation poses little or almost no hazard. In 
addition, due to the fact that strong gamma emitters are short-lived radon daughters 
with half-life between 160 microseconds and 27 minutes, it is less likely that one 
will be able to detect their presence at site of LNG fuel systems where radon is 
unsupported. In addition, there are LNG spill detectors installed on ships which help 
to identify the escape of LNG out of equipment. In the case of escape, alarm turns 
on, and needed procedures in order to prevent the leakage are applied. Therefore, it 
is assumed that in normal operational conditions, i.e. in the absence of leakage or 
the presence of systems which are able to warn about undesirable leakage, there is 
no risk or very low probability of radon and radon decay product presence in the air 
of the engine room. 
 
The region where alpha and beta particles can be detected outside of equipment is 
near flanged connections due to LNG leakage [108]. Therefore, flanged connections 
are regions with higher probability of alpha and beta radiation presence in air due to 
possible leakage. Nevertheless, ideally, there should be no leakage. Therefore, even 
though flanged connections and close regions are considered as regions where there 
is a higher probability to detect alpha and beta radiation compared to other regions, 
there is still a low overall probability of alpha and beta radiation presence. 
 
Overall, LNG storage tank, namely both land-based tank and ship tank, is the piece 
of equipment where there is the highest probability to observe and detect the 
presence of radon progeny due to long hold up. Apart from LNG storage tank, on 
inner parts of piping system, especially outer edge of elbows, valves, t-joints, 
sudden contraction and expansion, and any region where direction of LNG flow 
changes, there is a probability of radon and radon decay products deposition. Pumps 
also should be checked for NORM deposition. In general, LNG ship tank and land-
based tank should be especially investigated, and samples for investigation from 
inner surface of LNG tank preferably shall be taken. As it has been noted, the 
engine room is the location where the probability to detect radon and radon decay 
products is low, it is also assumed that there is no alpha and beta radiation due to 
inability to penetrate the pieces of equipment and due to the absence of leakage 
spots. There can be leakage spots in engine room but they should be detected, so, 
ideally, it is considered that there are no such spots from where radon and radon

2.4.3 
Maintenance procedure and radiation exposure  
The maintenance should be carried twice within a five years period on LNG cargo 
containment systems. The typical lifespan of LNG cargo systems and LNG facilities 
is beyond 20 years [109]. It implies that workers might be subjected to radiation at 
least 8 times. There are also other possible ways of being exposed to radiation such 
as gamma radiation exposure from short-lived radon daughters, inhalation of radon 
and radon decay products from combustion of natural gas as well as from possible 
leaks [110]. In order to measure radiation damage to tissue, the concept called 
absorbed dose is used. The absorbed dose shows the intensity of the energy 
deposited in a matter per unit of mass, and the unit of absorbed dose is called gray 
(Gy). Nevertheless, each type of radiation has its own damage weight which is 
important to take into consideration when one attempts to evaluate the damage 
which has been done to tissue. Therefore, the concept of equivalent dose has been 
introduced which is the absorbed dose multiplied by a radiation weighting factor. 
The tissue affected by alpha particles receives more damage compared to the tissue 
from beta radiation. For instance, for gamma rays, X-rays and beta particles, the 
factor equals to 1 whereas for alpha particles the factor is 20. The unit both for 
equivalent dose is the sievert (Sv) [45]. The sievert is also the unit for effective dose 
which is, as defined by [111], “summation of the tissue or organ equivalent doses, 
each multiplied by the appropriate tissue weighting factor”. Therefore, the sum of 
the weighted equivalent doses is effective dose. The effective dose concept is 
widely used in radioprotection since it determines how detrimental and hazardous 
the exposure to radiation is. In addition, effective dose also shows how much dose 
was absorbed by all organs of the entire body, as well as the sensitiveness of each 
part of the body to radiation. Taking into consideration aforementioned facts, it is 
essential to evaluate the risk associated with the presence of radon decay products in 
LNG and LNG fuel systems.  
 
International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) published International Basic Safety 
Standards for Protection Against Ionizing Radiation and for the Safety of Radiation 
Sources where recommendations on three types of situation according to 
International Commission on Radiological Protection (ICRP) can be found, namely 
planned exposure situations, existing exposure situations and emergency exposure 
situations. According to the document, for workers who are over the age of 18, the 
dose limits are [111]:  
 
I. 
A dose of 20 mSv annually averaged over five consecutive years making 
100 mSv in 5 years, or 50 mSv in a single year. 
II. 
A dose to the lens of the eye of 20 mSv annually averaged over 5 
consecutive years making 100 mSv in 5 years, and of 50 mSv in a single 
year. 
III. 
A dose to the extremities (hands and feet) or to the skin of 500 mSv in a 
year.

2.5 Radon and radon progeny measurement 
Different ways of measuring radon and radon progeny concentration are proposed in 
this study. In the first place, a theoretical background will be provided. Due to the 
fact that radon and radon progeny will be measured not only in natural gas but also 
in engine room, techniques and comprehensive review of radon measurement both 
in air and natural gas are provided.  
2.5.1 
Introduction 
Radon concentration measurement in air is much more common compared to the 
measurement of the radon progeny concentration. Nevertheless, in the case when a 
more accurate estimate of the radiation exposure is required, radon decay products 
are analysed even though the measurement procedure is more sophisticated and 
costly compared to the one of radon itself. Sometimes, both radon and radon 
progeny levels are measured, and one of the measurement ways is the use of the 
two-filter method. The two-filter detector operates in the following way [112]: 
 
I. 
The air where both radon and radon daughters are present passes through 
the first filter where radon progeny is left on the filter whereas radon itself 
travels further.   
II. 
After radon progeny is removed via the first filter, radon together with 
sample air enters a decay chamber, where the daughters produced are 
collected on a second filter.  
III. 
The concentration of radon progeny and radon can be evaluated separately 
by different techniques such as alpha spectroscopy, scintillation counting, or 
a passive detector. 
Apart from different measurement techniques that will be discussed in depth further, 
there are three different time regimes for sampling, namely instantaneous, or “grab” 
sampling; continuous, or “sniffing” sampling; and integrated sampling.  
Instantaneous sampling has several advantages such as prompt delivery of results 
and the ability to easily carry equipment due to the fact that used equipment is 
usually portable. However, there are several disadvantages, and one of them is the 
need of result correction for the presence of Rn-220. The precision of grab sampling 
is relatively low unless counting times on the order of several hours are used. 
During the grab sampling, air is collected in the container which can be ranged 
between 5 and 15 litres, and carried to the laboratory for further investigation. In 
order to perform more accurate radon analysis, air with radon can be captured via 
activated charcoal trap submerged in liquid nitrogen. At this low temperature, radon 
remains on the trap. Afterward, the trap charcoal is warmed in the presence of 
helium, and the concentration of radon is analysed using a counting cell or 
ionization chamber [112].  


--- Page 36 ---
 
30(68) 
 
 
In continuous sampling, first, the sample air passes through a filter so radon decay 
products can be removed. Afterward, the sample air goes through a sensitive volume 
where the analysis can be conducted. In the case if active system is used, the air is 
drawn through the sensitive volume with the help of a pump; however, in the case of 
passive system, the air diffuses into the volume. The latter process requires around 4 
hours in order to reach equilibrium state. Compared to instantaneous sampling 
method, continuous sampling has relatively good precision. Some of continuous 
sampling devices can track hourly fluctuations of radon levels. The continuous 
tracking of radon levels can help to indicate poor ventilation and other vital 
parameters. The sampling period can range from 48 hours to months in the case if 
the filter is maintained or replaced on a regular basis. Continuous sampling usually 
requires trained staff: one of the reasons behind is due to the fact that devices should 
be calibrated annually, with semi-annual rechecks. In order to obtain a real picture 
of radon concentration, continuous monitoring should be conducted in the absence 
of dusty, smoky, and high-humidity environment. In the case if continuous 
monitoring is performed in an undesirable environment, it should be carefully 
maintained [112].  
 
Integrated sampling gives a single average concentration over the measuring time. 
The length of the period varies starting from a few days to years. In order to perform 
integrated sampling, both active and passive devices are used; nevertheless, the use 
of passive devices is the most common [112]. There are numerous advantages of 
integrated sampling over other types of sampling methods. For instance, integrated 
sampling methods are less expensive compared to continuous sampling. In addition, 
integrated sampling devices can be used for radon levels that are too low for 
continuous and grab sampling methods. In addition, obtained samples can be 
analyzed later, at a more convenient time and place [113]. The most common type 
of integrated sampling device is integrating dosimeter. There are two types of 
testing periods: long-term or short-term [112].  
 
I. 
Short-term testing. There is a wide range of short-term testing devices 
starting from the ones which should be deployed for 2 days to 3 months. As 
it has been mentioned above, for integrated sampling method, mostly 
passive devices are used among which the most common ones are alpha 
track detectors, charcoal canisters, charcoal liquid scintillation detectors, 
and electret ion chambers. Due to the fact that radon concentration depends 
on numerous factors such as season, ventilation, and lifestyle of residents, a 
short-term test will more likely demonstrate a peak or valley of radon levels 
which in reality does not represent a complete picture of radon levels 
throughout a year. Therefore, the majority of public health agencies opt for 
long-term test in order to understand whether some actions are required. 
Nevertheless, short-term test is preferred for initial sampling, and in the case 
if the radon levels exceed the acceptable standards, long-term test is used 
for more representative results.  
II. 
Long-term testing: Long-term tests, unlike short-term tests, are utilized for 
more than 3 months. The most common devices which are used in order to 
perform long-term tests are alpha track and electret ion chamber detectors.

As it has been mentioned above, a long-term test is better for obtaining an

2.5.2 
Passive detectors for radon measurement in air 
2.5.2.1 
Activated Charcoal Detector (ACD) 
Activated charcoal detector (ACD) is usually comprised of a cylindrical canister 
which contains activated charcoal. The charcoal is used in radon concentration 
measurement due to the fact that the charcoal has adsorptive properties, and radon 
can be captured on active sites of charcoal. Prior to measurement procedure, the 
canister should be prepared. When measurement procedure should take place, 
canister is unsealed. The exposure time usually varies between three and four days 
[112]. Nevertheless, it can be also between 2 and 7 days [114]. During 
measurement, radon enters via diffusion mechanism and is absorbed by charcoal. 
After the exposure period is over, the top of canister should be replaced, and 
canister itself should be sealed. The level of radon can be analyzed with the help of 
different devices. One of the most common devices is NaI(Tl) scintillation detector 
which helps to measure the intensity of the gamma radiation generated by radon 
daughter products, usually Pb-214 and Bi-214. Nevertheless, high purity intrinsic 
germanium (HPGe) semiconductor detectors can also be used. Another activated 
charcoal detection technique utilizes liquid scintillation counting method.  One to 
two grams of charcoal for radon capturing is used, after which the charcoal is placed 
into an organic solvent in which radon is highly soluble. Afterward, aforementioned 
scintillation counting in order to detect alpha and beta radiation types is used [112].  
 
The activated charcoal detector is considered as one of the most inexpensive devices 
for radon level measurement. In addition, there is no need for trained staff in order 
to carry the measurement; therefore, the ACDs are very popular for screening of 
private dwellings. Nevertheless, ACD has several disadvantages: the radon 
adsorption is temperature and humidity dependent process. Therefore, ACD should 
be used only in indoor environments where there are slight fluctuations of 
temperature and humidity [112]. Though there is a correction for humidity, there is 
no any correction factor for temperature. In addition, ACD has one more drawback: 
in the case if the concentration of radon experiences significant fluctuations, the 
obtained results may not depict the real radon levels. The reason behind is the 
equilibrium phenomenon, namely the shift between radon adsorption and desorption 
processes in the case of radon concentration change. This is particularly essential 
when there is a decrease in radon concentration closer to the end of the exposure 
since the amount of absorbed radon may decrease. This, in turn, leads to results 
which do not depict real radon concentration levels [115].  
2.5.2.2 
Electret Ionization Chambers 
The electret ionization chamber is comprised of a conducting plastic chamber 
containing the electret [112]. The electret is the type of dielectric material 
containing quasi-permanent electric charge or molecular dipoles which are able to

Radon enters to the chamber, more precisely through filter to the chamber, in the 
same way as it enters to ACD – via diffusion phenomenon. There, radon undergoes 
decay process, and at the same time ionize the air. The electrons generated during 
ionization process are drawn to the electret with further decrease of the potential 
difference, or voltage, on the surface of the electret. The voltage change is measured 
by a special calibrated voltmeter. The electret ionization chamber is used until the 
electret surface potential is depleted. Afterward, the electret should be replaced

The electret ion chambers may be utilized both for indoor and outdoor environments 
due to the fact that they are less sensitive to the changes in temperature and 
humidity compared to other types of detectors. Electric ion chambers are suitable 
both for short-term and long-term measurements: in the case of short-term 
measurement, the thinner electret is used whereas for long-term measurement a 
thick electret is required. Nevertheless, though this type of detector is not sensitive 
to changes of certain environmental factors, electret ionization chamber is sensitive 
to all types of natural background radiation. Therefore, calibration is required [112]. 
2.5.2.3 
Alpha Track Etch Detectors 
The alpha track detectors are based on the following mechanism: alpha particles 
which were generated during the decay process of radon and radon progeny leave a 
so-called track of broken chemical bonds as they passing through particular solid 
materials. In order to make created tracks visible, some of these solid materials can 
be subjected to chemical etching. Cellulose nitrate (LR-115) and allyl diglycol 
carbonate (CR-39) films are two of the most commonly used materials [112]. For 
instance, CR-39, to be more precise its polymer, i.e. polyallyl diglycol carbonate 
(PADC), is consisted of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen atoms. When emitted alpha 
particles passes through the film, they collide with the electrons of oxygen atoms 
which leads to loss of the energy resulting in the positive ionization of oxygen 


--- Page 39 ---
 
33(68) 
 
atoms in detector film. By applying strong acidic or basic solutions, track can 
become visible [117].  
 
Despite the fact that the etching procedure can be performed both in acidic and basic 
solutions, mostly NaOH, i.e. basic solution, is used. Afterward, the visible track 
density is either analyzed by manual scanning with the help of a microscope or via a 
computer-based optical scanning program. One of the advantages of using alpha 
track etch detectors is that there is no need for ambient background correction. The 
reason behind is that the tracks which are left by beta and gamma radiation are not 
imaged by the etching, and only tracks left by alpha particles are visible after 
etching. Nevertheless, not all alpha particles are capable of creating the track: only 
the portion of the alphas with sufficient kinetic energy creates a track which is large 
enough to be imaged after etching. Other advantages are the cost and simplicity of 
this type of detector. The alpha track etch detectors are inexpensive and easy to 
deploy. Consequently, they gained the widespread popularity and are used for long-
term measurements of radon levels. Compared to other detectors, alpha track etch is 
less sensitive: therefore, for more precise and accurate measurements, the large 
period, namely the period of 3 months to 1 year, is required [112]. 
2.5.2.4 
Thermoluminescent Dosimeters 
Thermoluminescence (TL), also known as thermally stimulated luminescence, is a 
process in which a solid, usually minerals in crystalline form, emits light by being 
subjected to a heat. The light is emitted due to the fact that the energy which was 
absorbed from radiation is re-emitted. Despite the fact that re-emitted light is 
usually a reason of previously absorbed energy from radiation, the excitation can 
also occur via pressure, friction, or light [118]. The mechanism of TL is the 
following: after material is being subjected to the radiation, both types of charge 
carriers, i.e. electrons and holes, are trapped in the forbidden gap. By applying a 
heat to the material to a few hundred degrees centigrade, either electrons or holes 
recombine with charges of the opposite sign, and recombination results in a visible 
light. Released light is nothing but previously absorbed energy, i.e. the radiation 
dose which material received. Due to the fact that thermoluminescent dosimeters 
(TLDs) are capable of recording all radiation doses, TLDs should be accordingly 
adjusted. The working principle of TLDs is the following: first, radon decay 
products are filtered from the ambient air. Afterward, the daughters from the radon 
decay tend to attach to the surface of the TLD. In order to identify radon 
concentration, the TLD should be removed from the detector assembly and placed 
in a reader. Here, in a reader, TLD is subjected to heat and the released light is 
measured. Several configurations of TLD exist. For instance, in one type of the 
detectors, the moisture first is removed with the help of silica gel. Afterward, the air 
passes through a filter that removes radon progeny, and then into a funnel-shaped 
chamber. In the chamber, with the help of electrical field generated by battery, the 
daughters tend to attach to the surface of the TLD [112].  
2.5.3 
Active detectors for radon measurement in air 
2.5.3.1 
Scintillation Detectors 
Scintillation cells, or scintillation detectors, are widely used and established 
technology for radon detection. When radon undergoes decay process, alpha particle 


--- Page 40 ---
 
34(68) 
 
is generated. The alpha particle hits the scintillation substance, and a portion of the 
kinetic energy is converted to a photon that can be detected by a photosensitive 
device such as a photomultiplier tube or a photodiode. The electrical pulse is 
generated, amplified, and can be analyzed in order to determine the radon 
concentration.  
 
Different types of scintillation materials exist. The most common one as well as one 
of the earliest known material is silver activated zinc sulfide [ZnS(Ag)]. First, the 
air together with radon is drawn into the cell. There can be filter in order to 
eliminate radon progeny prior to interaction with the cell. The counting procedure 
should be done in the dark environment: alpha particles strike ZnS resulting in light 
which can be observed by photosensitive monitor. Apart from alpha particles, beta 
particles can also be identified; however, ZnS is not sensitive to gamma radiation. 
Silver activated zinc sulfide is also referred to as radon cell, or as Lucas cells, after 
the inventor of the original cell [112]. The Lucas cell (LC) detectors, which are 
among the most sensitive devices, are capable of detecting low radon levels [119].  
 
Another type of scintillation substance which is also widely spread is sodium iodide 
(NaI) doped with an impurity (Tl). Unlike ZnS, this type of detector is sensitive to 
gamma radiation and insensitive to alpha and beta particles. The vessel which is 
used together with NaI should be made from the material which does not block 
gamma radiation. Therefore, such materials as aluminum or polyethylene which 
allow gamma rays to pass through are used. Nevertheless, NaI detector is also 
sensitive to surrounding gamma radiation. Therefore, additional shield made from 
lead or concrete, materials which do not pass gamma radiation, is required. The 
working principle of NaI scintillation device type is following: first, air containing 
radon is drawn into the vessel, and the vessel is put in the position with respect to 
the photomultiplier tube. Gamma radiation leads to light flash generation that can be 
further analyzed [112].  
 
Cesium Iodide (CsI) crystal scintillator detectors can also be used. Another 
scintillator device, liquid scintillator, is also used for radon concentration 
measurement. The ability of radon to dissolve at low temperatures is actively used 
in liquid scintillator. Air containing radon passes through an organic solvent where 
radon is highly soluble. The remaining steps are almost the same as in all other 
types of scintillator: while radon is decaying giving away alpha and beta particles, 
generated light flashes are detected by a photosensitive detector and counted. Due to 
the fact that radon and radon progeny are closely mixed together with the scintillator 
substance, the counting efficiency of liquid scintillator is higher compared to other 
types of scintillator devices. However, liquid scintillator requires the use of wet 
chemistry which makes the procedure more sophisticated [112]. 
2.5.3.2 
Internal Gas Ionization Chamber Counters 
Ionization chamber is comprised of a chamber with two electrodes: anode and 
cathode. A voltage potential between the electrodes creates an electric field within 
the chamber. The scheme is shown in Figure 12. The radiation emitted during radon 
and radon progeny decay process ionizes the air molecules leading to generation of 
electrons and positively charged ions. Both electrons and positively charged ions 
move to the electrodes of the opposite polarity under the influence of the Coulomb

35(68) 
 
forces [112]. Different modes of operation for ionization chamber exist. One of the 
modes is a “current” mode: the electric field is strong enough for continuous 
process, where recombination of ion pairs can be avoided. Ionization chamber 
counter requires complicated measurement approaches due to the fact that the 
currents are small [120].

Due to the aforementioned complexity, this type of detector is also among the most

2.5.3.3 
Semiconductor Detectors 
Semiconductor detectors, compared to all other types of detectors, are able to 
measure radon concentrations with the highest accuracy. Semiconductor detectors 
possess certain advantageous features such as “high readout speed, the direct 
availability of signals in electronic form, the simultaneous precise measurement of 
energy and position and the possibility of integrating detector and readout 
electronics on a common substrate” [120]. Most of devices are capable of 
distinguishing between the alpha radiation from radon and alpha radiation from 
radon progeny. In addition, these devices are also able to distinguish between radon 
and thoron, which is radon isotope [112].  
 
The principle of working is similar to the one in ionization chamber counter. 
Semiconductor materials react to ionizing radiation: the particle which passes 
through the detector leads to generation of the electron-hole pairs. The number of 
the pairs corresponds to the energy which semiconductor received being exposed to 
radiation. Electrons and holes are received by electrodes in the presence of an 
electric field, and it results in an electric pulse which can be further analyzed. As it 
has been mentioned, the number of the pairs corresponds to the radiation energy; 
therefore, received pulse provides data on the radiation energy [112].

2.5.4 
Measurement of radon in LNG 
To our knowledge, there is no established method of radon level measurement in 
LNG. Nevertheless, there is a research conducted on measurement of LNG trace 
constituents including radon [11], where LNG was collected using cryogenic 
system. Later radon was collected using carbon cartridge based on EPA 520/5-87-
005 [114] standard followed by radon concentration measurement via gamma ray 
spectroscopy. However, aforementioned techniques are not considered in this paper 
due to the following reason: in research [121], numerous flaws of EPA 520/5-87-
005 standard were demonstrated. Therefore, obtained radon concentrations may not 
reflect the real radon concentration due to inaccurate algorithms. In addition, as it 
has been mentioned above, ACDs have numerous disadvantages which can have 
affect the results.     
 
Therefore, in this study, the following technique is proposed: LNG should be 
converted to gaseous state following a certain standard such as ISO 8943:2007 
[122], and the radon level in natural gas can be further measured with the help of 
different methods such as alpha scintillation cell and electret ion chamber. In order 
to identify the level of radioactivity of LNG equipment, the sample of material 
which was exposed to radiation, such as the sample from inner surface of LNG tank 
or any other type of equipment where radon concertation is assumed to be relatively 
high, should be taken in accordance with a certain standard such as ISO 7503, and 
delivered to the lab for further investigation. The wipe sampling can also be done 
according to the rules of certain organizations such as [123]. Apart from 
aforementioned techniques, for preliminary NORM identification, special and 
sophisticated though expensive equipment which is capable of identifying 
radioactive element at once, i.e. just by making device closer to the source of 
NORM, can be used. The principle of this device was demonstrated during the visit 
to the Swedish Radiation Safety Authority. The test was performed on Cs-137, 
where after closer approach, device identified the element as well as showed the 
concentration on the display. This device can be used during real measurement of 
radon and radon progeny on the areas of LNG installations. In addition, scintillation 
detectors such as NaI can also be used for initial detection, and then other 
techniques can be deployed for obtaining more precise data.   
To obtain representative sample of LNG, the sample should be converted from 
liquid to gaseous state without partial vaporization since partial vaporization leads 
to the loss of certain LNG components. In the case of prior vaporization, for 
instance in the sampling line, such components as nitrogen and methane will 
vaporize first resulting in so-called pockets of gas in the liquid stream resulting in 
the non-representative sample rich in heavy components [124]. To avoid 
aforementioned problems, one should follow strict instructions and standards in 
order to obtain representative analysis. ISO 8943:2007 provides guideline on how to 
carry sampling of LNG. Two sampling methods, namely continuous and 
intermittent, are described in ISO 8943:2007. The selection of the method depends 
on certain preferences; however, less equipment with moveable elements is required 
for continuous LNG sampling system compared to intermittent sampling one. In 
addition, during continuous sampling method, the process of cleaning sample 
containers can be less complicated [125].

2.5.4.6 
Metal surface wipe sampling of LNG equipment 
Wipe sampling is a method which helps to identify and estimate the possible 
contamination of the examined area [133]. In order to obtain representative results, 
it is of high importance to adhere all necessary procedures in right manner. For 
representative results, representative sample should be taken. In this study, it is 
suggested to follow the steps described in [123], which are, in turn, based on 
National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health (NIOSH) 9100 “Lead in 
Surface Wipe Samples” methodology. Certain assumptions which were developed 
based on the advice provided in [123] are also provided in this study. 
 
Prior to starting the wipe sampling itself, it is highly recommended by [123] to 
follow some precautions such as assessing the area and location of testing on 
possible hazards as well as wearing personal protective equipment (PPE). Following 
items are included in PPE, namely: gloves, protective clothing, shoe covering, 
respiratory, and safety glasses. Nevertheless, in the case of wipe sampling of LNG 
equipment, not all PPE items may be required during wipe sampling. For instance, 
protective clothing should be used in the case if there is a certain contact with 
contaminated area.  
 
It is assumed that a performed test, for instance, on the inner surface of LNG tank or 
spare equipment such as pump, may have a hazard related to the contact of body 
with contaminated equipment. It is assumed that there is no direct contact between 
contaminated equipment and body but rather a hazard related to beta radiation since 
contaminated surface can be a source of betta radiation due to the presence of long-
lived daughters such as Pb-210 and Bi-210. Beta particles have the ability to travel a 
few meters in the air [134], and, as it has been mentioned before, beta radiation can 
penetrate a skin and be a cause of skin irritation. Therefore, in order to prevent any 
adverse effect from beta radiation, it is suggested to use protective clothing since 
beta particles can be stopped by a piece of clothes. Apart from protective clothing, 
during test procedure on any piece of NORM contaminated equipment, it is highly 
recommended to use gloves. The reason behind the recommendation of glove usage 
is the same as the reason behind the suggestion to use protective clothing – beta 
radiation. In [123], different types of glove materials are suggested such as nitrile, 
polyvinyl chloride (PVC), and natural rubber. In the case if sample is taken from the 
surface of LNG tank, which implies that a person is supposed to enter the tank, it is 
also suggested to have a shoe covering. According to [123], shoe covering is used in 
the case if the contact between shoes and contaminated cover is expected. Due to 
the fact that there is a probability of NORM contamination of the bottom of LNG 
tank, shoe covering is a PPE item which should be taken into account prior 
conducting a wipe sampling. Respiratory protection is also highly recommended 
due to the fact that inhaled alpha particles which can be emitted from the inner 


--- Page 49 ---
 
43(68) 
 
surface of contaminated equipment can pose a hazard. Safety glasses should also be 
used since beta radiation causes the damage not only to the skin but also to the eyes. 
 
After all precautions are taken, the wipe sampling can be conducted. Different types 
of equipment can be used for wipe sampling. For instance, sample can be placed in 
vial made either from plastic or glass, or it can be placed in the plastic bag with 
“zip” type seal. Sample media can also differ: gauze, paper, or pre-moistened wipe 
can be utilized during the wipe sampling process. Different metals require different 
types of solvents. According to [135], for certain type of radioactive element 
measurement method such as liquid scintillation counting, it is important to keep 
radioactive elements in solution. The contaminants are commonly divided between 
the ones which are soluble in water, and the ones which are soluble in organic 
solvents. For lead, both types of solvent, i.e. water and organic solvents, are 
applicable. In this case, it is suggested by [123] to focus rather on the surface 
material from which sample is supposed to be taken. Since LNG equipment is 
commonly made from steel or nickel steel, it is suggested to use solvents which will 
not damage aforementioned materials. However, it is also essential to take into 
consideration the method with which further sample will be analyzed. Therefore, in 
the process of selecting the solvent, both factors should be taken into account. The 
template can be either a plastic sheet or a cardboard, and suggested size by [123] is 
one square foot or around 929 cm2.  
 
The sample can be collected in different ways; nevertheless, in this study, NIOSH 
method is suggested and described. In order to collect the sample, following 
procedures should be adhered [123]: 
 
1. 
In the first place, the wipe should be moistened in appropriate way since 
the wipe which contains too much solvent may cause the dilution of the 
sample. Therefore, the wipe with around 80% moistened area will be 
adequate and sufficient. 
2. 
The application of wipe on the surface is shown in Figure 14. In (a), the

should be noted on the bag or vial. 
4. 
Afterward, PPE should be taken off in appropriate way, and disposed or

way as well.

3 Radon level measurement 
3.1 Rapidos 
Rapidos is an accurate short-term passive radon detector which can measure radon 
concentrations as low as 50 Bq/m3 up to 150,000 Bq/m3 over a week. The accuracy 
of Rapidos is 10% at 50 kBqh/m3. The measurement is secure, and Rapidos detector 
is accredited according to ISO 17025 standard and approved all over Europe [136]. 


--- Page 51 ---
 
45(68) 
 
Rapidos is constituted of anti-static plastic material with a film inside. The alpha 
track technique is used for radon level measurement, and radon enters to Rapidos 
via diffusion. The detector material is poly allyl diglycol carbonate or CR39/PADC 
[137]. The working principle of alpha track etch detector was described in previous 
chapters. For precise measurement, Rapidos should be put indoor at a distance of at 
least 25 cm from floor, walls and ceiling, and 1.5 m away from air supply, external 
door and windows. 
3.2 RadonEye 
RadonEye is a smart radon detector with a high sensitivity (0.5cpm/pCi/l). 
RadonEye is the detector with high accuracy of <10% at 10pCi/l which can provide 
reliable data in a short period of time, usually less than one hour. It can be 
connected to the phone via Bluetooth where radon fluctuations can be easily 
observed. RadonEye employs pulsed ion chamber [138]. The ionization chamber 
detector is a type of alpha detector which is able to measure radon levels. The 
working principle of ionization chamber is following: alpha particles ionize 
different types of molecules which are present in air. These molecules alike alpha 
particles can travel longer distances with the help of air flow. These molecules help 
to identify radon level through the electrostatic detection of individual pulses from 
radon disintegration at a low level [139]. Measuring the average of radon level 
during the day does not depict the real picture since radon level varies depending on 
the time of day. In the study which was carried in numerous dwellings in order to 
check radon level during night and day, it is stated that the behavior of the 
inhabitants affects the radon and radon daughers’ concentration during day. When 
inhabitants are sleeping or outside, windows are closed; therefore, ventilation is 
very weak which leads to higher radon concentration compared to day time since 
radon does not leave the house. Nevertheless, during day time, when inhabitants are 
home and open windows, there is good ventilation resulting in lower concentration. 
In the case when inhabitants are not home, the same logic as during night time is 
applied: windows are closed, so radon concentration increases [140]. Therefore, it is 
important to obtain information on a real-time radon concentration. RadonEye 
provides a real-time measurement, and in the case if radon level exceeds 148 Bq/m3 
(4pCi/l), alarm will sound [138]. However, due to the fact that engine room is 
always ventilated, it is assumed that radon level is almost the same during the day. It 
is also assumed that possible radon and radon daughters’ presence in engine room is 
the highest while LNG fuel systems are deployed, and the lowest when LNG 
systems are off.

4 Radon management and safety 
The Swedish Radiation Safety Authority is the national body that among other 
things sets requirements for activities and practices involving radiation. In terms of 
naturally occurring radioactive materials and radon in workplaces, the authority has 
issued two special regulations, SSMFS 2018:4 and SSMFS 2018:10. The following 
is a brief summary of these regulations, the full version of which is available on the 
SSM website [141]. 
4.1 SSMFS 2018:10 Regulations on radon in workplaces  
These regulations [142], contain provisions to be observed by the activity operating 
in an environment with ionizing radiation, the person who employs someone in such 
an activity, or self-employed person, where: 
 
1. 
The radon content in air exceeds the reference level provided in Chapter 3, 
Section 6 of the Radiation Protection Ordinance (2018:506). The reference 
level for radon provided in the aforementioned document is 200 Bq/m3, 
which is an annual average activity concentration, in dwellings, places 
accessible to the public and at work; 
2. 
the annual average radon concentration in air cannot be identified but is 
expected to exceed the reference level more than temporarily; or 
3. 
exposure to radon in total from several temporary workstations or 
workplaces is expected to be equivalent to exposure to a radon content in air 
exceeding the reference level during working hours. 
Apart from aforementioned points, employees who are at risk of having an annual 
radon exposure exceeding 0.72 MBq h/m3 during working hours should be 
identified. Workers under the age of 18 also shall not be at risk of receiving an 
annual radon exposure exceeding aforementioned number during working hours. 
 
In the case if radon content exceeds above-mentioned reference level, the Swedish 
Radiation Safety Authority should be notified, unless there are measures which are 
expected to be taken which will reduce the radon content to below the reference 
level. The following scheme illustrates a simplified general approach to radon 
regulations in the workplace:

The notification shall be made on a form provided by the Authority and shall 
contain all required information. Activities that are covered by the provisions of 
these regulations within the Swedish Armed Forces, the Swedish Defense Materiel

Establishment agencies must be reported to the Defence Inspectorate for Health and 
Environment. The notification shall contain the information set provided in Annex 1

4.3 Activities towards the reduction of radon level 
When it comes to the reduction of radon level in engine room or areas with LNG 
equipment, several techniques are suggested in this study. In the case if elevated 
levels of radon have been detected, the ventilation system in the engine room can be 
upgraded. In addition, the whole system can be checked for the possible leakage, 
and fixed in the case of LNG escape. In the case if elevated levels of radon are 
detected in LNG installation areas, then the possible leakage from flanges and 
coupling should be checked. In the case of detected leakage, the connection system 
between LNG source and LNG tank on the ship should be changed or fixed.

5 Results and discussion 
In this study, the question of NORM presence in LNG equipment and LNG area 
was raised and investigated. The matter on possible NORM presence was raised due 
to the reason that radon is present in the sedimentary rock, and goes up together 
with natural gas during production process. To our knowledge, no measurement has 
been carried on the NORM detection in LNG; therefore, the question of NORM 
presence in LNG equipment and fuel system is open. Further investigation showed 
that radon and radon progeny might be present in LNG equipment since: 
1. 
The data on radon and radon progeny in LNG was recorded by [11]. 
2. 
Several papers are dedicated to the radon and radon progeny concentration 
measurement in LNG refinery. One is theoretical [8] proposing the overall 
picture of possible radon and radon progeny accumulation and movement in 
LNG refinery, showing that NORM presence in LNG refinery may exceed 
the reference level and shall be therefore investigated. In another work [9] 
real data on radon progeny concentration was gathered from Dehydration 
Unit confirming the fact of NORM movement through multiple units and 
accumulation. 
3. 
The depository nature of radon and radon decay products on the inner 
surface of equipment. Several papers such as [60], [62], [63] describe the 
presence of accumulated NORM in oil and gas industry in different forms 
such as invisible or gray films on the inner part of equipment, as well as in 
the form of sludge or scale deposition. 
Therefore, in this study, for better understanding of the overall picture, all steps 
starting from the production to the final LNG product including transportation were 
analyzed. Main treatment processes taking place in LNG refinery, and what happens 
to radon and radon progeny on each step, were also reviewed. The following 
assumptions regarding radon and radon progeny concentration in the final LNG 
product based on the nature of NORM and treatment processes were made: 
1. 
Radon concentration in final LNG product depends on the radon 
concentration in the initial feed.  
2. 
Radon concentration in final LNG product depends on the natural gas flow 
in LNG plant.  
3. 
Radon concentration in final LNG product depends on the materials and 
filters used in treatment processes. 
Assumptions regarding radon and radon progeny deposition in the final destination, 
e.g. LNG tank or LNG terminal, were also made: 
 


--- Page 62 ---
 
56(68) 
 
1. 
Sea motion partially prevents from the deposition of radon and radon decay 
products in LNG tank on ships.  
2. 
Truck motion also prevents from the deposition of NORM but to a lesser 
extent due to the fact that motions experienced by LNG during 
transportation by road have less amplitude.  
3. 
For land-based LNG tanks, it is assumed that NORM tends to accumulate 
more due to the absence of motion, i.e. particles are settled more easily 
In addition, different possible locations to be further investigated where radon and 
radon progeny are more likely to be detected were proposed: 
 
1. 
Pipeline, particularly elbows, sudden expansion and contraction, valves, and 
other parts of pipeline where the direction of LNG changes. 
2. 
Pumps, since the data on NORM contamination was recorded for this type 
of equipment. 
3. 
LNG land-based tank, since, as it has been mentioned above, absence of 
motion facilitates the deposition process. 
4. 
Other pieces of equipment such as BOG recondenser, AAV, and etc. 
Then, several different techniques for radon and radon decay products detection 
were suggested: 
 
1. 
The conversion of LNG to gaseous state following certain standard such as 
ISO 8943:2007, and then the measurement of radon concentration with the 
help of different techniques such as alpha scintillation cells or electret ion 
chamber. 
2. 
Wipe metal sampling, or scale deposition sampling following certain 
standards such as ISO 7503, or following certain procedures proposed by 
other organizations and institutions. 
3. 
The use of special device capable of identifying radioactive element at once. 
Afterward, real radon measurement for the purpose of equipment comparison for 
further investigation by the Swedish Radiation Safety Authority was carried. 
Several important points were drawn: 
 
1. 
Due to the drawbacks in the software, namely the missing data, it is 
suggested to place several RadonEye devices in the same location in order 
to reduce the possibility of missing data. 
2. 
There is an inverse relationship between ventilation and radon levels. In 
other words, better and stronger is ventilation, lower levels of radon are 
present, and vice versa. As it can be seen from Figure 21, radon 
concentration levels increased during night times due to the fact that 
ventilation was reduced. 
Finally, in the case if elevated levels of radiation are detected in the engine room or 
in the territory of LNG facilities, following methods to reduce radon concentration 
levels are suggested: 


--- Page 63 ---
 
57(68) 
 
 
1. 
For elevated levels in the engine room, to upgrade ventilation system and 
check LNG equipment for the possible leakage, and to fix in the case of 
leakage. 
2. 
For elevated levels in the territory of LNG installations, to check the 
possible leakage caused by flanges and other means of connection between 
LNG source and LNG tank on the ship. In the case of leakage, to fix the 
system. 
3. 
For NORM decontamination, one can refer to “Safe and responsible 
management of spent nuclear fuel and radioactive waste in Sweden” 
(2015:32) national plan report by the Swedish Radiation Safety Authority. 
Nevertheless, there are some points regarding NORM presence in LNG systems, 
engine room and in the territory of LNG installations which are worth discussing: 
 
1. 
It is assumed that there is a very low probability of high radon and radon 
decay products concentration presence in engine room. The reason behind is 
inability of alpha and beta particles to escape through equipment. The 
possible way of escaping is LNG leakage; however, the leakage may not be 
detected only in the case if detectors installed in the engine room are not 
working properly. Since it is assumed that the system is regularly checked, 
the possibility to detect concentrations exceeding reference level is very 
low, i.e. there is no hazard associated with radiation in engine room. 
2. 
The same logic is applied to the territory with LNG installations, i.e. only in 
the case of leakage, for instance from equipment connecting LNG tank on 
ship to LNG source, elevated radon levels can be detected. Otherwise, there 
is no hazard to employees engaged into activities in the territory of LNG 
installations. 
3. 
There is a high probability of hazard associated with maintenance of LNG 
equipment. The are several reasons behind such as accumulation of NORM 
on the inner surface of equipment leading to increase in radioactivity level, 
and the opening of equipment for cleaning and other required procedures 
where alpha and beta particles can be inhaled thus can pose a threat to the 
health. 
6 Conclusion 
In order to detect NORM contamination, several methods are proposed in this study. 
Certain detectors, such as NaI, can be used for preliminary radiation detection, and 
techniques for exact NORM identification, such as wipe or scale sampling, as well 
as sludge, can be used. Besides, LNG can be converted to the gaseous state, and 
further analyzed for radon presence. 
There are several locations where radon and radon progeny concentrations are 
expected to be the highest. The highest possible accumulation of NORM is expected 
to be in LNG tank due to the long hold up. Apart from LNG tank, NORM is 


--- Page 64 ---
 
58(68) 
 
expected to be deposited on the inner parts of pipes, particularly on outer edge of 
elbows, valves, t-joints, sudden contraction and expansion. Shortly, in any region 
where the direction of LNG flow changes, there is a higher possibility to detect 
NORM. In addition, pump also can be checked for NORM deposition.  
There are numerous techniques that can be used for radon level measurement. 
However, most of them are for radon measurement in air. In the case of LNG, LNG 
can be converted to gaseous state, as it has been already mentioned, and then radon 
levels can be measured via alpha scintillation cells, or via electret ion chamber. 
Activated charcoal detector can also be used; however, due to numerous factors 
such as dependence on the humidity, temperature, and radon fluctuations, it is 
suggested to use alpha scintillation cells or electret ion chamber. 
In order to obtain reliable results, it is essential to adhere to certain standards. For 
instance, in the case if radon measurement will be carried on LNG in the gaseous 
state, it is suggested to follow ISO 8943:2007 standard where the steps for right 
sampling of LNG are described. In the case if it is decided to go with wipe sampling 
for metals technique, it is suggested to follow ISO 7503 standards, or use 
instructions provided by different institutions and organizations such as [123]. In the 
case if scale deposition or sludge should be analyzed, the steps provided by the 
Swedish Radiation Safety Authority should be taken.  
Due to the fact that the diameter of radon and radon progeny particles varies a lot, 
different forces have effect on the particles of different sizes. For very small 
particles, thermophoresis, Brownian and diffusional forces are the main forces 
acting on the particles. For larger particles, or for relatively small particles but with 
high density, gravitational force is the main acting force. Therefore, it is assumed 
that particles with different size and density behave differently in LNG. 
Nevertheless, common assumptions regarding NORM deposition on the inner 
surface of LNG equipment based on the behavior of radon and radon decay products 
particles can be made, and locations were described in detail in this study. 
The matter of radiation is of high importance, and since, to our knowledge, there is 
no work carried on the NORM measurement in LNG systems, the question is still 
open. It is essential to ensure a safe work environment, especially for the 
maintenance workers since maintenance workers are under the highest risk of 
radiation exposure. This study provides a ground and base for further investigation 
and measurement of radon and radon decay products in LNG systems and areas of 
LNG installations.
