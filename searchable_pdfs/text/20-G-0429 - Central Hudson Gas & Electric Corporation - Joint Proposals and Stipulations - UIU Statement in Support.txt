I. 
INTRODUCTION 
The Utility Intervention Unit (“UIU”) of the New York State Department of State’s 
Division of Consumer Protection submits this statement in support of the Joint Proposal filed by 
Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corporation (the “Company”) in the above-captioned proceedings 
on August 24, 2021. UIU is an office of the Division of Consumer Protection within the 
Department of State and has the statutory responsibility to represent the interests of consumers in 
utility proceedings, with a particular focus on residential and small commercial customers.  UIU 
recommends the Commission approve the Joint Proposal given the diversity among signatory 
parties and their willingness to make meaningful concessions to reach a comprehensive settlement 
in these cases and because it is in the public interest and satisfies the Commission’s Settlement 
Guidelines.1 
The Joint Proposal is the result of several months of negotiations among several parties, 
including UIU. The Joint Proposal was executed by Central Hudson, together with the Department 
of Public Service Staff (“DPS Staff” or “Staff”), UIU, Multiple Intervenors, the Public Utility Law 
Project of New York (“PULP”), Alliance for a Green Economy (“AGREE”), Dutchess County, 
New York Power Authority (“NYPA”), New York Geothermal Energy Organization (“NY-
GEO”), Family Energy, Marathon Power LLC, and M&R Energy Resources Corporation 
(collectively the “Signatory Parties”).  
If approved, the Joint Proposal would set the Company’s electric and gas rates and services 
for three rate years commencing July 1, 2021 and ending on June 30, 2024. Compared to the rate 
plan initially filed by the Company on August 27, 2020, during the COVID-19 pandemic, the Joint 
Proposal imposes a negative total revenue change in Rate Year 1 for the electric business at -0.2%, 
and an increase of 1.9% for the gas business, and then caps the remaining revenues over the next 
two years to 1.2% for the electric business and 1.8% for the gas business. In addition, the Joint 
Proposal provides COVID-19 relief, a progressive set of commitments related to climate change 
that further the goals of the Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act (“CLCPA”), and 
improvements to the Company’s interactions with customers.

II. 
STANDARD OF REVIEW 
The Commission’s settlement procedures and guidelines establish the standard of review 
for the adoption of a joint proposal resulting from settlement negotiations.2 The standard of review 
is fundamentally grounded in the Commission’s responsibilities and duties to ensure safe and 
reliable service at just and reasonable rates.3 As such, a Commission decision to approve a joint 
proposal “must be . . . just, reasonable, and in the public interest.”4 To be in the public interest, a 
joint proposal should be consistent “with the regulatory, economic, social, and environmental 
policies” of the State, mirror a “likely result of full litigation and [be] within the range of reasonable 
outcomes,” and balance the interests of ratepayers and the long-term financial viability of the 
utility.5 There must also be a rational basis for the Commission’s decision, which can be reflected 
in the development of a reasonably supportive record.6 In addition, the Commission will likely 
consider “the completeness of the record” and “whether the settlement is contested.”7  
The Joint Proposal is consistent with each of these criteria. The Joint Proposal reflects 
compromise on the part of the Signatory Parties; balances the Parties’ positions (including the 
factual support for those positions), including parties that would be normally adversarial; mirrors 
a likely result of full litigation; and is consistent with Commission and State policy objectives. 
Additionally, the record is complete and adequate to support the Joint Proposal. Many of the 
Signatory Parties have submitted testimony and/or engaged in extensive discovery in these cases. 
UIU believes that the resulting factual record is ample to allow the Commission to adopt the Joint 
Proposal. The Joint Proposal therefore satisfies the Commission’s Settlement Guidelines.

Multilingual Translation 
Public Service Law § 44 (4) requires that gas, electric, and steam utility companies provide 
translated bills and notices for customers who speak non-English languages in counties with a 
population of 20% or more who speak such language. Over time, utilities in New York State have 
also provided interpretation services in their call center customer service departments as well as 
translations of the utility’s website, even though not required by law. In addition, utility companies 
have implemented this important customer service in territories where a non-English language is 
spoken widely but the underlying population does not meet the statutory threshold. While the 
Central Hudson service territory does not meet the population threshold for the requirement, the 
utility has provided translation and interpretation services in Spanish because of the need 
demonstrated by the community it serves.  
Testimony by DPS Staff and Citizens for Local Power filed in this case revealed that the 
Company’s translation and interpretation services have not been meeting the community’s needs, 
which includes a failure to maintain its website in Spanish.36 Testimony and exhibits filed by 
Citizens for Local Power indicated instances of customer service representatives employing 
obstructive and insensitive behavior when interacting with customers or representatives of 
customers seeking assistance in Spanish, and an exhibit submitted by Citizens for Local Power 
evidence discrimination by a customer service representative during a chat session.37 Even more

New York, September 17, 2020 -- Moody's Investors Service, ("Moody's") today affirmed the ratings of New
York State Electric and Gas Corporation (NYSEG), including its A3 Issuer rating, and Rochester Gas & Electric
Corporation (RG&E), including its A3 Issuer rating, and changed the outlook for both companies to negative
from stable. See a complete list of debt affected at the end of this press release.
RATINGS RATIONALE
The negative outlooks reflect the potential financial impact resulting from a 22 June 2020 rate case settlement
filing [1] ("Joint Proposal") made between the two companies, the New York Department of Public Service
(DPS) and several intervenor groups. Based on the details in the filing, we expect that both NYSEG and RG&E
will have a cash flow to debt metric in the mid-to-high teen's percent range over the next three years, down
from over 20%, on average, from 2017-2019. Furthermore, the companies have committed to structure their
gas planning with the objective of achieving a zero-net increase in billed gas use, normalized for temperature,
in their service territories through April 2023. We view this trend as posing longer-term challenges for the gas
business.
"NYSEG and RG&E's Joint Proposal will result in a weaker financial profile for both companies, due to a lower
allowed return on equity, cash refunds of excess deferred income taxes and a capitalization ratio based on
52% long-term debt" said Ryan Wobbrock, Vice President -- Senior Credit Officer. "Longer-term, we expect
challenges for NSYEG's and RG&E's gas business, since the filing includes commitments to cut the growth of
billed gas use, with an eye toward more significant gas reductions to comply with the state's Clean Leadership
and Community Protection Act (CLCPA)" added Wobbrock.
NYSEG and RG&E's 3-year settlement includes certain features that will limit the incremental cash flow of
each company, such as an allowed ROE of 8.8% and sizeable cash refunds to customers for excess tax
collections in years past. Since 2016, each company has operated under an allowed ROE of 9.0% and had
only limited exposure to cash refunds associated with the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act, while awaiting the
current rate proceeding. Furthermore, the settlement maintains the companies' capital structure, which is
around 52% long-term debt, meaning that debt levels will continue to grow in order to fund increasing capital
spending.
If the Joint Proposal is approved by the New York State Public Service Commission (NYPSC), without
modification, we estimate that the ratio of cash flow from operations, before changes in working capital (CFO
pre-WC) to debt will be between 16-18% for both utilities over the next three years. This is down from historical
CFO pre-WC to debt ratios of over 20% for both companies. By way of comparison, other A3-rated electric
transmission and distribution (T&D) and local gas distribution companies (LDCs) have averaged over 20%
since 2017, while the Baa1 peer group has averaged 18% over the same time period.
The settlement also includes plans to achieve a zero-net increase in billed gas use through the three-year
plan. According to the filing, this means that weather-normalized levels of billed gas use for NYSEG and
RG&E may not grow beyond the gas use projected for the April 2020 -- April 2021 time frame, or 56 million
dekatherms for NYSEG and nearly 59 million dekatherms for RG&E. In addition, the proposal puts forth
several limitations on gas infrastructure capacity investment, which are to be focused, on developing non-
pipeline alternatives and enhancing electrification strategies.
We see the agreement as a marked effort to limit the scope of the gas utilities and as a first step to help
achieve the state's objectives to limit carbon and methane emissions. While the plan is focused on achieving a
net zero increase in billed gas use over the next three years, we expect more significant measures to be


--- Page 20 ---
implemented over the long-term, since the state's CLCPA targets a 40% reduction of greenhouse gas
emissions by 2030 and 80% by 2050. Therefore, it is likely that more material gas reductions will follow in the
years after the rate plan, which increases risk for the rate base if mitigating measures are not put in place, such
as adjustments to gas asset depreciation rates.
The long-term erosion of the gas business is a credit negative for that segment, but we recognize that both
NYSEG's and RG&E's electric businesses will benefit from the carbon transition. The electric T&D assets
should have more rate base investment opportunities in order to absorb the increased demand for electricity,
incorporate new renewable generation onto the grid and transport cleaner energy to customers.
The Joint Proposal has positive social implications since it shows that the utilities are focused on maintaining
cooperative and constructive customer relationships amid COVID-19 related economic challenges. The rate
plan is designed to limit rate increases in Rate Year 1 (approximately April 2020 to May 2021) by delaying the
initial rate increase to 1 November 2020 and includes several spending deferrals in order to help customers
cope with current economic difficulties. However, the plan provides for more material increases in Rate Years 2
and 3, including a make-whole charge that keeps NYSEG and RG&E unaffected by delayed rates at the end of
the 3-year plan.
We also view this as a good corporate governance strategy since it is tone-sensitive to customers, but in a
manner that preserves cost recovery over the three-year horizon. Moreover, a three-year plan provides
NYSEG and RG&E with greater regulatory and financial certainty through the spring of 2023, whereas other
utilities may be exposed to rate proceedings at a time of even greater economic uncertainty, if progress with
the coronavirus and the economic recovery is slow.
Outlook
The negative outlook for NYSEG and RG&E is based on the potential for financial metric deterioration (e.g.,
CFO pre-WC to debt averaging around 17%, down from over 20% historically), should the NYPSC approve the
Joint Proposal, as well as the beginning stages of limitations on natural gas investments which affects roughly
20% of the utilities' business.
FACTORS THAT COULD LEAD TO AN UPGRADE OR DOWNGRADE OF THE RATINGS
Factors that could lead to a downgrade
NYSEG and RG&E could be downgraded if we expect the final rate settlement approval to result in average
CFO pre-WC to debt metrics below 19% over the next three years. The ratings of either company could also be
downgraded if regulatory provisions are inadequate for NYSEG or RG&E recover the full costs of carbon
transition, resulting in stranded assets or further financial deterioration.
Factors that could lead to an upgrade
Given the negative outlook for each company, it is unlikely that either NYSEG or RG&E will be upgraded over
the next 12-18 months. However, either company could be upgraded if its ratio of CFO pre-WC to debt were to
increase to 22% or higher on a sustainable basis.
A stable outlook could occur, for either NYSEG or RG&E, if the final rate approval is likely to keep CFO pre-
WC to debt levels above 19%.
