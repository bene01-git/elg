reading support systems, and conservation voltage optimization. Please list and quantify all cost 
reduction benefits provided by AMI implementation in 2017-2021 to date and provide any 
forecasts for cost reduction benefits provided in 2021-2025. Provide any supporting workpapers 
and documentation. 
a. Please explain how the Company will track reduced costs due to AMI and how the Company 
will pass back the savings to individual service classes. 
b. Please explain where each type of cost that has the potential to be reduced (meter reading 
labor, field services labor, meter capital, contractor and Company outage management labor, call 
center labor, meter reading support systems, and conservation voltage optimization) is grouped 
or assigned with respect to the categories that are used in your ECOS studies (e.g. Transmission,

Subject to the foregoing objection previously submitted, as set forth in the Company’s AMI 
Metrics Report for December 31, 2020 filed in Case 17-M-0178 and which are available on the 
Commission’s website, during calendar year 2020 the Company quantified the following costs 
reductions due to AMI on a combined Orange and Rockland/Rockland Electric Company basis: 
 
 Reduction in manual meter operations costs – $1,055,000

lower allocations of these costs to customer classes.  For example, the reduction in meter reading 
costs would be allocated to all customers based on their respective number of meters. 
 
The Company does not currently forecast the prospective annual monetary benefits of AMI.

Question No. : 38 
 
  
Please provide the following information pertaining to electric SC-1 Residential General: 
a. Please explain when and why seasonal rates were established for this service classification 
i Please provide the supporting case number and PSC Order. 
b. Has the seasonal rate structure evolved over the last 10 years? If so, please explain how. 
c. Please explain when the Company performed its last season rate differential study to see if the 
winter/summer seasonal rate differential is appropriate. 
d. Please provide the documentation that discusses why the first 250 kWh winter/summer rates 
must be the same. 
 
 
Response 
a. As a summer peaking utility, the Company’s delivery rates are higher in the summer 
months than in the winter months.  Seasonal rate differentials were originally established 
when the Company was vertically integrated and reflected differentials in total rates 
which included both delivery and generation. In the early 1980s, there was a concern 
about the seasonal differentials that were included in residential rates. This led to a 1982 
change in Public Service Law, which still stands today, and prohibits utilities from 
reflecting seasonal differentials in residential rates for the first 250 kWh of monthly 
consumption. 
 
The Company’s current seasonal rate differentials are a holdover from that time. When 
rates were unbundled about 20 years ago, electric production costs were removed from 
the bundled rates to create the Company’s unbundled delivery rate structure. The 
seasonal differentials resulting from that original determination of delivery service rates 
have been perpetuated in rate cases since then. 
 
 
 
b. In Case No. 10-E-0362, the Company eliminated the declining block rate structure for the 
SC No. 1 General Service winter rates.  
 
c. The Company has not performed such study to the best of its knowledge.

d. The Company follows the New York Public Service Law related to the treatment of the 
first 250 kWh summer and winter rates.  Specifically, Article 4 of Public Service Law § 
65, Paragraph 10(a) states the following: 
 
Notwithstanding any other provision of law, in any case where electric corporations are 
permitted incremental seasonal differentials in rates or charges, such differentials shall 
not be imposed in any month upon any residential customer for the first two hundred fifty 
kilowatt hours used by such customer in such month; provided, however, that the 
commission may promulgate rules to permit the imposition of such seasonal differentials 
where a customer requests a special rate that may include a seasonal differential or 
where service is to be provided to a residential customer for a temporary period not to 
exceed six months.
