13 
Q. The Company proposes rebates for Direct Current Fast Charging (“DCFC”) stations. Is 
14 
it important that the Company support DCFC beyond make-ready infrastructure?  
15 
A.  Yes, it is critical for the Company to support DCFC beyond just make-ready infrastructure. 
16 
Due to the rapid nature of the state’s climate goals, it is important to remove barriers for 
17 
charging station deployment, which supports transportation electrification and reduce 
18 
greenhouse gas emissions. The two main barriers to widespread DCFC deployment are 1) high 
19 
capital costs of infrastructure and make-ready costs and 2) rates that make the stations 
20 
uneconomical. Removal of both of these barriers is necessary to support a robust network of 
21 
DCFC charging infrastructure.  The proposed DCFC rebate addresses this first barrier and 
22 
complements the current efforts the state is undertaking to reduce the make-ready cost barriers. 
23

8

1

2 
    The Company, in its EV Panel testimony, noted that based on National Grid’s experience 
3 
in Massachusetts and Rhode Island,12 incentives for make-ready infrastructure alone was 
4 
insufficient to encourage widespread uptake of DCFC and still left barriers for EV deployment. 
5 
When incentives for DCFC became available, however, uptake in the make-ready program 
6 
increased, indicating that in order for the Company to meaningfully support DCFC, utilities 
7 
need to think beyond in-the-ground investments.13  Therefore, in order to stimulate the EV 
8 
charging market, DCFC rebates—such as those proposed by the Company—are seemingly 
9 
necessary.
