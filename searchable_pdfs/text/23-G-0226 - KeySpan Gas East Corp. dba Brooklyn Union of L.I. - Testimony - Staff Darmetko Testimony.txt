Q. 
Please explain the terms “depreciation” and “net 
1 
salvage” as used in ratemaking. 
2 
A. 
Depreciation, as used in ratemaking, is a method 
3 
of recovering capital costs, less net salvage, 
4 
related to plant-in-service over the expected 
5 
useful life of the plant.  In its most basic 
6 
terms, it is the allocation of the value of 
7 
utility plant consumed by customers, in each 
8 
year of the assets expected life, inclusive of 
9 
an estimate of net salvage.  Net salvage is any 
10 
cash received for a removed asset, less the cost 
11 
to remove it. 
12 
Q. 
Please explain the term “net salvage factor.” 
13 
A. 
Net salvage factor refers to the component of 
14 
the depreciation rate that allows recovery of 
15 
estimated net salvage.  It is expressed as a 
16 
percentage, based on original cost of plant.  
17 
For example, assume that a unit of property has 
18 
a total installed cost of $1,000, $0 estimated 
19 
future salvage, and estimated cost of removal of 
20 
$500.  The net salvage factor is calculated as 
21 
follows: Rate = ($0 - $500) / $1,000.  This 
22 
equates to a negative 0.50, or negative 50 
23 
percent net salvage factor. 
24

Q. 
Please explain the term “average service life.” 
1 
A. 
Average service life is the arithmetic average 
2 
of the length of the lives of the units of 
3 
property in a specific account.  It equates to 
4 
the area under the survivor curve from age zero 
5 
to the maximum life divided by 100 percent.  A 
6 
survivor curve is a graphical depiction that 
7 
represents the percentage plant surviving in 
8 
each age interval. 
9 
Q. 
Please explain the term “depreciation rate.” 
10 
A. 
A depreciation rate is a percentage rate that is 
11 
applied to the value of gross plant-in-service, 
12 
by account, in order to determine the annual 
13 
depreciation expense. 
14 
Q. 
Please explain the term “depreciation expense.” 
15 
A. 
Depreciation expense, in any given year, is the 
16 
product of gross plant-in-service multiplied by 
17 
the applicable depreciation rate.  Depreciation 
18 
expense allows for the recovery of an 
19 
investment, less net salvage, over the expected 
20 
life of the investment. 
21 
Q. 
What is the “book reserve”? 
22 
A. 
The book reserve is the continued accumulation 
23 
of the annual depreciation expense accruals,
