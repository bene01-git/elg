Dear Secretary Phillips: 
 
AARP New York files this petition for rehearing alleging an error of fact pursuant to Section 3.7 
of 16 NYCRR.  AARP New York asserts the Commission erred in concluding that Consolidated 
Edison’s Energy Affordability Policy (EAP) low-income discount programs are not under 
enrolled by a significant number of customers.1  In making this sweeping conclusion, it is 
apparent that the Commission ignored the evidence and arguments regarding Medicaid contained 
in our post-hearing letter filed on May 5, 2023.2

The Commission also rejected the assertion of the Public Utility Law Project (PULP), based on 
census data, that 440,000 income-eligible households are not enrolled in the utility’s low-income 
programs. The Commission rejected AARP New York’s assertion, based on OTDA data,  that 
460,000 recipients of HEAP benefits are not enrolled in the utility’s low-income programs.  The 
Commission anchored its conclusion that Con Ed need not do any additional outreach and 
education beyond the efforts set forth in the Joint Proposal on the agreement of these parties that 
there are an unknown number of low-income households in Con Edison’s territory not enrolled 
in the utility’s EAP programs because they lack eligibility, either because they are not a customer 
of Con Edison or a recipient of an eligible program.  
The Commission erred by (1) ignoring the information on Medicaid present by AARP New York 
and (2) not requiring Con Ed to take additional steps, as described in AARP’ New York’s post-
hearing letter, to increase enrollment in a meaningful amount. 
 
The Order’s Reasoning and Conclusion 
On page 85, the Order acknowledged two of the key points made by PULP.  First, the 
Order pointed to testimony regarding census data, stating: 
In its direct testimony, among other things, PULP asserted that, based on 
Witness Yates’s review of American Community Survey (ACS) Microdata, 
there were an estimated 912,806 low-income households in Con Edison’s 
electric service area in 2020, but that Con Edison’s EAP was under-
enrolled, contending that only approximately 49 percent of low-income 
households in the Company’s service territory were enrolled in 2020.  
[Footnote 164:  Hearing Exhibit 700, Yates Direct Testimony, pp. 23-24.] 
 
Second, the Order acknowledged that the City of New York’s Human Resources Agency (HRA) 
advised Con Edison in June 2020 that it would cease including Medicaid in the group of public 
benefit programs used in the data matching process.  This process results in automatic enrollment 
of eligible customers in the low-income programs.   The Order noted that “PULP Witness Yates 
opined that, in his estimation based on his review of ACS Microdata, Medicaid-only households 
may account for 239,100, or 26 percent of all the low-income households in Con Edison’s 
service territory in 2020.  [Footnote 169:  Hearing Exhibit 700, Yates Direct Testimony, p. 29; 
Hearing Exhibit 710, WDY-01, p. 49.]”  The Order did not provide any further discussion of  
PULP’s assertion.

On page 86,the Order summarized part of the assertions of AARP New York and PULP, 
stating: 
PULP and AARP oppose the JP, among other reasons, because they contend 
it is unaffordable, particularly for those low-income households that they 
allege are eligible for, but not enrolled in, Con Edison’s EAP - 
approximately 440,000 low-income households. [Footnote 170:  PULP 
Statement in Opposition, p. 5; AARP Statement in Opposition, pp. 1-3.] 
PULP and AARP maintain that the JP does not require Con Edison to 
expand its outreach and education efforts to increase EAP enrollment and 
recommend the Commission modify the JP to require Con Edison to 
identify customers potentially eligible for EAP and conduct direct outreach. 
[171:  PULP Statement in Opposition, pp. 16-19; PULP Post-Hearing Brief, 
pp. 2-3; AARP Post-Hearing Brief, p. 3.]  AARP also recommends Con 
Edison and DPS Staff be directed to close the enrollment gap and that Con 
Edison be required to conduct outreach in other languages, and develop and 
implement an advertising/promotional campaign focused on Medicaid 
recipients. [Footnote 172: AARP Post-Hearing Brief, pp. 3-6.]  However, 
both parties recognize that there are some low-income households in Con 
Edison’s territory not enrolled in Con Edison’s EAP because they lack 
eligibility, either because they are not a customer of Con Edison or a 
recipient of an eligible program. [Footnote 173:  Tr. 184, 205, 207, 212-
214; PULP Post-hearing Brief, p. 4; AARP Post-hearing Brief, pp. 2-3.] 
The Order (at 86-87) then narrowly focuses on the 440,000 number to the exclusion of the 
evidence regarding the impact of not including Medicaid in the match, stating, “NYC, DPS Staff, 
and Con Edison all assert that PULP and AARP have not substantiated their claims that 
approximately 440,000 low-income households in Con Edison’s territory are eligible, but not 
enrolled in Con Edison’s EAP.”   The Order points to the “failures” of AARP New York and 
PULP to demonstrate that each of the 440,000 people who not enrolled in Con Edison’s EAP 
programs but who meet the income-eligibility thresholds according to the census data or receive 
HEAP benefits are actually Con Edison customers.  The Order concluded on page 88: 
While we share the interests of PULP and AARP to ensure that low-income 
customers are provided with appropriate assistance to reduce their energy 
burden, this record does not support PULP and AARP’s assertions 
regarding the level of customers that may be eligible and unenrolled in Con 
Edison’s EAP.

The Order failed to confront the evidence and arguments regarding PULP’s analysis of census data 
and the impacts of not including Medicaid in the data match process.  The Order further failed to 
direct actions to close the enrollment gap. 
The Order Ignored Factual Evidence Regarding Medicaid 
AARP New York presented evidence in our post-hearing letter (at 3) showing the impact of not 
including Medicaid in the data match process, stating:  
It is commonsense that some number of New York City’s 4.37 million 
residents 
receiving 
Medicaid 
benefits 
[footnote

enrolled in the Company’s low-income programs. AARP New York 
provided testimony relevant to the Medicaid issue, pointing to an analysis 
prepared by HRA in 2014. [Footnote 4: Tr. 185, l. 22 – Tr. 186, l. 20.]  
During the course of Con Ed’s 2013 electric rate case (13-E-0030), parties 
realized that Medicaid was included as a qualifying program for the gas 
low-income discount program but not for the electric program.  The 
Commission directed the Company to file a report detailing the number of 
Medicaid recipients living in New York City, totaling about 3.7 million in 
2014, [2014-05-01.pdf (ny.gov)] who are Con Ed electric customers but are 
not participating in its electric discount program.  Con Ed stated on page 3 
of the August 21, 2014 report that “The City of New York Human 
Resources 
Administration 
(‘HRA’) 
advised 
the 
Company 
that 
approximately 123,000 additional customers would become eligible for the 
electric Low Income Program if receipt of Medicaid qualified customers for 
the electric Low Income Program.” [Footnote 5:  Emphasis added;  
https://documents.dps.ny.gov/public/Common/ViewDoc.aspx?DocRefId=
{96A60307-F613-4F3F-BA49-A2C75A2F4C16] 
. 
It 
is 
a 
logical 
assumption, therefore, that at least 100,000 New York City residents 
receiving Medicaid are also Con Ed customers who are not enrolled in the 
EAP programs.  Had the Settling Parties been so inclined, the JP could 
easily have addressed the Medicaid issue. 
Moreover, PULP provided testimony demonstrating the negative impact of not including 
Medicaid in the data match process, stating: 
Upon receiving the Company’s response to I/R PULP-71, I reviewed the 
quarterly low income reports again and noted that EAP enrollment rose 
materially (6.5%) during the first three quarters of 2020, from 432,103 to 
459,994 participants. Exhibit ___(WDY-01), 2). I also verified that this was

PULP also testified (at 28-29) that “It’s not possible from the ACS Microdata to determine 
exactly how many of such [Medicaid-only] customers there were in 2020, but a good estimate 
can be made by computing the number of low-income households in the service area who 
received Medicaid benefits but did not receive SNAP, SSI or cash assistance. According to the 
ACS Microdata, such households accounted for about 239,100, or 26%, of all of the service 
area’s low-income households in 2020. (Exhibit ___(WDY-01), 46).”  It would be unreasonable 
to assert that none of those households are Con Edison customers. 
There are now approximately 600,000 more residents of New York City receiving Medicaid 
benefits than in 2014 (4.3 million versus 3.7 million).  HRA’s analysis in 2014 showed that the 
addition of Medicaid as a qualifying program for the electric EAP program data match would 
result in the enrollment of 123,000 additional customers.  It would be surprising if a similar 
analysis did not yield similar results.
