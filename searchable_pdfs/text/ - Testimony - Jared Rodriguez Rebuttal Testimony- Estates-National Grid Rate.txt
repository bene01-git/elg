Q. Should the Company be granted regulated monopoly status with regard to 
1 
implementing geothermal shared loop systems?  
2 
A. Yes. When geothermal loops are shared by separate private entities for the 
3 
purpose of accessing remote thermal “sinks or sources” like geothermal well fields 
4 
or industrial waste heat, and when these shared loops are located in public rights of 
5 
way, the Company should function as a regulated monopoly in a similar fashion to 
6 
its natural gas delivery regulated monopoly status. Granting a shared loop thermal 
7 
energy business model to the Company provides a number of significant benefits 
8 
to the service territory and to the people of New York State. These benefits include 
9 
but are not limited to: (1) acceleration of the electrification of heat; (2) reduction of 
10 
risk to the Company and to rate payers related to the acquisition of stranded assets 
11 
in the form of natural gas infrastructure; (3) radical reduction of financial, 
12 
regulatory and logistical barriers to end-user owners of geothermal heating and 
13 
cooling systems; and (4) provides a platform for market transmission and the 
14 
development of new economic activities by private sector actors as thermal energy 
15 
providers. These concepts expand on my previous testimony as it relates to the 
16 
development of a regulatory framework for thermal energy within the context of 
17 
New York State’s Renewing the Energy Vision. Over the medium and long-term, 
18 
granting regulated monopoly status to the Company for the purpose of developing 
19 
and maintaining shared thermal energy infrastructure (i.e. geothermal shared loops)
