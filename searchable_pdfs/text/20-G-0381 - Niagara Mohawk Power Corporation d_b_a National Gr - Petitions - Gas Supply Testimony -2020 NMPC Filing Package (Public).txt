Q. 
What is the role of storage in satisfying the Company’s city gate 
7 
requirements? 
8 
A. 
A significant volume of the Company’s winter normal supply obligation and 
9 
design day demand requirement are met by deliveries of gas withdrawn from 
10 
storage.  Under the Company’s storage contracts with DETI, daily storage 
11 
deliverability declines as inventory decreases.  The provisions of the contracts 
12 
or tariffs that provide for these declines are known as withdrawal ratchets.  Once 
13 
reached, these ratchets cannot be reversed until the following year; however, 
14 
the Company’s transportation capacity associated with storage can also be used 
15 
to transport gas supplies purchased in the marketplace on a primary firm basis 
16 
in place of storage withdrawals.  The Company’s storage withdrawal plan 
17 
(storage rule curve) is established prior to the winter season and designed to 
18 
maintain inventories at levels that allow sufficient storage deliverability to meet 
19 
forecast winter peak conditions including a design day and a design winter 
20 
season.    
21 
 
22 


--- Page 15 ---
Testimony of the Gas Supply Panel 
 
Page 13 of 40 
 
 
Market area storage provides the Company with services that cannot be easily 
1 
duplicated with other assets.  The most important attribute that storage provides 
2 
is flexibility, which is vital to meet fluctuating customer requirements.  The 
3 
Company’s DETI storage service contract provides no-notice end-of-day 
4 
balancing that minimizes the risk of balancing penalties.  Storage allows 
5 
enhanced flexibility to account for customer load changes on weekends and 
6 
holidays.  In contrast, typical supply purchases are ratable, meaning they must 
7 
be called on at the same volume for each day of a weekend or holiday period.  
8 
In addition, storage improves the load factor of flowing pipeline assets during 
9 
the off-peak season and is critical to the Company’s efforts to meet design 
10 
weather conditions.  The Company uses a least cost dispatch model to withdraw 
11 
from storage in conjunction with its rule curves, but ensures that it turns over 
12 
the minimum inventory required by its storage provider.  The Company strives 
13 
for a ratable injection plan to refill storage during the May through October 
14 
period, but incorporates flexibility to adapt to unexpected cold weather in the 
15 
spring and fall and to accommodate injection ratchets imposed by its storage 
16 
provider.

V.  
Portfolio Management 
1 
Q. 
Please describe the Company’s gas supply planning goals.  
2 
A. 
The Company’s primary gas supply planning goals are to:   
3 
(i) 
Dispatch the gas supply portfolio assets under a least-cost strategy to 
4 
reliably meet projected primary firm demand;  
5 
(ii) 
Maintain a diverse portfolio of gas supply, storage, and transportation 
6 
capacity contracts with varying terms and pricing provisions; and 
7 
(iii) 
Implement a formal hedging program to mitigate price volatility.  
8 
 
9 
These goals are consistent with the Commission’s “Statement of Policy 
10 
Regarding Gas Purchasing Practices” issued in Case 97-G-0600 and updated by 
11 
letter issued March 31, 2011.  The Company maintains a portfolio that meets 
12 
forecast customer requirements under design conditions while maintaining 
13 
sufficient flexibility for mild winters. 
14 
 
15 
Reliability is maintained by contracting for pipeline capacity that enables the 
16 
Company to arrange for the delivery of gas from reliable and diverse supply 
17 
points to the Company’s city gates in quantities sufficient to meet forecast 
18 
design day and design hour primary firm load.

VIII.  Gas Cost Volatility Management 
10 
Q. 
What steps does the Company take to try to mitigate the impact of gas cost 
11 
volatility on its customers? 
12 
A. 
The Company mitigates volatility in the gas commodity markets in several 
13 
ways.  First, the Company maintains a balanced portfolio that includes contract 
14 
storage in the market area.  This allows the Company to inject gas during the 
15 
summer for withdrawal during the winter, which enables the Company to 
16 
mitigate price volatility during the winter when demand is greatest.  Second, 
17 
the portfolio incorporates pricing diversity that minimizes exposure to volatility 
18 
at a single pricing point or market index.  Finally, the Company mitigates price 
19 
volatility with a formal hedging program. 
20 
 
21 
Q. 
Please describe the Company’s hedging program.  
22 


--- Page 36 ---
Testimony of the Gas Supply Panel 
 
Page 34 of 40 
 
A. 
The Company targets to hedge 50 percent but no more than 60 percent of 
1 
forecast sales in the November through March period assuming normal 
2 
weather.  The hedged portfolio includes financial transactions and forecasted 
3 
storage withdrawals.  Prices are fixed through a combination of planned storage 
4 
withdrawals, which provide a natural hedge at the average price of summer 
5 
period injections, and financial hedges which include swaps and options.   
6 
 
7 
To determine the hedge quantity required, the Company first forecasts firm 
8 
sales (excluding rate class SC-8 Standby Sales Service) for the November 
9 
through March period, assuming normal weather conditions and multiplies the 
10 
results by 50 percent (Step 1).  Next, forecast monthly storage withdrawals are 
11 
subtracted from the result obtained in Step 1 (Step 2).  The results from Step 2 
12 
equal the quantity of gas to be financially hedged to achieve the 50 percent 
13 
target for the November through March period.  Once the volume of gas to be 
14 
financially hedged each month is known, a monthly hedging plan is created.  
15 
The purchases are spread evenly over 16 months starting 18 months prior to the 
16 
start of November.  Each financial transaction is settled against the expiring 
17 
month’s last day settlement price and the applicable Platt’s Inside FERC Gas 
18 
Market Report’s “First of the Month” index price.  The gross settlement payout 
19 
or gain is included in gas costs for that month.  
20 
 
21

Has the Company’s hedging strategy changed in the past year? 
22 


--- Page 37 ---
Testimony of the Gas Supply Panel 
 
Page 35 of 40 
 
A.  
Yes.  Beginning in winter 2015-16, the Company began to complement 
1 
NYMEX hedges with basis hedges to better align hedges with forecast purchase 
2 
locations sourced in the northeast producing region of Marcellus and Utica.  The 
3 
basis markets have developed with enough liquidity so that the Company can 
4

21

22 
November through March period.
