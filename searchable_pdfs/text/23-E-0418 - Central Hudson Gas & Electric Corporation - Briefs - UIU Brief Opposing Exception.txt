I. INTRODUCTION
The Utility Intervention Unit (“UIU”) of the New York State Department of State’s 
Division of Consumer Protection submits this brief in response to certain exceptions asserted by 
Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corporation (“Central Hudson” or the “Company”) in the 
Company’s brief that was filed with the Public Service Commission (the “Commission”) on May 
21, 2024 (“Brief on Exceptions”).1 Specifically, UIU writes to clarify its opposition2 to the 
Company’s recommendation that the Commission adopt Central Hudson’s proposal to (1) update 
the performance targets associated with the  Percent of Calls Answered within 30 Seconds metric 
(the “ Metric”) and (2) include customer callbacks in the Metric.

A. Customer Service Performance Metrics 
a. Call Answer Rate 
For the reasons set forth below, the Commission should reject the Company’s proposal to 
include callbacks in the Metric. As the ALJs observed in their Recommended Decision, including 
callbacks in the Metric deviates from the Company’s metrics manual and previous joint proposals, 
and is in contravention of the Commission’s Customer Service Performance Indicators (“CSPI”) 
Order issued in Case 15-M-0566: “Neither the CSPI Order nor the language in the various rate 
plan calculations anticipates or authorizes the use of callbacks or virtual holds to satisfy the Call 
Answer Rate metric . . . .”3 Significantly, while the ALJs recognize some benefit with using virtual 
hold and callback queue technologies, the ALJs conclude on page 347 of the Recommended 
Decision that “we do not find any rational basis in the record for including calls routed to those 
technologies as having satisfied the metric.” 
In conjunction with excluding callbacks, the Commission should reject the Company’s 
proposal to update the performance targets associated with the Metric, “to align with the 
Company’s actual performance based on a modified Staff calculation methodology that includes 
customer callbacks for the period 2015 through 2019.” As the ALJs succinctly state in the 
Recommended Decision, updating the targets would result in a lowering of the associated negative 
revenue adjustments, thus providing Central Hudson with “little financial motivation to improve 
the service quality that customers deserve.”4 Moreover, as UIU noted in its initial brief, customers 
deserve to see their utility’s performance improve over time; lowering the targets would 
disincentivize the Company from furthering this important objective.

UIU’s objection extends to any retroactive inclusion of callbacks in this Metric, including 
within periods subsequent to 2019 and the Rate Year. As stated above, the ALJs rightly found - 
and UIU agrees - that neither the Commission’s CSPI Order nor Commission orders adopting the 
Company’s 2018 and 2021 joint proposals authorize the use of callbacks for the purpose of the 
Metric.  
 
XIX. CONCLUSION 
UIU respectfully requests that the Commission deny the Company’s proposals to include 
callbacks, update performance targets, and lower negative revenue adjustments.
