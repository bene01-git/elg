Q. 
Do the Companies agree with Staff’s assumptions, on page 25 of the SSEP testimony, 
4 
regarding the Companies’ ability to reach the NE:NY Order’s unit savings targets? 
5 
A. 
No.  Staff has incorrectly assumed that future unit savings can be achieved at the same 
6 
dollar/unit cost associated with savings achieved in prior years.  This is an unrealistic 
7 
assumption.  Certain high volume, lower-cost programs, such as lighting, will continue to 
8 
have diminishing returns going forward as the target audience for these programs has 
9 
been more fully saturated.  This makes it nearly impossible to achieve unit savings at the 
10 
same or reduced cost/unit, as the costs associated with reaching a greater number of 
11 
customers (e.g., LMI customers) inevitably become greater.  In addition, the impact of 
12 
global inflation needs to be taken into consideration, meaning the cost to deliver the same 
13 
items or actions has and will continue to rise.

Q. 
Can the Panel further elaborate on how Staff’s proposed spending levels are inadequate 
1 
to achieve NE:NY unit savings targets? 
2 
A. 
Yes.  First, as it relates to funding LMI programs, Staff’s proposed spending levels are 
3 
much lower than the national average on a dollar per unit of savings basis.  A 
4 
benchmarking study conducted in 2019 looked at over 260 low-income energy efficiency 
5 
programs across North America.  The average cost/unit to deliver savings to LMI 
6 
customers under electric programs was $950/MWh and under gas programs was 
7 
$160/MMBTU.  This average included less expensive kit programs that are now being 
8 
passed over in favor of holistic programs, which include deeper envelope sealing and 
9 
other forms of weatherization.  If the less expensive kits were removed from the 
10 
benchmark information, the benchmark costs for deeper holistic home energy sealing 
11 
programs can be as high as $3,680/MWh and $2,762/MMBTU.  A copy of this study is 
12 
provided as Exhibit __ (EE-R1).  To place this into perspective, the cost per unit of 
13 
savings in the NE:NY Order is estimated at $576/MWh and $108/MMBTU.  
14 
In addition, estimated costs for delivering LMI programs have increased and have 
15 
been affected by recent inflationary pressures.  For example, in the most recent LMI 
16 
Portfolio Implementation Plan (“Plan”) filed in Case 18-M-0084, several Program 
17 
Administrators projected targets that differed materially from those provided in the 
18 
NE:NY Order, with the result being a decrease in the projected level of targeted savings.1  
19 
These differences were the result of: 1) an array of current economic factors, including 
20 
inflation, COVID-19 pandemic-related costs, and global supply chain issues, which

1 
Q. 
Please summarize Staff’s recommendation on the Companies’ proposal of six Energy 
2 
Efficiency EAMs. 
3 
A. 
As further discussed by the Companies’ EAMs Panel, Staff recommended eliminating all 
4 
EAM metrics associated with Energy Efficiency or Heating Electrification.  Staff argues 
5 
that “EAM metrics should not provide utility shareholder incentives for outcomes that are 
6 
required by statute and supported by utility-specific programs and targets.” 
7 
Q. 
Do the Companies agree with this recommendation? 
8 
A. 
No.  The current and proposed Energy Efficiency and Heating Electrification EAMs 
9 
support cost-effective Energy Efficiency investments and only provide incentives to the 
10 
Companies if unit savings are provided at a cost/unit that is less than the target.  In 
11 
addition, policies that incentivize performance, such as EAMs, are widely considered best 
12 
practices for making energy efficiency policies a reality.  For example, the 2022 EPA 
13 
State Energy and Environment Guide to Action notes that financial incentives for utilities 
14 
address barriers to energy efficiency that are inherent in traditional utility business 
15 
models.2  Further, a 2018 study conducted by the American Council for an Energy-
16 
Efficient Economy (“ACEEE”) noted that EAMs are among the most important factors 
17 
contributing to the higher savings and increase of utility energy savings from year to 
18 
year.3
