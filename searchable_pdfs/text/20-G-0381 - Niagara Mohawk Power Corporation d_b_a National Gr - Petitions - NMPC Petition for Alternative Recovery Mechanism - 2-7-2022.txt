As discussed in the Unbilled Fees/Lost Revenues Deferral Letter, in response to the COVID-
19 pandemic, the Governor issued Executive Order No. 202 declaring a State of Emergency on March 
7, 2020 (the “State of Emergency”) and Executive Orders 202.6 and 202.8, characterized as “New 
York State on PAUSE” (the “PAUSE Order”), requiring suspension of non-essential work including 
utility field collections.7   Further, on June 17, 2020, the Governor signed into law Chapters 108 and 
126 of New York’s Laws of 2020, known as the Parker-Mosley Law, which, among other things, 
amended Public Service Law (“PSL”) §32 to require utility corporations to provide residential 
customers a right to enter into deferred payment agreements “without the requirement of a down 
payment, late fees, or penalties, as such is provided for in this article.”8  On May 11, 2021, the 
Governor signed into law further amendments to the PSL, referred to as the Parker-Richardson Act, 
expanding the protections to include qualified small businesses and extending the moratorium on 
termination of electricity and natural gas customers during and after the ongoing COVID-19 State of 
Emergency.  Parker-Richardson also requires utilities to offer deferred payment agreements without 
down payments, late fees, or penalties to eligible residential and small commercial customers during 
the COVID-19 State of Emergency and for 180 days thereafter.  The prohibition on down payments, 
late fees, and penalties in deferred payment agreements is applicable to all arrears incurred throughout 
the duration of the COVID-19 State of Emergency.9  Finally, the Parker-Richardson Act provides that 
the PSL amendments shall not prohibit a utility from recovering lost or deferred revenues after either 
the lifting or expiration of the COVID-19 State of Emergency or December 31, 2021, whichever is 
earlier.10

In response to the PAUSE Order and the amendments to the PSL discussed above, in March 
2020, the Company suspended field collections activities and stopped collecting the late payment fees 
and other fees associated with customer non-payment including, but not limited to, reconnection and

II. 
The Commission Should Approve Niagara Mohawk’s Proposed Alternative Recovery 
Mechanism 
Although the Company met the threshold to defer Unbilled Fees for Rate Year Three and the 
Bridge Period and has recognized the deferral for regulatory accounting purposes, it is unable to do 
so under GAAP. Under GAAP, the Company may only record the deferral on its income statement 
if the Company will be recovering it within 24 months from the end of the annual period in which it 
was recognized.12  The Company has recognized the Rate Year Three and Bridge Period deferrals 
for ratemaking purposes.  In order to record the deferral on its GAAP financial statements, the 
Company would need to recover the deferral by the end of March 2024. As discussed supra, the 
Commission has recently approved a new rate plan13 for the Company that will run through June 30, 
2024, and thus, the Company cannot begin recovery of the deferral until at least 2024 when its next 
electric and gas rate plans may begin. As such, the Company’s GAAP financial statements must omit 
the deferral even though that is an unreasonable financial outcome and results in an unintended 
discrepancy with the Company’s regulatory accounting.14

To prevent GAAP from effectively undercutting the Commission-approved regulatory 
approach, the Company proposes to establish a surcharge/surcredit recovery mechanism that permits 
full recovery/refund of any applicable deferred fees within GAAP’s 24-month recovery timeframe.  
The proposed recovery mechanism will not only permit the Company to recognize deferred fees in 
its GAAP financial statements, it will also modestly improve the Company’s cash flow and decrease 
the likelihood of further downgrades to the Company’s credit ratings.15  At the same time, the 
Company emphasizes that this proposal is not for surcharge/surcredit recovery of all deferrals 
authorized under its rate plans’ Externally Imposed Costs provisions. The Company is requesting the 
alternative recovery mechanism only for the limited subset of deferrals (i.e., related to the Unbilled 
Fees), that cannot currently be recognized on its financial statements under GAAP. Moreover, the 
Company’s request does not seek to alter the criteria for deferrals under its Externally Imposed Costs 
provision. 
For the first year of recovery beginning in July 2022, the Company proposes to recover the 
deferrals for the 15-month period encompassing Rate Year Three (i.e., April 1, 2020 through March 
31, 2021) and the Bridge Period April 1, 2021 through June 30, 2021.  The Company proposes to 
begin recovery of the Unbilled Fees deferral for the first period with a surcharge beginning in July 
2022 and ending in June 2023.  For the second year of the Unbilled Fees deferral, the Company

In addition to providing relief under the terms of its current and former rate plans, the 
Commission has acknowledged a further statutory basis for recovery of the Unbilled Fees deferrals.  
In its November 18, 2021, “Order Authorizing Alternative Recovery Mechanism for Unbilled Fees” 
in Case 19-E-0065, et al., the Commission approved a similar surcharge/surcredit mechanism for 
Consolidated Edison Company of New York, Inc.  In the Consolidated Edison Order, the 
Commission highlighted amendments to PSL §32(9), noting that the implementation of the 
provisions of PSL §32 “…shall not prohibit a utility or municipality from recovering lost or deferred 
revenues after the lifting or expiration of the COVID-19 State Disaster Declaration pursuant to such 
means for recovery as are provided in this chapter, and by means not inconsistent with any of the 
provisions of this article.”  Consistent with the Commission’s findings in the Consolidated Edison 
Order, the amendments to PSL §32 provide further authority for the Company to seek recovery of 
these lost and deferred revenues.  
Finally, the Company acknowledges that customers have been adversely impacted by the 
COVID-19 pandemic.  Thus, the Company proposes to commit $1.9 million of the deferred Unbilled 
Fees/Lost Revenues toward addressing customer utility arrearages.
