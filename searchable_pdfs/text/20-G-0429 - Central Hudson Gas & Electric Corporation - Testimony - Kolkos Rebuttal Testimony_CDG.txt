Recommendation 
3 
Q. 
Please describe the relationship between the Cost Sharing 2.0 and CDG 
4 
Interconnection Program proposals. 
5 
A. 
The Cost Sharing 2.0 Proposal and CDG Interconnection Program proposals both address 
6 
the same overall goal of mitigating financial barriers to DER interconnection. However, 
7 
they are distinct mechanisms that provide different benefits to developers. As described 
8 
at pages 9-10 in my direct testimony, the Cost Sharing 2.0 Proposal facilitates more 
9 
equitable distribution of interconnection costs for certain types of upgrades by allowing 
10 
projects to pay for their pro rata share of the hosting capacity increase associated with an 
11 
upgrade, rather than the full upgrade cost. The CDG Interconnection Program would 
12 
complement, not replace, the Cost Sharing 2.0 Proposal by serving as an alternative 
13 
financing option for CDG projects that would allow CDG developers to amortize all 
14 
project interconnection costs (not just costs for upgrades that provide a hosting capacity 
15 
benefit to multiple projects), up to a dollar-per-MW cap, over a ten-year period.  
16 
Q. 
Is it appropriate to push the CDG Interconnection Program proposal to Case 20-E-
17 
0543? 
18 
A. 
No. The IPWG Petition requesting Commission approval of the Cost Sharing 2.0 
19 
Proposal represents a multi-year collaboration of numerous stakeholders and a balancing 
20 
of diverse, and sometimes competing, interests. The Petition was the subject of a public 
21 
comment process that ended on January 19, 2021.  Reopening it to incorporate aspects of 
22 
the CDG Interconnection Program, as proposed by the Staff Panel, unnecessarily would 
23

4

3

4

Q. 
Is there a precedent in New York for developing innovative programs and policies 
18 
in individual utility rate proceedings before Statewide implementation?  
19 
A. 
Yes. The Commission has adopted innovative programs in utility rate cases that have 
20 
informed development of statewide programs. For example, in the 2017 Niagara Mohawk 
21 
Power Corporation d/b/a National Grid (National Grid) rate case (Case 17-E-0238), the 
22 
Commission approved creation of an Electric Transportation Initiative. Under the Electric 
23 


--- Page 6 ---
5 
 
Transportation Initiative, National Grid made capital upgrades at commercial sites to 
1 
accommodate future installation of electric vehicle charging stations. Findings from this 
2 
program ultimately informed the statewide “Make-Ready Program” that was approved by 
3 
the Commission on July 16, 2020 in Case 18-E-0138.  
4 
Additionally, the Order on Standby and Buyback Service Rate Design and 
5 
Establishing Optional Demand-Based Rates (issued May 16, 2019 in Case 15-E-0751), 
6 
highlights the benefits of utilities pursuing innovative initiatives that can then be 
7 
implemented statewide.  Under that order, the Commission approved for statewide 
8 
implementation of new standby rates the use of the Multi-Party Offset provisions and 
9 
Granular As-Used Demand Charges initially developed by Consolidated Edison of New 
10 
York, Inc. in its separate rate proceedings.  Similarly, the order directed statewide 
11 
adoption of the allocated embedded cost of services (ACOS) methodology initially 
12 
developed by National Grid for use in its rates approved in Case 17-E-0238. 
13 
Q. 
Has the Commission ever encouraged experimentation with utility-specific 
14 
programs prior to statewide implementation in any other context? 
15 
A. 
Yes. At the outset of New York’s Reforming the Energy Vision (REV) initiative, in the 
16

17 
February 2015 Order Adopting Regulatory Framework and Implementation Plan (REV 
18 
Framework Order), the Commission established support for using “Demonstration 
19 
Projects” to generate data, best practices, and other lessons learned to inform the most 
20 
effective strategies to deploy DER. In the context of REV, the Commission found that 
21 
effective demonstration projects should test and explore innovative concepts including, 
22 
but not limited to, new business models for DER implementation, energy technologies, 
23

6 
 
and products that can be incorporated into utility operations, new pricing structures, and 
1

Q. 
Did any programs developed in a single utility’s service territory inform 
4 
development of the Cost Sharing 2.0 Proposal?  
5 
A. 
Yes. The development of the Cost Sharing 2.0 Proposal was informed by findings and 
6 
lessons learned from a Distributed Generation Interconnection REV Demonstration 
7 
Project proposed by National Grid on February 14, 2017 (National Grid Demonstration 
8

9

10 
Grid as well as an alternative cost allocation methodology. The program ultimately 
11 
provided insights into cost allocation strategies that could reduce upfront interconnection 
12 
costs and construction times while providing increased cost certainty to developers.

As explained above, this goal also can be met through programs developed in utility rate 
21 
cases.
