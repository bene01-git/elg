As described in greater detail in the Staff Report, 
the utilities propose that the customer satisfaction measure be 
administered by email.  The survey would be conducted as a

The utilities propose the following language for the

The utilities propose a word scoring scale of: very satisfied, 
satisfied, neither satisfied nor dissatisfied, dissatisfied, and 
very dissatisfied.

The Commenting Utilities state that they have reviewed 
the Staff Report and have no objection to conducting the 
statewide customer satisfaction survey that resulted from the 
collaborative. 
 
 
National Fuel states that consensus was not reached on 
the survey, as it disagrees with several of its features.  
National Fuel further contends that experts were not involved 
with the development of the proposed survey, and that it is not 
scientifically designed.  The methodological flaws identified by 
National Fuel include that email surveys do not broadly reach 
the customer population, and the proposed survey lacks check 
questions which typically help to measure response accuracy.  
National Fuel states that due to these deficiencies, the 
proposed survey’s results may contain significant bias, which 
could lead to questionable measurement of customer satisfaction.  
Therefore, National Fuel states the proposed uniform survey 
should not be adopted. 
 
DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION 
 
 
The Commission acknowledges that National Fuel has 
several concerns regarding the survey as proposed, but does not 
find its arguments compelling.  The Commission finds that the 
design of the pilot is likely to reveal any significant response 
bias in the survey.  Among other things, the fact that the pilot 
survey will be administered concurrently with each utility’s 
existing survey, will allow the results to be compared. 
 
 
National Fuel states that its current survey was 
designed for quality assurance purposes, and that the existing 
survey should continue as a good business practice.  The 
Commission agrees and shall require the same of all utilities in

this proceeding.  If the responses to the pilot survey are 
biased, as National Fuel suggests, this flaw is likely to be 
revealed in significant differences in results between the 
existing and proposed surveys.6  
 
 
Furthermore, several of the utilities involved their 
survey contractors to the collaborative process.  It is 
therefore inaccurate to say that survey design experts were not 
involved in the development of the proposed survey. 
 
 
Finally, while National Fuel may be correct that it 
(and perhaps other utilities) does not have valid email 
addresses for many of its customers, and that response rates for 
email surveys are relatively low, the selection of email as the 
vehicle allows the pilot survey to be administered by the 
utilities with relative ease and at low cost, compared to 
telephone or mailed surveys.  This facilitates implementation of 
the statewide survey as a pilot effort.  If the pilot shows that 
the emailed survey produces insufficient responses and/or 
inconsistent results compared to the existing surveys, this can 
be considered by the Commission when evaluating whether the 
survey should be continued, and/or any modifications made.  
 
 
The pilot survey, if it produces valid and reliable 
results, would achieve the Commission’s goal of establishing a 
uniform statewide measurement of customer satisfaction.  
Therefore, the survey method described above shall be 
implemented on a pilot basis starting on January 1, 2019.  After 
one year, Staff, the utilities, and interested parties shall 
reconvene, to evaluate the results of the survey and consider
