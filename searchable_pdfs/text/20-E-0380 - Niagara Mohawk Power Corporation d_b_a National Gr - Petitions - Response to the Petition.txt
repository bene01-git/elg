PRELIMINARY STATEMENT 
 
 
 
 Multiple Intervenors, an unincorporated association of over 55 large industrial, 
commercial, and institutional energy consumers with manufacturing and other facilities located 
throughout New York State, including the Niagara Mohawk Power Corporation d/b/a National 
Grid (“National Grid”) service territory, hereby responds in opposition to the “Petition of Niagara 
Mohawk Power Corporation d/b/a National Grid for Limited Waiver of the Revenue Decoupling 
Mechanism to Allow the Recovery of Prior Period Reconciliation Amounts” (“Petition”), filed on 
November 18, 2022, in Case 20-E-0380, National Grid’s most recent electric rate proceeding.1  
Notice of the Petition was filed in New York State Register published on December 7, 2022 (PSC-
49-22-00020-P). 
 
In its Petition, National Grid seeks authorization from the New York State Public 
Service Commission (“Commission”) for waivers of its electric tariff to recover certain previously-
unrecovered, prior period reconciliation amounts pursuant to its Revenue Decoupling Mechanism 
(“RDM”).  National Grid reports that certain credits associated with Electric Energy Efficiency 
Transition Implementation Plan costs inadvertently were omitted from RDM calculations for the 
period April 2018 through March 2021.  (Petition at 1-2.)  According to National Grid, this 
omission resulted in the following under-recoveries during the period in question:

National Grid proposes in the Petition to recover those amounts from customers in 
the aforementioned service classes pursuant to different schedules.  Specifically, National Grid 
proposes to recover: (a) $22,923 from S.C. 2D customers as part of the utility’s next scheduled 
RDM reconciliation, the filing for which will be made in June 2023; (b) $2,147,614 from S.C. 3 
customers over a 15-month period beginning April 2023, representing an incremental increase in 
the RDM rate of $0.11 per kW; and (c) $16,240,635 from  S.C. 3A customers over a 27-month 
period beginning April 2023, resulting in an incremental increase in the RDM rate of $0.63 per 
kW.  (Id. at 4-5.)  National Grid is not seeking to recover any carrying charges in addition to these 
amounts.  (Id. at 4, n.4.) 
 
Multiple Intervenors hereby advances two concerns with respect to the relief sought 
by National Grid in the Petition.  First, it appears that at least a portion of the recoveries proposed 
by the utility are barred by the Commission’s regulations prohibiting certain forms of backbills.  
Second, if, arguendo, the Commission is inclined to grant National Grid’s Petition in whole or 
material part, the period applicable to recoveries from S.C. 3A customers should be increased 
substantially to moderate what otherwise would be excessive and extremely detrimental rate 
impacts.  For instance, if National Grid is authorized the recover all or most of the amount sought 
from S.C. 3A customers in the Petition, then the proposed recovery period applicable to those 
customers should be extended from 27 months to at least 63 months (i.e., by an additional three 
years) in order to moderate the resulting customer impacts.

ARGUMENT 
 
POINT I 
 
 THE PROPOSED RECOVERIES APPEAR TO VIOLATE 
COMMISSION REGULATIONS PROHIBITING CERTAIN 
FORMS OF BACKBILLS 
 
 
 
Section 13.9 of the Commission’s Rules and Regulations contains certain 
limitations on a utility’s ability to issue backbills, which appear applicable to the relief sought in 
the Petition, at least in material part.  To the extent National Grid’s Petition seeks authorization to 
issue backbills in violation of the Commission’s prohibitions of same, it must be denied. 
 
A backbill is defined, in pertinent part, as: “that portion of any bill, other than a 
levelized bill, which represents charges not previously billed for service that was actually delivered 
to the customer during a period before the current billing cycle.”  16 N.Y.C.R.R. § 13.1(b)(17).  
Here, National Grid is seeking authorization to charge customers, beginning in April 2023, for 
service that was actually delivered to customers during the period running from April 2018 through 
March 2021 – i.e., a period that began five years prior to the start of the proposed incremental 
collections. 
 
While backbills of certain types are allowed, other types are prohibited by the 
Commission’s regulations.  For instance, Section 13.9(c)(1) of the Commission’s Rules and 
Regulations provides that: 
 When the failure to bill at an earlier time was due to utility 
deficiency, a utility shall not bill a customer for service rendered 
more than 12 months before the utility actually became aware of the 
circumstance, error or condition that caused the underbilling, unless 
the utility can demonstrate that the customer knew or reasonably 
should have known that the original billing was incorrect.

16 N.Y.C.R.R. § 13.9(c)(1).  Section 13.9(c)(2) of the Commission’s Rules and Regulations 
provides that: 
 A utility shall not bill a customer for service rendered more than 24 
months before the utility actually became aware of the circumstance, 
error or condition that caused the underbilling, unless the utility can 
demonstrate that the customer knew or reasonably should have 
known that the original billing was incorrect. 
 
16 N.Y.C.R.R. § 13.9(c)(2). 
 
Here, the service in question was rendered as early as April 2018, or over four and 
one-half years before the Petition was filed.  Customers receiving electric service from National 
Grid during the period April 2018 through March 2021 had a reasonable expectation that the bills 
received for such service were accurate, and certainly had no way of knowing that the utility’s 
original billing was incorrect.  The Commission’s regulations prohibiting certain forms of 
backbills recognize that, at some point, customers’ bills need to be treated as final, and a utility’s 
ability to correct prior, inaccurate bills is not unlimited. 
 
The fact that National Grid is not serving new bills on customers for the entire 
previously-unrecovered amounts but, instead, is seeking to recover such amounts over time (for 
S.C. 3 and S.C. 3A customers) via an inflated RDM surcharge does not alter whether such activities 
would run afoul of the Commission’s prohibitions on backbills.  Under the utility’s proposal, the 
inflated RDM surcharge would constitute a “portion of any bill … which represents charges not 
previously billed for service that was actually delivered to the customer during a period before the 
current billing cycle.”  See 16 N.Y.C.R.R. § 13.1(b)(17).  In accordance with the backbill 
prohibitions, National Grid only can go back either 12 or 24 months from when it “actually became 
aware of the circumstance, error or condition that caused the underbilling,” depending on whether

the failure to bill the amounts in question is found to have been caused by “utility deficiency.”  See 
16 N.Y.C.R.R. §§ 13.9(c)(1) and (2). 
 
For the foregoing reasons, it appears that at least some of the recoveries proposed 
by National Grid in the Petition would violate the Commission’s prohibitions against backbills. 
 
POINT II 
 
 IF, ARGUENDO, NATIONAL GRID IS AUTHORIZED TO 
RECOVER THE AMOUNTS SOUGHT IN THE PETITION, 
IN WHOLE OR MATERIAL PART, THEN THE PERIOD 
PROPOSED TO EFFECTUATE SUCH COLLECTIONS 
FROM S.C. 3A CUSTOMERS SHOULD BE EXTENDED 
 
 
 
For the reasons set forth in Point I, supra, it appears that National Grid’s proposal 
to recover previously-unrecovered RDM reconciliations would run afoul of the Commission’s 
prohibitions against backbills, at least with respect to a portion of the amounts in question.  If, 
arguendo, the Commission nevertheless grants National Grid’s requested relief in whole or 
material part, then the period proposed to effectuate such collections from S.C. 3A customers 
should be extended for the reasons set forth below.  Indeed, if the requested recoveries are 
authorized in full or some amount close thereto, then the period used to recover prior period 
amounts from S.C. 3A customers should be extended from the 27 months proposed by the utility 
to at least 63 months (i.e., an three additional years). 
 
The amount that National Grid seeks to recover from S.C. 2D customers is $22,913.  
The utility proposes to recover that amount in the next RDM reconciliation period (filing to be 
made in June 2023).  (Petition at 4-5.)  To the extent such recovery is allowed in whole or material 
part, Multiple Intervenors agrees that, spread over the entire S.C. 2D service class, the amount in

question should be considered de minimis and, therefore, has no objections to the utility’s proposed 
recovery period. 
 
The amount that National Grid seeks to recover from S.C. 3 customers is 
$2,147,614, and the utility proposes to utilize a 15-month period to effectuate such recovery.  (Id. 
at 5.)  According to National Grid, the monthly delivery rate impact associated with such proposal 
would be $0.11 per kW, and range within voltage subclasses from 0.56% to 1.66%.  (Id., Ex. 1, p. 
1, and Ex. 2.)  To the extent such recovery is allowed in whole or material part, the resulting rate 
impact, while unfortunate, is not unreasonable.  Therefore, Multiple Intervenors does not object to 
the 15-month recovery period proposed by National Grid for S.C. 3 customers. 
 
The circumstances are materially different, however, with respect to S.C. 3A 
customers.  The amount that National Grid seeks to recover from S.C. 3A customers is 
$16,240,635, and the utility proposes to utilize a 27-month period to effectuate such recovery.  (Id. 
at 5.)  To the extent such recovery is allowed in whole or material part, the resulting delivery rate 
impacts would be huge and, Multiple Intervenors contends, unacceptable.  According to National 
Grid, the monthly delivery rate impact associated with such proposal would be $0.63 per kW, and 
range by voltage subclass from 3.90% to 9.14%.  (Id., Ex. 1, p. 2 and Ex. 2.) 
 
Such proposed delivery rate impacts would be on top of, inter alia, material, annual 
delivery rate impacts authorized in National Grid’s existing electric rate plan, any RDM surcharges 
from the most-recent reconciliation period, surcharges related to Earnings Adjustment 
Mechanisms and other rate plan provisions, as well as increasing costs associated with the State’s 
many, ongoing clean energy initiatives.  Such additional, unforeseeable delivery rate impacts 
would be extremely detrimental to S.C. 3A customers, and also could frustrate continuing efforts 
to attempt to retain and attract businesses to Upstate New York.

Inasmuch as customers are not at fault for the under-recoveries identified in the 
Petition, the Commission should adopt reasonable measures to moderate the rate impacts 
associated with any recoveries that are authorized herein.  If, arguendo, National Grid is authorized 
to recover the amount requested from S.C. 3A customers in whole or material part, the Commission 
should extend the proposed 27-month recovery period by at least three years to 63 months.  Such 
timetable could be reduced if the amount of the recovery is reduced meaningfully.  While there is 
no “magic” associated with potential recovery periods, the Commission should endeavor to limit 
the annual delivery rate impacts associated with this matter to approximately 2% or less – a 
threshold that the utility is able to achieve for S.C. 2D and S.C. 3 customers.2 
 
Accordingly, for the reasons set forth herein, if, arguendo, National Grid is 
authorized to recover the amounts proposed in the Petition in whole or material part, then the 
recovery period utilized for S.C. 3A customers should be extended by a meaningful amount to 
moderate what otherwise would be enormous, and unacceptable, delivery rate impacts.
