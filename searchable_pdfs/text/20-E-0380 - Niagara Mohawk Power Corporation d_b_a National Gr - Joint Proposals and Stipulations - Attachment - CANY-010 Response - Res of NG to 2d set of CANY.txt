Request: 
Unless otherwise noted, the following information requests pertain to the gas and electric rate 
filings for both Cases 20-E-0380 and 20-G-0381, and the Joint Proposal in these cases, dated 
September 27, 2021 (the “Joint Proposal” or “JP”). 
1.
In regard to the CLCPA Study (the “Study”) proposed at Joint Proposal § 18.1.3 
beginning on page 115: 
a.
The JP provides that the “CLCPA study will provide further analysis to identify the 
projects and programs needed to achieve specific decarbonization goals (e.g., 85% 
CO2e reductions by 2050).”   
i.  
Will the “specific decarbonization goals” measure the “projects and 
programs needed” against specific interim targets for greenhouse gas 
(GHG) emissions reductions for years prior to 2050?  
ii.  
If so, please specify the length of the proposed interim targets the Study will 
consider: for example, will it set goals for every five years, two years, or 
some other period?  
  
iii.  
If so, please also specify the percentage reduction in Company greenhouse 
gas (GHG) emissions the Study will consider for each interim period.  
 
iv.  
If the length of the interim targets in years (see question 1a(ii)) and/or the 
percentage reduction in Company GHG emissions reductions (see question 
1a(iii)) has not been determined to date, explain whether the Study will 
consider each of these questions and provide any information you now 
possess as to the criteria the Study will use to make these determinations.  
b.
Explain the criteria which you will use to determine whether electrification is 
“reasonably capable” of providing “an alternative energy option.” To what extent 
will the cost of full electrification play a role in the determination as to whether 
electrification is a “reasonably capable” option?

c.
Please explain the meaning of a “pathways analysis” as set forth in Joint Proposal 
§ 18.1.3. 
d.  
Will third party consultants be used to perform the Study, in whole or in part? If so, 
explain how the consultant or consultants will be selected and provide the names 
of what firms or individuals are being considered and furnish the curriculum vitae, 
resume or similar background information as to the qualifications and affiliations 
of such firms or individuals.   
e.  
Will the Department of Public Service (“DPS”) staff or any other entity approve 
the selection of the consultant or consultants? 
Response: 
1.
a. 
Yes.  As this question anticipates, the CLCPA study will consider, inter alia, the  
interim GHG emissions reductions targets needed to be achieved over time in  
support of decarbonzation goals.  This is a typical feature of a pathways analysis  
(see the response to part 1c., below).   
b. 
Several factors will determine whether electrification is reasonably capable of 
providing an energy alternative for various customer segments, including:  
technical feasability, supporting infrastructure, customer adoption, and cost.   
c. 
A “pathways” analysis in this context is generally understood to mean an 
assessment of the opportunities for achieving decarbonization/emissions reductions 
goals based on different assumptions around the roles of various solutions.  See, 
e.g., New York City’s Pathways to Carbon-Neutral NYC: Modernize, Reimagine, 
Reach (April 2021).  In the New York City pathways study, National Grid 
collaborated with the NYC Mayor’s Office of Sustainability and Con Edison to 
assess various scenarios for achieving decarbonization goals and the role of natural 
gas and other energy options in meeting those goals.   
As described in the Joint Proposal, the CLCPA study is intended to build on 
pathways analyses and provide specific recommendations that will be used to 
inform future rate filings on the investments and programs necessary to further the 
State’s climate goals. 
d. 
The Company anticipates using external consultants to provide technical expertise 
in various areas in support for the study, which could include system modeling, 
emissions analysis, market assessments, etc. 
The Company will solicit feedback on the type of technical and/or consulting 
expertise required to complete the study.  Based on that feedback, and the 
Company’s experience with potential service providers, the Company will select 
any consultants necessary to support the study.
