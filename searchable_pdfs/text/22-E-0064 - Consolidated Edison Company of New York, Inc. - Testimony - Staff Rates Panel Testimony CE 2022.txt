Embedded Cost of Service Studies 
5 
Q. 
Please briefly describe the purpose of an 
6 
Embedded Cost of Service, or ECOS, study. 
7 
A. 
The purpose of an ECOS study is to provide a 
8 
comparative analysis of the cost of providing 
9 
utility services to each service class and the 
10 
rate of return for each class.  A historic ECOS 
11 
study is based on an analysis of the rate base, 
12 
operating expenses, and revenues of a historic 
13 
12-month period.  An ECOS study has three major 
14 
steps: functionalization, classification, and 
15 
allocation. 
16 
Q. 
Please explain the three major steps. 
17 
A. 
Functionalization is the process of assigning 
18 
the Company’s rate base and expense items to 
19 
specific utility operating functions, generally, 
20 
transmission, distribution, and consumer.  
21 
Classification is used to further define the 
22 
functionalized rate base and expense items into 
23 
demand, energy, and customer components.  The 
24 


--- Page 17 ---
Cases 22-E-0064 and 22-G-0065 
    
Staff Rates Panel 
 
 
-16- 
 
final step, allocation, assigns the components 
1 
to customer classes consistent with the costs 
2 
that the class imposes on the utility. 
3 
Q. 
Please describe the primary output of the 
4 
Company’s ECOS study. 
5 
A. 
The primary output of the ECOS study is the rate 
6 
of return for each individual customer class and 
7 
for the system as a whole.  The rate of return 
8 
for a class is an effective way to measure how 
9 
well the class revenues align to the Company’s 
10 
costs to provide service to that particular 
11 
customer class.  The Panel agrees with the 
12 
results of the Company’s ECOS study and with the 
13 
methodology used to achieve these results. 
14 
Q. 
What is a “tolerance band” and why is it used?  
15 
A. 
A tolerance band is a certain percentage above and 
16 
below the system average rate of return.  In this 
17 
instance, the Company uses a ten percent band for 
18 
electric and gas.  Service classes are considered 
19 
deficient or in surplus if the service class’s 
20 
specific rate of return falls outside the ten 
21 
percent tolerance band.  The purpose of a tolerance 
22 
band is to account for the imprecise nature of an 
23 
ECOS study.
