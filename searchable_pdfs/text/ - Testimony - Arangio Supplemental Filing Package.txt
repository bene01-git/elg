Purpose of Testimony 
14 
Q. 
What is the purpose of your supplemental direct testimony in these 
15 
proceedings? 
16 
A. 
The purpose of my supplemental direct testimony is to discuss the impacts 
17 
to the Companies’ rate filings if KEDNY and KEDLI are unable to obtain 
18 
incremental natural gas supplies from Transcontinental Gas Pipe Line 
19 
LLC’s (“Transco”) Northeast Supply Enhancement (“NESE”) project 
20 
during the Rate Year and Data Years.  Specifically, I will describe the recent 
21

1 
Companies’ April 2019 rate filings in these proceedings and explain how 
2 
the failure to place the project in service affects the Companies’ proposals 
3 
in these cases.  While the Companies remain cautiously optimistic the 
4 
NESE project will go forward, and therefore are not proposing any changes 
5 
to their April 2019 filings at this time, I will address the impact that 
6 
continued gas supply constraints would have on the Companies’ forecast of 
7 
gas supply costs, ability to add new load, and certain aspects of the 
8 
Companies’ forecast of capital additions.  These and other impacts resulting 
9 
from the unavailability of the NESE project are further discussed in the 
10 
supplemental direct testimonies of the Revenue Requirements Panels, the 
11 
Gas Infrastructure and Operations Panels (“GIOPs”), and Company 
12 
Witness Theodore Poe, Jr.

Q. 
What recent developments have occurred that indicate that the NESE 
12 
project may not be available during the Rate Years and Data Years? 
13 
A. 
On May 15, 2019 the New York State Department of Environmental 
14 
Conservation (“DEC”) denied Transco’s application for a federal Clean 
15 
Water Act Section 401 Water Quality Certificate (“WQC”) for the NESE 
16 
project.  The denial was without prejudice to the resubmission of the 
17 
application to address the deficiencies in Transco’s application identified 
18 
by DEC.  Furthermore, on June 5, 2019, the New Jersey Department of 
19 
Environmental Protection denied (without prejudice) Transco’s application 
20 
for necessary permits to construct the NESE project.  While Transco has 
21 


--- Page 6 ---
Supplemental Direct Testimony of Elizabeth D. Arangio 
Page 4 of 8 
 
already resubmitted its application for a WQC to the DEC and expects to 
1 
resubmit the applications in New Jersey shortly, it is not clear when or 
2 
whether the requests will be granted.  Without the necessary approvals from 
3 
both states, the NESE project cannot proceed. 
4 
 
5 
Q. 
Did the Companies’ April 2019 filings in these proceedings discuss the 
6 
possibility that the NESE project might not be available and that this 
7 
could affect the Companies’ filings? 
8 
A. 
Yes.  While the Companies’ filings assumed the NESE project would be 
9 
completed by the winter of 2020/2021, my direct testimony (at 14-16) 
10 
identified the possibility that the NESE project would not be available as 
11 
planned and explained that the Companies would not be able to prudently 
12 
satisfy new or additional service requests if the NESE project were not 
13 
placed in service. 
14

III. 
Impact of the Unavailability of the NESE Project 
16 
Q. 
What actions have the Companies taken as a result of the DEC’s 
17 
decision to reject Transco’s application for a WQC for the NESE 
18 
project? 
19 
A. 
Effective May 15, 2019, consistent with the Commission’s rules and 
20 
regulations and the terms of the Companies’ tariffs, and in consideration of 
21 


--- Page 7 ---
Supplemental Direct Testimony of Elizabeth D. Arangio 
Page 5 of 8 
 
public safety, the Companies advised the Commission and customers that 
1 
they will not fulfill applications for new firm service connections, or 
2 
requests for additional firm load from existing customers, in the affected 
3 
areas of their service territories until the current supply constraints are 
4 
addressed.  The Companies, however, will continue processing applications 
5 
for non-firm service from qualifying customers to the extent the Companies 
6 
are able to provide such service.  The impact of these actions on the 
7 
Companies’ sales forecast is discussed by Company Witness Poe. 
8 
 
9 
Q. 
Why was it necessary for the Companies to take such actions? 
10 
A. 
Yes.  The Companies have an obligation to ensure they have access to 
11 
supplies of gas that are sufficient to serve the peak day, winter season, and 
12 
annual demands of their firm customers.  While the Companies obtain a 
13 
small portion of their total gas supply from local sources, the vast majority 
14 
must be accessed in various supply basins and transported on a firm basis 
15 
to the Companies’ city gates to ensure reliability.  Based on current 
16 
forecasts, the Companies believe that without the NESE project they will 
17 
not have access to quantities of reliable firm supplies sufficient to serve 
18 
future increases in firm demand beyond those that the Companies have 
19 
committed to as of May 15, 2019.  Exhibit __ (EDA-5S), which updates a 
20 
chart included on page 15 of my direct testimony, reflects the Companies’ 
21 


--- Page 8 ---
Supplemental Direct Testimony of Elizabeth D. Arangio 
Page 6 of 8 
 
peak day supply/demand balance based on the revised forecast by Company 
1 
Witness Poe as well as the current supply portfolio.  
2 
 
3 
Q. 
Have the Companies added incremental supplies to partially replace 
4 
the supplies that will not be available if the NESE project is not placed 
5 
in service? 
6 
A. 
Yes.  The Companies have procured 261,000 dekatherms per day of 
7 
incremental peaking supplies under contracts that provide for delivery at the 
8 
Companies’ city gates during the 2020/2021 winter period.  These 
9 
incremental peaking supplies will enable the Companies to provide service 
10 
reliably to their existing firm customers, but are not sufficient to serve 
11 
additional firm customer load in the affected areas of the Companies’ 
12 
service territories.  These incremental peaking supplies are included in the 
13 
revised peak day supply/demand balance set forth on Exhibit __ (EDA-5S). 
14 
 
15 
Q. 
Will the unavailability of the NESE project have any impact on the 
16 
Companies’ projected capital spending for gas supply-related projects? 
17 
A. 
Yes.  As discussed by the Companies’ GIOPs, the Companies planned to 
18 
undertake tank modernization projects at KEDNY’s Greenpoint and 
19 
KEDLI’s Holtsville Liquefied Natural Gas (“LNG”) facilities.  These 
20 
projects require the Companies to take each LNG facility out of service for 
21 


--- Page 9 ---
Supplemental Direct Testimony of Elizabeth D. Arangio 
Page 7 of 8 
 
one winter period each, with Holtsville being taken out of service first, 
1 
followed by Greenpoint.  Because the supplies from the LNG facilities are 
2 
essential components of the Companies’ peak day supply portfolio, without 
3 
access to incremental supplies from the NESE project, the Companies 
4 
cannot take either tank out of service and cannot proceed with these 
5 
projects.  In addition, as further discussed by the GIOPs, the unavailability 
6 
of the NESE project will affect the Companies’ projected capital and related 
7 
operating expenditures associated with new business during the Rate Year 
8 
and Data Years. 
9
