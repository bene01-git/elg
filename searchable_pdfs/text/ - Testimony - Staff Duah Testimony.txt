Q. 
Generally speaking, what is a fair rate of return 
4 
for a regulated utility? 
5 
A. 
A fair rate of return for a regulated utility 
6 
enables the company to provide safe and adequate 
7 
service to its customers, while at the same time 
8 
ensuring continuing support for the utility to 
9 
have access to the capital markets for both its 
10 
long-term debt and common equity securities, at 
11 
terms that are reasonable given the company’s 
12 
level of risk.  The fair rate of return, 
13 
therefore, allows the utility to recover its 
14 
prudently incurred cost of debt, while providing 
15 
its common equity investors the opportunity to 
16 
earn a return that is commensurate with the risk 
17 
of their investment.   
18

The fair rate of return for a utility is 
21 
calculated through a weighted average of the 
22 
individual cost components of its expected 
23 
capitalization during the rate year.  The two

Q. 
Can you explain the concept of ring-fencing? 
8 
A. 
Yes.  Ring-fencing, with respect to a regulated 
9 
public utility subsidiary, occurs when a 
10 
subsidiary financially separates itself from its 
11 
holding company/parent company to protect the 
12 
subsidiary from financial instability or the 
13 
bankruptcy of the parent.  The ring-fencing 
14 
measures intend to insulate assets in the 
15 
subsidiary from the risks of the holding 
16 
company/parent and the other subsidiaries in the 
17 
holding company.  In theory, if a subsidiary is 
18 
ring-fenced, and the holding company or another 
19 
subsidiary goes bankrupt, creditors would not be 
20 
able to attach their claims to the assets of the 
21 
ring-fenced subsidiary.  In addition, a fully 
22 
ring-fenced company should be insulated from its 
23 
parent’s influence regarding the financial 
24

Cases 23-G-0225 & 23-G-0226

19 
 
decisions of the subsidiary.  In theory, ring-
1

Capital Asset Pricing Model 
4 
Q.  Please describe the basic theory underlying the 
5 
CAPM. 
6 
A. 
The principle behind the CAPM theory is that 
7 
there is no premium, in terms of expected return, 
8 
for bearing risks that can be eliminated through 
9 
diversification.  According to the CAPM, rational 
10 
investors will hold a well-diversified portfolio, 
11 
such that the overall risk of the portfolio, in 
12 
terms of variability of its returns, closely 
13 
approximates that of the market as a whole.  
14 
Thus, the only risk that matters in the CAPM 
15 
equation is said to be a systematic risk or risk 
16 
that cannot be diversified away.  
17 
Q. 
What is systematic risk? 
18 
A. 
Systematic risk is a risk common to all equity 
19 
securities that cannot be eliminated through 
20 
diversification.  It is measured by a variable 
21 
known as beta.  Systematic risk also refers to 
22 
market risk, which is the uncertainty inherent in 
23 
the entire market or market segment.  Changes in 
24 


--- Page 91 ---
Cases 23-G-0225 & 23-G-0226 
Duah 
 
90 
 
interest rates, recessions, and wars are all 
1 
examples of systematic risks.   
2 
Q. 
What is unsystematic risk? 
3 
A. 
Unsystematic risk is the risk that is specific to 
4 
a particular stock.  While it is assumed that 
5 
most stocks tend to go along with the general 
6 
market, at least to some extent, factors that are 
7 
specific to an individual stock are said to 
8 
affect its unsystematic risk.   
9 
Q.  How does the CAPM measure an individual stock’s 
10 
risk? 
11 
A. 
According to the CAPM, the appropriate way to 
12 
measure an individual stock’s risk is through a 
13 
correlation of its return relative to the market 
14 
as a whole, known as beta.  A stock with a beta 
15 
of 1.0 has a return that mirrors the return of 
16 
the market, which is usually taken to mean the 
17 
S&P 500, as a whole.  Betas of less than 1.0, 
18 
which are typical for utility stocks given the 
19 
moderating influence of regulation and the 
20 
accompanying perception of reduced risk, indicate 
21 
that the stocks are less volatile than the market 
22 
as a whole.  Therefore, the CAPM informs us that 
23 
equity investors will only be compensated for 
24 


--- Page 92 ---
Cases 23-G-0225 & 23-G-0226 
Duah 
 
91 
 
their actual risk, as measured by beta.  Thus, in 
1 
the case of estimating the return requirements of 
2 
utility investors, the CAPM result will reflect 
3 
the degree to which utility stocks are typically 
4 
less volatile than the market as a whole. 
5 
Q. 
What assumptions is the CAPM based upon? 
6 
A. 
The CAPM model is based on the assumptions 
7 
that:(1) the capital market is competitive and 
8 
efficient; (2) investors are risk-averse and 
9 
demand higher returns for higher risk; (3) market 
10 
participants can lend and borrow unlimited 
11 
amounts under the risk-free rate of interest; and 
12 
(4) investors hold diversified portfolios and 
13 
operate in capital markets with no transaction 
14 
costs, taxes, or restrictions on financial 
15
