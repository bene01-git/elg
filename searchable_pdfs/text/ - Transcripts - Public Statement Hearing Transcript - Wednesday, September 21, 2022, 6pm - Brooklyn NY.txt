What is liquefied natural gas, 
commonly referred to as L.N.G.?  It's a cryogenic, 
colorless, odorless liquid stored at atmospheric 
pressure in tanks at minus 260 degrees Fahrenheit.  
It occupies approximately 1/600th the volume of 
liquid -- in liquid state as it would in the gaseous 
state. 
A process called liquefaction cools 
and condenses the gas to a liquid utilizing onsite 
equipment to efficiently store it in the tanks.  The 
process to fill our tanks from empty takes 
approximately 220 days.  This is done during the 
warmer months of the year. 
During very cold weather, approaching, 
typically around 15 degrees Fahrenheit, the liquid is 
withdrawn from the storage on site.  It's reheated to 
approximately 40 degrees Fahrenheit, which is part of 
the vaporization process and reinjected into the gas 
distribution system to meet our customer needs. 
The storage provides approximately 
five days of vaporization, if running continuously at
