Rating Action: Moody's downgrades Central Hudson Gas & Electric to A3,
stable outlook
12 Jul 2019
Approximately $300 million of debt securities downgraded
Toronto, July 12, 2019 -- Moody's Investors Service ("Moody's") downgraded the ratings of Central Hudson
Gas & Electric Corporation (CHG&E), including its senior unsecured ratings to A3 from A2. The outlook is
stable. See a complete list of affected ratings below.
Downgrades:

..Issuer: Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corporation

RATINGS RATIONALE
"CHG&E's financial ratios have deteriorated as a result of a combination of its large capital expenditure
program combined with lower operating cash flow generation resulting from the passage of federal tax reform",
said Gavin MacFarlane VP -- Senior Credit Officer.
CHG&E is currently in the midst of a period of elevated capital expenditures, mostly for system enhancement.
This high capital investment plan coincides with the passage of federal tax reform at the end of 2017, which
has created material operating cash flow headwinds for the company. Taken together, we forecast that
CHG&E's financial ratios will deteriorate to levels lower than its historical performance and more consistent
with an A3 rating.
In its last general rate case settlement in June 2018, CHG&E received regulatory approval to move forward
with a three year, capital expenditure plan of about $644 million, representing a substantial 33% increase over
the previous three year capital plan of $486 million. About 27% of the spending will target non-discretionary
needs, while investments aimed at maintaining system standards and completing system enhancements
account for the balance. Discretionary system enhancements, in particular, are driving most of the increase.
The settlement included annual step ups for the recovery of this investment plan, albeit on a lagged basis.
When combined with the sizeable incremental debt being issued (estimated at about $300 million; LTM 31
March 2019 total Moody's adjusted debt was $710 million), this will cause the company's financial ratios to
decline. Further exacerbating the stress placed on financial ratios is the passage of federal tax reform in
December 2017, which has reduced the company's operating cash flow. The combination of these two
developments will likely result in a sustained deterioration in the company's cash flow from operations pre-
working capital (CFO pre-WC) to debt ratio which we expect will be sustained at about 18%, compared to their
historical mid-20% levels, and relatively weak for an A3 rating.

CHG&E has low carbon transition risk within the regulated utility sector given its T&D-only utility operations
and lack of any direct exposure to fossil-fuel generation. We generally consider T&D and LDC utilities to have
low carbon transition risk because, unlike integrated utilities, T&D utilities have no carbon emissions.
Outlook
CHG&E's stable outlook incorporates Moody's expectation of a weaker but consistent financial profile going
forward with CFO pre-WC to debt remaining in the high teens despite increased capital expenditures, higher
incremental debt as well as the negative operating cash flow impacts of federal tax reform. The outlook also
reflects a credit supportive and predictable regulation under the New York Public Service Commission
(NYPSC).
What Could Change the Rating Up
The company's rating could be upgraded following an improvement in the New York regulatory environment
including, for example, more formulaic, transparent or timely recovery mechanisms. Moody's could also
upgrade the rating with a forecasted sustained improvement in financial metrics including CFO pre-WC to debt
exceeding 21%.
What Could Change the Rating Down
A downgrade could occur if CHG&E experiences a weakening in its financial ratios such that CFO pre-WC to
debt falls below 18% on a sustained basis. A downgrade could also be triggered if the company experiences
an adverse change in New York's regulatory support which might include an increase in recovery lag, less
transparency, more contentiousness or insufficient cost recovery and returns.
Profile
Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corporation is a regulated electric and natural gas T&D utility, serving
approximately 300,000 electric and 80,000 natural gas customers in the mid-Hudson valley region of New York
State. CHG&E also owns about 65 MW of small hydro and gas generation plants. CHG&E is regulated by the
New York Public Service Commission (NYPSC) and is the principal operating subsidiary of CH Energy Group,
Inc. (CHG, not rated), an intermediate holding company that also conducts minor non-core unregulated
business activities through indirect, partial ownership in energy related ventures. CHG is wholly-owned by
Fortis, Inc. (Fortis, Baa3 stable), the largest investor-owned gas and electric transmission and distribution
utility in Canada.

Fitch Affirms Central Hudson's IDR at 'BBB+'; Outlook Stable
Fitch Ratings - New York - 17 December 2019:
Fitch Ratings has affirmed the 'BBB+' Long-Term Issuer Default Rating (IDR) of Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. (CHG&E).
The Rating Outlook is Stable. Fitch also has affirmed CHG&E's short-term debt at 'F2.' CHG&E ratings reflect the low-risk nature
of its regulated transmission and distribution (T&D) operations, a relatively beneficial rate order that supports regulatory
predictability through mid-2021, credit metrics in line with rating expectations despite the elevated capital program, and the
financial flexibility provided by Fortis Inc. ownership to maintain the authorized statutory capital structure.
Key Rating Drivers
Low-Risk Business Model: CHG&E operates a low-risk T&D regulated utility business, which bears no commodity price risk. The
New York regulatory framework features balanced tariff mechanisms, including forward-looking test years, trackers for significant
operating expenses, and a revenue decoupling mechanism that breaks the link between sales and net margins. Furthermore,
the inclusion of a storm reserve in rate design allows CHG&E to recover incremental storm-related costs on a timely basis.
CHG&E has had success negotiating multi-year rate plan settlements, adding regulatory predictability. On a negative note,
authorized ROEs continue to be among the lowest in the nation.
Relatively Balanced Rate Order: CHG&E is in the second year of a three-year rate plan that provides stable earnings and cash
flows through mid-2021. The multi-year rate plan reflects aggregate base rate increases for electric and gas service of $84.9
million over July 1, 2018 through June 30, 2021. The base rate increases are net of $22 million in 2018 due to tax reform. In
addition, $37.5 million of bill credits will be provided over the three-year agreement to moderate the bill impact. The order reflects
a below-average 8.8% authorized ROE, which is partly offset by annual step-ups in the common equity ratio to 50% by 2021.
The order provides for performance penalties and incentives and allows for up to $4.7 million annually in incentives for
deployment of energy efficiency and distributed energy resources.
Elevated Capex: Fitch Ratings projects CHG&E's capital investments to approximate $900 million from 2019-2022. By contrast,
total capital investment was about $600 million over the previous four years. The elevated investment requires significant
external funding that is likely to pressure operating cash flows in the intermediate term. Capital is primarily allocated toward the
upgrade of T&D infrastructure, advanced grid initiatives that support the state energy policy, and growing IT and facilities
investments. Fitch expects capex to be funded with a mix of internally generated cash flow, long-term debt issuance and
parental support in a manner consistent with the authorized regulatory capital structure.
Pressured Credit Metrics: Fitch expects credit metrics will remain within expectations for the current rating despite funding for the
elevated investment boosting leverage during the forecast period. The cash flow benefit of rate relief offsets the higher capital
investment, with moderate near-term leverage of 4.1x-4.3x under the current rate order. Fitch forecasts FFO-adjusted leverage
rises to 4.5x-4.7x from 2021-2022 under the next rate order based on a flat to slightly lower ROE in line with recent New York
State Public Service Commission (NYPSC) rate orders. The credit metrics provide headroom and are an improvement over
Fitch's previous expectations, which projected higher leverage due to the impact of tax reform and the winding down of bonus
depreciation.
REV Neutral to Credit Profile: Fitch believes efforts to transform the traditional utility distribution business model of New York
T&D utilities, under the scope of the Reform the Energy Vision (REV) initiative, bear no immediate impact on CHG&E's credit
profile. Fitch expects REV-related capital investment to continue to represent a modest portion of CHG&E's capital investments
over the forecast horizon. Recovery of REV-related capital spending is currently assessed as part of the rate-making process,
and the utility can earn additional incentives through NYSPSC-approved earnings adjustment mechanisms (EAMs), based on
success in achieving peak system reduction targets. Execution risk associated with digitizing the grid and emergence of
disruptive technologies are potential longer-term concerns.
Clean Energy Legislation: New York enacted the Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act in July 2019. The legislation
requires that 70 percent of electricity procured by utilities in the state be produced by renewable energy systems by 2030 and

Ownership by Fortis: CHG&E operates as a stand-alone entity under Fortis ownership, benefiting from several ring-fencing

Derivation Summary
CHG&E's business risk profile as a regulated T&D utility is comparable to that of its peers Orange & Rockland, Inc. (ORU;
BBB+/Stable), Consolidated Edison Co. of New York, Inc. (CECONY; BBB+/Stable), and New York State Electric and Gas Corp.
(NYSEG; BBB+/Stable). CHG&E's financial scale is similar to ORU and NYSEG but materially smaller than CECONY, whose
EBITDA was more than 20.0x greater at Sept. 30, 2019. CHG&E's smaller financial scale is nonetheless compensated by the
financial flexibility provided under its ownership by a large utility holding company. The quality of service territory is slightly better
at CECONY and ORU than at CHG&E and NYSEG due to a stronger local economy. CHG&E and its peers are regulated by the
NYSPSC. Fitch considers the New York regulatory construct to be moderately restrictive, with below-average authorized ROEs
somewhat balanced by credit positive regulatory mechanisms.
CHG&E's financial profile is consistent with that of its peers. For the forecast period through 2022, FFO-adjusted leverage at
CHG&E will increase to mid to high 4.0xs, similar to CECONY averaging 4.7x, and ORU declining to 4.6x, but higher than
NYSEG ranging from 3.7x-4.2x through 2021.
Key Assumptions
Fitch's key assumptions within the rating case for the issuer include:
- Current rate plan in place until June 2021 with new multi-year rate case effective July 2021 in line with recent New York state
regulated utility orders;
- Flat to slight customer growth;
- Capital investment of approximately $900 million over 2019-2022;
- Parental equity and supportive dividend policy to maintain the regulatory capital structure.
RATING SENSITIVITIES
Future Developments That May, Individually or Collectively, Lead to Positive Rating Action
- While not anticipated at this time given the size of the capital program, sustained FFO-adjusted leverage of 4.0x or better;
- An unexpected improvement in the regulatory environment.
Future Developments That May, Individually or Collectively, Lead to Negative Rating Action
- An adverse change in New York's regulatory environment;
- FFO-adjusted leverage greater than 5.0x on a sustained basis.

Liquidity and Debt Structure
Adequate Liquidity: CHG&E has adequate liquidity. As of Sept. 30, 2019, it had $6 million of cash and cash equivalents. It has
access to a $200 million bank credit facility that expires Oct. 15, 2020. There were no borrowings outstanding under the facility
at Sept. 30, 2019. CHG&E has also access to up to $15 million of LOCs and to additional liquidity through uncommitted short-
term credit arrangements with three commercial banks totaling $40 million. There were no borrowings under the LOC and $15
million outstanding under the uncommitted facility at Sept. 30, 2019. There is no corporate money pool, and CHG&E cannot lend
funds to Fortis or its affiliates. Long-term debt maturities are manageable, with evenly spaced maturities due over the next three
years through 2022.

FITCH DOWNGRADES CENTRAL HUDSON'S
IDR TO 'BBB+'; OUTLOOK REVISED TO STABLE
Fitch Ratings-New York-02 July 2015: Fitch Ratings has downgraded Central Hudson Gas & Electric
 Co.'s (CHG&E) long-term Issuer Default Rating (IDR) to 'BBB+' from 'A-' and revised the Rating
 Outlook to Stable from Negative. In addition, Fitch has downgraded CHG&E's senior unsecured debt
 to 'A-' from 'A'. Fitch has also affirmed CHG&E's short-term IDR at 'F2'.  
The ratings downgrade is driven by Fitch's expectation of continued deterioration of CHG&E's
 financial profile over the next several years, despite the beneficial impact of the recently approved
 multi-year rate plan. Based on Fitch's forecast, projected credit metrics are not supportive of an 'A'
 utility credit profile. The downtrend is largely due to incremental debt financings required to fund
 persistently elevated capex through 2019 and to the deferral of projected manufactured gas plant
 clean-up costs. The deferred costs result in a significant build-up of regulatory assets and generate
 lag in cash recovery.  
The revision to a Stable Outlook reflects Fitch's expectations that CHG&E will operate its low-risk
 transmission and distribution (T&D) utility business in a conservative manner through cost control
 and commitment to maintain a balanced capital structure.   
KEY RATING DRIVERS 
Rising Capex: Management plans to spend approximately $790 million in capital investment over
 2015-2019, an increase of nearly 65% compared with the $481 million spent over the previous
 five years. The high capex will require significant external funding that Fitch anticipates will
 pressure operating cash flows. Capital spending is primarily allocated toward the upgrade of T&D
 infrastructure, advanced grid initiatives, and heating oil to natural gas conversions. Capex also reflects
 accelerated investments in cast iron pipeline replacement projects. Fitch projects CHG&E's internally
 generated cash flows to fund on average 60% of capex requirements over the next five years, with the
 balance funded primarily with long-term debt issuances, and manage to a 50%-50% debt equity mix.  
Relatively Balanced Rate Order: The recent order that results in the establishment of a three-year rate
 plan for CHG&E's electric and gas businesses is consistent with Fitch's prior rating case expectations
 and provides regulatory predictability through June 2018. The utility will continue to benefit from
 timely and full recovery of commodity costs and a revenue decoupling mechanism that insulates net
 margins from variation in sales, weather, and energy efficiency and conservation. In addition, the
 NYPSC approved the use of a storm reserve that will allow CHG&E to recover incremental storm-
related costs on a timelier basis. 
Under the multi-year plan, CHG&E will receive aggregate base rate increases for combined electric
 and gas service of approximately $56 million over the period July 1, 2015 through June 30, 2018.
 However, the benefit of tariff increases will be largely offset by the NYPSC's use of approximately
 $31 million of existing regulatory liabilities as one-time bill credits to moderate the immediate impact
 of the rate increase. The reliance on balancing accounts for rate setting protects earnings but does
 not provide immediate cash flows.  
In addition, the 9% ROE authorized by the NYPSC is significantly below what was granted, on
 average, to utilities nationwide in 2014, and the multi-year plan does not include a tracker that
 accounts for the possibility of a future rise in interest rates.

Decline in Credit Metrics: Credit metrics are currently sound but are projected to weaken materially 
 through the forecast period to levels that are more consistent with a 'BBB' utility credit profile. Fitch's 
 rating case projects FFO lease-adjusted leverage to weaken by almost a full turn in 2015 to 4.2x and 
 be above 4.5x in 2016 and beyond. Adjusted debt/EBITDAR is forecasted to remain near 4x while 
 FFO fixed charge coverage is expected to be below 4x from 2015 to 2019. For the LTM period ended 
 March 31, 2015, FFO adjusted leverage stood at 3.4x, adjusted debt/EBITDAR, 4x, and FFO fixed 
 charge coverage, 4.7x. 
REV-based Regulation: The future implementation of the 'Reforming the Energy Vision' (REV)
 initiative spearheaded by the NYPSC appears to be credit neutral at this time. Under a REV 
 framework, rate plans that span over multiple years and provide long-term rate visibility could be 
 favorable to utilities, in Fitch's view. 
Furthermore, a successful penetration of distributed energy resources, one of REV's key objectives, 
 could lead to reduced peak energy demand and allow utilities to scale back capital spending on costly 
 infrastructure-related projects. This is particularly relevant in the case of CHG&E given its large 
 capex cycle and funding requirements.  
CHG&E has proposed four demonstration projects before the NYPSC that focus on investments in 
 community solar, micro-grid, demand side management, and smart meters. Those projects, whose 
 funding was determined as part of the 2015 rate order, must be approved by the NYPSC Staff.  
KEY ASSUMPTIONS 
Fitch's key assumptions within the rating case are as follows: 
--Tariff increases per the 2015 rate order; 
--Capital spending of approximately $790 million over 2015-2019 excluding any REV-related 
 investments; 
--Core O&M kept relatively flat.  
RATING SENSITIVITIES 
Positive: Future developments that may, individually or collectively, lead to a positive rating action 
 include:  
Given the current downgrade, any positive rating action is unlikely in the near term. However, adjusted 
 debt/EBITDAR below 3.5x on a sustained basis could lead to positive rating actions.  
Negative: Future developments that may, individually or collectively, lead to a negative rating action 
 include: 
Negative regulatory developments including the inability to timely recover commodity costs or the 
 termination of the revenue decoupling mechanism. 
Sustained FFO adjusted leverage greater than 4.75x and adjusted debt/EBITDAR greater than 4.1x. 
LIQUIDITY AND CAPITAL STRUCTURE 
Fitch assesses CHG&E's ratings on a stand-alone basis under the Fortis corporate umbrella. The utility 
 maintains a separate board, has its own bank credit facility, and can issue long-term debt on its own. 
 The Fortis ownership does provide the utility with some financial flexibility with respect to parent 
 equity infusions or upstream dividends. Fitch expects upstream dividend distributions to be relatively 
 modest over the next five years during the high capex cycle.
