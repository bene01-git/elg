Q. 
Please describe the depreciation analysis that MAC performed. 
1 
A. 
The analysis involved reviewing the most recent seven years of historical data (2012-
2 
2018) for certain plant accounts relating to the Company’s LPP.  KEDNY’s LPP assets 
3 
consist of 1,458 miles of unprotected steel and cast or wrought iron main as of December 
4 
2018, as well as 104,988 copper services, and 24,773 unprotected steel or wrought iron 
5 
services.   Although the Company recently determined that high density polyethylene 
6 
(“HDPE”) plastic services should be classified as LPP, the analysis I am presenting has 
7 
not been updated to include it, due to the timing of this recent decision.    LPP assets were 
8 
installed by the Company some time ago and are currently the subject of an accelerated 
9 
replacement program that will replace/retire all LPP on the Company’s system over the 
10 
next 20 years.

Q. 
Why is the Company providing a limited study of its LPP assets in this proceeding? 
4

As mentioned above, the Company’s depreciation rates were last reviewed and approved

9

Q. 
What Company facilities are considered LPP? 
12 
A. 
LPP facilities consist of all mains of 12-inches or less in diameter that are (i) unprotected 
13 
(i.e., non-cathodically protected) steel pipe whether bare or coated, and (ii) cast and 
14 
wrought iron pipe.  LPP facilities also include bare steel, wrought iron, HDPE, and 
15 
copper services.

18

20 
Panel, the Company is undertaking an ongoing 25 year program to replace and retire all 
21 
LPP facilities on its gas system.  The program began in 2015; thus, the remaining term of 
22 
the program is approximately 20 years - at which time the Company anticipates that it 
23 
will have replaced all of its LPP facilities.

Q. 
What is the purpose of the depreciation analysis you performed for KEDNY? 
1 
A. 
The purpose of the depreciation analysis is to derive appropriate accrual rates reflective 
2 
of engineering judgment and current projections for the next 20 years, relative to the LPP 
3 
assets under study.  Because the Company is proceeding with a program through which 
4 
these LPP assets will be replaced and retired on an accelerated schedule, this analysis is 
5 
intended to produce depreciation rates that will reflect an annual amortization and 
6 
recovery of these LPP assets along with their retirement costs, commonly called net 
7 
salvage, over the proposed amortization period.

What procedures did you employ in compiling your depreciation studies? 
10 
A. 
First, MAC created the depreciation study databases that utilized the Company’s vintage 
11 
survivors by LPP account as of December 31, 2018.  KEDNY also provided the cost of 
12 
removal, annual retirements, annual mileage of retirements, and annual number of 
13 
services history for the most recent seven years (2012-2018) to complete MAC’s analysis 
14 
of net salvage for the LPP accounts (See Exhibit ___ (PMN-4)). 
15

Q. 
Why have you chosen the seven-year period for your analysis of LPP? 
17 
A. 
The Company began its program of retiring/replacing LPP on an accelerated basis in 
18 
2015 and has ramped up its annual retirement miles for these specific assets to an 
19 
expected level going into the future.  Because the primary costs incurred in connection 
20 
with the program are labor-related, the most recent period was deemed to be most 
21 
representative of the costs that are likely to be incurred in connection with future 
22 
retirement efforts for these plant assets.  Exhibit ___ (PMN-4) sets forth the three-year 
23 
period of historical annual costs used to derive our proposed recommendations.  MAC

WHOLE LIFE SCHEDULE WITH RESERVE VARIANCE 
 
EXPLANATORY NOTES 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
The following is an explanation of each column of the Whole Life Schedule with Reserve Variance: 
 
 
 1. 
Column (1) presents the book balance for each account at the indicated date.  
 
 2.  
Column (2) labeled "DISP TYPE" is an SQ (Square survivor curve or straight line 
where the actual life is equal the average life) dispersion for each Leak Prone Pipe 
account.  
 
  
 3. 
Column (3) indicates the average dollar service life in years which in this study is a 
20-year amortization period. 
 
4. 
Column (4) is the unadjusted whole life accrual rate developed by dividing unity by 
Column (3), and expressing the quotient as a percentage.  
 
5. 
Column (5) is the whole life accrual with no salvage adjustment, based upon the 
average service life associated with each investment group.  These accruals are 
developed by multiplying Column (1) by Column (4).  
 
6. 
Column (6) is the percent net salvage expectation; net salvage equals gross   salvage 
minus removal cost.  
 
7. 
Column (7) is the salvage factor, derived by subtracting the (signed) net salvage ratio 
from unity; e.g., a salvage factor of 1.10 is the result of 1.00 minus an expected net 
salvage ratio of minus 0.10; i.e., 1.00 - (-0.10) = 1.10.  
 
8. 
Column (8) is the whole life accrual rate, reflecting adjustment for net salvage 
expectations; it is developed by multiplying Column (4) by Column (7), and 
expressing the product as a percentage.  
 
  
 9.

developed by multiplying Column (8) by Column (1).  
 
10. 
Column (10) shows indicated depreciation reserves, unadjusted for net salvage 
expectations.  
 
11. 
Column (11) is the indicated depreciation reserve, adjusted for net salvage   
expectations by multiplying Column (10) by Column (7).
