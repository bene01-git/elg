Letter of Support Introduction 
 
As a party to the ConEdison rate case (25-E-0072 and 25-G-0073) The New York Geothermal 
Energy Organization (NY-GEO) has attended the majority of general and breakout sessions in 
advance of submitting its position of support. We appreciate the opportunity to represent the 
HVAC installers, geothermal heat pump manufacturers, geothermal drillers, system designers, 
organized labor, developers, and other professionals comprising our membership who are 
focused on providing the safest, most reliable and affordable heating and cooling energy to NY 
businesses and residents. Ground Source Heat Pumps (GSHP's) have been proven to be the 
most efficient, dispatchable, emissions reduction and peak-time energy shaving technology 
available. Because of these and other GSHP advantages, including eliminating costly 
infrastructure and stranded assets, NY-GEO is hopeful that the emphasis on customer 
affordability will accelerate action toward better solutions in this rate case. The following 
information summarizes the reasons for our position of support as well as concerns. 
 
Continuation and expansion of SC1-IV, including a price guarantee, is a benefit for customers 
with GSHP’s and is a practical business decision given the positive results from the current rate 
case data showing customer savings using SC1-IV. Aggressive follow-through by the Company 
to execute marketing commitments outlined in the joint proposal's Rates Marketing, Education 
and Outreach Plan will be important to maximizing the value to the company, customers and 
those who will benefit from a more equitable demand-based rate. Inclusion of an easily accessible 
page where information on SC1 Rate IV and instructions for signing up, combined with other 
information on heat pump adoption, including the Clean Heat Program is positive. Also positive is 
providing proactive rate comparison reports, at a minimum of once per year, to residential 
customers that have adopted heat pumps both via a company program (and not via a company 
program if possible). 
 
Rate participation analysis. Thorough analysis and evaluation of the results of this expansion 
will be important in order to fine tune rate attributes and accelerate customers 
understanding/acceptance in future years as well as encourage other utilities to consider similar 
rate structures.  The tool’s backend will be designed to save historic data on the number of 
customers enrolled in Rate IV, number of customers that lowered their bills with Rate IV, and 
average and median savings. For even greater understanding, if possible, NY-GEO feels the 
existing and proposed SC1-IV report needs to be expanded to include all demand-based rates, 
including the Rider Z rates, and would be further improved if the report could provide not only the 
averages (means), but also the medians and the standard deviations. This added data could 
make it much easier to understand the distribution of the rates' benefits. 
NEW YORK GEOTHERMAL ENERGY ORGANIZATION

Elimination of  ratepayer funding for the Differentiated Natural Gas Pilot Program and Gas 
Methane Capture Project. At a time when reduction of fossil fuel use and affordability are key 
objectives, NY-GEO agrees no ratepayer funds should be spent on these types of programs that 
allow fossil fuel combustion. 
 
Electric Advantage and Energy Exchange Programs. NY-GEO is in favor of the flexibility 
availed to the Company by implementing the Electric Advantage Program and Energy Exchange 
Program as two separate, annual portfolios, which must pass the Societal Cost Test (SCT) test 
on a portfolio basis (instead of current implementation whereby each Leak Prone Pipe NPA or 
Service Line NPA project must individually pass the SCT) 
 
Beneficial Electrification and Transportation Timeline EAM. Including an incentive for the 
Company to focus on improving electrification connectivity is supported. We feel the proposed 
targets are reasonable and seem to strike a balance between transportation and building 
electrification incentives. 
 
Greenhouse Gas Emissions/CLCPA: Reduction of greenhouse gas emissions (and resulting 
climate disruptions/warming) is the main objective of the CLCPA and subsequent scoping plan. 
NY-GEO is pleased to see important and meaningful emissions reporting included in this joint 
proposal.  
 
Leak Prone Pipe Replacement versus Removal/Repair. NY-GEO is disappointed and 
concerned about insufficient planned reduction of Leak Prone Pipes (LPP's). We support the 
approach of NRDC which suggests the Gas Infrastructure Replacement or Reduction Program 
set a goal of meeting 20% of the cumulative target through non-replacement strategies, such as 
the Electric Advantage Program or another NPA, relining, repairs, or deferral in lower-risk 
situations until a cost-effective NPA can be implemented. Under the NRDC plan the company 
would receive a 50% shared savings incentive for NPAs and 30% for other non- deferral 
alternatives. Affordability and electrification are not just 3-year issues and NY-GEO feels strongly 
that this rate case is the proper time to more aggressively pursue savings associated with safe 
non pipeline alternatives incentives.  In addition, reduction of long-lasting pipeline assets would 
reduce the stranded asset costs that will burden future gas customers. 
 
We appreciate the opportunity to participate in this rate case and offer NY-GEO expertise, 
experience and input throughout the rate case’s duration.
