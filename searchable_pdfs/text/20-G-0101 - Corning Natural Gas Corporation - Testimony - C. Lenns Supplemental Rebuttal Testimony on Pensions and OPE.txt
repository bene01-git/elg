A. 
Yes, I am. 
Q. 
What is the purpose of your Supplemental Rebuttal Testimony? 
A. 
Beginning at page 36 of its direct testimony in this rate case, the Staff Accounting Panel 
(the "Panel") testified that it had concerns with the management of Corning's pension plan 
and trust fund, and its OPEB plans. According to the Panel, this concern was based on an 
actuarial loss that the plan experienced in fiscal 2019 which is included in the net periodic 
pension cost calculation in the fiscal year ended September 30, 2020. Staff also raised 
concerns regarding the 7.5% expected long-term rate of return on plan assets. Staff 
therefore suggested that the Company should, in its rebuttal testimony, "provide 
information on its pension plan and fund and show that the investment policies are 
appropriate and aligned with the expected obligations to pension plan participants." 
Q. 
Did Corning include the requested information in its rebuttal testimony filed on July 24, 
2020? 
A. 
No. We were not able to pull together all of the requested information prior to the deadline 
for the filing. We have now been able to do so and, accordingly, we are submitting this 
Supplemental Rebuttal Testimony in response to the Staff Panel's request. 
Q. 
Are you sponsoring an exhibit as part of your Supplemental Rebuttal evidence?

Q. 
Please address the concerns raised by the Staff Panel regarding fund management. 
A. 
In 2019, interest rates declined significantly, which impacts the net present value of 
Corning's pension liability which then produces an actuarial loss in the plan. This 
phenomenon is not unique to Corning. Virtually every well managed pension fund relies 
on interest-bearing obligations to some extent to balance its portfolio of assets in order to 
minimize risk and maximize returns to the plan. In periods of sharply declining interest 
rates, pension plans will experience short term actuarial losses in the plan. See "Exhibit A" 
for a graph depicting the decline in interest rates on U.S. Corporate debt instruments during 
fiscal 2019. With regard to the Staff Panel's concerns regarding the 7.5% expected long-
term rate of return on plan assets, "Exhibit B" shows the fiscal year 2019 performance and 
"Exhibit D" details return performance over a 1-10 year period. Given the performance 
shown on "Exhibit D," the Company remains comfortable that the fund manager has the 
ability to achieve a 7.5% expected return over a full market cycle. The fund's long-term 
performance has been above benchmark with the total, all-in fee being well below the 
average of other plans of comparable size. The fund fees are detailed in "Exhibit E." In 
response to Staff's request for information on the pension plan's investment policies, please
