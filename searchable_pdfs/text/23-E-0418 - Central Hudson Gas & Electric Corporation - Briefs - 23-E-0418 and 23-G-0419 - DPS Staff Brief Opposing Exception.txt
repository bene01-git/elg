STAFF BRIEF OPPOSING EXCEPTIONS 
 
INTRODUCTION 
 
 
On May 21, 2024, Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corporation (Central Hudson 
or the Company), Multiple Intervenors (MI), Public Utility Law Project of New York, Inc. 
(PULP), and Key Capture Energy filed briefs on exceptions to the May 1, 2024 recommended 
decision (RD) in these rate proceedings.  The Department of Public Service Staff (Staff) submits 
this Brief Opposing Exceptions to address many of the disputed issues raised by these parties. 
 
 
Staff’s review of the majority of the exceptions raised by the Company and other 
parties leads to the conclusion that they should be denied, and administrative law judges (ALJ) 
Costello and Moreno’s recommendations should be approved and adopted by the New York 
State Public Service Commission (Commission).1  Staff submits that the RD, modified as 
recommended in Staff’s Brief on Exceptions (BOE), and this Brief Opposing Exceptions, 
constitutes a reasonable resolution to all the issues raised in this case and is well-supported by 
the record.

XVII.  REVENUE ALLOCATION AND RATE DESIGN 
A.  ECOS Studies 
MI excepts to the RD’s recommendation to utilize the historical embedded cost of 
service (ECOS) study in tandem with the pro forma ECOS, arguing that by doing so, the 
Commission would abandon a cost of service-based approach to regulation.108  It purportedly 
supports this contention by expounding that “if the comparison reflected a cost-based result, the 
historical and pro forma studies more often than not should yield results that at are aligned at 
least directionally for each rate case.” 109  That unfounded presumption unravels, however, with 
the observation that such divergence in directionality between the pro forma and historical ECOS 
studies is precisely the cost-based insight revealed by the application of the methodology.  In 
other words, if a class relative rate of return (RROR) directionally deviated between the 
historical and pro forma ECOS studies, it is reasonable to restrain allocation modification; 
likewise, it is reasonable to modify allocation when such RRORs are directionally congruent and 
exceed the dead band.   
Moreover, MI’s underlying premise—that using the historical ECOS study 
prevents the pro forma ECOS study from allocating revenue on a cost-of-service basis—is 
invalid in the present proceeding.  Staff notes that seven of eleven electric service classes, and 
four of six gas service classes, had directionally congruent RRORs exceeding the dead band and 
therefore received modified allocation factors.  MI also argues that previous Central Hudson rate 
cases resulted in negotiated settlements, and the Commission should not rely on allocation 
factors utilized in those cases and should instead review the rationale underlying the continues 
use of two cost studies.  As amply demonstrated by Staff, the record in this case provides plenty 
of support for continuing to use the existing methodology.
