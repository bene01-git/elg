V. 
DISAGREEMENT ON PROPOSED CONTINUATION OF THE PROPERTY TAX 
8 
RECONCILIATION MECHANISM 
9 
Q. 
Please describe the Companies’ proposal for a property tax reconciliation mechanism. 
10 
A. 
Within the Direct Testimony of the Revenue Requirements Panel, the Companies 
11 
proposed to continue the deferral and sharing mechanism currently in place.  Specifically, 
12 
the Companies proposed to defer 90% of the variation between the actual property tax 
13 
expenses and the amount allowed in rates for future recovery from or credit to customers.  
14 
The non-deferred 10% amount that would be borne by the Companies would be capped at 
15 
an annual amount equal to ten basis points on the common equity for the Rate Year.  The 
16 
Companies would defer 100% of the variation above or below the level at which the cap 
17 
takes effect for future recovery from or credit to customers.   
18

20 
taxes in the context of a one-year rate filing.  Staff further asserts that not allowing such 
21 
true-ups in a one-year litigated case is a long-standing Commission practice.  Staff also 
22 
suggests that updating the estimate of property taxes included in rates to reflect the latest

20

22

Q. 
Do the Companies agree that the sales and use tax refunds referenced in the Staff 
1 
Accounting Panel testimony at pages 129-131 are subject to the notice obligations in 16 
2 
NYCRR § 89.3? 
3 
A. 
The refunds in question are considered by the Companies to be ordinary operating 
4 
refunds that were based upon determinations and corrections made by the New York 
5 
State Department of Taxation and Finance with respect to the Companies’ original tax 
6 
filings.  Such refunds fall within the exclusionary language in 16 NYCRR § 89.3, which 
7 
states, “[t]his Part shall be inapplicable to ordinary operating refunds that are not 
8 
attributable to negotiation or to new legislation, adjudication or rulemaking ….”   
9 
Because the refunds in question are ordinary operating refunds that are not the result of 
10 
negotiations or a settlement or compromise of the Companies’ tax obligations, it is the 
11 
Companies’ position that § 89.3 is inapplicable to the refunds in question.  
12 
Q. 
Do the Companies recommend any changes to the treatment of these refunds at this time, 
13 
consistent with Staff proposals on page 132? 
14 
A. 
Not at this time.  The Companies’ treatment of the refunds is protective of customer 
15 
interests in that it reduces the Companies’ rate base and therefore revenue requirements, 
16 
which would continue to benefit customers over an extended period of time.  
17 
Notwithstanding, the Companies acknowledge Staff’s position that modifying the 
18 
accounting for the refunds and then using the net refunds to moderate rates in the Rate 
19 
Year merits further consideration during settlement discussions.

VII. 
SELF-ASSESSMENT OF TAXES ON CAPITAL SPENDING  
1 
Q. 
The Staff Accounting Panel testimony notes that Staff is concerned that NYSEG does not 
2 
have the expertise to accurately self-assess on capital spending, and/or is not being 
3 
conservative in its self-assessments due to the significant refund on capital-related 
4 
spending.  Do the Companies agree with Staff’s assertions about whether the Companies 
5 
have adequate expertise in this area? 
6 
A. 
No.  Company personnel with many years of subject matter expertise involving New 
7 
York Sales and Use Tax have been involved on behalf of the Companies in addressing 
8 
these issues.  The Companies have appropriately initiated a reverse audit project, which 
9 
resulted, to date, in the refund to NYSEG of the over-accrued tax.  Consultants have been 
10 
engaged to help manage this process due to the high degree of complexity and nuance in 
11 
the New York tax code relative to the purchasing activities of the Companies.  The 
12 
experience the Companies have gained in this area over the past two years has been 
13 
informative, and has resulted in process improvements that are being put into place.  
14 
These improvements include additional active front-end assessments of Sales and Use tax 
15 
amounts owed, and the identification of potential system changes to better address these 
16 
issues.  As a further step, to increase the accuracy of use tax paid on capital expenditures, 
17 
the Companies are now reviewing and posting on a monthly basis Use Tax accrual 
18 
reversals.  We will leverage this analysis and the results of the completed audits to 
19 
develop a plan for improving business administration allowing our systems to more 
20 
effectively segregate purchasing activity and determine taxability.  Over time, this will 
21 
allow our accruals to more effectively align with the complex rules and regulations for 
22 
New York’s Sales and Use taxes without the need for manual adjustment.
