LIMITED OPPOSITION OF NRG ENERGY, INC. 
TO JOINT PROPOSAL OF SETTLEMENT 
On behalf of itself and its affiliates doing business as Energy Service Companies 
(“ESCOs”) in the service territory of the Consolidated Edison Company of New York (“Con 
Edison”). NRG Energy, Inc. (“NRG”) respectfully submits this Limited Opposition to the Joint 
Proposal of Settlement filed in these proceedings on February 16, 2023 (the “JP”).1
INTRODUCTION AND 
SUMMARY OF POSITION 
NRG’s opposition to the JP is limited to the failure of the Signatory Parties to take three 
basic measures required to permit ESCOs to effectively serve customers in Con Edison’s service 
territory.  First and foremost, the JP should be modified to require Con Edison to take the

information on whether the customer accounts they serve are net metered; and (2) that Con Edison

ANALYSIS 
I.
THE COMMISSION SHOULD MODIFY THE JP TO REQUIRE CON EDISON 
TO TAKE AGGRESSIVE ACTIONS TO RESOLVE ITS UNACCEPTABLY 
LARGE BACKLOG OF UNBILLED CUSTOMER ACCOUNTS 
 
As Mr. Daniel Allegretti testified on behalf of the Retail Energy Supply Association 
(“RESA”) “Timely and accurate access to customer data is critical to an ESCO’s ability to serve 
customers.”2  Unlike Con Edison, which is guaranteed full recovery of its costs of service through 
its revenue decoupling mechanism (“RDM”),3  ESCOs serving customers in Con Edison’s territory 
must be able to bill their customers and collect those bills in order to operate profitably. 
The testimony filed in these proceedings makes the shortcomings of Con Edison’s billing 
practices abundantly clear.  NRG Exhibit NRG-1 provides a 36-page list of over 1,650 individual 
electric commercial accounts of NRG’s affiliate Direct Energy Business in Con Edison’s service 
territory that cannot be correctly billed, as well as 1,945 commercial gas accounts that cannot be 
correctly billed due to missing or incomplete data.  Since that testimony was filed, the number of 
billing issues have increased rather than decreased, including unbilled accounts, accounts that are 
currently NRG customers that are also receiving a supply bill from Con Edison, and incorrect tax

Other ESCOs and customers have reported similar issues.  RESA witness Allegretti

RESA members have experienced several issues. First, for whatever

Con Edison’s failure to provide timely and accurate billing information is particularly

Missing usage makes it impossible for ESCOs to accurately bill their

customers at all. As a consequence, customers are not receiving 
accurate pricing signals that could result in changes in their energy 
consumption or purchasing behavior. This effect is exacerbated 
when customers do not receive bills for months at a time. In that 
case, customers have no real sense of what they are paying each 
month for their energy usage or a meaningful way to budget their 
energy costs. Consequently, customers who may have otherwise 
curtailed their usage or switched service providers to obtain more 
favorable terms do not have the information necessary to make those 
decisions. Moreover, once those customers are actually billed for the 
missing months, they are hit with a staggering energy bill that 
negatively impacts their cash flow. This issue is particularly acute 
for low-income customers. If these customers cannot afford to pay 
these substantial and unexpected bills, they face the potential 
disconnection of their electric or natural gas service.6

As the NRG Panel noted, these billing problems are particularly disruptive to ESCOs providing 
gas service to commercial customers: 
Missing and inaccurate data also impacts base loads and heat factors 
of our gas customers. When meter read data is missing for various 
months, and Con Ed eventually provides the corrected data, it 
appears that little to no effort is expended to accurately assign which 
usage occurred in which month. This “smooths” out a customer’s 
load causing their Baseload and Heat Factors to be inaccurate. As a 
result of this, some customers have inadvertently become heat load 
customers when typically, they are not. Our load forecasts and gas 
scheduling are impacted by this data, as are the total asset 
assignments as required by the Utility Program. We have seen an 
inaccurate shifting of the NRG Energy loads resulting in 
dramatically higher delivery requirements in the winter and lower 
delivery requirements in the summer. This leads to large cash outs 
in each direction with a major financial impact. We are supplying 
dramatically inflated DCQ requirements (by our records up to 25% 
higher than the actual customer usage) to the Utility at peak prices 
during the winter and then receiving large cash out payments when 
the actualization occurs. We see the opposite in the summer with 
lower than anticipated load (by our records up to 15 percent lower) 
which causes large cash outs due the Utility from NRG Energy. The 
cash out formulas under the current environment could be 
detrimental to the ESCO’s given that we are unable to calculate the 
risk associated with the erroneous customers DCQ requirements.7
While the JP proposes measures to encourage Con Edison to accelerate its transition to 
AMI meters going forward, the JP provides no mechanism to force Con Edison to devote the 
necessary resources to resolve its substantial backlog of billing issues for commercial customers.  
To address this important issue, the NRG Panel proposed the following measures: 
In some cases, large business customers have gone four years 
without receiving a correct invoice for their commodity service. 
Unfortunately, there is nothing in place that makes Con Ed 
accountable for providing accurate and timely data to ESCOs. While 
we are hopeful that full AMI deployment will correct this in the 
future - we are concerned that current outstanding backlog will not 
be corrected before the new system is implemented, and that it may 
be impossible to correct these issues once that system is no longer

NRG and other ESCOs are handicapped in their efforts to serve the non-NYPA portion of 
the electric power requirements of commercial customers that receive a portion of their electric 
power requirements from NYPA.  ESCOs’ need for this information to be able to compete on a 
level playing field with Con Edison’s own commodity supply was explained by the NRG Panel, 
who testified that: 
In addition, Con Ed needs to provide full information on NYPA 
allocations to suppliers serving customers receiving a portion of 
their energy requirements from NYPA. When customers receive 
ReCharge NY allocations or other NYPA benefits, suppliers often 
do not receive crucial information required for accurate pricing and 
billing of our customers. When trying to competitively price a 
NYPA account, suppliers must take the kWh from the main 
summary account and add it to the kWh usage of the summary 
companion account to get the total servable load, The problem with 
this process is that there is no way for the ESCO to tell what the 
customer's ReCharge NY allocation is. While there is a website 
showing the award, that website does not indicate the portion of any 
award that the receiving customer drawn down. In order for ESCOs 
to price correctly and competitively, we need the absolute value of

the allocation acceptance. If ESCOs estimate the ReCharge NY 
volumes, and estimate the allocation incorrectly, then that volume 
differential will directly impact the price specific to capacity. 
ESCOs need to know the accepted allocation and the servable load 
volume for these customers. Con Ed readily has this information 
available and can easily share it with ESCOs.9
In the Rebuttal Testimony of its Customer Operations Panel, Con Edison seeks to justify 
its failure to provide ESCOs with such information by claiming that ESCOs should get this 
information from their customers directly.10  This claim ignores the facts that: (1) Con Edison 
already has access to all of this information to administer its own commodity supplies to such 
customers; and (2) that the billing departments of such customers are frequently unaware of 
changes in their NYPA allocations and/or the need to communicate such changes to their ESCO 
supplier.  The best way for ESCOs to avoid such mistakes and to permit ESCOs to compete with 
Con Edison on a level playing field to serve customers receiving allocations of NYPA power is by 
requiring Con Edison to provide this information to the ESCOs serving those customers on the 
same basis that it provides such information to its own billing department. 
In the event that the Commission decides not to require Con Edison to make these changes 
immediately, it should nonetheless accept Con Edison’s offer to include this information in its 
replacement for RAIS.

Edison’s service territory is that Con Edison does not provide adequate visibility into whether or

not accounts are net metered.  As the NRG Panel explained, this can cause serious billing problems 
that could be avoided with better disclosure of the extent to which customers are net metered: 
NRG Energy often prices and serves large business customers based 
upon a minimum usage. If a customer installs renewables and in turn

To remedy this problem, the NRG Energy Panel recommended that Con Edison be required to take 
two actions:  First, Con Edison should be required to use the REF*KY field in EDI to inform 
ESCOs if a customer is net metered at the time of enrollment, as some other New York utilities 
already do.  Second, Con Edison should be directed to report any type of change request when a 
customer switches on or off a net meter on the account SYNC list so that ESCOs can monitor such 
changes.12
In their rebuttal testimony, Con Edison’s Customer Operations Panel asserts that Con 
Edison’s legacy system cannot accommodate this request, but that Con Edison would consider 
implementing this change, if feasible, when it replaces its current RAIS.13  NRG Energy 
respectfully submits that the fact that other New York utilities are already using the REF*KY field 
as NRG Energy requests demonstrates that this request is fully compatible with Con Edison’s 
existing EDI systems.  Moreover, adding information on customer net meter status to its monthly 
account SNYC list would not require any changes to Con Edison’s existing EDI systems, as that 
information would be reported outside of EDI.  Here too, in the event that the Commission decides
