STATEMENT‌ ‌IN‌ ‌SUPPORT‌ ‌OF‌ ‌THE‌ ‌CENTRAL‌ ‌HUDSON‌ ‌JOINT‌ ‌PROPOSAL‌‌ ‌
  
BY‌ ‌ALLIANCE‌ ‌FOR‌ ‌A‌ ‌GREEN‌ ‌ECONOMY‌ ‌
 
‌
 
1.‌ ‌Introduction‌ ‌-‌ ‌Why‌ ‌AGREE‌ ‌Supports‌ ‌the‌ ‌Joint‌ ‌Proposal‌ ‌
 
‌
 
Alliance‌ ‌for‌ ‌a‌ ‌Green‌ ‌Economy‌ ‌(“AGREE”)‌ ‌supports‌ ‌the‌ ‌Joint‌ ‌Proposal‌ ‌filed‌ ‌on‌ ‌August‌ ‌24,‌ ‌2021‌‌ 
in‌ ‌the‌ ‌above‌ ‌referenced‌ ‌proceeding.‌‌ ‌
  
‌
 
AGREE‌ ‌works‌ ‌for‌ ‌safe,‌ ‌affordable‌ ‌energy‌ ‌and‌ ‌the‌ ‌development‌ ‌of‌ ‌a‌ ‌just,‌ ‌green‌ ‌economy‌ ‌in‌ ‌New‌‌ 
York‌ ‌State.‌ ‌We‌ ‌educate‌ ‌the‌ ‌public‌ ‌and‌ ‌our‌ ‌policymakers‌ ‌about‌ ‌renewable‌ ‌alternatives‌ ‌that‌ ‌can‌‌ 
both‌ ‌revitalize‌ ‌the‌ ‌economy‌ ‌and‌ ‌safeguard‌ ‌human‌ ‌health‌ ‌and‌ ‌the‌ ‌environment.‌ ‌AGREE‌ ‌is‌ ‌the‌‌ 
only‌ ‌environmental‌ ‌nonprofit‌ ‌to‌ ‌participate‌ ‌in‌ ‌every‌ ‌major‌ ‌rate‌ ‌case‌ ‌across‌ ‌the‌ ‌state‌ ‌in‌ ‌order‌ ‌to‌‌ 
promote‌ ‌a‌ ‌collaborative‌ ‌transition‌ ‌to‌ ‌a‌ ‌renewable‌ ‌energy‌ ‌future.‌ ‌
 
‌
 
AGREE‌ ‌participated‌ ‌thoroughly‌ ‌in‌ ‌the‌ ‌negotiation‌ ‌of‌ ‌the‌ ‌Joint‌ ‌Proposal.‌ ‌We‌ ‌commend‌ ‌the‌‌ 
individual‌ ‌Department‌ ‌of‌ ‌Service‌ ‌Staff‌ ‌(“Staff”)‌ ‌and‌ ‌Central‌ ‌Hudson‌ ‌(“Company”)‌‌ 
representatives‌ ‌that‌ ‌led‌ ‌the‌ ‌settlement‌ ‌process.‌ ‌We‌ ‌have‌ ‌participated‌ ‌in‌ ‌several‌ ‌rate‌ ‌case‌‌ 
negotiations‌ ‌over‌ ‌the‌ ‌years,‌ ‌and‌ ‌this‌ ‌was‌ ‌by‌ ‌far‌ ‌the‌ ‌most‌ ‌orderly‌ ‌and‌ ‌well‌ ‌facilitated‌ ‌settlement‌‌ 
process‌ ‌we‌ ‌have‌ ‌seen,‌ ‌even‌ ‌while‌ ‌being‌ ‌conducted‌ ‌entirely‌ ‌remotely.‌ ‌We‌ ‌believe‌ ‌the‌ ‌nature‌ ‌of‌‌ 
how‌ ‌this‌ ‌process‌ ‌was‌ ‌run‌ ‌is‌ ‌an‌ ‌important‌ ‌factor‌ ‌that‌ ‌contributed‌ ‌to‌ ‌the‌ ‌near‌ ‌unanimous‌ ‌support‌‌ 
for‌ ‌the‌ ‌outcome,‌ ‌and‌ ‌we‌ ‌hope‌ ‌the‌ ‌practices‌ ‌and‌ ‌tone‌ ‌employed‌ ‌in‌ ‌this‌ ‌settlement‌ ‌will‌ ‌be‌ ‌used‌ ‌in‌‌ 
future‌ ‌negotiations.‌‌ ‌
  
‌
 
The‌ ‌agreement‌ ‌hammered‌ ‌out‌ ‌through‌ ‌settlement‌ ‌contains‌ ‌numerous‌ ‌provisions‌ ‌that‌ ‌represent‌‌ 
meaningful‌ ‌compromise‌ ‌among‌ ‌normally‌ ‌adversarial‌ ‌parties‌ ‌and‌ ‌which‌ ‌specifically‌ ‌concede‌ ‌to‌‌ 
the‌ ‌public‌ ‌interest‌ ‌positions‌ ‌taken‌ ‌by‌ ‌not-for-profit‌ ‌public‌ ‌interest‌ ‌organizations‌ ‌who‌ ‌represent‌‌ 
constituencies‌ ‌within‌ ‌the‌ ‌Companies’‌ ‌service‌ ‌territory.‌ ‌In‌ ‌particular,‌ ‌we‌ ‌call‌ ‌attention‌ ‌to‌‌ 
commitments‌ ‌in‌ ‌the‌ ‌Joint‌ ‌Proposal‌ ‌that‌ ‌begin‌ ‌to‌ ‌align‌ ‌the‌ ‌gas‌ ‌Company's‌ ‌activities‌ ‌and‌ ‌planning‌‌ 
with‌ ‌the‌ ‌Climate‌ ‌Leadership‌ ‌and‌ ‌Community‌ ‌Protection‌ ‌Act‌ ‌(“CLCPA”)‌ ‌by‌ ‌reducing‌ ‌the‌ ‌sale‌ ‌of‌‌ 
fracked‌ ‌gas.‌ ‌We‌ ‌also‌ ‌appreciate‌ ‌that‌ ‌the‌ ‌rate‌ ‌increases‌ ‌in‌ ‌the‌ ‌case‌ ‌were‌ ‌mitigated,‌ ‌such‌ ‌that‌ ‌no‌‌ 
annual‌ ‌bill‌ ‌increase‌ ‌in‌ ‌this‌ ‌Joint‌ ‌Proposal‌ ‌will‌ ‌result‌ ‌in‌ ‌more‌ ‌than‌ ‌a‌ ‌1.9%‌ ‌bill‌ ‌increase‌ ‌for‌ ‌the‌‌ 
average‌ ‌customer.‌ ‌Finally,‌ ‌we‌ ‌point‌ ‌to‌ ‌a‌ ‌commitment‌ ‌from‌ ‌the‌ ‌Company‌ ‌to‌ ‌translate‌ ‌its‌ ‌website,‌‌ 
customer‌ ‌materials,‌ ‌and‌ ‌bills‌ ‌into‌ ‌Spanish.‌ ‌
 
‌
 
To‌ ‌our‌ ‌knowledge,‌ ‌this‌ ‌Joint‌ ‌Proposal‌ ‌is‌ ‌the‌ ‌first‌ ‌in‌ ‌New‌ ‌York‌ ‌to‌ ‌pledge‌ ‌a‌ ‌commitment‌ ‌on‌ ‌the‌‌ 
part‌ ‌of‌ ‌a‌ ‌gas‌ ‌company‌ ‌to‌ ‌reduce‌ ‌gas‌ ‌sales‌ ‌in‌ ‌the‌ ‌coming‌ ‌years.‌ ‌The‌ ‌Joint‌ ‌Proposal‌ ‌commits‌‌ 
Central‌ ‌Hudson‌ ‌to‌ ‌a‌ ‌reduction‌ ‌of‌ ‌2.5%‌ ‌in‌ ‌gas‌ ‌sales‌ ‌over‌ ‌the‌ ‌next‌ ‌4‌ ‌years‌ ‌from‌ ‌2019‌ ‌levels,‌‌ 
which‌ ‌will‌ ‌be‌ ‌achieved‌ ‌through‌ ‌a‌ ‌combination‌ ‌of‌ ‌energy‌ ‌efficiency‌ ‌and‌ ‌renewable‌ ‌heating‌‌ 
technologies.‌ ‌The‌ ‌Company‌ ‌has‌ ‌also‌ ‌agreed‌ ‌to‌ ‌remove‌ ‌aggressive‌ ‌gas‌ ‌marketing‌ ‌language‌‌ 
from‌ ‌its‌ ‌website‌ ‌and‌ ‌to‌ ‌make‌ ‌renewable‌ ‌heating‌ ‌options‌ ‌more‌ ‌visible‌ ‌to‌ ‌website‌ ‌visitors.‌ ‌ ‌
   
‌
 
Taken‌ ‌as‌ ‌a‌ ‌whole,‌ ‌the‌ ‌Joint‌ ‌Proposal‌ ‌is‌ ‌a‌ ‌dramatic‌ ‌improvement‌ ‌over‌ ‌the‌ ‌Company’s‌ ‌original‌‌ 
proposal.‌‌ ‌

2.‌ ‌Further‌ ‌Action‌ ‌is‌ ‌Necessary‌ ‌
 
‌
 
The‌ ‌backdrop‌ ‌for‌ ‌the‌ ‌Company’s‌ ‌commitment‌ ‌to‌ ‌reduce‌ ‌gas‌ ‌sales‌ ‌is‌ ‌the‌ ‌CLCPA,‌ ‌which‌ ‌went‌‌ 
into‌ ‌effect‌ ‌in‌ ‌January‌ ‌2020‌ ‌and‌ ‌binds‌ ‌state‌ ‌agencies‌ ‌to‌ ‌meet‌ ‌aggressive‌ ‌greenhouse‌ ‌gas‌‌ 
reductions‌ ‌of‌ ‌40%‌ ‌below‌ ‌1990‌ ‌levels‌ ‌by‌ ‌2030‌ ‌and‌ ‌85%‌ ‌by‌ ‌2050.‌ ‌To‌ ‌meet‌ ‌these‌ ‌targets,‌ ‌the‌‌ 
New‌ ‌York‌ ‌Public‌ ‌Service‌ ‌Commission‌ ‌(“Commission”)‌ ‌will‌ ‌need‌ ‌to‌ ‌require‌ ‌that‌ ‌gas‌ ‌and‌ ‌electric‌‌ 
utilities‌ ‌work‌ ‌quickly‌ ‌and‌ ‌systematically‌ ‌to‌ ‌switch‌ ‌their‌ ‌customers‌ ‌from‌ ‌on-site‌ ‌fossil-fuel‌‌ 
combustion‌ ‌for‌ ‌their‌ ‌heating‌ ‌and‌ ‌other‌ ‌thermal‌ ‌energy‌ ‌needs‌ ‌to‌ ‌efficient‌ ‌electric‌ ‌thermal‌ ‌energy‌‌ 
systems.‌ ‌The‌ ‌pace‌ ‌and‌ ‌the‌ ‌scale‌ ‌of‌ ‌the‌ ‌changes‌ ‌needed‌ ‌is‌ ‌dramatic:‌ ‌40%‌ ‌reductions‌ ‌in‌‌ 
fossil-fuel‌ ‌emissions‌ ‌from‌ ‌the‌ ‌thermal‌ ‌energy‌ ‌sector‌ ‌by‌ ‌2030‌ ‌will‌ ‌require‌ ‌hundreds‌ ‌of‌ ‌thousands‌‌ 
of‌ ‌buildings‌ ‌to‌ ‌be‌ ‌converted‌ ‌each‌ ‌year‌ ‌for‌ ‌the‌ ‌next‌ ‌decade.‌‌ ‌
  
‌
 
As‌ ‌a‌ ‌state‌ ‌agency,‌ ‌the‌ ‌Commission‌ ‌is‌ ‌required‌ ‌to‌ ‌enforce‌ ‌the‌ ‌CLCPA‌ ‌and‌ ‌to‌ ‌ensure‌ ‌that‌ ‌its‌‌ 
decisions‌ ‌are‌ ‌consistent‌ ‌with‌ ‌the‌ ‌greenhouse‌ ‌gas‌ ‌emissions‌ ‌reductions‌ ‌mandated‌ ‌by‌ ‌the‌ ‌law.‌ ‌In‌‌ 
August,‌ ‌2021,‌ ‌the‌ ‌Commission‌ ‌expressly‌ ‌acknowledged‌ ‌this‌ ‌requirement‌ ‌extends‌ ‌to‌ ‌rate‌ ‌case‌‌ 
decisions.‌1‌ ‌T‌o‌ ‌truly‌ ‌enforce‌ ‌the‌ ‌CLCPA‌ ‌and‌ ‌to‌ ‌understand‌ ‌whether‌ ‌rate‌ ‌case‌ ‌proposals‌ ‌are‌‌ 
CLCPA-consistent,‌ ‌especially‌ ‌in‌ ‌contested‌ ‌rate‌ ‌proceedings,‌ ‌actual‌ ‌emissions‌ ‌analyses‌ ‌and‌‌ 
annual‌ ‌emissions‌ ‌reduction‌ ‌targets‌ ‌are‌ ‌needed.‌ ‌Without‌ ‌such‌ ‌an‌ ‌analysis‌ ‌and‌ ‌a‌ ‌standard‌ ‌by‌‌ 
which‌ ‌to‌ ‌compare‌ ‌a‌ ‌utility’s‌ ‌actions‌ ‌against‌ ‌what‌ ‌is‌ ‌required‌ ‌by‌ ‌the‌ ‌law,‌ ‌it‌ ‌is‌ ‌not‌ ‌possible‌ ‌to‌‌ 
determine‌ ‌whether‌ ‌any‌ ‌particular‌ ‌set‌ ‌of‌ ‌actions‌ ‌by‌ ‌a‌ ‌utility‌ ‌company‌ ‌is‌ ‌consistent‌ ‌with‌ ‌the‌‌ 
CLCPA.‌ ‌ ‌
   
‌
 
That‌ ‌said,‌ ‌Central‌ ‌Hudson’s‌ ‌commitments‌ ‌put‌ ‌it‌ ‌ahead‌ ‌of‌ ‌all‌ ‌other‌ ‌gas‌ ‌utilities‌ ‌in‌ ‌New‌ ‌York‌ ‌so‌‌ 
far.‌ ‌While‌ ‌other‌ ‌utilities‌ ‌are‌ ‌continuing‌ ‌to‌ ‌increase‌ ‌their‌ ‌gas‌ ‌sales‌ ‌(such‌ ‌as‌ ‌in‌ ‌recently‌ ‌approved‌‌ 
KEDNY/KEDLI‌ ‌case)‌ ‌or‌ ‌holding‌ ‌their‌ ‌gas‌ ‌sales‌ ‌at‌ ‌net‌ ‌zero‌ ‌(such‌ ‌as‌ ‌NYSEG/RG&E),‌ ‌Central‌‌ 
Hudson‌ ‌has‌ ‌taken‌ ‌the‌ ‌unprecedented‌ ‌step‌ ‌of‌ ‌working‌ ‌toward‌ ‌a‌ ‌net‌ ‌decrease.‌ ‌This‌ ‌moves‌ ‌the‌‌ 
utility‌ ‌in‌ ‌the‌ ‌right‌ ‌direction‌ ‌while‌ ‌other‌ ‌utilities‌ ‌are‌ ‌going‌ ‌backwards‌ ‌or‌ ‌standing‌ ‌still.‌ ‌This‌ ‌is‌ ‌why‌‌ 
AGREE‌ ‌supports‌ ‌this‌ ‌Joint‌ ‌Proposal.‌ ‌We‌ ‌see‌ ‌this‌ ‌compromise‌ ‌agreement‌ ‌as‌ ‌representing‌‌ 
meaningful‌ ‌progress‌ ‌that‌ ‌can,‌ ‌as‌ ‌needed,‌ ‌be‌ ‌accelerated‌ ‌by‌ ‌a‌ ‌statewide‌ ‌policy.‌ ‌
 
‌
 
Therefore,‌ ‌in‌ ‌addition‌ ‌to‌ ‌urging‌ ‌the‌ ‌Commission‌ ‌to‌ ‌adopt‌ ‌the‌ ‌Central‌ ‌Hudson‌ ‌Joint‌ ‌Proposal,‌ ‌we‌‌ 
urge‌ ‌the‌ ‌Commission‌ ‌to‌ ‌immediately‌ ‌require‌ ‌all‌ ‌gas‌ ‌utilities,‌ ‌whether‌ ‌or‌ ‌not‌ ‌they‌ ‌are‌ ‌currently‌‌ 
engaged‌ ‌in‌ ‌a‌ ‌rate‌ ‌case,‌ ‌to‌ ‌quantify‌ ‌the‌ ‌greenhouse‌ ‌gas‌ ‌emissions‌ ‌associated‌ ‌with‌ ‌their‌‌ 
operations‌ ‌and‌ ‌the‌ ‌fuels‌ ‌they‌ ‌sell‌ ‌and‌ ‌to‌ ‌develop‌ ‌a‌ ‌plan‌ ‌to‌ ‌align‌ ‌their‌ ‌operations‌ ‌and‌ ‌sales‌ ‌with‌‌ 
the‌ ‌CLCPA.‌ ‌Along‌ ‌with‌ ‌our‌ ‌allies,‌ ‌AGREE‌ ‌has‌ ‌been‌ ‌calling‌ ‌for‌ ‌this‌ ‌since‌ ‌the‌ ‌CLCPA‌ ‌was‌‌ 
passed‌ ‌in‌ ‌June‌ ‌2019,‌ ‌and‌ ‌we‌ ‌are‌ ‌astounded‌ ‌that‌ ‌two‌ ‌years‌ ‌later,‌ ‌the‌ ‌Commission‌ ‌has‌ ‌still‌ ‌taken‌‌ 
no‌ ‌action‌ ‌to‌ ‌put‌ ‌in‌ ‌place‌ ‌the‌ ‌requirements‌ ‌for‌ ‌utility‌ ‌gas‌ ‌rate‌ ‌cases‌ ‌that‌ ‌are‌ ‌needed‌ ‌so‌ ‌that‌ ‌the‌‌ 
agency‌ ‌can‌ ‌comply‌ ‌with‌ ‌the‌ ‌CLCPA.‌‌ ‌

3.‌ ‌Conclusion‌ ‌
 
‌
 
We‌ ‌support‌ ‌this‌ ‌agreement‌ ‌in‌ ‌the‌ ‌hopes‌ ‌that‌ ‌it‌ ‌represents‌ ‌a‌ ‌turning‌ ‌point‌ ‌for‌ ‌gas‌ ‌utilities‌ ‌in‌ ‌New‌‌ 
York.‌ ‌The‌ ‌settlement‌ ‌builds‌ ‌on‌ ‌and‌ ‌goes‌ ‌further‌ ‌than‌ ‌the‌ ‌Joint‌ ‌Proposal‌ ‌developed‌ ‌by‌ ‌multiple‌‌ 
parties‌ ‌in‌ ‌the‌ ‌NYSEG/RG&E‌ ‌rate‌ ‌case,‌ ‌in‌ ‌which‌ ‌the‌ ‌first‌ ‌gas‌ ‌utility‌ ‌in‌ ‌New‌ ‌York‌ ‌agreed‌ ‌to‌ ‌plan‌‌
