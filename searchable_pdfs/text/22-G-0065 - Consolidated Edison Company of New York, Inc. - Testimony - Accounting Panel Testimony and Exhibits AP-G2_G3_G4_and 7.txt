Question No. :546 
 
Subject:  DG Application Fees 
  
1)    Refer to page 47, of the Company’s Accounting Panel Testimony, which indicates the DG 
Application Fee revenues represent the revenues the Company receives for solar applications.  
Further, the Company indicates that the Rate Year forecast was set at the Historic Test Year 
level.   
a.     Provide the number of DG Application Fees (e.g., solar applications) the Company received 
in calendar year 2019, 2020 and 2021. 
b.     Refer to the Company’s Excel Workpaper “AP-EG3, Schedule 5 – OOR”, (3 Year History 
and Forecast tab), which indicates DG Project Application Fee revenues increased by 4% from 
calendar year 2020 through 2021.  Given the Company’s increased focus on clean energy 
investments, as described in the Company’s CLCPA Panel, and the increase in DG Project 
Application Fee revenues from calendar year 2020 to 2021, explain why the Company used the 
Historic Test Year level, as the Rate Year forecast of DG Project Application Fee revenues. 
 
 
Response 
1a.  See “Attachment DPS-19-546” for solar application fees for calendar years 2019 to 2021. 
 
1b.  The Company is updating its forecast for solar application fees associated with larger 
projects that are over 50 KW in its Preliminary Update.  The updated forecast shows a 5% 
increase over the HTY level for RY1, 7% increase over the HTY level for RY2, and 10% 
increase over the HTY for RY3.   
 
Such forecast is based on factors such as the local laws requiring solar or green roofs for new 
buildings and refurbishes premises (Local Laws 94 and 97); the approval of energy storage 
systems in a more timely fashion by FDNY than today’s practice; Build Back Better Legislation 
incentivizing storage in the form of tax credits; Electric Vehicle and Vehicle to Grid proliferation 
with storage components and the updated 10 GW solar Roadmap adding an additional 4 GW of 
PV to current state goals.

Question No. :563 
 
Subject:  Bargaining Unit Contract Costs 
  
Refer to the Company’s Excel Workpaper “AP-EG3 – Sch 06, Bargaining Unit Contract Costs,” 
which indicates a Historic Test Year amount for Bargaining Unit Costs of $15K and $3K for 
electric and gas, respectively. 
1.     Explain in detail, the nature of this item, including the Company’s activities and the timing 
of those activities, related to negotiation and strike contingency efforts. 
2.     How often does the Company incur costs for negotiation and strike contingency efforts? 
3.     Provide the Bargaining Unit Costs for the 12-months ending September 30, 2018, 2019 and 
2020. 
4.     Refer to the tab labeled “Non Labor,” provide the rationale, calculation and supporting 
documentation, for the Rate Year 1 Program Changes, in the amount of $450K. 
5.     Explain why the proposed electric program change was not reflected in the Exhibit___(AP-
E3), CECONY Initial Filing RevReq. 
 
 
Response 
 
1. This item relates to the various costs that the Company incurs in preparing for and 
engaging in the negotiation of Collective Bargaining Contracts with its two unions.  
Several months prior to the expiration dates of the Collective Bargaining Contracts with 
Local 1-2 UWUA (most recently in 2020) and Local 3 IBEW (most recently in 2021) the 
Company and the Unions engage in contract negotiations.  These efforts include 
preliminary negotiation sessions leading up to full-time meetings at a hotel, and round the 
clock bargaining as contract expiration approaches.  Prior to these negotiating sessions, 
the Company provides training for the various individuals that will be representing 
management in these negotiations. 
 
In order to maintain Company operations in the event of a work stoppage, our 
contingency program includes the training, refresher training, and qualification of 
management employees to perform the bargaining unit’s jobs functions.   
 
Finally, Contract Costs include the printing and distribution of copies of the Collective 
Bargaining Contract books, which is the product of negotiations with the Unions.

4. Several years ago, it was recommended that rather than request funding in contract years,

Question No. :670 
 
Subject:  Storage Dispatch 10 Years-Deferrals 
  
Refer to the Company’s Excel Workpaper “AP-EG3 Schedule 4 - Deferral & Amortization” 
(“EE Detail” tab). Provide calculations and detailed explanations for how the Company 
forecasted the following amounts: 
1.     The 2022 electric budget of $950,000. Provide all supporting documentation. 
2.     The 2025 electric budget of $140,750,000. Provide all supporting documentation. 
 
 
Response 
 
The budget for 10-year Storage Dispatch Rights includes initial base level forecast for 
consultants, and legal costs pursuant to Case 18-E-0130 – In the Matter of Energy Deployment 
Program.  In April 2021, the PSC authorized utilities to extend the maximum contract duration 
from “up to seven years” to “up to ten years”.  The company anticipates resources will be 
necessary to work with NYSERDA and Staff to comply with the Energy Storage 2.0 Roadmap. 
 
In the preliminary update filing, the Company will decrease the 2025 budget from $140,750,000 
to $700,000.  The company had previously anticipated contracts to be awarded in conjunction 
with a second round of solicitations.  Such solicitation efforts yielded no addition contracts.  As 
such, the budget has been decreased.
