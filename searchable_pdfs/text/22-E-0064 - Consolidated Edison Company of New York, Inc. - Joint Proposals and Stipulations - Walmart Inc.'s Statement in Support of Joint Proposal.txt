I. 
INTRODUCTION 
On February 16, 2023, Consolidated Edison Company of New York, Inc. ("ConEd" or 
"Company") filed with the New York State Public Service Commission ("PSC" or "Commission") 
in the above-referenced proceeding an executed Joint Proposal memorializing the settlement 
achieved between multiple parties.  Walmart Inc. ("Walmart") actively participated in the 
settlement negotiations and is a signatory to the Joint Proposal as it pertains to the above-captioned 
electric base and gas base rate proceedings initiated by the Company.  Walmart hereby respectfully 
submits this Statement in Support of the Joint Proposal. 
Although Walmart did not address every issue raised by the Company or other parties in 
the course of these proceedings or resolved by the Joint Proposal and the settlement among the 
various parties, Walmart supports the Joint Proposal and settlement in full as a just, reasonable, 
and fair resolution of the issues in the electric and natural gas cases that Walmart believes is in the

best interest of the Company, its customers, and the New York public.  Accordingly, and for the 
following reasons specific to the issues raised by Walmart in the course of these proceedings, 
Walmart respectfully requests that the Joint Proposal be approved without modification.   
II. 
ARGUMENT 
As noted, Walmart did not address every issue presented by the Company in their initial 
filing or raised by other parties in the evidence presented in the course of these cases.  Rather, 
Walmart presented evidence and arguments in the form of the Direct Testimony of Alex J. 
Kronauer, addressing distinct and discreet issues:  (1) the Company's proposed return on equity 
("ROE");1 (2) cost of service and revenue allocation;2 and (3) rate design of electric Service 
Classification No. 9 General – Large ("SC-9").3  This Statement in Support of the Joint Proposal 
accordingly does not address each and every issue resolved by the settlement.    
The Company originally requested an ROE of 10.0 percent.  In response to this request, 
Walmart submitted testimony noting concerns that the Company's requested ROE was counter to 
recent ROEs awarded both in New York and nationwide and that the ROE determination would 
have an impact on ConEd's customers.4  After many months of settlement discussions addressing 
extensive issues, the settling parties agreed to a 9.25 percent ROE, which is considerably lower 
than the Company's original proposal.5  Walmart believes that a 9.25 percent ROE reflects a 
significant compromise between the parties and provides the Company the opportunity to earn a

Regarding rate design, the Company's originally proposed design of rates for schedule SC-
9 would gradually (but not fully) move the SC-9 rates closer to cost of service by shifting five 
percent of the usage revenue from volumetric kWh charges to per-kW demand charges on a 
revenue neutral basis, as the current rate design creates intra-class subsidies by recovering 
substantial amounts of customer-related fixed costs through energy charges.  Walmart recognized 
the Company's proposal to begin shifting these fixed costs to demand costs so that rates would 
move closer to cost of service and thus did not oppose the Company's SC-9 rate design.  Walmart 
did, however, request that a lower revenue requirement than that originally requested by the 
Company flow through the energy delivery charge to further bring rates closer to cost of service.6
The Joint Proposal also incorporates an important adjustment to the Company's as-filed 
rate design.  Specifically, as noted by Walmart in its testimony, the Company currently recovers a 
significant amount of demand-related costs for certain demand-billed classes (including SC-9) 
through the volumetric energy component of rates, which does not produce cost-based rates.7  The 
Company originally proposed to reduce the amount of demand costs recovered through energy 
rates by five percent.  The Joint Proposal reflects an agreement among the parties to increase that 
shift to seven percent, thereby bringing those classes somewhat closer to cost of service at a slightly 
faster rate.8  While this movement is moderate and will not result in fully cost-based rates for those 
demand-billed classes, the seven percent shift ensures that a greater percentage of the Company's 
fixed cost to serve SC-9 are incorporated in the demand charge, thus reducing intra-class

From a broader perspective, the avoidance of ongoing time and expenses related to 
litigation of these issues is a valuable and important component of achieving settlements, which

pending for well over a year supports the substantial benefit provided to the parties, and the

III. 
CONCLUSION 
For these reasons, Walmart believes that the Joint Proposal represents a fair, just, and 
reasonable resolution of the issues in these cases.  The Joint Proposal also reflects many hours of 
negotiation between traditionally adverse parties representing numerous public interests within the 
state, and as a whole represents compromise and agreement among these parties on a wide array 
of issues that were not likely to have been resolved in a manner that these parties would deem to 
be mutually favorable apart from the Joint Proposal.  Walmart therefore believes that the Joint 
Proposal is in the public interest and respectfully requests that the Commission adopt it without 
modification.
