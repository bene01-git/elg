STAFF VMP’S PROPOSED SIX-YEAR DISTRIBUTION VEGETATION 
6 
MANAGEMENT TRIM CYCLE FOR NYSEG 
7 
Q. 
Do you have any concerns with the Staff VMP’s testimony at page 22, lines 15 through 
8 
24, that proposes to move NYSEG’s entire system to a six-year distribution vegetation 
9 
management trim cycle instead of NYSEG’s proposed five-year trim cycle? 
10 
A. 
Yes.  While the Companies recognize that the Staff VMP’s proposal will improve 
11 
NYSEG’s current reliability outlook, a six-year trim cycle will not provide customers 
12 
with benefits and reduced risks of outages as soon as would happen by moving to a five-
13 
year trim cycle.  Most importantly, customers would have delayed improvements to 
14 
system reliability under a six-year trim cycle as compared to a five-year trim cycle.  Also, 
15 
a five-year trim cycle is likely to be more cost effective than a six-year trim cycle, 
16 
because a six-year trim cycle will have a higher likelihood of causing more 
17 
tree/conductor contacts at the time of pruning.  Costs associated with pruning trees in 
18 
contact with energized wires may be more than 1.5 times higher than pruning trees that 
19 
are not in contact because pruning trees in contact with energized wires requires extra 
20 
time, special skills, roping techniques, and/or specialized equipment for worker safety.

11 
provided in response to IR NYSEG-RGE-139. 
12 
Q. 
Why else should NYSEG be on a five-year trim cycle instead of a six-year trim cycle?  
13 
A. 
No other major utility in New York is on a six-year trim cycle.  In fact, as noted in the 
14 
2018 Storm Report, Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corporation, PSEG-LI and Orange 
15 
and Rockland Utilities, Inc. all have four-year trim cycle programs and most of 
16 
Consolidated Edison of New York’s (“Con Edison”) circuits are on a three-year trim 
17 
cycle program.  The lack of a complete five-year trim cycle for NYSEG thus makes the 
18 
Company an outlier in New York.  Moreover, given the tree species, growth patterns and 
19 
topology of NYSEG’s system, it would only be possible to maintain reliability with a six-
20 
year trim cycle if the specifications for clearances were more aggressive compared to 
21 
current five-year specifications, and/or if the tree species on the system had low growth 
22 
rates, which they do not.

Q. 
What are NYSEG’s current clearance specifications? 
6 
A. 
The current NYSEG clearance specifications for distribution vegetation management are 
7 
for vegetation to be cut back 10’ from the power lines at the time of maintenance, with an 
8 
overhanging canopy no closer than 15’ above the power lines.  As noted in the 2018 
9 
Storm Report (page 163), except for Con Edison’s New York City service territory, these 
10 
are the clearance specifications used by all New York utilities. 
11

12 
changing to a six-year cycle, with no change in clearance specification?   
13 
A. 
After reclamation, a greater portion of the NYSEG distribution system would risk having 
14 
tree contact from above and to the sides of the power lines.  
15 
Q.

16 
cycle? 
17

18

19 
clearance specifications, and these increased costs would need to be built into delivery 
20 
rates.
