• We view Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. (CHG&E) as a moderately
strategic subsidiary of Fortis Inc.
• We view the cumulative value of insulation measures in place, including a
non-consolidation opinion, as sufficient to separate the ratings of CHG&E

Rating Action
On April 27, 2016, Standard & Poor's Ratings Services affirmed its 'A'
corporate credit and senior unsecured debt ratings on Central Hudson Gas &
Electric Corp. (CHG&E). The negative outlook remains unchanged.
Rationale
The negative outlook reflects the possibility of a near-term, one-notch
ratings downgrade, reflecting our reduced confidence in CHG&E's ability to
sustain a durable regulatory advantage that differentiates it from its peers,
including the prospects of operating under multiyear rate plans with reduced
authorized returns by the New York Public Service Commission (NYPSC). We had
initially revised the outlook to negative from stable on Feb. 9, 2016,
following parent Fortis Inc.'s announcement of its planned acquisition of ITC
Holdings Corp.
We base CHG&E's ratings on the company's stand-alone business and financial
risk profiles and the consolidated group credit profile of its ultimate
parent, Fortis Inc. Our assessment of CHG&E's business risk reflects the
company's lower-risk electric and gas transmission and distributions (T&D)
operations, and its effective management of regulatory risk. These factors are
offset by the company's small service territory, limited customer growth, and
lack of geographic diversity. CHG&E serves about 375,000 customers in eight
counties of New York's mid-Hudson River Valley.

Outlook
The negative outlook reflects the possibility of a one-notch ratings downgrade
at CHG&E in the near term, reflecting our reduced confidence in CHG&E's
ability to sustain a durable regulatory advantage that differentiates it from
its peers in New York.
Downside scenario
We could downgrade CHG&E if pending regulatory decisions in New York for New
York State Electric & Gas Corp. and Rochester Gas & Electric Corp. confirm a
lack of a distinct and sustainable regulatory advantage that differentiates
CHG&E from its peers in New York. We could also lower the ratings if CHG&E's
stand-alone financial measures weakened, including an FFO-to-debt ratio that
consistently falls below 13%. This could occur if CHG&E experiences adverse
regulatory outcomes that impede its ability to manage regulatory risk.
Upside scenario
We could affirm the ratings if we view CHG&E's regulated returns as comparing
favorably with its peers in New York, or if the company materially improved
its financial measures such that FFO-to-debt is consistently at about 21% and
the ratings at Fortis remain unchanged.

Rating Action
On April 3, 2017, S&P Global Ratings affirmed its 'A-' issue credit rating and

Our reassessment of the relationship between parent Fortis and subsidiary

of Fortis' long-term commitment to its U.S.-based subsidiaries and CHG&E's

strategic goals. Our stand-alone credit profile and issuer credit rating on

business risk profile and significant financial risk profile.

customer growth, and lack of geographic diversity. CHG&E serves about 380,000

Outlook: Stable

Outlook: Stable
The stable outlook on Rochester Gas & Electric Corp. (RG&E) mirrors that of ultimate parent, Iberdrola S.A.
(Iberdrola). S&P Global Ratings' view is that Iberdrola's business activities are highly diversified and resilient, with
the majority of revenues stemming from regulated activities. At the same time, we perceive Iberdrola management
as committed to the current rating and to maintaining adjusted funds from operations (FFO) to debt in the
18%-20% range over the coming two to three years. Iberdrola's FFO to debt was 18.5% at year-end 2016.
However, we expect consolidated credit metrics will remain at the low end of the range until 2018. We see
headroom increasing somewhat from 2019, notably once Iberdrola commissions its large renewables projects and
as the contribution of U.S. network activities continues to increase.
Downside scenario
We see rating downside as remote, but pressure could arise if parent Iberdrola's cash flow and leverage weakened
more than we expected, with FFO to debt below 18%. In our view, this could occur if:
• The company engaged in a large debt-financed acquisition;
• Unexpected adverse regulation in one of Iberdrola's key markets occurred;
• Political or country risk in Spain intensified; or
• Risks from the U.K.'s planned exit of the E.U. affected operations in the U.K. and Scotland.
Other factors, in our view, include execution risks to the group's large offshore wind projects and a degree of
volatility from currency risks. Although debt is adequately spread by currency, a material weakening in the U.S.
dollar or British pound to the euro could somewhat reduce the group's financial flexibility within the 'BBB+' rating.
Upside scenario
Given the group's growth appetite and financial policy, we see rating upside as unlikely in the coming years. Given
Iberdrola's particularly strong business profile among European integrated utilities, a one-notch upgrade could
ultimately depend on a combination of:
• A continuous focus on network activities in favorable regulatory frameworks and highly rated countries;
• Improving conditions in liberalized power markets and in Spain's economic environment;
• Timely delivery on the ambitious renewables pipeline; and
• A more supportive financial policy, with the company targeting adjusted FFO to debt at 20% or higher.
Additionally, we assess the structural and regulatory protections in place as insulating RG&E up to two notches
higher than its parent. We could upgrade RG&E if its stand-alone financial measures consistently reflect the very
high end of the range for its financial risk profile category while maintaining the current strength of its business risk
profile. Specifically, this would reflect FFO to debt consistently greater than 22%.

Business Risk: Excellent
Our assessment of RG&E's business risk reflects the company's lower-risk electric and natural gas utility operations
and effective management of regulatory risk. RG&E provides electric transmission and distribution services to about
380,000 customers and natural gas distribution services to about 310,000 customers around Rochester, N.Y.

The New York Public Service Commission (NYPSC) regulates RG&E. We view the regulatory environment in New
York as generally constructive. The company benefits from a multi-year rate plan, forward-test year, and revenue
decoupling that protects the utility from adverse weather, conservation, and adverse economic conditions, thus
reducing regulatory lag. Currently, as part of a three-year rate plan effective 2016, RG&E has an authorized electric and
gas rate increase of about $35 million scheduled for May 2018. Overall, we view the company's management of
regulatory risk as in line with peers and expect that the company will continue to effectively manage regulatory risk.
