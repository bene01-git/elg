WHAT IS YOUR OPINION OF EAMS? 
3 
A. 
In general, the City is not opposed to providing a utility with shareholder incentives 
4 
if it performs at an exceptional level.  However, incentives need to be designed 
5 
solely or primarily to reward exceptional performance, not standard or expected 
6 
behavior, and the amount must be measured and reasonable to avoid unduly 
7 
burdening customers. As an example, in an Order issued on April 24, 2019 in Cases 
8 
16-M-0429 and 14-M-0101, related to interconnection EAMs, the Commission 
9 
specifically indicated that an EAM should be “an effective measure of exceptional 
10 
utility performance.”  We also believe that EAMs should be tied to some 
11 
demonstrable action taken by the utility, and that utility shareholders should not be 
12 
rewarded for actions taken solely by third parties, or for circumstances over which 
13 
the utilities have no control. 
14 
 We also note that the fact that an EAM, inclusive of its shareholder 
15 
incentives, may have a cost-benefit ratio in excess of 1.0 is not sufficient to 
16 
demonstrate exceptional performance or warrant payment of shareholder 
17 
incentives.  The size of the incentives, their impact on customers, and the quality 
18 
of the performance leading to the outcome must be considered.
