II. 
Standard of Review 
In determining whether a rate case settlement is just, reasonable, and in the public 
interest, the Commission looks to the factors set forth in the Settlement Guidelines,3 including: 
whether the rates and other terms of the agreement are just and reasonable; consistency with 
State law and policies; whether the result compares favorably4 with the likely result of full 
litigation and is within the reasonably expected range of a potential litigated outcome; whether 
the agreement strikes a fair balance among customers, investors, and the Company; whether the 
agreement was developed in accordance with all applicable procedures; whether all parties had 
the opportunity to participate in settlement discussions; whether the agreement is supported by 
parties with normally adverse interests; and whether there is a rational basis for decision. 
III. 
The Joint Proposal Is Just, Reasonable, and in the Public Interest  
The electric and gas revenue levels established in the Joint Proposal, while significantly 
reduced from the Company’s initial request, are sufficient to provide safe and reliable service 
and fund essential utility and clean energy projects while attracting investment and providing 
investors the opportunity to earn a reasonable return.  The Joint Proposal also continues 
accounting mechanisms and other rate provisions that the Commission has found just and 
reasonable and in the public interest and includes new provisions that allow the Company to 
recover costs incurred for customers on a timely basis, thereby mitigating future base rate

2. 
Cost of Capital and Related Terms 
The Joint Proposal provides for a return on common equity (“ROE”) of 9.25 percent, a 
capital structure that is 48 percent common equity, a long-term debt rate of 4.46 percent, and 
reconciliation of variable-rate long-term debt.  The Commission should approve these terms 
because they reflect Con Edison’s significant compromise given current market conditions, 
account for the multi-year nature of the rate plans, and have the potential to benefit customers by

return is accordingly consistent with the applicable constitutional standard and Commission

Under longstanding law, utility equity owners must have an opportunity to earn a return 
that is both commensurate with returns on investments in other enterprises having corresponding 
risks and reasonably sufficient to assure confidence in the utility’s financial soundness.8  Thus, it 
is essential that the Commission provide the Company with a market-based cost of capital.   
According to data from S&P Global, the national average of ROEs authorized since 
January 1, 2022 is 9.6 percent and the average common equity percentage is 50.9 percent.9  
Looking specifically at the circumstances of Con Edison, Company Witness Villadsen 
recommended an ROE between 10.0 and 10.5 percent and a 50 percent common equity ratio.  
Dr. Villadsen relied on the discounted cash flow model, the capital asset pricing model, and the 
risk premium model, and her testimony explains how she accounted for Con Edison’s risks and 
tested the reasonableness of her recommendations.

explaining that the rising interest and inflation rates environment exposes the Company to the

7. 
Computation and Disposition of Earnings 
An earnings sharing mechanism (“ESM”), under which a utility shares with customers 
earnings above pre-established thresholds, is a traditional component of multi-year utility rate 
plans.  An ESM is intended to balance the interests of parties who are concerned that a utility’s 
earnings under a multi-year rate plan may significantly exceed projected earnings with parties 
who, like Con Edison, maintain that customers will derive greater benefits from rate mechanisms 
that eliminate sharing requirements for the period of a rate plan and thereby produce stronger 
earnings and efficiency incentives that will redound to customers’ benefit in the long term.   
The Joint Proposal continues current electric and gas ESM sharing thresholds and rules 
(established in the 2019 Rate Plans).  These rules favor customers by requiring Con Edison to 
use 50 percent of its share of any shared earnings to reduce deferred Site Investigation and 
Remediation (“SIR”) costs, which reduces the amount of such costs deferred for future collection 
from customers.  If the amount that would be used to reduce SIR costs exceeds SIR deferrals, the 
Company is required to use the overage to reduce other interest-bearing deferred costs 


--- Page 16 ---
 
12 
chargeable to customers.  Thus, customers get their full 50 percent of total shared earnings and 
benefit from 50 percent of the Company’s nominal share.  This arrangement is responsive to the 
Commission’s expectation that rate plan ESMs will provide the opportunity to allocate some 
portion of shared earnings to offset SIR costs.13  The Company’s agreement to these customer-
favoring ESMs is another concession it made to reach a comprehensive settlement.

2. 
Electric and Gas Net Plant Reconciliation 
The Joint Proposal also continues, with modifications discussed below, existing 
downward-only net plant reconciliation mechanisms.  As with full reconciliation mechanisms, 
downward-only net plant reconciliation mechanisms protect customers from paying in rates for 
capital investments that Con Edison does not make.  Unlike full reconciliation mechanisms, 
however, downward-only reconciliation mechanisms do not protect Con Edison during the Joint 
Proposal’s term from absorbing the carrying costs of prudent and necessary capital expenditures 
that exceed the Joint Proposal’s net plant targets.  
The Joint Proposal provides for upward net plant reconciliation only for certain municipal 
electric and gas interference expenditures, which are subject to a cap.  Both reconciliations are 
included in the current rate plans.  The Joint Proposal modifies them slightly to remove upward 
reconciliations for categories/projects that are no longer relevant.  Otherwise continuing these 
full reconciliations is appropriate because covered municipal interference costs are often 
significant and generally outside of Con Edison’s control.  The Company should be able to 
recover such costs if they cause the Company to exceed the overall net plant targets.  For these 
reasons, Con Edison continues to maintain that all interference costs should be subject to full 
reconciliation.  In the interest of reaching a settlement, however, the Company agreed to the 
limited upward reconciliations in the Joint Proposal.

The Commission routinely adopts reconciliation mechanisms for costs and expenses that

mechanisms identified in the Joint Proposal are specifically addressed here.  The reconciliation 
mechanisms and their recovery approaches are reasonable and should be adopted.

Revenue Allocation 
The Joint Proposal allocates electric revenues based on Con Edison’s electric Embedded 
Cost of Service (“ECOS”) study.  The electric ECOS study analyzes costs and revenues, at 
current rates, associated with the Company’s electric delivery system for 2019 (i.e., transmission,

development of an ECOS study is a highly technical undertaking that includes the exercise of 
reasonable judgment, the Company performs this study in an unbiased manner, allocating costs 
to the appropriate service classification (“SC”) to fairly assess cost responsibility to each 
category of customers.  
The results of the ECOS study are expressed as Total Company (“total system”) and class 
rates of return.  Based on established practice, class revenue responsibilities (i.e., resulting 
revenue surpluses or deficiencies) are measured with respect to a plus/minus 10 percent tolerance 
band around the total system rate of return.  The revenue allocation reflects one-third of the 
revenue surplus/deficiency indications, resulting from the Company’s electric ECOS study, in a 
revenue neutral manner in each Rate Year.  This balances the interests of correcting surpluses 
and deficiencies and mitigating customer bill impacts in accordance with prior Commission 
decisions.

AMI-Enabled NGDs are safety devices installed indoors near the gas point-of-entry and

presents a first-of-a-kind and unique opportunity to pair remote methane detection with the AMI 
communication infrastructure that will improve public safety.  The Joint Proposal requires Con

V. 
Remaining Settlement Guidelines Factors 
The Settlement Guidelines require the Commission to consider whether the Joint 
Proposal was developed in accordance with all applicable procedures and whether all parties had 
the opportunity to participate in settlement discussions; whether the agreement is supported by 
parties with normally adverse interests; whether the result compares favorably with the likely 
result of full litigation and is within the reasonably expected range of a potential litigated 
outcome; whether the agreement strikes a fair balance among customers, investors, and the 
Company; and whether there is a rational basis for decision.  The Joint Proposal satisfies these 
criteria.   


--- Page 59 ---
 
55 
First, Con Edison provided appropriate notice of settlement negotiations in accordance 
with 16 NYCRR §3.9 (a) and provided all active parties the opportunity to fully participate in 
settlement discussions.  In particular, the Company provided notice of all settlement meetings 
and/or teleconferences.  The settling parties reached agreement after following all applicable 
Commission settlement procedures. 
Second, the settling parties represent a broad range of diverse interests, including major 
commercial, residential, low income, and governmental customers and/or their representatives, 
environmental advocacy organizations, geothermal developers, gas marketers, and distributed 
resources service providers. 
Third, as explained throughout these comments, the Joint Proposal reflects compromises 
on contested issues that have produced results within the reasonably expected range of potential 
litigated outcomes. 
Fourth, the Joint Proposal balances the interests of customers, the Company, and other 
important stakeholders.  The Joint Proposal reflects compromises of the widely differing 
viewpoints of the numerous participants in the settlement process, including those of non-
signatory parties. Indeed, certain aspects of the Joint Proposal reflect the input of non-settling 
parties.  All participants had the opportunity to voice their concerns and a significant effort was 
made to address them.  
A driving factor in Con Edison’s consideration and support of the Joint Proposal was the 
benefit of multi-year rate plans, which provides customers rate stability and allows the Company 
to focus on operational improvement opportunities and implementation of the Commission’s 
clean energy initiatives.

The settlement process enabled parties to fully explore their respective positions and 
reach a compromise.  As with any compromise, individual provisions of the Joint Proposal may 
not be to the liking of one or more parties, and all parties, including Con Edison, made 
substantial and difficult concessions to reach this agreement.  For example, the Joint Proposal 
authorizes significantly lower revenues than Con Edison sought, particularly with respect to 
ROE and operation and maintenance costs, which create greater challenges in attracting capital 
and meeting its service obligations.  The Company notes in particular that it will face continuing 
challenges in earning its authorized ROE given the continuing (although reduced) risks related to 
property taxes and storm response.  The Joint Proposal also exposes Con Edison to new and 
increased negative revenue adjustments, most notably for its customer service performance 
metrics.  Given the wide range of complex issues presented and the inherent litigation risk, the 
Company ultimately concluded that the Joint Proposal was reasonable on an overall basis, 
accepting the many provisions that were a significant departure from the Company’s position as 
part of the give and take inherent in any compromise.  Any opposition to one or more elements 
of the Joint Proposal must be measured against the numerous compromises that were negotiated 
to reach an agreement.

VI. 
Conclusion 
The Joint Proposal complies with the Settlement Guidelines and is just, reasonable, and 
in the public interest.  In addition, the Joint Proposal will permit Con Edison to continue 
providing safe and reliable service, complies with the CLCPA, and will advance the State’s clean 
energy agenda.  The Commission should adopt the Joint Proposal without modification.    
The Joint Proposal is a reasonable comprise that reflects a balancing of interests.  The 
Commission’s Settlement Guidelines provides that “as a general proposition, settlement 
agreements will not be modified by the Commission without giving parties an opportunity to re-
negotiate or litigate the modified positions.”  The Guidelines further state that if a remand is not 
feasible, the parties will still be given a “reasonable opportunity” to comment any proposed 
modification.36 Accordingly, if the Commission decides not to approve the Joint Proposal in its 
entirety, without modification, the Commission should remand the Joint Proposal to the parties 
or otherwise provide an opportunity for comments.
