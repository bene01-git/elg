I.    INTRODUCTION  
AARP New York has 2.5 million members, more than seven hundred thousand of whom reside 
in the service territories of the Companies.  AARP members are 50 and older and many are 
elderly and thus more susceptible to the pandemic.  Others are on fixed incomes or work at 
businesses now virtually shuttered and already struggle to make ends meet.  Accordingly, AARP 
is supportive of any positions taken by any parties in these proceedings that would improve, or at 
least not impair, the lives of its members.  In particular, AARP agrees with the largely unrefuted 
testimony filed by the Public Utility Law Project of New York (PULP), the testimony of New 
York City (NYC) on KEDNY’s low-income program discounts, and the use by Department of 
Public Service (DPS) Staff of the Generic Financing Methodology for determining the cost of 
capital and rate of return.  
AARP supports these key recommendations of PULP: 
• Two percent cap on residential bill increases. 
• Allocation of 100% of all earnings greater than the rate of return on which 
KEDNY’s rates are based to reduce deferred Site Investigation and Remediation 
(SIR) costs or,  alternatively, in the absence of any “over earnings,” a requirement 
that KEDNY’s shareholders contribute to the payment of SIR costs (90% 
customers/10% shareholders). 
• Requirement that KEDLI take vigorous steps to enroll more customers in its energy 
assistance program (EAP) by working to achieve the same file match scope as 
KEDNY’s.  (KEDLI has failed to enroll more than two-thirds of its eligible 
customers; KEDNY has enrolled 80-90 percent of its eligible customers.)   
• Addition of one full-time equivalent (FTE) resource assigned to enrollment issues 
in KEDLI’s low-income program. 
• Accelerate the return to customers of excess unprotected Deferred Federal Income 
Taxes (EDFIT) such that all net benefits are returned during the rate plan resulting 
from these proceedings. 
• Oppose generous Earnings Adjustment Mechanisms (EAMs) because there is no 
need to reward utilities for fulfilling their obligations.

Objective consideration of the issues raised by the parties in these cases is clouded by the 
knowledge that the record’s factual and policy foundation is likely flawed, inaccurate, 
misleading and incomplete.  The current personal, social and economic impacts of the pandemic 
in the service territories of KEDNY and KEDLI (collectively, the Companies) cannot be 
overstated.  Mid-term economic projections are dire, with some economists predicting a 
downturn more severe than the 2007 recession.  Long-term projections are less certain because

   A.    OPERATIONS AND MAINTENANCE 
           2.  Customer Information System (CIS) Project: Enhancements  
AARP agrees with PULP and DPS Staff that replacing the Companies’ Legacy system warrants 
a separate petition filed with the Commission in the expectation that a full proceeding would 
better protect the interests of customers.  Tr. 1571, lines 11-24.  A separate proceeding would 
allow the parties to focus on designing a financially reasonable CIS upgrade that provides 
material benefits to customers.  In the event the Commission approves in these proceedings 
changes to the CIS system, AARP supports PULP’s proposal for the creation of a customer 
portal. A properly designed portal would make it easier for the customer to access billing and 
account statuses, thereby not only benefitting customers but also providing efficiencies for the 
Companies.   
      3.     Site Investigation and Remediation 
In its Order in Case 11-M-0034, the Commission stated that “sharing [of SIR costs] may be 
appropriate in specific cases, either to serve as an incentive where utilities have failed to 
adequately constrain SIR costs, or as part of multiyear rate plans incorporating excess earnings 
mechanisms.”  Tr. 3750, lines 13-19; Exhibit 506, page 12.  AARP agrees with PULP that Order 


--- Page 5 ---
 
4 
11-M-0034 affords the Commission the ability to tailor allocation of cost responsibility to suit 
specific circumstances.  Because costs associated with the cleanup of both the Gowanus Canal 
and Newtown Creek Superfund are expected to increase dramatically, AARP agrees with PULP 
that the Commission should allocate 100% of KEDNY’s earnings greater than the rate of return 
on which KEDNY’s rates are based to reduce deferred SIR costs or, alternatively, in the absence 
of any “over earnings,” should require KEDNY’s shareholders to contribute to the payment of 
the SIR costs. 
AARP supports a cost responsibility allocation of 10% shareholders and 90% ratepayers of net 
SIR costs (exclusive of insurance recoveries and third-party contributions), with continued 
review of expenses in future rate proceedings and full recovery of prudently incurred and 
supported expenses.  This is the optimal regulatory regime to encourage the expedient, cost-
effective remediation of manufactured gas sites. This modest level of shareholder responsibility 
provides heightened scrutiny on the part of management while minimizing the potential for a 
negative reaction from investors.  Sharing in this manner is, in effect, a proxy for the type of 
Commission oversight (for example, daily involvement) of SIR activities that is not possible or 
practical.  
In addition to requiring management to heighten its focus on SIR costs, AARP’s approach is 
equitable. Sharing between investors and ratepayers recognizes that few of today‘s captive 
ratepayers were alive when these sites operated and that current shareholders and society as a 
whole benefit from the legacy of economic development resulting from past manufactured gas 
operations. Further, all New Yorkers and utility investors benefit today from their remediation.

VI.    RATE BASE ISSUES 
A.  CAPITAL PROJECTS AND EXPENDITURES 
With the changes occurring in the social and economic environment, AARP requests that the 
ALJs closely examine the proposed capital projects and make recommendations as to whether 
any projects should be deferred or modified. AARP believes that the current economic 
challenges means all such spending should be limited to that which is absolutely essential.  
VII.   FINANCIAL ISSUES 
A. RETURN ON EQUITY 
The Commission and DPS Staff have used the Generic Financing Methodology to determine the 
appropriate return on equity (ROE) for the better part of three decades.  The Companies did not 
provide any compelling reason for the Commission to depart from its consistent practice.  In 
light of the recent onset of a deep recession and dramatic changes in the stock and bond markets, 
AARP urges the ALJs to include in their RD an updated ROE using the Generic Financing 
Methodology.  DPS Staff, for instance, mentioned forecasted inflation rates numerous times in 
its testimony (Tr. 1337; 3368-72; 3380; 3404; 3825; 3827).    
VIII.    REVENUE ALLOCATION AND RATE DESIGN 
 
C.   RATE DESIGN 
AARP supports lower customer charges to encourage conservation and energy efficiency, but 
more importantly to allow customers to control their energy bills.  AARP agrees with PULP that 
the Commission should direct DPS Staff and the Companies to facilitate an all-parties 
collaborative that would explore alternative rate designs.  This multi-party collaborative would 
study and prepare a report upon the advisability of having the Companies adopt alternative 
residential class rate designs, the purpose of which would be to achieve greater affordability for 
all average and lower usage customers, including low-income customers, through the 


--- Page 7 ---
 
6 
implementation of rate designs that reward energy conservation and efficiency.  Specifically, 
AARP urges a thorough review of the customer charges that are already too high. AARP 
supports customer charges which reflect metering and billing costs only. High customer charges 
make it more difficult for customers to control their bills.   
The collaborative should also address DPS Staff’s observation that high use customers may be in 
the wrong service class.  Tr. 3248, lines 2-16.   Average residential monthly usage is 118 therms; 
highest usage is 103,000 therms.  Tr. 1270; Exhibit 626.  Better understanding the type of 
customers in the class is important because of the disparate impact of different levels of customer 
charges on customers with widely different usage.   
 
D.    BILL IMPACTS 
AARP urges the ALJs and the Commission to keep in prominent view the cumulative impact on 
residential customers’ bills of all of the decisions in these proceedings.  Decision-makers often 
focus on delivery increases and ignore higher bills caused by various surcharges, reconciliations 
and adjustments including in these proceedings SIR costs, financial expenses relating to the self-
imposed moratorium on natural gas, and the Companies’ ability to fully achieve the proposed 
Earnings Adjustment Mechanisms (EAMs).  Moreover, as a recession grabs hold more tightly on 
the economies of the Companies’ service territories, we join PULP in requesting the Commission 
to limit bill impacts in total, on the Average Usage Customers, to no more than two percent 
(2.0%) per rate year or lower if possible.  As the Governor has indicated, the economy is on 
pause; now is not the time to be imposing rate increases 
IX.      GAS POLICY AND ENERGY EFFICIENCY 
 
M.   EARNINGS ADJUSTMENT MECHANISMS 
AARP opposes EAMs because, as DPS Staff testified, the PSC has the statutory authority to 
direct utilities to engage in activities related to the provision of service without bonus or 
incentive mechanisms. Tr. 623, lines 10-13.  Rewarding utilities financially is unnecessary and is 
an unreasonable use of ratepayer money.  If, nevertheless, EAMs are included in the rate order, 
AARP urges the ALJs and the Commission to rigorously examine efforts and outcomes of the 
Companies, other New York utilities and utilities throughout the countries to ensure that the 
EAM targets are not easily achievable.  Regarding the Companies’ proposed Affordability EAM,

XI.      CUSTOMER SERVICE 
B.  TERMINATIONS/UNCOLLECTIBLE INCENTIVE MECHANISM 
AARP supports PULP’s proposal to modify the terminations/uncollectible incentive mechanism 
to include an arrears component, updated positive revenue adjustments (PRAs) targets and new 
negative revenue adjustments (NRAs).  Both KEDNY and KEDLI have achieved a positive 
revenue adjustment for exceeding its respective targets in each of the years of current rate plans 
while also reducing the number of terminations well below the targets.  It is poor practice to 
spend ratepayer money by setting goals that are easily achievable.  KEDNY/KEDLI 
acknowledged that the data used in the calculation of the metric are more than a year old.  Tr. 
1267, lines 10-11 and 25.  AARP agrees with PULP that the targets should be updated as of the 
month prior to the month in which a rate order in these proceedings is issued to ensure the 
accuracy of the metrics. 
D.   COLLECTIONS 
1.  Deferred Payment Agreements: Implementation of electronic DPAs and 
reporting requirement; KEDNY requirement for customer physical appearance at 
customer service center to demonstrate need for more affordable DPA terms;  
AARP urges the ALJs to recommend to the Commission that it direct KEDNY to relieve its 
customers immediately of the burden of applying in person for what is termed an “affordable” 
deferred payment agreement (DPA).  Customers facing termination are first offered what is 
known as a “standard” DPA.  Tr. 1238, lines 3-6; 1239, lines 2-8. An “affordable” DPA option is 
discussed if a customer indicates that the standard DPA option is not affordable.  Tr. 1239, lines 
9-24.  Obtaining an affordable DPA is contingent on completion of a financial statement form.  
Tr. 1239, lines 19-25; 1240, lines 1-6.   
KEDNY and KEDLI use the exact same financial statement form but the completion process is 
different.  KEDLI customers have the option to complete the form over the phone but KEDNY

customers are required to complete the form in person.  Tr. 1240, lines 7-14; 1242, lines 6-9.  
This requirement unreasonably burdens KEDNY customers because KEDNY maintains only two

XII.      LOW INCOME PROGRAM 
A.   CALCULATING DISCOUNTS AND ELIGIBILITY 
AARP urges the ALJs to take to heart the decision of the Commission in Consolidated Edison’s 
recent rate case and recommend modifying the Companies’ low-income programs in the manner 
proposed by NYC in these proceedings.  Increasing discounts for existing participants and 
adding new low-income customers to the programs are important steps towards achieving the 
Commission’s six percent energy burden goal. 
E.   REQUEST FOR ADDITIONAL FULL-TIME EQUIVALENT EMPLOYEE 
FOR  ENERGY ASSISTANCE PROGRAM PARTICIPATION 
There is a critical need for a full-time equivalent (FTE) employee dedicated to increasing 
enrollment in KEDLI’s low-income program.  PULP’s testimony, which DPS Staff and the 
Companies failed to rebut, showed that KEDLI has not enrolled more than two-thirds of the 
customers eligible to participate in its Energy Assistance Program (EAP).  This is unacceptable. 
It is especially necessary during a recession for KEDLI to have sufficient staff dedicated to 
increasing program enrollment.  The Companies request to add an FTE is helpful, but the refusal 
to commit the FTE to working solely on KEDLI’s EAP should be rejected. 
F.   INCREASING EAP PARTICIPATION (KEDLI) 
It is disappointing that DPS Staff and the Companies showed little interest in or knowledge of 
the design features of the EAPs or gave any attention to PULP’s testimony on this issue.  See, for 
example, Tr. 434-46; 1273-79; 1584-88.  Neither the Companies nor DPS Staff challenged 
PULP’s estimates that only 20-30% of potentially eligible KEDLI households are enrolled, 
compared to 70-90% for KEDNY.  Tr. 1611, lines 19-23.  DPS Staff did not review KEDLI’s or 
KEDNY’s enrollment data while preparing its testimony.  Tr. 1584, lines 16-24; 1586, lines 6-9.

On re-direct (Tr. 1304-07), the Companies suggested that KEDNY’s participation rates are 
higher than KEDLI’s because it is easier to identify and enroll eligible customers in KEDNY’s 
service territory than in KEDLI’s service territory, but that no concrete steps have been taken to 
replicate KEDNY’s file matching process in KEDLI’s service territory.  On cross-examination 
DPS Staff did not express any concern about KEDLI’s participation level and offered no ideas 
about ways to increase participation.  DPS Staff did not consider making any recommendations 
regarding outreach and education to increase KEDLI’s participation level when developing its 
testimony.  Tr. 1587, lines 14-17; 1588, line 19.   
NYC’s Human Resources Administration periodically computer matches a list of KEDNY 
customers with lists of people receiving a panoply of benefits such as the Supplemental Nutrition 
Assistance Program (SNAP) and Medicaid.  This approach identifies a large percentage of 
eligible customers, who are then seamlessly enrolled in KEDNY’s EAP.  The county social 
service agencies in KEDLI’s service territories do not do this.  Instead, only HEAP recipients are 
identified and seamlessly enrolled.  Because the federal government provides sufficient funding 
to provide HEAP benefits to only about one-third of heating customers who meet income-
eligibility criteria, it is no surprise that KEDLI’s participation rate is so low.  Neither the 
Companies nor DPS Staff indicated they had concrete plans to work expeditiously with county 
social service agencies to implement a matching protocol like HRA’s.   
KEDLI’s participation level is unacceptable, even more so during a recession, and completely 
undermines the Commission’s six percent energy burden goal.  AARP urges the ALJs to 
recommend to the Commission that it direct the Companies and DPS Staff to work with the 
county social service agencies to implement a computer match process similar to the one used by 
KEDNY and HRA within the next six months. 
XIX.      CONCLUSION

elements of the record affected by the social and economic changes resulting from the COVID-
19 pandemic; (2) develop and establish a temporary rate plan pursuant to Section 114 of the 
Public Service Law, which would include (a) a total residential bill increase no greater than two 
percent or lower, (b) service charges and blocks consistent with energy conservation and 
efficiency as well as customer control of their bills, and (c) changes to the Companies’ low-
