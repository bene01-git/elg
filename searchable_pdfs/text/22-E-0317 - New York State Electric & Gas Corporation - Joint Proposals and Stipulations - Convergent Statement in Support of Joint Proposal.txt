PRELIMINARY STATEMENT 
 
Convergent Energy and Power, LP (“Convergent”) submits this statement in support of the 
Joint Proposal filed with the New York State Public Service Commission (“Commission”) on June 
14, 2023 in the above-captioned proceedings.1  Convergent respectfully urges the Commission to 
find that the proposed electric rate plans for New York State Electric & Gas Corporation 
(“NYSEG”) and Rochester Gas and Electric Corporation (“RG&E”; together, the “Companies”) 
set forth within the Joint Proposal satisfy the requirements of Public Service Law § 65(1) and 
should be adopted by the Commission.  Compared to the likely outcome had the cases been fully 
litigated, the Joint Proposal represents a better outcome for customers, especially related to the 
Companies’ plans for new energy storage projects.

PROCEDURAL HISTORY 
 
The Companies filed requests for new rates on May 26, 2022 (“Initial Filing”), to take 
effect on May 1, 2023.  On August 15, 2022, the Companies filed updates to the Initial Filing.  On 
September 26, 2022 and September 27, 2022, eleven parties submitted direct testimony in response 
to the Companies’ Initial Filing and updates.  The Companies and five other parties submitted 
rebuttal testimony on October 18, 2022. 
On October 19, 2022, the Companies filed a Notice of Impending Settlement Negotiations 
with the Secretary to the Commission.  On November 2, 2022, the active parties to these 
proceedings commenced discussions to determine if the differences in their respective positions 
could be overcome and if compromise rate plans could be negotiated.  After numerous negotiating 
sessions, many parties, including Convergent, were able to reach agreement on compromise 
positions, as reflected in the Joint Proposal.  On June 14, 2023, the Joint Proposal was filed with 
the Commission. 
STATEMENT IN SUPPORT 
 
THE JOINT PROPOSAL IS IN THE PUBLIC INTEREST 
AND SHOULD BE APPROVED 
 
 
A. 
The Joint Proposal Complies With The Commission’s Settlement Guidelines 
 
The Commission has established the following criteria for evaluating a joint proposal: (a) 
the extent to which a joint proposal is supported by adverse parties; (b) whether the record 
supporting the settlement is adequate; (c) whether the settlement is consistent with law and public 
policy; (d) whether there exists a rational basis for the recommendations to the Commission, (e) 
whether the settlement balances the interests of customers and shareholders; and (f) that the

settlement compares favorably with the probable outcome of litigation.2  The Commission should 
adopt the Joint Proposal because it satisfies these requirements.3 
First, the Companies provided all parties reasonable and sufficient notice of the settlement 
negotiations, as required by 16 NYCRR § 3.9(a).4  Throughout the settlement process, the 
Companies routinely provided notice of upcoming settlement meetings via e-mail to all active 
parties.  All meetings were held virtually, with some in-person options, and all parties had the 
opportunity to participate.  
Second, the Joint Proposal is supported by a diverse set of parties representing a broad 
range of interests.  As demonstrated by the pre-filed testimony filed by many of the signatories to 
the Joint Proposal,5 their positions in these proceedings clearly were adverse.  Specifically with 
respect to the issues important to Convergent, the initial position of the Companies to build all 
storage facilities directly and without any opportunity for competition was adverse to Convergent’s 
interests in these proceedings.

CONCLUSION 
For the reasons set forth herein, the Commission should find that the Joint Proposal was 
developed consistent with the Settlement Guidelines.  The record in these proceedings provides a 
rational basis for the Commission to find that the terms and conditions of the Joint Proposal related 
to the Companies’ electric businesses are in the public interest, produce just and reasonable rates, 
and provide for safe and adequate service.  Based on these findings, the Commission should adopt 
these provisions of the Joint Proposal without conditions or modifications.
