Q.    
Does the Company agree with Staff’s rationale for denying the Company’s 
1 
deferral requests? 
2 
A. 
No.  Staff fails to recognize the important distinction between the reactive 
3 
filing of a deferral petition seeking recovery of costs in excess of amounts 
4 
provided for in delivery rates and the prospective request to provide 
5 
deferral authority on a going-forward basis for costs that are expected to 
6 
be of an uncertain and/or volatile nature.  The language within the USoA 
7 
quoted by Staff pertains to when the Company has already incurred costs 
8 
and files a deferral petition requesting authority to defer and seeking 
9 
recovery of costs beyond what was previously provided for in a rate 
10 
proceeding.  However, this should not be confused with requests to 
11 
establish deferral authority during the course of a rate proceeding, where it 
12 
is prudent for the Company and Staff to consider protections for both the 
13 
customers and the Company for certain items whose outcomes have a 
14 
high degree of uncertainty and/or complexity, or whose timing and costs 
15 
are outside of the Company’s control.  Deferral authorization in a rate case 
16 
is prudent and reasonable particularly in situations where an item is the 
17 
subject of a pending proceeding or when, based on current facts and 
18 
circumstances or historical experience, an activity that will impact future 
19 
cost of service is likely to occur, but those future costs are uncertain.
