LPP REPLACEMENT IN THE FLOODPLAINS 
1 
Q. 
WHAT IS STAFF’S POSITION ON LPP REPLACEMENT? 
2 
A. 
The SSP supports a more aggressive LPP replacement program and recommends 
3 
minimum removal targets of 55, 60 and 65 miles for KEDNY in 2017-2019, and 115, 135 
4 
and 155 miles for KEDLI during those same years (SSP, p. 19).  Staff also recommends 
5 
that the Companies rely on their risk assessment models to prioritize areas where LPP is 
6 
most at risk (SSP, pp. 21-22). 
7 
Q. 
IS THE STAFF RECOMMENDATION ACCEPTABLE? 
8 
A. 
Not entirely.  While we agree with the annual minimum targets for LPP removal 
9 
and agree that the risk assessment model should be utilized, the SSP testimony does not 
10 
even mention the additional risk of LPP in floodplains due to WOF (weather-related and 
11 
outside forces) factors.  As we stated in our direct testimony, LPP in floodplains presents 
12 
an additional risk of failure and associated dangers (KEDNY Direct, pp. 19-22; KEDLI 
13 
Direct, pp. 21-22).  For example, 45 miles of the 55 miles of LPP located in the KEDNY 
14 
flood zone is cast iron or non-protected steel that operates at low pressure, which makes it 
15 
very vulnerable to water intrusion during flooding.  Because of this additional risk, the 
16 
removal of LPP in current and future floodplains should be expedited.   
17 
Q. 
PLEASE CONTINUE. 
18 
A. 
By accepting the Companies’ LPP proposals, Staff implicitly accepts their 20 year 
19 
plan for LPP replacement in floodplains.  We disagree with that proposition.  As we 
20 
explained in our direct testimony, the Companies’ replacement model does not adequately

2 
Q. 
WHAT ARE THE CITY’S CONCERNS? 
3 
A. 
First, the City respects the SGRP’s goal of reducing customer bills.  However, it is 
4 
concerned with developing a program that would move customers away from individual 
5 
meters and separate bills, which provide important usage information that encourages 
6 
conservation.  Indeed, the program suggested by the SGRP is likely to discourage 
7 
conservation at a time when the Commission is trying to encourage energy efficiency.  We 
8 
believe that before any such program is adopted, the negative impact on energy 
9 
conservation must be addressed. 
10 
Q. 
DO YOU HAVE ANY OTHER CONCERNS? 
11 
A. 
Yes.  The SGRP proposal would shift the responsibility for gas billing to the 
12 
landlord, and the utility and the Commission would lose control over how these gas 
13 
customers are billed for gas usage.  Thus, in addition to discouraging conservation, the 
14 
SGRP proposal opens the door to potential overbilling or even gouging of gas customers 
15 
by landlords.  Again, before any program is adopted, the potential for overbilling for gas 
16 
usage  by unscrupulous building owners should be resolved to the Commission’s 
17 
satisfaction.
