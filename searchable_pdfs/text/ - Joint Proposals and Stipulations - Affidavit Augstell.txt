Upon information and belief, the Brooklyn Union Gas

6. 
The Joint Proposal contains some modifications to 
KEDNY’s cost of capital provisions to align such provisions

changes do not result in any material change in base delivery

7. 
Upon information and belief, these changes would not 
have been made at this time absent the Joint Proposal in that 
the Company had not filed for new rates or policies to go into 
effect on January 1, 2013. 
Rate of Return 
8. 
A utility's overall rate of return represents the 
weighted cost of capital used by the utility to finance the 
assets that provide regulated utility service to customers.  The 
determination of a utility’s rate of return usually requires an 
estimate of the company’s projected capital structure and the 


--- Page 3 ---
cost rates associated with each component.  In a traditional 
ratemaking process, the regulatory body strives to provide a 
utility with a reasonable opportunity to earn a return on 
utility investments comparable to the return available on 
investments of similar risk. 
9. 
In this matter, we know KEDNY’s capital structure as 
it would exist had the Company not entered into the Joint 
Proposal.  KEDNY would have continued at a 45.0 percent equity 
ratio (55.0 percent debt), and an allowed return on equity (ROE) 
of 9.8%. 
Return On Equity 
10. 
 The first modification reduces KEDNY’s allowed return 
on equity (ROE) from 9.8 percent to 9.4 percent, or by 40 basis 
points.  Upon information and belief, 10 basis points for KEDNY 
is equivalent to approximately $1.7 million.  Accordingly, a 40 
basis point decrease in the allowed ROE is equal to 
approximately a $6.8 million reduction in the Company’s earnings 
to meet their allowed ROE. 
11. 
The Joint Proposal’s allowed ROE of 9.4 percent aligns 
favorably with recent Commission decisions establishing 
acceptable ROE’s for New York utilities.  The 9.4 percent 
allowed ROE fits squarely within the range established by the 
Commission’s June 2012 Order in the Orange and Rockland rate 
case, Case 11-E-0408 which tiers the ROE at 9.4 percent through 
9.6 percent over a three year period, and the expected March 
2013 Commission decision that would adopt the Niagara Mohawk 
rate case joint proposal of 9.3 percent over three years.      
12. 
Based on my experience, a 9.4 percent ROE will allow 
KEDNY to maintain an acceptable bond rating allowing it to 
borrow capital at reasonable rates.

Common Equity Ratio 
13. 
The second modification to KEDNY’s cost of capital 
provisions is an increase to its common equity ratio component 
from 45 percent to 48 percent.  This increase to 48 percent 
aligns KEDNY’s common equity ration with that of other New York 
utilities that have had rate plans recently adopted by the 
Commission.   
14. 
The increase in the common equity ratio will reduce 
the amount of debt held by the company.  Reducing the amount of 
KEDNY’s debt reduces its risk, but requires it to have more 
capital on hand. 
15. 
When the above-described changes are combined, the 
result is effectively a net zero change in revenue requirement.  
Accordingly, the Joint Proposal accomplishes aligning KEDNY’s 
capital structure in two important respects with the 
Commission’s most recent rate Orders, while requiring no change 
to base revenues.  
16. 
This net zero effect is important in light of the 
other changes made in the Joint Proposal that allow the company 
to continue to finance its capital expenditures and operations, 
but also align certain provisions of KEDNY’s rate plan with more 
contemporaneous Commission decisions given that the company has 
not filed for new base rates when its 2007 rate plan expired. 
17. 
Based on my experience, I believe that the Joint 
Proposal taken as a whole provides KEDNY with a reasonable 
opportunity to earn a fair return on its investment without 
harming consumers by minimizing the ability of the company to 
over-earn at the expense of its ratepayers.

Earnings Sharing 
18. 
In addition to reducing the percentage of allowed ROE, 
the Joint Proposal pro~ides for an_Earnings Sharing Mechanism 
that returns a portion of any KEDNY overearnings to customers 
for use against KENDY's site investigation and remediation costs 
deferral balance. 
19. 
The earnings sharing mechanism has no "deadband" 
associated with it such that 80 percent of all earnings received' 
by the company that exceed its allowed ROE will be shared with 
customers. 
20. 
This provision protects customers from any 
unanticipated revenue windfall by ensuring that to the extent 
KEDNY records earnings in excess of projections made by Staff 
after adjusting the provisions of the existing rate plan for 
rate years six and seven, customers will benefit in a clear and 
defined manner. 
21. 
Finally, should KEDNY stay out longer than the term of 
this extension and its associated adjustments intended to reduce 
the r~cent historical levels of KEDNY's overearnings, the 
earnings sharing mechanism provides that 100 percent of KEDNY's 
earnings exceeding its allowed ROE will be returned to customers 
by reducing KENDY's site investigation and remediation costs 
deferral balance.
