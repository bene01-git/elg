I. 
INTRODUCTION 
 
 
On May 14, 2021, The Brooklyn Union Gas Company d/b/a National Grid NY 
(KEDNY) and KeySpan Gas East Corporation d/b/a National Grid (KEDLI) (collectively, the 
Companies); Staff of the New York State Department of Public Service (Staff); Environmental 
Defense Fund (EDF); Estates NY Real Estate Services LLC (Estates); NY-Geo; Bob Wyman; 
and the Long Island Power Authority (LIPA), collectively the “Signatory Parties,” submitted a 
Joint Proposal recommending a comprehensive resolution of all issues raised in the above-
captioned proceedings.  By this Statement in Support of the Joint Proposal (Initial Statement), 
Staff recommends that the Commission adopt the provisions of the Joint Proposal and establish 
three-year gas rate plans for KEDNY and KEDLI to begin as of April 1, 2020.1 
II.

March 31, 2021, referred to in the context of the proposed three-year rate plans as “RY1.”  In 
May 2019, the New York State Department of Environmental Conservation (DEC) denied

III. 
OVERVIEW OF THE JOINT PROPOSAL 
 
 
As discussed in greater detail below, the Joint Proposal contains a number of 
provisions designed to provide for just and reasonable rates, particularly in light of the impacts 
the COVID-19 pandemic has had on ratepayers; maintain and improve KEDNY and KEDLI’s 
ability to provide safe and adequate service; and to the extent feasible and appropriate, provide a 
resolution to other issues raised by intervenors.  Section I of the Joint Proposal provides the 
procedural background for the proceedings addressed in the Joint Proposal.  Section II provides 
the overall framework for the Joint Proposal.  Section III provides definitions, including for the 
rate years: April 1, 2020 through March 31, 2021 (RY1); April 1, 2021 through March 31, 2022 
(RY2); and April 1, 2022 through March 31, 2023 (RY3) (collectively, Rate Years).  Section III 
also defines “Stayout Period” as the period beginning April 1, 2023 and ending on the earlier of 
the effective date of revised base rates for KEDNY and KEDLI or March 31, 2024.  Sections IV 
presents the rate plans for KEDNY and KEDLI.  Section V sets forth some additional provisions 
governing the relationship between the Signatory Parties. 
 
 
Turning to the provisions of Section IV, Section 1 sets forth the three-year term of 
the proposed rate plan.  Section 2 identifies the recommended annual revenue requirement and 
overall revenue changes.  Note that, as discussed above, the overall revenue changes for RY1 
include the change in revenue requirement, plus the impacts associated with the elimination of 
the existing tax savings sur-credit, the energy efficiency surcharge and impact of change in GRT.  
The net impacts associated with the elimination of the sur-credit, surcharge, and change in GRT 
results in a RY1 revenue increase of $22.7 million for KEDNY and $20.3 million for KEDLI. 
 
 
For KEDNY, the Joint Proposal recommends a revenue requirement decrease of 
$4.710 million in RY1, and revenue requirement increases of $47.019 million and $73.272

IV. 
THE JOINT PROPOSAL IS IN THE PUBLIC INTEREST 
1. 
Benefits of the Joint Proposal 
 
 
The Joint Proposal is in the public interest and should be adopted by the 
Commission without modification.  Adoption of the multi-year rate plans set forth in the Joint 
Proposal provides a number of benefits that would not be feasible if the Commission adopted a 
one year rate order based on the litigation record in these proceedings.  This Initial Statement 
explains how the Joint Proposal comports with the Commission’s “Settlement Guidelines” and 
then addresses the individual elements of the Joint Proposal.  Below is an overview of six of the 
key benefits that are possible in these cases only through adoption of the Joint Proposal. 
 
 
First, RY1 revenues are held flat, meaning that customer’s bills will not be 
impacted for RY1.  Indeed, the Joint Proposal’s RY1 revenue requirements are decreases of 
$4.710 million for KEDNY and $22.839 million for KEDLI.  As explained in footnote 7, above, 
the overall revenue changes differ from the revenue requirement changes in RY1 due to the

applying available deferral credits to offset the revenue requirement increases that are necessary 
to enable KEDNY and KEDLI to continue to provide safe and adequate service to their 
approximately 1.9 million customers in Staten Island, Brooklyn, Queens, and on Long Island.

2. 
Standard of Review 
 
 
The Commission’s Settlement Guidelines state that all decisions, including those 
to adopt the terms of settlement agreements (joint proposals) must be just and reasonable and in 
the public interest.28  In addition to compliance with proper procedures, determining whether the 
terms of a joint proposal are in the public interest involves substantive consideration of the 
following: 
1. consistency with the law and regulatory economic, social and environmental 
State and Commission policies; 
2. whether the terms of the joint proposal compare favorably with the likely result 
of a fully litigated case and produce a result within the range of reasonable 
outcomes; 
3. whether the joint proposal fairly balances the interests of ratepayers, investors 
and the long-term soundness of the utility; and 
4. whether the joint proposal provides a rational basis for the Commission’s 
decision.29

3. 
The Joint Proposal Comports with the Commission’s Settlement Guidelines 
 
 
The Joint Proposal entered into in these cases resolves all outstanding issues 
presented in testimony and settlement negotiations.  In doing so, it fully comports with the 
Commission’s Settlement Guidelines.  The fact that KEDNY, KEDLI, Staff, EDF, Estates, NY-
GEO, Bob Wyman, and LIPA have executed the Joint Proposal is a testament to the extensive 
efforts employed by the Signatory Parties to address key issues and the equitable resolution, 
comprehensiveness and reasonableness of the Joint Proposal’s provisions.30  Given the various 
interests of the specific parties involved in the negotiations, it is clear that the Joint Proposal is an 
agreement reached between normally adversarial parties. 
3.1 
The Joint Proposal is Consistent with State Policies 
 
 
The Joint Proposal recommends outcomes that are consistent with the State’s and 
Commission’s policies.  The recommended outcomes will continue KEDNY and KEDLI’s 
efforts to retire LPP in their service territories, enhancing the safety of the communities served 
by the Companies.  Additionally, the rate mitigation recommended in the Joint Proposal helps to 
shield customers from significant rate increases, particularly during RY1, RY2, and RY3, while 
the economy in the Companies’ service territories continues to recover from the impacts of the 
COVID-19 pandemic.  Regarding environmental matters, the Joint Proposal is consistent with 
the Commission’s energy efficiency policies as set forth in the 2020 NE:NY Order, and also with 
the CLCPA. 
 
 
The adoption of the Joint Proposal is consistent with the CLCPA’s intent to 
reduce GHG emissions.  The Joint Proposal advances energy efficiency programs, which help 
end-users of natural gas use it more efficiently, thus emitting less carbon dioxide than they 
otherwise would.  Additionally, the Joint Proposal continues efforts to repair pipeline leaks and 
retire segments of LPP.  These efforts help to decrease the amount of methane released from the 
Companies’ distribution systems. 
 
 
While the Joint Proposal does provide a mechanism for recovery of the Long-
Term Capital Capacity Project costs, that mechanism requires an additional demonstration that

3.3 
The Joint Proposal Appropriately Balances the Interests of 
Stakeholders 
 
 
The variety of interests represented by the Signatory Parties demonstrates that the 
Joint Proposal appropriately balances the interests of stakeholders.  Signatories include, but are 
not limited to: EDF, which works to advance environmentally beneficial policies; Estates, which 
owns large multi-family residential properties served by the Companies; and NY-GEO, which

pandemic.  As a result of the onset of the pandemic and other impacts, the cases have continued 
for significantly longer than the traditional 11-month period.  These cases have presented 
numerous complex and changing circumstances.  While Staff does not purport that this, or any, 
Joint Proposal is perfect, this Joint Proposal sets forth a reasonable resolution of the issues 
presented.  Further, in Staff’s view, it balances the competing interests of the parties to these 
cases, and provides an outcome far superior to any that litigation could produce, particularly at 
this phase of these proceedings. 
3.4 
The Commission has a Rational Basis on which to Adopt the Terms of 
the Joint Proposal 
 
 
In these cases, the ALJs have already held a multi-day evidentiary hearing 
regarding parties’ litigation positions.  Following that hearing, parties have submitted initial and 
reply briefs.  Thus, these cases present the Commission with a practically complete litigation 
record.  The Joint Proposal provides for the resolution of all of the issues raised in these cases in 
a reasonable manner, reflecting parties’ positions in litigation and compromises between those 
positions.  All parties to these cases had the opportunity to participate in the settlement 
negotiations and many parties actively participated in the settlement negotiations process.  
Moreover, the ALJs have scheduled an evidentiary hearing regarding this Joint Proposal to begin 
on June 28, 2021.  At that evidentiary hearing, parties will once again have the opportunity to 
supplement the evidentiary record in these proceedings.

Commission has established, pursuant to the PSL, for judging the reasonableness of settlements, 
namely that safe and adequate service be provided at just and reasonable rates.  Furthermore, the

4.3 
Earnings Sharing Mechanism 
 
 
The Joint Proposal proposes Earnings Sharing Mechanisms (ESM) as described 
below.  In order to both incentivize the Companies to find and employ efficiencies in their 
business operations and to protect customers if the Companies’ earnings are greater than 
allowed, the proposed ESM allows customers and shareholders to share earnings attributable to 
an achieved ROE in excess of 9.30%.  Under the ESM, the actual earned ROE will be calculated 
on an annual basis for each Rate Year of the rate plan commencing April 1, 2020.  Earnings for 
each Rate Year will be measured individually, and any overearnings that are owed to customers 
will be placed in a deferred credit account for future disposition to be determined by the 
Commission.  In the event of a partial or “stub” period, the earnings sharing threshold of greater 
than 9.30% will be prorated to develop a stub period earnings sharing threshold.  The stub period 
earnings will be calculated by adjusting the actual average rate base for that period by an 
operating income ratio factor.  The operating income ratio factor will be calculated as the ratio of 
operating income during the same partial period in the previous Rate Year to the total operating 
income for that Rate Year.  The operating income ratio factor adjustment is necessary and 
reasonable because it addresses the impact of seasonality of the Companies’ sales given that their 
earnings could be depressed in the stub period which is between the end of the Rate Plan 
(March 31, 2023) and the beginning of the rate year under a new rate plan. 
 
 
The common equity ratio to be used in the calculation of the achieved ROE will 
be the 48% used to develop the cost of capital in the Joint Proposal.  To avoid any potential 
disagreement regarding the earnings sharing computation, the Joint Proposal also provides that 
the calculation of individual ROEs shall exclude the effects of discrete incentives, amounts 
previously in excess of earnings threshold, and negative and positive revenue adjustments.  An 
example of the calculation for the ESM is shown in Appendix 5 to the Joint Proposal.

“deadband” (first band) above the 8.80% allowed ROE (from 8.80% to 9.30% earned ROE) 
within which additional earnings will be retained by the Companies.  The 50 basis point 
deadband is reasonable given that it is within the range of other ESM deadbands approved in 
recent cases, and is designed to give the Companies every incentive to cut costs and retain all of 
the initial savings.  Examples of other recent ESMs include the 2018 Niagara Mohawk Rate

12. 
Filing for New Rates 
12.1 
During the Term of the Rate Plans 
 
 
The Companies commit to not file for new base delivery rates to be effective prior 
to April 1, 2023.  This provision includes a number of standard exceptions, which allow for 
consideration of modifications that result in de minimis impact on rates, to address new services 
or, should the Commission determine that unforeseen circumstances have had a substantial 
impact on the Companies’ rates of return so as to render the return unreasonable, unnecessary or 
inadequate for the provision of safe and adequate service.  This provision is a standard provision 
of rate proceeding joint proposals, is reasonable and should be adopted.

the conclusion of RY3.  These mechanisms cease on March 31, 2024 or the date new rates 
become effective, whichever is sooner.  Additionally, the Companies cannot avail themselves of
