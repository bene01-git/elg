DISCUSSION  
1. Preliminary Procedural Issue 
PULP moves to “deny” Central Hudson’s rate filings 
made in these cases, i.e., a final action on the merits.  Motion 
practice before the Commission is governed by the Commission’s 
procedural rules contained in 16 NYCRR Chapter 1, Subchapter A.  
Under 16 NYCRR §3.6 “Motion Practice,” where, as here, a 
presiding officer has been assigned and a recommended decision 
has not yet been issued, motions are required to be addressed to 
the presiding officer(s).  Although PULP filed its motion before 
the assigned Judges, as required by 16 NYCRR §3.6, PULP moves 
for a denial of the Company’s filed rate matters, i.e., final 
action on the merits.  Only the Commission is empowered to take 
such action.12  Requiring the Judges to devote time and effort on 
the motion, which is beyond the Judges’ legal authority to 
grant, is unwarranted.  In addition, the Commission’s 
consideration of this motion at this juncture eliminates the 
need for the parties to divert their resources to any 
interlocutory review of such a ruling by the Judges.  Moreover, 
the Commission is issuing an Order on PULP’s motion today, as

3. Alternative Request for a Hearing 
The PULP Motion also seeks alternative relief -- a 
hearing on the Company’s rate request.  In support of this 
particular request, PULP does cite the Appellate Division’s New 
York Tel. Co. ruling.17  With this alternative request, PULP 
essentially asks to apply the rate case process with due rigor 
and expediency.  Such a request is unnecessary.  We agree and 
observe that while PULP’s sentiment summarizes the Commission’s 
expectations for the rate case process outlined by PSL §66(12) 
and 16 NYCRR, the Judges in the Office of Hearings and Dispute 
Resolution are best positioned and qualified to ensure a fair, 
open, and expedient process.   
As noted, the primary argument made by PULP in support 
of its motion relates to the Department’s ongoing proceedings 
related to the Company’s billing system, which PULP contends 
creates an inference that the cost and other supporting data 
provided by the Company in support of its tariff filings is 
unreliable.     
Although PULP’s motion requests relief that appears to 
assume the unreliability of Central Hudson’s data, it also 
requests a hearing for the sole purpose of establishing such 
assertion.  PULP then posits that, after such hearing, its 
ultimate requested relief, denial of the requested increases, be

6. Settlement Discussions 
Lastly, we address PULP’s apparent understanding 
concerning the Commission’s role regarding settlement 
discussions.  PULP requests that the Commission order the 
parties to pursue their respective positions through a fully 
litigated evidentiary hearing, rather than through settlement 
negotiations.  We decline this request.  Not only would granting 
such relief be unprecedented, it would also serve as an 
unwarranted intrusion into a process that is left to the parties 
as explained in the Commission’s Opinion, Order and Resolution 
Adopting Settlement Procedures and Guidelines (Settlement 
Guidelines Order).24 
As stated in the Settlement Guidelines Order, “In the 
late 1970s and early 1980s, active parties in various Commission 
proceedings began to seek ways to avoid unnecessary litigation 
and save resources by agreeing on mutually acceptable 
resolutions of some or all of the contested issues in the
