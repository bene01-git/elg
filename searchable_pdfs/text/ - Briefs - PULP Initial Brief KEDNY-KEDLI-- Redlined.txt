I. 
INTRODUCTION 
 
The Public Utility Law Project, Inc.  (“PULP”) is a nonprofit organization incorporated 
in 1982 to promote and defend the rights and interest of low- and fixed-income utility 
consumers in matters affecting affordability, consumer protection and universal service. 
PULP’s mission includes educating the public, regulators, and elected officials about the 
impacts of utility rates; conducting research on the rights and energy, housing, and water 
burdens borne by utility consumers; and advocating with an emphasis on the rights and needs 
of low-income utility consumers. Consequently, PULP’s testimony in this case has been 
focused on providing examples of how the Brooklyn Union Gas Company d/b/a National Grid 
NY (“KEDNY”) and KeySpan Gas East Corporation d/b/a National Grid (“KEDLI”) 
(collectively, the “Companies”), the Department of Public Service (“DPS”), and the Public 
Service Commission (“PSC” or “Commission”) could ameliorate the difficulties many of the 
Company’s low- and moderate-income customers are having paying their bills.  PULP also 
raised questions and concerns with proposed actions by the Companies, including site and 
remediation costs as well as costs associated with the moratorium that in our opinion, would 
create harmful bill impacts that have not properly been considered during this proceeding. 
PULP’s initial testimony, filed on August 31, 2019, included several recommendations 
including modifications to customer service programs and operations that would better assist 
customers while providing efficiencies for the Companies.  PULP also made recommendations 
regarding alternate rate designs that would reverse regressive impacts upon low-income and 
low-usage (i.e.: “green”) households and would incentivize excessive energy users to conserve.  
To best serve the public’s interest, PULP urges the Administrative Law Judges (“ALJs”) to 
recommend that the Commission adopt the following conditions: 
• A commitment by the Companies to limit the total Bill Impacts for average 
usage customers to no more than two percent (2.0%) per rate year; 
• A commitment by the Companies to refresh the underlying forecasts and 
calculations in the record before a Recommended Decision (“RD”) is issued due 
to the economic hardship created by the “novel coronavirus” (“CV-19). 
Alternately, if Your Honors do not require this and other portions of the record 
to be refreshed before an RD is issued, PULP respectfully requests that the 
least-cost alternative affecting residential and small business ratepayers

II. 
PROCEDURAL BACKGROUND 
 
On April 30, 2019, KEDNY and KEDLI filed amendments to their gas tariff schedules 
with the Commission, seeking an increase to revenues by $236.8 million (19.3%) and $49.4 
million (6%), respectively, for the twelve months ending March 31, 2021 (“Rate Year”).  A 
procedural and technical conference was held on May 13, 2019 in Albany, New York.  On 
June 11, 2019, the Companies filed their first set of supplemental testimonies discussing the 
revenue requirement and other impacts to the Companies’ rate filings in the event the 
Transcontinental Gas Pipe Line LLC’s Northeast Supply Enhancement (“NESE”) project was 
not available during the proposed rate plan period(s).     
Public statement hearings were held for KEDNY: Wednesday, July 31, 2019 at City 
University of New York Law School in Long Island City, New York and Brooklyn Borough 
Hall, in Brooklyn New York; Wednesday, August 21, 2019 at St. George Library Center in 
Staten Island, New York.  For KEDLI, public statement hearings were held on Wednesday, 
July 31, 2019 at Nassau County Legislative Chambers in Mineola, New York and the Suffolk 
County Legislature in Smithtown, New York.  A hearing was also held on Thursday, August 1, 
2019 at the Suffolk County Legislature in Riverhead, New York.

III. 
SUMMARY OF REVENUE REQUIREMENT AND RATE DRIVERS 
PULP generally supports Staff’s identifications of the major rate drivers in these cases, 
and their imputation of such drivers in the revenue requirements.  In an non-exhaustive list, 
PULP notes the following proposed investments and non-investment expenditures: the 
Companies are continuing to invest in resilience and survivability of their networks, and gas 
safety generally, including leak prone pipe replacement; KEDNY proposed spending on a new 
Customer Service System (“CSS”) and information technology (“IT”) expenses generally; 
replacing aging pipeline and work on KEDNY’s Metropolitan Reliability Infrastructure (“MRI”) 
project; and a series of pilot and “Future of Heat” (“FOH”) programs. Concerning the non-
investment spending, both KEDNY and KEDLI’s local tax burdens have increased, with 
KEDNY’s increases and cost of construction in New York City both skyrocketing. 
All of these added up to initial proposed rate increases of just short of $300 million, a 
number that was altered during the Companies’ two supplemental/refresh testimonies required 
by the ALJs.  PULP notes here that it believes that these numbers are “stale” and need to be 
refreshed through a complete resubmission of KEDNY and KEDLI’s calculations, base statistics, 
forecasts and other elements making up all of the drivers of their revenue requirement.  That 
said, as noted above, PULP generally supports Staff’s identification of major rate drivers, 
although we question the need for any of the Companies’ pilot and Future of Heat expenditures 
during the current public health and economic crisis.  Instead, PULP believes the Companies 
should be required to present “austerity” rate plans such as those imposed by the Commission 
during the Great Recession in an attempt to avert a deeper unaffordability, shutoff and arrears 
crisis.  Austerity rate plans are discussed in greater detail in the following section.

IV. 
REVENUES 
A. Gas Sales Forecast  
 
PULP asserts herein that the forecasts of gas sales for the proposed one-year rate plan 
cannot be relied upon due to a number of factors, chief among which is Governor Cuomo’s 
“New York on PAUSE” Executive Order, which required 100% of non-essential nonprofit, 
private sector and public sector personnel to work from home and maintain “social distancing” to 
stop the spread of CV-19. This policy has resulted in a significant transfer of electric and gas 
usage from commercial and industrial customers to residential customers (including small and 
large MDUs, small and large owner-occupied structures, and religious/nonprofit customers), and 
the reasonably complete cessation of use by small and large commercial “non-essential” 
customer premises. At this time the outcome(s) of the inter- and intra-class transfers resulting 
from said policy are neither represented nor forecasted in the record of this proceeding, and thus 
PULP respectfully requests that the underlying forecasts and calculations be refreshed in the 
record before an RD be issued.  
Alternately, if Your Honors do not require this and other portions of the record to be 
refreshed before an RD is issued, PULP respectfully requests that the least-cost alternative 
affecting residential and small business ratepayers proposed by Staff or other active parties be 
given strong consideration as a temporary (i.e., one year) measure to limit harmful bill impacts 
during this public health emergency period and the Statewide and Industry-wide effects of CV-
19 and State “shelter at home” policies. While the current public health crisis and its effects upon 
utilities are reasonably unprecedented in the 20th and 21st centuries, the economic impacts are 
similar to those felt only approximately one decade ago during the Great Recession of 2008-
2010, at which time the Commission took action to address a severe economic downturn and 
mass unemployment through imposition of “austerity rate plans” upon the utilities.  See, Case 
08-3-0539, Con Edison Electric Rates, Austerity Plan, May 26, 2009, where the Commission 
imposed an austerity adjustment in the electric rate case; see also, Case 09-M-0435, Proceeding 
on Motion of the Commission Regarding the Development of Utility Austerity Programs- Notice 
Requiring the Filing of Utility Austerity Plans, issued May 15, 2009, where the Commission 
directed each electric and gas utility classified as a Class A utility under the Commission’s 


--- Page 12 ---

Uniform System of Accounts, to provide a report within 30 days detailing the utility’s plans for 
achieving austerity.  PULP believes that the issuance of “austerity rate plans” is an appropriate

respectfully urge the ALJs to order KEDNY/KEDLI to submit austerity plans to the Commission 
within 30-days of the issuance of the RD.

investment are deferred so there will be no bill impacts until New York’s economy and the 
Companies’ ratepayers have recovered from the effects of the conjoined public health and 
economic crises arising from the CV-19 pandemic.  PULP additionally respectfully requests that 
the underlying forecasts and calculations imputed to the Newtown Creek project be refreshed in 
the record before an RD be issued.  EHT, Volume 2, page 666, lines 9-22.

3. Site Investigation and Remediation 
 
It is indisputable that the cleanup of both the Gowanus Canal and Newtown Creek 
superfund sites will be extremely expensive.  Similarly, it is indisputable that the cleanups are 
necessary and should occur in an expeditious manner.  However, PULP argues that the issues of 
“who” should be paying the site investigation and remediation (“SIR”) costs and “how much” 
they should be paying are not appropriately addressed in this proceeding.   
During the Evidentiary Hearing, DPS staff acknowledged that they were familiar with the 
Commission’s Order in 11-M-0034 (“Generic SIR Order”), issued on November 28, 2012, 
indicating that “sharing may be appropriate in specific cases, either to serve as an incentive 
where utilities have failed to adequately constrain SIR costs, or as part of multiyear rate plans 
incorporating excess earnings mechanisms.” EHT, Volume 7, page 3750, lines 13-19; 11-M-
0034 page 12.  The Generic SIR Order 11-M-0034 is very clear that the Commission chose not 
to adopt a specific policy for handling SIR costs for the purpose of providing DPS staff with 
leniency and room to recommend alternatives.  Generic SIR Order at 2. PULP believes that the 
factors discussed below give rise for the Commission to find that an alternative SIR approach to 


--- Page 17 ---
 
 
 
- 17 -
allocation of cleanup costs is warranted in this rate case, and that shareholders should be assessed 
a fair share of the cleanup. 
In the previous rate case, KEDNY proposed a 2% surcharge to collect, over time, the 
amount of funds it asserts it will need to cover investigation, remediation, and legal costs 
associated with the SIRs.   EHT, Volume 7, page 3460, lines 6-16.  In PULP’s Initial Testimony 
we used the Companies’ annual reports to show that KEDNY’s estimated environmental 
liabilities associated with clean-up costs have been skyrocketing since 2009.  Specifically, the 
Company’s necessary environmental estimated reserves almost quadrupled between 2009 and 
2018 from $371 million to $1.212 billion.  At the same time, the amount the Company plans to 
recover from rate payers increased from $539 million to $1.485 billion.   EHT, Volume 4, page 
1626, lines 15-19; page 1627, lines 1-18.  Moreover, KEDNY’s past SIR spend on an annual 
basis for 2016 and 2017 alone shows $6,971,628 (2016) and $19,747,168 (2017) for the 
Gowanus Canal and $8,235,029 (2016) and $8,959,038 (2017) for the Newtown Creek 
Superfund site. See, Exhibit 179, page 1 of 1.   
Those figures alone should raise the Commission’s concern. The Generic SIR Order 
specifically recognizes that “sharing as an incentive in specific cases may be a useful tool to 
ensure utility attention to cost controls.”  See, 11-M-0034, page 21. In a case such as this one 
where the potential cleanup cost has practically quadrupled in less than a decade, it reasonably 
qualifies as a “specific case” consistent with 11-M-0034, providing sufficient reason for Your 
Honors to order KEDNY shareholders to share some portion of the SIR costs. Despite the facts 
at hand clearly suggesting that DPS should recommend, and the Commission should order, cost 
sharing, neither KEDNY nor DPS staff has proposed any amount of cost sharing by the 
Companies in this or the 2016 rate cases filed by the Companies. EHT, Volume 7, page 3705, 
lines 2-9; page 3751, lines 5-9.

lower overall SIR costs in both the short and long term.  For instance, KEDNY asserted it has 
been working to identify additional potentially responsible parties (“PRPs”) to lower its 
investigation and remediation costs included in the proposed rate plan filed in this rate case.  
EHT, Volume 7, page 3702, lines 1-8. KEDNY/KEDLI witness Charles Willard testified, for 
example, that KEDNY did expect potentially responsible parties for both Newtown Creek and

XIII. MANAGEMENT AUDIT COMPLIANCE 
 
PULP expresses here its concerns that the existing process for conducting the management 
and operations audits of the Companies as was used in Case 18-M-0195 (the Audit) is 
insufficiently transparent and accountable.  Concomitant with this concern, PULP argues that all 
communications and information exchanged in Case 18-M-0195 through April 6, 2020, 
providing details of the scope of procedures to be performed involving the Companies’ 
compliance with Article 2 of the Public Service Law, known as the Home Energy Fair Practices 
Act (“HEFPA”), should be made available to the parties and the ALJs in Cases 19-G-0309 and 
19-G-0310.   
 During the hearing, the ALJs requested an update from DPS Staff on the status of the 
Audit. EHT Volume 9.  DPS Staff responded that: 
“So, as stated in Mr. Holst's testimony, at the time of assessment it was still 
ongoing. And I believe at that time it was already known that is was going to be 
concluded by department staff as opposed to the consultant who was then 
conducting the audit. And, if it wasn’t stated that way in his testimony, it -- it --
that occurred shortly thereafter and so I'm informing the -- Your Honors in the 
record that -- that that is the case that the consultant was removed from the case. 
And department staff is concluding the audit; however, that -- the audit is still 
ongoing and so there is no report yet having been -- I can't talk this morning -- A 
report has not yet been issued and I do not anticipate one will be issued at least in 
the -- the near future, at least in the next I would say couple months at the very 
least. I don't honestly have a -- a known date for an expected release of that 
report.” EHT, Volume 9, page 4219, lines 11-15. 
Historically, the scope, procedures and findings of utility management and operations 
audits have direct implications for the issues addressed in rate cases.  Included in most of these 
audits are procedures pertaining to HEFPA compliance, an area of strong importance to PULP. 
Given DPS Staff’s statement that the consultant assigned to perform the current audit was 
removed, PULP has heightened concern that HEFPA compliance may not have been handled 
appropriately by the consultant. PULP is also concerned that the scope of audit procedures as

originally articulated in section 3.2.3.12.1 of its May 17, 2018 request for proposals in Case 18-
M-0195 do not adequately provide specific guidance for auditing of HEFPA compliance. See,

0195, Appendix 1 at 17.  In that section, DPS simply states “Examine the adequacy and 
effectiveness of each utility’s internal controls related to the Home Energy Fair Practices Act and

report in the Audit Case, it is entirely possible that such a report will not be available to the ALJs

any order(s) in these proceedings.  PULP respectfully suggests that such a circumstance is
