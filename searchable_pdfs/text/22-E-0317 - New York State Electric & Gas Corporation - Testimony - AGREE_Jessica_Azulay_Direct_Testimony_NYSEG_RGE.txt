3. SUMMARY OF CONCLUSIONS 
1 
Q: Please summarize your conclusions and recommendations. 
2 
A:  First, the gas Companies have no comprehensive plans to comply with the CLCPA, even 
3 
though the Company has explored several elements necessary for CLCPA compliance. A full 
4 
three years after the CLCPA was passed into law, the Company has yet to conduct a full 
5 
CLCPA-compliant greenhouse gas emissions inventory. In the Companies’ previous Joint 
6 
Proposal, signatory parties laid crucial groundwork to set the Companies up for success in 
7 
complying with the CLCPA in future rate cases, such as a net-zero gas sales commitment, an 
8 
NPA screening process, a Natural Gas and Grid Modernization Study, an RNG inventory, a 
9 
geothermal district pilot study, and more. The Companies completed these requirements, for the 
10 
most part, but have failed now to take the next step of pulling these pieces into a coherent plan 
11 
for their gas investments and customer programs that would put them on path for achieving the 
12 
greenhouse gas emissions reduction mandates of the CLCPA. As a result, the Companies’ gas 
13 
rate plans likely represent a standstill state, where the Companies are not significantly growing 
14 
their gas sales or emissions, but they are not significantly reducing their emissions either. This 
15 
rate case may result in a three-year settlement that would cover investments for the years 2023-
16 
2025. During that period of time, gas utilities will need to have made dramatic changes to their 
17 
gas businesses in order to achieve the legally mandated climate emissions reductions. The 
18 
Commission should require a much more proactive and comprehensive plan from the 
19 
Companies for CLCPA compliance during the upcoming three years. 
20 
 
21 
Second, many of the Companies’ energy efficiency and heat pump programs fall short of both 
22 
their overall targets established by the Commission in the New Efficiency New York (“NENY”) 
23 
proceeding and the requirement that 20% of funding benefit low and moderate income 
24

customers, established by both NENY and the CLCPA. The Companies need to put plans in

4. THE COMPANY HAS NO CLCPA COMPLIANCE PLAN  
14 
Q. What are the state’s legally mandated greenhouse gas emissions reduction targets? 
15 
A. In 2019, the New York State Legislature passed and Governor Cuomo signed the Climate 
16 
Leadership and Community Protection Act (“CLCPA”). The CLCPA requires economy-wide 
17 
greenhouse gas emissions reductions in the amount of 40% from 1990 levels by 2030 and 85% 
18 
by 2050. The requirements apply not just to the electricity sector, but to all sectors, including 
19 
from on-site combustion in buildings and transportation. 
20 
 
21

6. HYDROGEN BLENDING PILOT 
3 
Q. RG&E is proposing a pilot to blend hydrogen in the City of Rochester. Has the 
4 
Company quantified the greenhouse gas reductions associated with this project?   
5 
A. The Company states that it intends to test different percentage blends as part of the pilot. 
6 
According to the Company’s own statement, “the Company has not quantified the greenhouse 
7 
gas and criteria pollutants information.”13 The Company state’s that if it achieves a blend of 20% 
8 
hydrogen, this will likely only decrease greenhouse gas emissions by 7%14. However, this does 
9 
not take into account the greenhouse gas emissions associated with the electricity generation 
10 
needed to produce the hydrogen in the first place. The Company states that the fuel source for 
11 
the hydrogen in the pilot will at first be RED’s own gas fired turbines and that later the source 
12 
may be renewable energy but may be grid power.15   
13 
 
14 
Q. Has the Company quantified the criteria pollutants associated with the hydrogen 
15 
blending pilot? 
16 
A. No. The Company states that it has not quantified these emissions,16 but states the likely 
17 
criteria pollutant to be emitted is NOx. The Company states that given the intent and design of 
18 
the project that it anticipates that the NOx emissions associated with the pilot will be the same 
19 
or lower than simply burning natural gas by itself. The Company provides as evidence a 
20 
footnote that states: “As the CO2 emissions associated with combusting the hydrogen are equal 
21 
to zero and given the facility is capable of Flue Gas Recirculation (FGR), NOx emissions levels 
22

would be maintained or reduced. FGR is a process of taking a portion of the flue gas from a 
1 
combustion process and recirculating it back through a boiler or burner. Once the exhaust gas is 
2 
recirculated, the combustion process is delayed and the flame temperature is maintained, hence 
3

4

5

Q. Are there studies that address the NOx emissions associated with hydrogen 
8 
blending? 
9 
A. Yes. Several studies have found that low blends of hydrogen with natural gas achieve very 
10 
few GHG emissions reductions while increasing NOx pollution.17 Pingping Sun et al find that 
11 
hydrogen production causes 1.3 percent of all U.S. greenhouse gas emissions and causes 
12 
other air pollution, primarily in the form of NOx.18 Additionally, hydrogen burns hotter than 
13 
methane19 and generates up to six times the NOx emissions as compared to methane.20 21 One 
14 
study estimates that a 50-50 blend with natural gas would drive 35 percent higher NOx 
15 
emissions relative to burning 100 percent natural gas.22
