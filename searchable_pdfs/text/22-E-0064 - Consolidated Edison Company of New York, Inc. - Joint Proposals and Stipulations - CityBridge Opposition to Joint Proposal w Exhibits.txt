Introduction 
 
CityBridge LLC (“CityBridge”) here opposes adoption of the Joint Proposal filed 
February 16, 2023 (“Joint Proposal”) in case no. 22-E-0064 (referred to herein as the “rate 
case”).  Adoption is not in the public interest as it does not include measures necessary to ensure 
enforcement of portions of General Rule 5.2.7 entitled “Temporary Service” (the “Rule”), of the 
Consolidated Edison Company of New York, Inc. (the “Company”) Schedule for Electricity 
Service (referred to herein as the “Tariff”), requiring the Company to convert service initially 
designated as temporary to permanent status, and to refund service line installation charges borne 
by the customer by virtue of the initial temporary service classification, when emerging 
circumstances provide reasonable assurance that uptake of service will be non-temporary.   
The Company has repeatedly stated that with respect to customers situated as CityBridge, 
it will never redesignate temporary services to permanent and will never refund service line 
installation costs under any set of emergent circumstances, in defiance of Rule 5.2.7.  The 
Company’s stated intent to ignore the relevant provisions of the Rule with respect to these 
customers is notwithstanding the Public Service Commission’s repeated and explicit 
confirmations of the language’s intent and its applicability to such customers.

Background

design, construct and maintain a network of Public Communication Service kiosks providing, 
among other things, emergency 911, telephone and Wi-Fi hotspot services to the public.   The 
kiosks, known as “Links,” are immovable structures, at least 9½ tall and 1,300 lbs. in weight, 
permanently embedded in sidewalks across the five boroughs.  Links generally include two LCD 
panels that display public service messages and emergency information as required by the City, 
and advertising, revenue from which is shared with the City and is a funding source for the 
program.  Nearly 1,900 Links were deployed during a four-year period between 2015 and 2019, 
and CityBridge has committed to provide approximately 2,100 more new Links, as part of a 
second multi-year deployment currently underway.1

respect to classification of electrical service and financial responsibility for service lines 
connecting Links to the Company’s existing infrastructure.  In general, the Company does not

Rule 5.2.7 Re-Designation and Refund Obligations 
Rule 5.2.7 states in its third paragraph that “[t]he Customer’s payment” in respect of 
service installation costs by virtue of temporary service designation  
shall be refundable if circumstances change after the Customer commences to take 
service, and the Company has reasonable assurance that the use of the service will 
not be temporary and that the Company’s facilities will be used for permanent 
supply. 
 
Therefore, even if the Company correctly designates service as temporary based on 
information available at the time an account is opened (which CityBridge does not concede to be 
the case with respect to Links), the plain language above affirmatively requires the Company to 
reevaluate that designation on a continuing basis with reference to emergent circumstances.

CityBridge Rule 5.2.7 Proposal 
The Joint Proposal is deficient in not incorporating revised Tariff language reasonably 
calculated to ensure the Company’s compliance with its affirmative reclassification and refund 
obligations, as such obligations have been repeatedly confirmed by the Agency including in its 
various court submittals.   Set forth in Appendix 1 are revisions proposed by CityBridge which, 
if adopted in parallel with or incorporated into the Joint Proposal together with the revisions 
described in the latter part of this submission, would allow CityBridge to lend support to the 
Joint Proposal. 
To ensure that the Temporary Services rule is not being improperly applied or abused by 
the Company to forever relegate to “temporary” status services evolving into permanency, 
CityBridge proposes that as of the proposed revision’s effective date, the Company be obligated 
to review the designation of services that have been, or in the future remain, under temporary 
designation for at least five years following commencement of service.  In instances where (1) 
service uptake has been substantially continuous over the prior five years, and (2) circumstances 
reasonably assure that service uptake will continue, the Company will be obligated to reclassify 
the service to permanent status, and refund service installation outlays.  Customers are to be 
provided advance notice of the review and an opportunity to submit relevant evidence.  
Consistent with Agency statements in court submissions, where services are provided to a 
customer operating pursuant to a municipal franchise, the extension, renewal or re-award of the 
franchise will satisfy prong (2) of the inquiry.  Where a determination is made at the five-year 
mark not to re-classify service, the Company shall be obligated to undertake re-review applying 
the criteria described above, periodically on a going-forward basis.

CityBridge also proposes that the Rule expressly state that, in any instance where the 
temporary services designation is retained beyond the fifth-year review, the customer’s refund 
remedy in connection with future conversion required by the Rule explicitly include 
compensation for time value of money.  The reasoning is quite simple: the Temporary Service 
rule is intended to apply to what are truly temporary use cases, and certainly was never intended 
as a vehicle to provide long-term, interest-free financing to the Company.  Making explicit the 
time-value element will also tend to incentivize integrity in the initial and five-year review 
processes, and for customers emerging from long-term temporary service limbo, expressly 
providing this remedy is the sole means to afford truly fair and equitable compensation.  
Certainly, the Company is positioned to undertake the necessary financial planning measures to 
address exposure to such reimbursement – and reasonably would be expected to have adopted 
such measures already in view of the Rule’s long-existing refund remedy and the Company’s 
prolonged retentions of service installation fees. 
Based on its prior conduct, CityBridge anticipates that in response to its revision proposal 
reasonably calculated to assure that the Company complies with its affirmative reclassification 
and refund duties as confirmed by the Agency, the Company will repeat prior statements 
maligning CityBridge as somehow seeking subsidization of the Link program at the expense of

CityBridge Proposed Service Classification No. 2 Special Provision

Classification No. 2 (SC-2) rates – applicable to small business customers and which rate is 
applied by the Company to Links – to include a special provision providing for a discount to

None of Con Edison's existing tariffs precisely fits 
the type of electric service that Metricom is seeking. 
Further

Metricom's Microcell radio transceiver installations 
resemble street lighting installations in some respects, but 
resemble small commercial uses in others. 
The radio transceivers 
themselves draw a small amount of electricity taken directly from 
the host street light through an adaptor. 
As with street lights,
