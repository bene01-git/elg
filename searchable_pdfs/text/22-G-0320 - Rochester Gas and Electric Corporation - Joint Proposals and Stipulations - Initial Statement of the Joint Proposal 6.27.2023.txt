PRELIMINARY STATEMENT 
 
 
 
Multiple Intervenors, an unincorporated association of over 55 large industrial, 
commercial, and institutional energy consumers with manufacturing and other facilities located 
throughout New York State, including the New York State Electric & Gas Corporation 
(“NYSEG”) and Rochester Gas and Electric Corporation (“RG&E”) service territories, hereby

A. 
The Proposed Revenue Requirements Should Not Be 
Evaluated in a Vacuum 
 
 
These proceedings are utility rate proceedings and, therefore, an examination of 
the proposed electric and gas revenue requirements should focus primarily – but, as detailed 
herein, not exclusively – on the Companies’ projected costs and revenues.  Before turning to the 
specific electric and gas revenue requirements proposed herein, the context in which they should 
be evaluated warrants discussion and consideration by the Commission.  For the reasons set forth 
below, the proposed revenue requirements should not be evaluated in a vacuum. 
 
A utility’s revenue requirement reflects, by necessity, a balancing of potentially-
competing interests.  One such interest is the desire to maintain – and preferably improve – the 
reliability of electric and gas service, which is exceedingly important to customers, including 
Multiple Intervenors members.  Another interest shared by many parties, including Multiple 
Intervenors, is a desire to reduce emissions in the Companies’ service territories.  A third major

It is incumbent upon the Commission to approve electric and gas delivery rates 
that reflect a balancing of interests that is in the public interest.  Frequently, interests and 
priorities can compete against each other.  For instance, electric and gas service theoretically 
could be rendered more reliable and/or emissions reduced through the spending of exorbitant 
sums of money that render such services unaffordable.  Similarly, affordability theoretically 
could be improved by forsaking necessary investments in reliability and/or emission-reducing 
activities, but then performance in those important areas would be compromised.  Thus, the 
Commission needs to ensure that a balance is maintained between reliable utility service, 
emissions reductions, and affordability for customers of all types.   The proposed electric and gas 
revenue requirements advanced in the Joint Proposal reflect merely one attempt at balancing 
these often competing interests.  Multiple Intervenors contends, however, that the Joint Proposal 
accords grossly inadequate priority to the interest of maintaining the affordability of electric and 
gas service.  Indeed, were the Joint Proposal to be adopted without material modification, 
affordability in the Companies’ service territory would suffer enormously. 
 
When determining whether a contested Joint Proposal is in the public interest or 
requires rejection or modification, the Commission should refrain from evaluating its provisions 
– particularly the proposed electric and gas revenue requirements – in a vacuum.  There are a 
multitude of other proceedings ongoing at the Commission, and elsewhere, that place the 
balancing of competing interests into context.  For instance, on the issue of affordability, the

6. 
EAMs 
 
EAMs (as defined previously, an acronym for “Earnings Adjustment 
Mechanisms”) refer to positive-only incentives for a utility and its shareholders that are funded 
by customers.  The Joint Proposal advances numerous proposed electric EAMs that, if realized, 
would add materially to customers’ financial burdens.  Multiple Intervenors has long advanced 
concerns regarding the Commission’s reliance on EAMs.  In these proceedings, the inclusion of 
EAMs in the Joint Proposal is particularly distasteful – and inappropriate – given the magnitude 
of the rate relief under consideration. 
 
Pursuant to the Joint Proposal, NYSEG would be authorized annual 5.6% 
increases in net base gas delivery rates.  (JP, App. A at 1.)  RG&E would be authorized annual, 
double-digit percentage increases in net base electric and gas delivery rates.  (Id.).  Finally, 
NYSEG would be authorized annual 17.1% increases in net base electric delivery rates.  (Id.)  
Such delivery rate increases are exorbitant and, in the case of NYSEG’s electric service, 
unprecedented for a major electric or gas utility in New York.  Given these proposed delivery 
rate increases, requiring electric customers (many of which also are gas customers) to potentially 


--- Page 33 ---
30 
fund millions of dollars in additional financial incentives (in the form of EAMs) for the benefit 
of the Companies’ shareholders is abhorrent.  Even if, arguendo, the Commission wishes to 
continue relying on EAMs in the future, their potential use should be rejected where, as is the 
case in these proceedings, customers may be forced to bear above-average delivery rate increases 
for a multi-year period.  Quite simply, the Commission should not require customers facing 
extremely large delivery rate increases to fund additional shareholder incentives.51

EAM; (2) Storage DER Utilization EAM; (3) Demand Response EAM; and (4) Electric Vehicle

Monthly (gas) Charges.  Without expressing any opinion on such issue with respect to residential 
customers, such sensitivity does not exist for large non-residential customers.  For such 
customers, the Customer/Minimum Monthly Charge often represents a modest or even small 
portion of the overall delivery bill and, therefore, there often is a strong interest in assigning a 
portion of a proposed increase thereto for recovery so as to moderate increases to volumetric 
charges (i.e., Demand Charges on the electric side and Delivery Charges on the gas side), which 
often are much more impactful (and, for many businesses, comparably unavoidable).

Finally, Multiple Intervenors contends that the electric and gas large non-

C. 
Appendix M (Natural Gas Matters) 
 
Appendix M addresses a number of matters pertaining to natural gas.  Multiple 
Intervenors contends that the provisions of Appendix M are reasonable and should be adopted in 
these proceedings. 
 
Pursuant to Appendix M, the Companies agree to, inter alia, (i) attempt to achieve 
a zero-net increase in billed gas usage over the three-year term, (ii) provide reports on gas usage 
within their respective territories, by sector, and on a weather-normalized basis, (iii) not seek 
funding or regulatory approvals for the Lansing Pipeline or the DeRuyter Pipeline, and (iv) 
continue to evaluate future gas projects for Non-Pipeline Alternative (“NPA”) solutions.  (JP, 
App. M at 1-2.)  Under the circumstances presented, these agreements appear reasonable. 
 
With respect to NPAs, Multiple Intervenors asserts that gas utilities, such as the 
Companies, should consider and evaluate NPAs as alternatives to gas pipeline projects where

such examination makes sense.58  Multiple Intervenors supports the consideration of NPAs, 
where practical, because they may address the system need at a reduced cost for customers.  
Where an NPA can solve a system need at lower cost, it should be pursued.  In fact, it arguably 
could be imprudent for a utility to fail to consider or, where cost-effective, implement an NPA.  
Notwithstanding Multiple Intervenors’ longstanding support for consideration of NPAs (and, on

only should be implemented where cost-effective and beneficial to customers.

CONCLUSION 
 
 
For the reasons set forth herein, the electric and gas delivery rate increases 
contained in the Joint Proposal are excessive, contrary to the public interest, and, if adopted, 
would result in unacceptable customer rate impacts.  Accordingly, Multiple Intervenors urges the 
Commission to reject the Joint Proposal or condition its adoption on the Companies’ 
unconditional acceptance of reduced electric and gas revenue requirements.  In contrast, the 
proposed electric and gas revenue allocations and large non-residential rate designs are 
consistent with cost-of-service principles and otherwise are reasonable, and should be adopted 
herein and applied to reduced revenue requirements.  Multiple Intervenors also supports the 
adoption of Appendix M, pertaining to natural gas matters.  Importantly, the methodologies 
underlying the proposed electric and gas revenue allocations and large non-residential rate 
designs, and the provisions of Appendix M, are not (or should not be) contingent upon the 
proposed revenue requirements set forth in the Joint Proposal.
