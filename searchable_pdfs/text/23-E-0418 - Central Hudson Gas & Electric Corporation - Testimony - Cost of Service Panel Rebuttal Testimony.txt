IV. 
ALLOCATION OF DEFERRED INCOME TAXES 
4 
Q.    
Please describe the recommendation made by MI regarding the Panel’s 
5 
allocation of deferred income taxes. 
6 
A. 
MI Witness Pollock recommends, on page 12 of his testimony, that “all 
7 
non-current deferred income taxes and [Net Operating Losses (“NOL”)] 
8 
should be allocated to service classifications based on previously 
9 
allocated rate base” instead of allocating “these tax expenses on Rate 
10 
Year pre-tax operating income and taxable income.” 
11 
Q.    
What is MI Witness Pollock’s justification? 
12 
A. 
In his testimony, on page 12, MI Witness Pollock indicates that it is 
13 
unreasonable to allocate past period income tax expenses on Rate Year 
14 
pre-tax operating income and taxable income because “[p]ast period 
15 
income tax expenses are not caused by Rate Year pre-tax operating 
16 
income and taxable income.” 
17 
Q.    
Did MI Witness Pollock identify a certain aspect of deferred income taxes 
18 
he was concerned with? 
19 
A. 
Based on his workpapers supporting Exhibit __ (JP-6), in which MI 
20 
Witness Pollock reflects his recommended changes, including his changes 
21 
to the classification and allocation of prior period deferred income taxes

6 
 
and NOL, MI Witness Pollock’s concern focuses on the functionalization of 
1 
NOL, which in turn affects the allocation of deferred income taxes. 
2 
Q.    
Does the Panel functionalize and allocate all NOL and deferred income 
3 
taxes in the same manner? 
4 
A. 
No.  The Panel performs a detailed analysis to functionalize each 
5 
component of deferred income tax.  The resulting functionalized deferred 
6 
income taxes are then allocated to the classes in the same manner as the 
7 
underlying function.  For example, deferred income taxes functionalized 
8 
on total plant are spread to each production, transmission and distribution 
9 
function based on the proportion that each function comprises total plant.  
10 
In turn, total deferred income taxes – which includes the component 
11 
spread to the functions based on total plant as just described – are then 
12 
allocated to the classes using the same allocator employed to allocate the 
13 
underlying asset or expense. 
14 
Q.    
Please describe how the Panel functionalized NOL, including the Panel’s 
15 
justification for doing so. 
16 
A. 
The Panel functionalized NOL on pre-tax operating income and taxable 
17 
income.  The NOL carryforwards reflect the outcome(s) of previous tax 
18 
calculations that incorporate a diverse set of items and, as a result, the 
19 
NOL lacks the link to the specific items necessary for more granular 
20 
functionalization. 
21

PROPERTY TAXES 
1 
Q.    
Please describe the recommendation made by MI Witness Pollock 
2 
regarding the Panel’s functionalization of property taxes. 
3 
A. 
MI Witness Pollock recommends, on page 14 of his testimony, that 
4 
property tax expenses should be functionalized using net plant as 
5 
opposed to land and land rights investments, which were utilized by the 
6 
Company in its electric ECOS study. 
7 
Q.    
What is MI Witness Pollock’s justification? 
8 
A. 
MI Witness Pollock indicates on page 13 of his testimony that 
9 
“[f]unctionalizing property taxes on land and [land] rights investments 
10 
assigns a disproportionate amount of taxes to the production and 
11 
transmission functions and far too little to distribution and other functions.” 
12 
Q.  
Does the Panel agree with this recommendation? 
13 
A.  
Yes.  Although the Panel has been functionalizing property taxes on land 
14 
and land rights for at least the past three rate proceedings, the Panel 
15 
agrees it would be more appropriate to functionalize property taxes on net 
16 
plant, consistent with gas, as property taxes are assessed both on the 
17 
land as well as on the structures and/or equipment erected or affixed to 
18 
the land.  However, the Panel is concerned about the impact the resulting 
19 
change in methodology could have on the electric ECOS study results and 
20 
corresponding class rates of return.

rather than the 70 HDD included in the current calculation.
