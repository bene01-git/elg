V. 
  Embedded Cost of Service Study 
19 
A. Purpose of ECOSS and Overview of Methodology 
20 
Q. 
Please describe what an ECOSS is and why it is prepared. 
21 
14


--- Page 17 ---
Testimony of the Rate Design Panel  
Page 12 of 76 
 
A. 
The purpose of an ECOSS is to apportion a utility’s total revenue 
1 
requirement, including plant and other investments, operating expenses, 
2 
depreciation, and taxes among the rate classes served by the utility.  The 
3 
ECOSS produces a revenue amount for each rate class equal to the 
4 
revenue that would need to be collected from that class to produce the 
5 
system average rate of return on rate base.  This information provides 
6 
guidance in revenue allocation, and in the development of rates, to recover 
7 
the utility’s revenue requirement from all rate classes. 
8 
 
9 
Q. 
Please summarize the methodology employed for the ECOSS. 
10 
A. 
The ECOSS is prepared by analyzing each element of the utility’s revenue 
11 
requirement, and assigning or allocating it among the rate classes.  The 
12 
ECOSS was performed using a specialized model developed by HSG 
13 
Group.  This model uses the traditional three-step process, as follows: 
14 
 In functionalization, the first step, the Company’s cost elements 
15 
(which include costs and expenses, as well as the rate base 
16 
components) are assigned or allocated among each of the gas 
17 
functions identified in Appendix A to the Commission’s November 
18 
9, 2001 Order in Case 00-M-. 
19 
 In classification, the second step, each functionalized cost element 
20 
is separated into demand, commodity, and customer cost 
21 
15


--- Page 18 ---
Testimony of the Rate Design Panel  
Page 13 of 76 
 
categories. 
1 
 In class allocation, the third step, each functionally classified cost 
2 
element is assigned or allocated among one or more of the 
3 
Company’s rate classes. 
4 
 
5 
Q. 
Please describe the functionalization step of the ECOSS. 
6 
A. 
In the functionalization step, cost elements are separated among the 
7 
utility’s basic service functions, by determining what physical purpose 
8 
each cost element serves. 
9 
 
10 
Q. 
What are the functions included in the ECOSS? 
11 
A. 
The functions included in the ECOSS are: 
12 
 Gas Supply and Storage - availability and supply of gas and 
13 
storage to meet seasonal and daily peak demands; 
14 
 Transmission - moves gas from supply and storage sources to 
15 
localized distribution facilities; 
16 
 Distribution - moves gas from the transmission system to 
17 
customers’ premises; includes service lines; 
18 
 Metering and Billing - connects customers to the system, measures 
19 
their usage and demand, and bills for the services provided; 
20 
 Collections;  
21

 Sales Promotion; and

 Gas Procurement.

Q. 
Please describe the classification step of the ECOSS. 
4 
A. 
The Company’s gas distribution system is designed to meet three primary 
5 
objectives: 
6 
1. Connect customers to the Company’s distribution system; 
7 
2. Meet the aggregate peak design day capacity requirements of all 
8 
customers entitled to service on the peak day; and 
9 
3. Deliver natural gas commodity to customers, whether they are 
10 
sales or transportation customers. 
11 
 
Based on these objectives, each functionalized cost element is classified as 
12 
being customer-related, demand-related, or commodity-related, depending 
13 
on which of the foregoing objectives it serves. 
14 
 
15 
 
Cost elements classified as customer-related are incurred to attach a 
16 
customer to the distribution system, meter gas usage and maintain the 
17 
customer’s account.  Customer-related cost elements are a function of the 
18 
number of customers served and are incurred by the Company whether or 
19 
not an individual customer uses any gas.  They may include capital costs 
20 
associated with the customer component of distribution mains, as well as 
21 
17


--- Page 20 ---
Testimony of the Rate Design Panel  
Page 15 of 76 
 
costs for services, meters and metering, billing, customer service, and 
1 
accounting and collection activities. 
2 
 
3 
 
Demand, or capacity, related cost elements are associated with plant that is 
4 
designed, installed and operated to meet peak hourly or daily customer gas 
5 
flow requirements.  Demand-related components include transmission and 
6 
distribution mains, and localized distribution facilities and dedicated 
7 
facilities that are designed to satisfy individual customer maximum 
8 
demands.  Demand-related cost elements are allocated among the classes 
9 
based on their contributions to the peak demand for which the system is 
10 
designed. 
11 
 
12 
 
Cost elements classified as commodity-related generally vary with the 
13 
volume of gas sold or delivered (i.e., throughput) to customers.  Costs 
14 
related to providing gas supply are classified as commodity-related. 
15 
 
16

The amount of costs recovered through the GAC was equal to the GAC

Q. 
Please describe the process for determining how much of the 
1 
investment in mains should be considered customer-related versus 
2 
demand-related. 
3 
A. 
Distribution mains are installed to connect customers to the distribution 
4 
system (i.e., customer-related) as well as to provide capacity to meet 
5 
customer requirements on the design day (i.e., demand-related).  Therefore 
6 
the cost of mains is classified as partly customer-related and partly 
7 
demand-related.  The Company estimated the customer component of 
8 
mains to be 37.91 percent based on the results of its minimum-system 
9 
study.  The customer component was allocated among the customer 
10 
classes based on the number of customers.  The demand component was 
11 
allocated among the customer classes based on their contribution to 
12 
design-day demand. 
13 
 
14 
Q. 
Please describe how the minimum-system study was performed. 
15 
A. 
Minimum-system studies are a widely accepted method to classify mains 
16 
between customer- and demand-related costs.  The customer-related 
17 
component is the portion of cost representing the least-cost system the 
18 
utility would install simply to connect customers, without regard to the 
19 
need to meet demand. 
20
