Dear Commissioner Rhodes and Secretary Phillips, 
Re: Case No. l 9-G-0678 
We are writing in response to the letter from Secretary Phillips dated July 2, 2020 (the 
"Response Letter"), which was a reply to the letter of this coalition dated June 23, 2020 in which 
we asked for an update on the status of the PSC's agreement on National Grid's long-term 
energy proposals and for greater transparency in the decision-making process going forward. (A 
copy of the Response Letter is attached). Under the Order Adopting and Approving Settlement 
dated November 26, 20 I 9 (the "Settlement Order") in Case No. I 9-G-0678 (the "Long Term 
Options Case"), the PSC was to agree on one or more of National Grid's long-term proposals by 
June 2020, which has now come and gone. 
Respectfully, the Response Letter is unacceptable and inadequate. The letter contains 
many words, but says little of substance and fails to provide any details about the status of the 
agreement and the cause of the delay. Our request for transparency in the decision-making 
process on our long-term energy plans has gone ignored. It is particularly offensive that the PSC 
sent the exact same response to the letters of several other groups and individuals also requesting 
greater transparency, when the letters were not identical. 
The Response Letter references Case l 9-G-0309 (the "Rate Case"), a pending rate case 
involving National Grid to which several members of this coalition are also parties. Your 
reference to the Rate Case in the Response Letter is cause for serious concern. The fossil fuel 
infrastructure options that National Grid has proposed in the Long Term Options Case - which 
includes, inter alia, an expansion of the Iroquois pipeline system and expanding the Greenpoint 


--- Page 2 ---
LNG facility by adding two new LNG vaporizers and a CNG truck terminal - should not, and 
cannot, be decided in the Rate Case. 
The public has submitted over 9,000 written comments in the Long Terms Options Case 
and hundreds of New Yorkers testified at virtual "public meetings," which National Grid held at 
the height of the COVID-19 pandemic. Additionally, a substantial amount of evidence has been 
submitted in the Long Term Options Case, including multiple professional reports on gas 
demand. 
To reach an agreement on National Grid's long-term proposals in the Rate Case without 
considering the thousands of public comments submitted in the Long Term Options Case would 
be to deprive the public of the right to be heard on their long-term energy future. Not only would 
this be a patently unfair and unjust result, it would also completely defy the very purpose of the 
Settlement Order, which states that the Long-Term Capacity Report "requires significant public 
input" and the Settlement Order "is in the public interest." 
We request that you respond to the following specific questions/inquiries: 
• 
When will the PSC be agreeing to a long-term option or options proposed by National 
Grid? The Settlement Order mandates a deadline of June 2020, which has not been met. 
• 
What has been the cause of the delay in reaching an agreement? 
• 
What factors go into the PSC's decision making process on the long-term proposals to 
which it will agree? 
• 
Please confirm that the PSC is weighing each of the over 9,000 written comments, as 
well as the recorded testimony from the public meetings, in its analysis of National 
Grid's long-term proposals. 
• 
Please confirm that the PSC will be making a decision on the long-term proposals in the 
Long Tenn Options Case and not in the Rate Case. 
• 
The Response Letter states that National Grid has developed solutions "to ensure that 
customers in the region will have safe and reliable access to the energy they need while 
we rapidly transition toward a clean-energy economy to meet the mandated goals of our 
nation-leading climate plan." In light of this, please confirm that the PSC is considering 
the "No Infrastructure Option" submitted by National Grid, which is the only option that 
complies with the CLCPA, our "nation-leading climate plan." The "No Infrastructure 
Option" is the option that this coalition, as well as other groups/individuals, has 
demanded. 
• 
The Response Letter states that "DPS staff otherwise continues to work with National 
Grid to implement a comprehensive approach to long-term capacity, including the use of 
aggressive energy efficiency and demand response." Aside from energy efficiency and 


--- Page 3 ---
demand response, what else is the DPS Staff working with National Grid to implement? 
Is DPS Staff working with National Grid to implement any of the gas infrastructure 
projects that have been opposed by thousands of public comments? 
The Response Letter also states that the "DPS staff is closely examining National Grid's 
construction of the MRI project within the context" of the Rate Case. This is inaccurate. This 
Commission is allowing National Grid to continue to build this pipeline without expressed 
approval in a rate plan, during a national health care crisis, and over the objections of local 
elected officials and thousands of New Yorkers who live in the pathway of this pipeline. This 
includes beginning construction of Phase 5, which DPS Staff recommended the Commission 
require National Grid to pause work on until all of the supply options have been vetted 1. 
This coalition, as well as the thousands of members of the public who have submitted 
comments in the Long Term Options Case, is losing faith in this Commission due to its lack of 
transparency and forthcomingness. Recently, and within a matter of days, 700 comments were 
submitted to the PSC demanding more transparency and accountability to the public in the 
Long-Terms Options Case. The Public Service Commission is supposed to be acting in the 
interest of the public. We are only asking that it do so.

Dear Ms. Picone: 
Thank you for writing to request an update and greater transparency in the decision-making 
process regarding National Grid's long-term capacity options, and to stress the public's interest in, 
and imp01tance of, these ongoing proceedings, as well as your concerns related to National Grid 
installing additional gas infrastructure known as the Metropolitan Reliability Initiative or MRI. I 
am responding on behalf of John B. Rhodes, CEO of the Department of Public Service (DPS). 
As part of the ongoing public examination of the natural gas market in New York City and 
Long Island, National Grid has been directed to prepare options in addition to the Northeast Supply 
Enhancement (NESE) pipeline, which was recently rejected by the New York State Department 
of Environmental Conservation for permitting.

Ms. Noelle Picone 
July 2, 2020 
As a result of this very public examination, National Grid has developed solutions to ensure 
that customers in the region will have safe and reliable access to the energy they need while we 
rapidly transition toward a clean-energy economy to meet the mandated goals of our nation-leading 
climate plan. These options are being reviewed by an independent monitor and DPS staff. In 
publicly filed quarterly reports, the independent monitor continues to address whether National 
Grid is complying with the requirements of the settlement agreement, including whether it properly 
identified long-term capacity options. 
To the extent the monitor or DPS staff determines the company is in non-compliance, 
recommendations of appropriate action would be made to the Commission as part of the ongoing 
public proceeding. DPS staff otherwise continues to work with National Grid to implement a 
comprehensive approach to long-term capacity, including the use of aggressive energy efficiency 
and demand response. 
Fmiher, please be advised that DPS staff is closely examining National Grid's construction 
of the MRI project within the context of the ongoing proceeding (19-G-0309), and know that the 
Commission has instituted a proceeding to examine the way National Grid and all of the State's 
local gas distribution companies plan for the future of the natural gas system, with the goal of 
modernizing natural gas planning to incorporate options that will help the State achieve its goals 
for reducing greenhouse gas emissions (20-G-0131 ). Your participation in that proceeding will be 
most welcome. 
We appreciate your interest in these important issues. Your comments have been included 
among those submitted in these matters, and thus are part of the records in these proceedings. 
Please be assured that they will be carefully considered by the DPS and the Public Service 
Commission as work continues in these proceedings.
