II. 
SUMMARY OF TESTIMONY  
1 
Q. 
PROVIDE A BRIEF OVERVIEW OF YOUR TESTIMONY. 
2 
A. 
Timely and accurate access to customer data is critical to an ESCO’s ability to serve 
3 
customers. Unfortunately, for years, ConEd has experienced significant and wide spread 
4 
issues with providing timely and accurate data to ESCOs. The issues with ConEd’s 
5 
provision of timely and accurate data affect fundamental aspects of ESCO retail supply 
6 
operations and have the potential to cause significant disruptions for ESCOs and their 
7 
customers alike. For example, as a consequence of ConEd’s data issues, customers do not 
8 
receive accurate information about their usage or timely bills. In fact, at times, because of 
9 
these data issues, customers will not receive bills for months at a time and then suddenly 
10 
receive a bill that covers multiple months; making it difficult for customers to budget 
11 
their energy costs and having a sudden and unexpected negative impact on customer cash 
12 
flows. This is especially problematic for low-income customers. 
13 
 
14 
These issues are exacerbated by ConEd’s lack of communication. In many cases, ConEd 
15 
is not even aware of an issue until an ESCO informs it of the problem. Further, ConEd 
16 
frequently fails to respond to ESCO outreach regarding these issues despite multiple 
17 
attempts to contact ConEd. Moreover, even when ConEd does respond, it can take 
18 
months and even years for ConEd to actually fix the issue.  
19 
 
20 
In its Customer Operations Panel Testimony, ConEd describes modifications to existing 
21 
information technology (“IT”) programs and new IT programs affecting customer data 
22 
for which ConEd is seeking funding in this proceeding. RESA supports funding for 
23 


--- Page 5 ---

20 
A. 
RESA members have experienced several issues. First, for whatever reason, actual 
21 
customer usage is missing or not provided to the ESCO for numerous months. During 
22

Q. 
HOW DOES THIS MISSING USAGE IMPACT CUSTOMERS, ESCOS AND THE 
11 
RETAIL ENERGY MARKET? 
12 
A. 
Missing usage makes it impossible for ESCOs to accurately bill their customers for 
13 
supply charges and, presumably, for ConEd to bill customers at all. As a consequence, 
14 
customers are not receiving accurate pricing signals that could result in changes in their 
15 
energy consumption or purchasing behavior. This effect is exacerbated when customers 
16 
do not receive bills for months at a time. In that case, customers have no real sense of 
17 
what they are paying each month for their energy usage or a meaningful way to budget 
18 
their energy costs. Consequently, customers who may have otherwise curtailed their 
19 
usage or switched service providers to obtain more favorable terms do not have the 
20 
information necessary to make those decisions. Moreover, once those customers are 
21 
actually billed for the missing months, they are hit with a staggering energy bill that 
22 
negatively impacts their cash flow. This issue is particularly acute for low-income 
23

13 
energy consumption or purchasing behavior.

associated with the canceled meter reads/reduced meter volumes. Moreover, ConEd does

2

Q. 
WHAT HAPPENS IF CONED FAILS TO PROVIDE EDI DATA TO THE 
4 
CORRECT LSE? 
5 
A. 
When customers switch LSEs, at times, ConEd sends the EDI data for those customers to 
6 
the wrong load-serving LSE (e.g., the previous LSE). ConEd then reports this incorrect 
7 
data to the NYISO. As a consequence, the previous LSE is still buying energy and 
8 
ancillary services for customers that it is no longer serving. The costs of these excess 
9 
purchases cannot be collected from the customers who are still being inaccurately 
10 
reported as belonging to the ESCO. Moreover, ConEd does not reimburse ESCOs for 
11 
these added costs. As a result, ESCOs must absorb those unnecessary costs.

Q.

In 2018, ESCOs challenged the accuracy of ConEd’s April, May, and June UFE values,

18 
ESCOs. ConEd was able to fix the calculations for April, May and June and report 
19 
corrected data for May and June during the NYISO version 3 (i.e., final bill closeout 
20 
period) true-up period. Because the version 3 window had already closed for April 2018, 
21 
however, ConEd had to get a tariff waiver from the Federal Energy Regulatory

Interval data offers valuable insight into a customer’s energy-usage patterns. This

19

20 
make it aware of the particular issue. However, it regularly takes multiple contacts before 
21

times, ESCOs have needed to escalate the problem in order to get a response. Moreover,

Q. 
HAS CONED TAKEN ANY STEPS TO HELP IDENTIFY PROBLEMS?

14

15 
customers, it does not resolve the underlying problem that created those issues.
