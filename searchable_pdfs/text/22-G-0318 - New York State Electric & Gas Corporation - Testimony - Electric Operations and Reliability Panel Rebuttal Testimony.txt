Q. 
Is the Companies’ fleet of circuit breakers increasingly exposed to failures? 
1 
A. 
Yes.  The circuit breaker asset class continues to age, and failure rates continue to 
2 
increase.  For example, over the past five years, circuit breaker failures have increased by 
3 
20%, with an average of 2,328 customers affected per each breaker failure.  The 
4 
investment necessary to proactively replace high risk circuit breakers is critical, and the 
5 
expenditures must be maintained at a rate commensurate with the increasing risk.  While 
6 
the SEIOP’s testimony extolls the benefits of this program, they are proposing to 
7 
significantly reduce the funding for this program, which reduces the programs benefits by 
8 
creating an increasing exposure (and corresponding replacement backlog) to high-risk 
9 
circuit breaker failures. 
10 
Q. 
Are there additional risks to running circuit breakers to failure?  
11 
A. 
Yes.  Circuit breakers can fail catastrophically, creating a safety risk for both the public 
12 
and utility workers.  Failed circuit breakers can also “stick” in fault conditions, resulting 
13 
in upstream protective device operation and widespread substation outages that affect 
14 
considerably larger amounts of customers. 
15 
Q. 
Is there a benefit to proactive replacement as opposed to a “run-to-failure” approach? 
16 
A. 
Yes.  A run-to-failure approach exposes our customers to significant reliability impact, as 
17 
well as safety risk if the equipment fails catastrophically.  Reactive failures are also 
18 
considerably more costly to address than planned replacements.  This can be 
19 
compounded by the long lead times associated with procuring this type of equipment.  By 
20 
proactively replacing high risk circuit breakers before they fail, the Companies are able to 
21 
mitigate both the reliability and safety concerns associated with this widening exposure.  
22

3

6

7 
Staff’s assumptions about the transition from the current program to the six-year trim 
8

NYSEG and its customers will continue to be exposed to unabated tree growth on the 
12 
unreclaimed circuits.

the nearly 10,000 miles of currently unreclaimed circuits until those circuits are 
17 
eventually reclaimed.  In the interim, the scope of the six-year trim cycle will focus on 
18 
the circuits that are already reclaimed.  As indicated on page 11 of our Direct Testimony, 
19 
18,600 miles (64%) of NYSEG’s distribution system is reclaimed and/or had been 
20 
trimmed within the last 5 years.  While converting these circuits to a recurring trim cycle 
21 
is a best practice employed by every other major electric utility in New York, its near-
22 
term benefits are negated by the continuing and significant presence of unreclaimed
