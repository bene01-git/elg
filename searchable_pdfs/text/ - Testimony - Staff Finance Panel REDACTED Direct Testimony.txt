In the context of Commission rate proceedings,

a company to finance its long-term utility

5 
its rate base. 
6 
Q. 
What is a fair rate of return for a regulated 
7 
utility? 
8 
A. 
A fair rate of return for a regulated utility is 
9 
one that enables the utility to provide safe and 
10 
adequate service to its customers, while at the 
11 
same time assuring a company continuing support 
12

15 
return, therefore, allows a utility to recover 
16 
its prudently incurred cost of debt, while 
17 
providing its common equity investors the 
18 
opportunity to earn a return that is 
19 
commensurate with the risk of their investment. 
20 
Q.

21 
A. 
The fair rate of return for a utility company is 
22 
calculated through a weighted average of the

individual cost components of its expected

DISCOUNTED CASH FLOW METHODOLOGY 
22 
Q.  Please explain the basic theory underlying the 
23 
 
DCF methodology. 
24 


--- Page 73 ---
Cases 25-E-0072 and 25-G-0073    
 
 STAFF FINANCE PANEL 
 
 
72 
 
A.  The DCF methodology can be applied to any 
1 
 
investment instrument that has an intrinsic 
2 
 
value.  The DCF approach, as it relates to 
3 
 
common stock, recognizes that companies create 
4 
 
value for their stockholders by using their 
5 
 
earnings in a number of ways, the most important 
6 
of which, by far is through the payment of cash 
7 
dividends to shareholders.  Alternatively, 
8 
earnings that are retained by companies can be 
9 
used to create value in other ways.  First, a 
10 
company can invest in capital projects designed 
11 
to increase future profits; second, a company 
12 
can retire debt, which reduces interest expense; 
13 
and third, a company can buy back some of its 
14 
common stock, which increases future earnings on 
15 
a per-share basis.   
16 
Q.  Please describe the relationship between the 
17 
value of a company’s common stock and that of 
18 
its future dividends. 
19 
A.  While earnings drive companies’ dividend payout 
20 
policies, the value of the companies’ common 
21 
stock is equal to the present value of all 
22 
future dividends.  This is because the earnings 
23 
retained will only have value to the stockholder 
24 


--- Page 74 ---
Cases 25-E-0072 and 25-G-0073    
 
 STAFF FINANCE PANEL 
 
 
73 
 
when they are paid as dividends in the future.  
1 
Underlying this principle is the strong 
2 
assumption in capital market theory that 
3 
companies earn the same return on retained 
4 
earnings as the market demands on their common 
5 
stock.  Also fundamental to the DCF methodology 
6 
is the sentiment that cash and/or earnings held 
7 
in the future do not hold the same worth as cash 
8 
or earnings held in the present.  Due to the 
9 
time preference of people to spend their cash 
10 
today, rather than waiting for the effects of 
11 
inflation and productivity to impact future cash 
12 
flows, the DCF discounts the future expected 
13 
cash flows according to investors’ return 
14 
requirements. 
15 
Q.  Please describe the Panel’s DCF methodology and 
16 
 
its result. 
17 
A.  Our DCF model is a two-stage dividend discount 
18 
 
model, which is consistent with the financial 
19 
theory that the value of a company’s stock is 
20 
 
equivalent to its future cash flows.  Our 
21 
dividend discount model forecasts those cash 
22 
flows into the future and discounts them back to 
23 
their present value.  This model embodies less 
24 


--- Page 75 ---
Cases 25-E-0072 and 25-G-0073    
 
 STAFF FINANCE PANEL 
 
 
74 
 
restrictive assumptions than the traditional 
1 
constant growth, or single-stage, DCF 
2 
methodology.  Our multi-stage DCF model is 
3 
preferred because it also explicitly recognizes 
4 
that a company’s short-term growth expectations

CAPITAL ASSET PRICING MODEL METHODOLOGY 
8 
Q.  Please describe the basic theory underlying the 
9 
CAPM. 
10 
A.  The principle behind the CAPM theory is that 
11 
 
there is no premium, in terms of expected 
12 
 
return, for bearing risks that can be eliminated 
13 
 
through diversification.  According to the CAPM, 
14 
 
rational investors will hold a portfolio of 
15 
 
stocks, such that the 
16 
 
overall risk of that portfolio, in terms of the 
17 
 
variability of its returns, closely approximates 
18 
 
that of the market as a whole.  Thus, the only 
19 
risk that matters in the CAPM equation is 
20 
systematic risk, or risk that cannot be 
21 
 
diversified away. 
22 
Q.  Please explain systematic risk. 
23 
A.  Systematic risk is a risk common to all equity 
24 


--- Page 82 ---
Cases 25-E-0072 and 25-G-0073    
 
 STAFF FINANCE PANEL 
 
 
81 
 
 
securities that cannot be eliminated through 
1 
 
diversification.  It is measured by a variable 
2 
 
denominated as beta.  Systematic risk is also 
3 
 
known as market risk, which is the uncertainty 
4 
 
inherent in the entire market or market segment. 
5 
 
Changes in interest rates, recessions, wars, and 
6 
 
pandemics are all examples of systematic risks. 
7 
Q.  What is unsystematic risk? 
8 
A.  Unsystematic risk is the risk that is specific 
9 
to a particular stock.  While it is assumed that 
10 
most stocks tend to go along with the general 
11 
market, at least to some extent, factors 
12 
specific to an individual stock are said to 
13 
affect its unsystematic risk. 
14 
Q.  How does the CAPM measure an individual stock’s 
15 
 
risk? 
16 
A.  According to the CAPM, the appropriate way to 
17 
 
measure an individual stock’s risk is through a 
18 
 
correlation of its return relative to the market 
19 
as a whole, known as beta.  A stock with a beta 
20 
 
of 1.0 has a return that mirrors the return of 
21 
 
the market, usually taken to mean the S&P 500, 
22 
 
as a whole.  Betas of less than 1.0 indicate 
23 
 
that the stocks are less volatile than the 
24 


--- Page 83 ---
Cases 25-E-0072 and 25-G-0073    
 
 STAFF FINANCE PANEL 
 
 
82 
 
 
market, as a whole.  Utility stocks, given the 
1 
 
moderating influence of regulation and the 
2 
 
accompanying perception of reduced risk, 
3 
 
typically have betas below 1.0.  Therefore, the 
4 
 
CAPM informs us that investors will only be 
5 
 
compensated for their actual risk, as measured 
6 
 
by beta.  In the case of estimating the return 
7 
 
requirements of utility investors, the CAPM 
8 
 
result will reflect the degree to which utility 
9 
 
stocks are less volatile than the market, as a 
10 
 
whole. 
11 
Q.  On what assumptions is the CAPM based? 
12 
A.  The model is based on the assumptions that: (1) 
13 
 
the capital market is competitive and efficient; 
14 
 
(2) investors are risk-averse and demand higher 
15 
 
returns for higher risk; (3) market participants 
16 
 
can lend and borrow unlimited amounts at the 
17 
 
risk-free rate of return; and (4) investors 
18 
 
hold diversified portfolios and operate in 
19 
 
capital markets with no transaction costs, 
20 
 
taxes, or restrictions on financial 
21 
transactions.  Under the CAPM, the expected 
22 
return of a security or a portfolio is equal to 
23 
the rate on a risk-free security plus a risk 
24 


--- Page 84 ---
Cases 25-E-0072 and 25-G-0073    
 
 STAFF FINANCE PANEL 
 
 
83 
 
premium multiplied by the asset’s systematic 
1 
risk.
