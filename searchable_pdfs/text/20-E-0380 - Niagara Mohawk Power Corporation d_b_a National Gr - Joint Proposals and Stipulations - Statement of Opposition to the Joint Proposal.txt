II. Procedural Background 
 
Niagara Mohawk Power Corporation (“NiMo” or “Company”) initially filed their tariff 
leaves and testimony on July 31, 2020, setting forth a one-year rate plan ending June 30, 2022. 
Prior to moderation, the increase in electric delivery revenue would have resulted in an average 
residential monthly delivery bill increase of 4.9% for an average 600 kWh per month customer.3 
Additionally, prior to moderation, the increase in gas delivery revenue would have resulted in an 
average residential monthly gas delivery bill increase of 9.8% increase for an average residential 
gas customer utilizing 82 Therms per month.  
The PSC issued a notice of suspension on August 8, 2020, after which discovery was 
begun by Department of Public Service Staff (“DPS Staff”) and interested parties. 
Administrative Law Judges James Costello and Ashley Moreno were appointed to conduct the 
proceeding and convened a procedural and technical conference on September 1, 2020, to 
propose a litigation schedule.  
Public statement hearings were held telephonically and virtually on September 30, 
October 1, October 6, and October 7, 2020. PULP provided statements at such hearings. PULP 
provided expert testimony on November 25, 2020. 
On December 9, 2020, the Company filed a Notice of Impending Settlement Negotiations 
pursuant to 16 NYCRR 3.9. Negotiations took place until September 27, 2021 when a Joint

III. Standard of Review 
 
The standard of review used by the Commission in reviewing settlement agreements is 
found in the Settlement Guidelines.4 The Settlement Guidelines require that all decisions must be 
just and reasonable, and in the public interest. Determining whether the terms of the Joint 
Proposal are in the public interest involves substantive consideration of the following:  
 
1. consistency with the law and regulatory, economic, social and environmental State and 
Commission policies;  
2. whether the terms of the Joint Proposal compare favorably with the likely result of a fully 
litigated case and produces a result within the range of reasonable outcomes;  
3. whether the Joint Proposal fairly balances the interests of ratepayers, investors, and the 
long-term soundness of the utility; and,  
4. whether the Joint Proposal provides a rational basis for the Commission’s decision.5  
The Commission must also give weight to the fact of whether a settlement reflects  
an agreement between normally adversarial parties.6

IV. Terms of the Joint Proposal 
 
PULP opposes the settlement arrived at in the JP. There are several areas of the JP that 
PULP wishes to draw attention to in its statement of opposition. While PULP will comment upon 
every area of the JP, that should not be interpreted to indicate support. 
 
a. Rates 
The Company, Staff and the parties made significant strides to lower rates for consumers 
in the upcoming rate term; however, the lowered rates result from extensive use of re-
collected/un-spent ratepayers funds. The electric revenue requirement increases $49.3, $95.5, 
and $109.8 million over the first three years, and delivery rate of 4.8%, 4.5% and 4.9%.9 The gas 
revenue requirement increase $12.5, $29, and $32.9 million over the first three years, and 
delivery rate increases are 5%, 6.4% and 6.7%. The JP then applies $199.4 million in rate 
moderation funds that reduce increases to electric delivery rate increases of 2.2%, 2.9% and 
2.8%, and gas delivery rate increases to 3.6%, 3.4% and 3.3%.10 The lowering of the rate

b. Low-Income Affordability 
The JP implements the Public Service Commission’s most recent Energy Affordability 
Policy Order12. In accordance with the Order, the Company’s low-income program discounts 
have been increased and customer eligibility for the low-income program has been expanded. 
The affordability programs will also require manual enrollment prior to action by the NYS 
Office of Temporary Disability Assistance (“OTDA”) to improve its ability to provide statewide 
matches to the individual utilities’ low-income customer records. Additionally, the Company 
will exempt low-income/affordability program enrollees from reconnection fees and institute use 
of the AARP Consumer Advocate Checklist to maximize use of federal funds for arrears 
resolution prior to using LIHEAP or state funds.13 
 
c. COVID-19 Provisions 
The JP considers both the short- and long-term impacts of COVID-19 on the economy 
and the financial hardship it has placed on Niagara Mohawk’s customers. In addition to the 
expanded low-income program eligibility, the Company has also made a commitment to work on 
equitable arrears resolution for residential and small business customers in a manner similar to 
that undertaken by National Grid-NYC and Long Island (aka “KEDNY” and “KEDLI”), with a 
goal of arriving at arrears resolution programs to benefit its customers. The Company has also 
paused their Residential Service Terminations/Uncollectible Target customer service 
performance metric for 2021 and 2022. All these actions will help mitigate the bill increases that 
will occur and show that the Company has considered their customers’ financial changes during 
COVID-19.

d. Consumer Protections 
In addition to the COVID-19 and low-income provisions, the JP takes additional actions 
to protect consumers. The Company plans to assess its language access activities in a manner 
similar to that undertaken by KEDNY and KEDLI. The Company has also agreed to implement 
additional cold weather protections and hot weather provisions, suspending electric residential 
terminations during periods of extreme heat.14 
 
e. Advanced Metering Infrastructure 
The JP authorizes the Company (Pursuant to Commission order) to recover $119 million 
in Advanced Metering Infrastructure (“AMI” or “Smart Meter”) deployment and operations and 
maintenance expenses. PULP acknowledges the ability of customers to opt out but continues to 
believe the “Benefit-Cost Analysis” does not reflect benefits for residential consumers that 
outweigh the costs inflicted upon them. PULP consequently continues to believe that the AMI 
rollout should not have been approved because it was not demonstrably in the public interest. 
 
V. Conclusion 
  
As stated above, PULP is not a signatory party of the Joint Proposal, but instead opposes it. 
PULP believes the JP has numerous provisions that are laudable including mitigation of rate 
increases, low-income provisions, COVID-19 considerations, and consumer protections, as well 
as efforts to promote the goals of the Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act 
(“CLCPA”),15 all of which PULP asserts are in the public interest. However, given the ongoing 
economic challenges and massive arrears faced by the Company’s residential and small business 
customers,16 PULP believes the JP taken as a whole is not in the public interest and should not be 
approved unless the Commission modifies it to lower the rate increases.  
While the Company, Staff and the parties worked together to lower the rate increases and 
bill impacts, it necessitated an immense amount of regulatory liabilities (i.e., ratepayer monies)
