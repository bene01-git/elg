II. 
OVERVIEW OF THE JOINT PROPOSAL  
 
As discussed in greater detail below, the Joint Proposal achieves a careful 
balancing of interests among the Signatories that favors customers by providing 
numerous provisions designed to protect and benefit customers, maintain and improve 
Central Hudson’s electric and gas systems so that the Company can continue to 
provide safe and reliable service, and to the greatest extent possible, resolve issues 
raised by the intervening Parties in these proceedings.  For example, the JP features:  
rate moderation to lessen the impacts of the Pandemic on customers; additional 
protections for customers during the winter season and periods of extreme heat; 
expansion of customer support channels for Spanish-speaking customers; performance 
metrics for customer service, gas safety and electric reliability which incent the 


--- Page 10 ---
 
6 
 
Company to continue providing customers with high-quality service; spending on capital 
improvements; and many “one-way” or downward only reconciliations that return any 
underspending by the Company to customers.  Consistent with New York’s energy 
efficiency goals including Commission policy as set forth in the Reforming the Energy 
Vision (“REV”) Proceeding7 and the New Efficiency: New York (“NENY”) Proceeding,8 
and to further align customer and company interests, the JP contains earnings 
adjustment mechanisms (“EAMs”); the inclusion of Energy Efficiency and Heat Pump 
program costs in base rates; cumulative energy savings targets; and a phase-out of the 
natural gas declining block rate.  In addition, as noted in detail below, the Joint Proposal 
contains provisions consistent with the emissions reduction goals of the CLCPA, 
including the development of a greenhouse gas (“GHG”) emissions reduction plan and 
other significant commitments designed to support New York State’s CLCPA goals.  As 
demonstrated by these programs and features, the JP is consistent with the 
Commission’s and New York State’s stated policy objectives to moderate customer bill 
impacts, increase assistance for low- to moderate-income (“LMI”) customers, reduce the 
number of service terminations, reduce the energy burden, and reduce the carbon 
footprint.



IV. 
THE JOINT PROPOSAL SATISFIES THE STANDARD FOR COMMISSION 
REVIEW AND APPROVAL AND SHOULD BE ADOPTED WITHOUT 
MODIFICATION 
 
The Joint Proposal fully satisfies the Commission’s Settlement Guidelines.  First, 
the JP is consistent with the PSL as well as Commission and New York State economic, 
social and environmental policy.  For example, the Joint Proposal contains a number of 
provisions that were specifically included to reduce the Pandemic’s financial impact on 
customers, such as: 1) expanded access to the Company’s Energy Affordability 
Program (“EAP”) which provides bill discounts to eligible low income customers; 2) a 
commitment to convene a collaborative to address residential and small business 
arrears; 3) a $4.55 million COVID-19 Adjustment - Customer Bill Moderation credit; 4) a 
postponement or reduction of certain capital investments and expenses; and 
5) continuation of the Back to Business economic development program which assists 
small businesses that continue to operate or that re-open as the economy is restarting 
during the Pandemic.  The Joint Proposal also includes provisions that are consistent

(e.g., EAMs aimed at programs to incentivize customers to use environmentally 
beneficial technologies, such as air- and ground-source heat pumps and electric 
vehicles (“EVs”)) and a commitment to prepare a report that evaluates how the 
Company may evolve in light of the CLCPA.   
Second, the Joint Proposal produces a result that is rational and is well within the 
range of reasonable outcomes had these proceedings been fully litigated.  As discussed 
in detail below, certain aspects of the Joint Proposal reflect positions advocated by the 
Company, Staff, and other Parties in pre-filed testimony, while other aspects represent 
a compromise among Parties.  Taken as a whole, therefore, the Joint Proposal 
compares favorably with the likely result of full litigation. 
Third, the Joint Proposal strikes a fair balance among the interests of customers, 
the Company, other Signatories, investors and the long-term soundness of the utility.  
For example, the Joint Proposal includes a return on equity (“ROE”) that allows the 
Company to earn a reasonable rate of return, provides for significant investments in the 
gas and electric systems to ensure the continued provision of safe, reliable and 
adequate service, and at the same time uses existing regulatory credits to offset bill 
increases. 
Fourth, the Joint Proposal is supported by the record evidence in this proceeding.  
The Joint Proposal is based on information exchanged during the course of these

The Joint Proposal represents a comprehensive, extensively negotiated and

adopted without modification.
