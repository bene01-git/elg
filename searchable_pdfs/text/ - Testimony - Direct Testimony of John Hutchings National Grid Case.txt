Q: What safety risks exist for Laborers performing work related to natural gas 
20 
transmission and distribution pipeline?  
21

6 
 
A: Working on natural gas facilities poses many significant risks, both to individual worker 
1 
safety and overall system reliability. Working with natural gas is extremely dangerous and, when 
2 
installing or maintaining gas lines, it is critical to minimize the risk of line ruptures or punctures, 
3 
which can cause accidental ignition or explosion. Even absent ignition, the high pressure in the 
4 
pipes could cause life-threatening damage if there were a rupture. Laborers perform the work of 
5 
digging out a line when it is necessary to access the line for repair, maintenance, or replacement. 
6 
While operators using mechanized equipment dig up the area of within two feet of an existing 
7 
transmission lines, Laborers dig the rest by hand. Given the associated risks, this job requires 
8 
painstaking accuracy. Along with rupture, ignition risks, there are concerns when trenching 
9 
about the potential for “cave ins.” This can happen when Laborers or Welders are in the ditch or 
10 
if Laborers or Welders are walking along the ditch above ground along the edge of the ditch. To 
11 
minimize this risk, Laborers employ safeguards, such as using creating shielding or shoring 
12 
around a trench. There are also risks associated with working in extreme climates, as Laborers 
13 
are frequently required to do. Laborers must constantly be aware of their surroundings, and these 
14 
concerns may be heightened when working in poor weather conditions. Working with and 
15 
directing heavy equipment and machinery presents other safety risks. Laborers are responsible 
16 
for acting as the Operator’s eyes because the Operator can’t see everything going on around him. 
17 
For instance, the Laborer must be alert to unexpected things like a child or dog coming near the 
18 
heavy machinery, and be prepared to signal the Operator to stop immediately. Of course, a 
19 
Laborer must also be alert to the possibility that the Operator could hit a line that had not been 
20 
located correctly. Acting as the Operator’s eyes requires having good communication skills and a 
21 
well-established relationship of trust with the Operator. The reason that the latter is important is 
22 
that each party needs to be able to read the other’s signals. Accidents are more likely to occur if 
23

7 
 
hand signals are used incorrectly or there is bad communication with the operator, or a Laborer is 
1 
not anticipating properly what the Operator is going to do.  In addition, because most of this 
2 
work happens in the street, there is always the constant danger of workers being injured by the 
3 
surrounding traffic.

16

17
