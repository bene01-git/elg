18

stations also may be equipped with an additional safety device called a relief

Q. 
WHAT IS THE SUSCEPTIBILITY OF THESE PIPES TO LEAKS? 
3 
A. 
Coated and cathodically protected steel and polyethylene plastic pipes are durable 
4 
and resistant to corrosion, cracking and leaks.  Non-cathodically protected coated 
5 
steel as well as bare steel pipes are prone to corrosion and leaks.  Small diameter 
6 
(less than or equal to 8” in diameter) cast iron pipe has lower beam strength than 
7 
larger diameter cast iron pipe, and is more susceptible to breaks and cracking 
8 
when it loses support.  Each joint or pipe connection is more susceptible to gas 
9 
leakage than the parent pipe and provides an opportunity for gas to leak.  Low 
10 
pressure pipe has an additional problem at joints, and corrosion holes, where not 
11 
only may gas leak out, but water may infiltrate causing gas interruptions or loss of 
12 
service and safety concerns.

Q. 
ARE THERE ANY DESIGN STANDARDS THAT INCORPORATE 
1 
WEATHER-RELATED RISKS INTO THE DESIGN OF UTILITY 
2 
INFRASTRUCTURE? 
3 
A. 
Yes.  Weather-Related and Outside Forces (“WOF”), which include cold weather, 
4 
lightning, heavy rains, floods, and earth movements, are identified in the 
5 
American Society of Mechanical Engineers B31.8S-2004 standard for managing 
6 
the integrity of gas pipelines.  The requirements of this standard are incorporated 
7 
by reference within the Natural Gas Pipeline Safety Code, 49 CFR Part 192.4  The 
8 
standard provides for a comprehensive and systematic management approach to 
9 
address the threats to gas piping systems, reduce the associated risks and improve 
10 
the safety of pipeline systems, with the ultimate goal of reducing the number of 
11 
gas incidents.  Pipeline operators, such as KEDNY, must address this WOF threat 
12 
and consider the history of weather-related events in determining the probability 
13 
of the threat, its related consequences, and risks of failure.
