Q.  Please explain the terms “depreciation” and “net 
23 
salvage” as used in ratemaking.  
24 


--- Page 9 ---
Cases 20-E-0428 and 20-G-0429          STAFF DEPRECIATION PANEL 
 
 
8 
 
A.  Depreciation is a method of recovering capital 
1 
costs, less net salvage, related to plant-in-
2 
service over the expected life of the plant.  
3 
Net salvage is any payment received for the 
4 
asset, less the cost to remove it, after it is 
5 
no longer in service.   
6 
Q.  Please explain the term “average service life” 
7 
(ASL).  
8 
A.  ASL is the arithmetic average of the lives of 
9 
the units of property and is the area under a 
10 
survivor curve from age zero to the maximum life 
11 
divided by 100 percent. 
12 
Q.  What is a “survivor curve”?  
13 
A.  A survivor curve is a graphical indication of 
14 
the percentage of original plant, by age, that 
15 
is still in-service.  Known curves, such as Iowa 
16 
or h-curves, are fit to the observed curve in 
17 
order to help determine an appropriate ASL.  
18 
These known curves include a mode that describes 
19 
the relative height of the curve.  Low mode 
20 
curves have wide retirement patterns, i.e. the 
21 
assets retire over a wide range of lives and 
22 
less retirements occur near the ASL.  Curves 
23 
with higher modes have much narrower retirement 
24 


--- Page 10 ---
Cases 20-E-0428 and 20-G-0429          STAFF DEPRECIATION PANEL 
 
 
9 
 
patterns, with more retirements occurring near 
1 
the ASL.  The area under the survivor curve is 
2 
the average service life or whole life.  The 
3 
observed curves are generated from a mortality 
4 
study that examines the actual retirement 
5 
patterns over multiple years. 
6 
Q.  Please explain the term “depreciation rate.”  
7 
A.  A depreciation rate is an annual percentage rate 
8 
that is applied to the value of gross plant-in-
9 
service, by account, and is used to determine 
10 
the annual depreciation expense. 
11 
Q.  Please explain the term “depreciation expense.”  
12 
A.  Depreciation expense allows for the recovery of 
13 
an investment, less net salvage, over the 
14 
expected life of the investment.  Depreciation 
15 
expense, in any given year, is the product of 
16 
gross plant-in-service multiplied by the 
17 
applicable depreciation rate.   
18 
Q.  Please explain the term “net salvage factor.”  
19 
A.  Net salvage factor refers to the component of 
20 
the depreciation rate that allows recovery of 
21 
estimated net salvage.  It is expressed as a 
22 
percentage, based on the original cost of plant. 
23 
For example, assuming that a unit of property 
24 


--- Page 11 ---
Cases 20-E-0428 and 20-G-0429          STAFF DEPRECIATION PANEL 
 
 
10 
 
has a total installed cost of $1,000, $0 
1 
estimated future salvage, and an estimated cost 
2 
of removal of $500.  The net salvage factor 
3 
would be calculated as follows: Rate = ($0 + (-
4 
$500)) / $1,000.  This equates to a -0.50, or -
5 
50 percent net salvage factor.  In other words, 
6 
it would take another 50 percent over the 
7 
original cost of plant to salvage this unit. 
8 
Q.  What is the “book reserve”?  
9 
A.  The book reserve is the continued accumulation 
10 
of the annual depreciation expense accruals, 
11 
minus any retirements and cost of removal, plus 
12 
any salvage received.  The book reserve is 
13 
calculated by plant account and subtracted from 
14 
the gross plant (original cost of the plant) to 
15 
calculate the net plant, or the remaining plant 
16 
balance that is not yet depreciated. 
17 
Q.  What is the “theoretical reserve”?  
18 
A.  The theoretical reserve is the amount of 
19 
depreciation expense that should have been 
20 
collected as of a particular date, if the given 
21 
survivor curves and ASLs and net salvage factors 
22 
were applied to each plant account throughout 
23 
its entire life.  
24

Q.  How are the depreciation rates calculated?  
1 
A.  The rates are calculated differently depending 
2 
on the depreciation system used.  Each system is 
3 
composed of a method, a procedure, and a 
4 
technique.  
5 
Q.  What depreciation system did Central Hudson use 
6 
for its plant accounts when deriving applicable 
7 
depreciation rates?  
8 
A.  Central Hudson used the straight-line method, 
9 
broad group procedure, and whole life technique 
10 
for its depreciable accounts. 
11 
Q. 
Please explain how the depreciation rate is 
12 
calculated following the whole life technique. 
13 
A.  Under the whole life technique, the depreciation 
14 
rate is calculated using the following formula:  
15 
 
Rate = (1-Net Salvage)/(Average Service Life). 
16 
 
For example, if the ASL for a plant account was 
17 
50 years and the net salvage factor was negative 
18 
50 percent, then the depreciation rate would be: 
19 
R = (1-(-0.5)) / 50.  In such a case, the 
20 
depreciation rate would equal 0.03, or 3.0 
21 
percent.  The rate is then multiplied by the 
22 
gross plant to obtain the depreciation expense 
23 
for ratemaking purposes.  The next step in the 
24 


--- Page 13 ---
Cases 20-E-0428 and 20-G-0429          STAFF DEPRECIATION PANEL 
 
 
12 
 
process for ratemaking purposes is to compute 
1 
composite rates, which are weighted average 
2 
depreciation rates based on gross plant-in-
3 
service as of a specific date for the electric, 
4 
gas, and common accounts.  The composite rates 
5 
are applied to forecasted plant balances in the 
6 
plant-in-service model to calculate the Rate 
7 
Year depreciation expense, which is used to 
8 
determine revenue requirements.
