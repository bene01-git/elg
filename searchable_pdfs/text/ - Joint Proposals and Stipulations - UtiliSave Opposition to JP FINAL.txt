PRELIMINARY STATEMENT 
UtiliSave, LLC (“UtiliSave”) hereby submits this Statement in Opposition of the Joint 
Proposal (“JP”) that Consolidated Edison Company of New York (“Con Edison” or the 
“Company”) filed with the New York State Public Service Commission (“PSC” or the 
“Commission”) on November 5, 2025. The JP advances Company billing practices that are not in 
the public interest because the JP (1) is not consistent with the regulatory, social, and economic 
policies of the Commission and the State and (2) does not strike a fair balance among the interests 
of ratepayers, investors, and the long-term soundness of the utility. 
Importantly, as discussed herein, the Commission and the State have clear, longstanding 
policies seeking utility bill transparency. These policies should be reflected in Con Edison’s 
practices. However, Con Edison’s current tariff, and the one proposed in the JP, lack measures 
which would require the Company to provide valuable data to their customers – insulating Con 
Edison from their responsibility to bill transparently. Ratepayers should have access to, and/or be 
able to request, the backup information that Con Edison uses to render their gas and electricity 
bills, so that billing transparency is ubiquitous – in the Tariff, as well as in the laws and regulations 
of New York State. UtiliSave therefore requests that the Administrative Law Judges (“ALJs”) 
recommend Commission modification and/or rejection of the JP.    
PROCEDURAL HISTORY 
 
Con Edison initiated the instant proceedings on January 31, 2025. UtiliSave and the other 
parties engaged in discovery before continuing settlement negotiations, which culminated with the 
JP.  
 
UtiliSave now submits this Statement in Opposition, which sets forth our basis for seeking 
the JP’s modification and/or rejection. The PSC should render an order remanding the cases to

ARGUMENT 
THE JP IS NOT IN THE PUBLIC INTEREST AND SHOULD BE MODIFIED AND/OR 
REJECTED 
 
“All Commission decisions, including those pertaining to proposed settlements,” such as 
this JP, “must be, and appear to be, just, reasonable, and in the public interest.”1 Importantly, “[t]he 
threshold requirement for any such decision is that it be reached in accordance with applicable 
procedures.”2 If the PSC fails to follow these procedures, “the decision [is] less likely to be in the 
public interest and … will not be perceived as being in the public interest.”3 Procedural soundness 
includes both “the settlement’s consistency with … the regulatory, economic, [and] social … 
policies of the Commission and the State” and “whether the settlement strikes a fair balance among 
the interests of ratepayers and investors and the long-term soundness of the utility….”4  
As discussed herein, the JP does not meet the above requirements for being procedurally 
sound. Therefore, the Commission should modify and/or reject the JP and render an order 
remanding the cases to establish temporary rates pending litigation. 
1. The settlement is not consistent with the regulatory, economic, and social policies of 
the Commission and the State.

• Senate Bill S7869: An act to amend the public service law, in relation to proposed rate 
changes for gas and electric service. 
o “The purpose of this bill is to provide transparency related to proposed changes in 
supply and delivery costs in utility rate cases for gas and electric service.”13 It 
would require the PSC to “make available a one-page summary of major rate cases, 
both after their initiation and upon conclusion, to include a breakdown of the 
proposed changes to the supply and delivery charges, respectively.” 14 According to 
the State, “[a]s rates continue to rise … in record-breaking proposed rate hikes, 
allowing customers to know what portions of that rate increase are due to 
commodity prices and maintenance of the distribution network, will provide 
necessary transparency.”15 
o The JP is not consistent with the State’s policy in S7869 because it fails to 
breakdown the proposed changes to the supply and delivery charges that will be 
reflected on ratepayers’ bills should the Commission accept the JP.  
o To be consistent with the State’s policy in S7869, the JP should be modified to 
contain a breakdown of the proposed changes to each supply and demand 
component that makes up a ratepayer’s bill.

foundational requirement of all billing practices. It is not reasonable or in the standard of 
acceptable business practices that a business should be permitted to assess charges but not provide 
the underlying components of the bill.  
Unfortunately, the JP fails to take these interests into account. Therefore, UtiliSave 
proposes the following modifications to the JP:
