INTRODUCTION 
 
This Statement In Reply To Opposition (Reply Statement) is filed by Department of 
Public Service Staff (Staff) in response to the following general issues raised by the opposing 
parties:1 1) compliance with the Commission’s settlement guidelines,2 2) the general 
affordability of the proposed rate increases, 3) the treatment of the Commission’s energy 
assistance program (EAP)3 and low income customers, compliance with the Climate Leadership 
and Community Protection Act (CLCPA), 4) gas issues, 5) electric issues, and 6) energy 
efficiency (EE) and electrification.  For the reasons stated in Staff’s Statement in Support of the 
Joint Proposal and the reasons stated herein, the Commission has ample grounds to find that the 
Joint Proposal filed on June 14, 2023, in these proceedings is in the public interest.  Accordingly, 
Staff recommends that the Commission adopt the terms of the Joint Proposal and establish rate 
plans for New York Electric and Gas Corporation (NYSEG) and Rochester Gas and Electric 
Corporation (RG&E) as set forth therein. 
 
REPLY TO OPPOSITION 
Joint Proposal Satisfies the Requirements of the Settlement Guidelines

Procedures and Guidelines, establishing its criteria for evaluating proposed rate case 
settlements.4  The Settlement Guidelines states that proposed settlements, like all Commission 
decisions must be and appear to be “just, reasonable, and in the public interest,” and in 
accordance with applicable procedures.5  In addition to these standards, the Commission stated 
that its substantive review of a proposed settlement would consider:  
(1) the settlement's consistency with law and with the regulatory, economic, 
social, and environmental policies of the Commission and the State; (2) whether

Distribution Vegetation Management 
 
AGREE and AARP disagree with the six-year trim cycle for NYSEG’s distribution 
system vegetation management program (VMP) contained in the Joint Proposal because it is not  
the industry standard of a five-year cycle.  AGREE also states that the Joint Proposal does not 
provide evidence or a cost benefit analysis that a six-year cycle over a five-year cycle is in the 
long-term interest of ratepayers.  AARP and AGREE both claim that failure to put NYSEG on a 
five-year full cycle VMP will result in paying high storm recovery costs.  Despite these 
concerns, the VMP expansion that began as part of the 2020 Rate Order has already shown 
improvements in tree outages.  The six-year cycle for NYSEG’s entire territory will further these 
benefits to ratepayers considering that NYSEG is currently closer to a 10-to-12-year cycle 
average for many circuits.  Thus, the ramp up to a six-year cycle is itself a massive effort for 
NYSEG.  With an additional year in the cycle, NYSEG will be able to spread out the remaining 
circuit miles to be reclaimed, while coordinating additional resources to support the increased 
reclamation.  This effort sets a definitive and manageable timeline that will improve customer 
reliability.  By adding an additional year to the five-year cycle, the six-year cycle will reduce the
