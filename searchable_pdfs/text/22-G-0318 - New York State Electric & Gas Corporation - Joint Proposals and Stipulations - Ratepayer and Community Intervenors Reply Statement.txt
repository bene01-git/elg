The Rate Case Joint Proposal is flawed and cannot be approved due to Procedural Errors 
Impacting the Outcome of the NYSEG-RGE Rate Case 
 


--- Page 2 ---
As discussed in more detail below, handling of the matters assigned to a parallel case #22-E-
0298, rather than being directed for participation in the Cases 22-E-0317 et. al. did not meet 
procedural guidelines for settlements.  This has denied participation to hundreds of interested 
parties or their representatives.  
 
Several Joint Proposal Statements and instructions issued this week are relevant. These include 
the Cedar Valley Homeowners Association and AARP Statements of Opposition of June 27, 
2023.  
 
Stipulation from CASES 90-M-0255 and 92-M-0138 were provided, cited and attached by ALJ 
Lecakes in email of July 5, 2023 as applicable in the NYSEG-RGE Rate Cases at issue here, and 
contained the following: 
 
PROCEDURAL GUIDELINES FOR 
SETTLEMENTS (1992) 
Page 2: “The rights of interested parties to participate fully in 
shaping a settlement agreement must be protected, especially when 
early settlement of issues among the major parties is possible. 
Each party need not attend or participate in all negotiating 
sessions, but actual notice to all parties of such meetings is a 
prerequisite for approval by the Commission of a settlement 
agreement and should be protected by regulation.” (emphasis added) 
 
Page 5: “A reasonable time will be given so that 
parties entering negotiations for the first time can 
develop a position.” 
 
However, the Procedural Errors made in these rate cases led to incomplete and unequal 
treatment of complaints in Cases 22-E-0317 et. al. and related Case 22-E-0298, undermined 
the public interest and sidelined hundreds of ratepayers:  
 
Because of how the Cedar Valley Homeowners Association case was handled, they were not 
given due notice and they have lost their opportunity to participate in the NYSEG joint proposal 
negotiations.  
 
Because of how the Northside petition was handled, they have similarly been denied due 
process and have lost their opportunity to participate in the RG&E negotiations. 
 
Creation of a parallel case sidelined interested parties: Parties to Case 22-E-0298 were not 
informed until June 15, 2023 of the inclusion of their case within Cases 22-E-0317 et. al.  
 
Cedar Valley Homeowners Association filed a request for an audit of NYSEG operations and 
complaint about high rates in April 2022, assigned a Case #22-E-0298 but never informed of the 
establishment of that case or of the PSC’s intent to address those case issues with the larger 


--- Page 3 ---
NYSEG-RGE Rate Cases until June 15, 2023, when a notice to that effect was issued but also 
never received by the homeowners association or any of the 326 signatories to the original 
request..  
 
According to the June 15, 2023 Notice Seeking Comments issued by the Commission, “Public 
Service Law §71 requires an investigation into a written complaint signed by no less than 
twenty-five customers.” It goes on to inform members of both cases that, “On May 3, 2022, the 
Cedar Valley Townhomes Association (Petitioners) filed a petition objecting to the rates 
charged by New York State Electric & Gas Corporation (NYSEG) in 2022.  The petition was 
signed by 326 NYSEG customers.” The notice further says: “The Petitioners state that the 
commodity and delivery rates for 2022 are “grossly unreasonable and unsustainable,” and, in 
some cases, constitute a 50% in increase.” The notice also informs the parties in both cases that 
comments for review are requested prior to July 31, 2023.  
 
That copy of the June 15, 2023 Notice Seeking Comments, which the Cedar Valley Townhomes 
Association did not receive directly from DPS and only learned about their case or of the 
existence of any other relevant case in which they might have an interest. after being contacted 
by other parties the week of June 19, 2023. To their knowledge, they were not informed 
directly by DPS of the online Matter Master, the assignment of a case number, or of additional 
process available to them regarding their utility rates. Only on June 15, 2023 was there a notice 
from DPS that,  
 
         “On May 26, 2022, NYSEG filed tariff leaves seeking new 
electric rates in its service territory.  NYSEG’s rates, 
charges, and practices are being reviewed in Case 22-E-0317, the 
proceeding that was initiated as a result of NYSEG’s filing.” 
 
In addition, because Cedar Valley Townhomes was not aware of their ability to receive 
electronic delivery or existence of the matter master, it is not clear whether they ever received 
any notice prior to June 27, 2023, when they were able to register as parties to the rate case 
only a half hour prior to the deadline to file statements of opposition. 
 
Cedar Valley dates: 
 
1). Cedar Valley Townhouse Association original complaint and request for investigation 
of April 23, 2022, with specific requests, sent return receipt requested, to both PSC 
locations, the governor, electeds, and others (Cedar Valley received the post office 
receipt but no mail from the PSC): 
 
Specific requests:  "We insist on reopening an investigation into NYSEGS's application for 
rate increases; knowing how the new rates were determined and justified; a WELL 
ADVERTIZED public hearing; actual (not estimated) monthly meter readings and a 
reduction of current rates, to more reasonable, consumer tolerable levels, while still 
allowing NYSEG to make a reasonable profit."

2) The PSC notice seeking comments of June 15, 2023 to both Cedar Valley and the Rate 
Case. (Which Cedar Valley received from Ratepayer and Community Intervenors but not 
from the PSC). 
 
3) Cedar Valley's Statement of Opposition to all 3 cases (NYSEG Electric, NYSEG Gas, and 
Cedar Valley Case) filed June 27, 2023, filed less than a week after learning about any 
case from Ratepayer and Community Intervenors. Cedar Valley sent a cover letter and 
filed their original complaint/request as a letter of opposition a half hour after being 
recognized as an Intervening party at 4 pm on the day of the deadline for statement of 
support or opposition in the rate case. 
 
 
Unequal treatment of Rochester Parties: However, a similar complaint signed by 132 
signatories by Northside was treated differently and left as one of the 4,214 comments of 
objection that have been filed to the 22-E-0317 record.  
 
That is a complaint filed on April 26, 2022 by the Sisters of Saint Joseph Ministry (also known as 
Northside), attaching their petition that the PSC “follow its legal mandate ‘to protect the utility 
consumers from potential abuse of monopoly power;’ ‘to protect the rights of New Yorkers 
while ensuring access to reliable and low-income utility services;’ and to ‘ensure access to 
affordable, reliable, sustainable and modern energy for all.’” The Northside complaint was not 
assigned a case number, although it had 132 signatories (well above the 25-customer 
threshold) and was submitted multiple times by various complainants from Rochester. 
Procedural errors impact the outcome of the Joint Proposal and undermined the Public 
Interest: On the one hand, the Northside complaint was not assigned a case number, although 
it had 132 signatories (well above the 25-customer threshold). On the other hand, the Cedar 
Valley Association was not informed of their need to register as parties to the 20-E-0317 case in 
order to have their complaint included in the relevant matter where it will be addressed until a 
few weeks ago. Each of these constituted errors of impact to the Joint Proposal and 
undermined the public interest. 
Consclusion: Ratepayer and Community Intervenors submits this Reply Statement as further 
information indicating that the NYSEG RGE Joint Proposal should be denied until such time as 
these matters, as well as the matters addressed in our Reply Statement made with the joint 
Consumer and Environmental Parties, can be addressed in a meaningful way that is consistent 
with all relevant laws and stipulations for the handling of Rate Cases in NYS.
