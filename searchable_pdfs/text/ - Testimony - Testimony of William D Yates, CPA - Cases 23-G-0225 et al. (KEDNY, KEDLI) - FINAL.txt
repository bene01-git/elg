Purpose Of Testimony 
20 
Q.  
What is the purpose of your testimony in these proceedings? 
21 
A. 
The purpose of my testimony is to provide evidence demonstrating that the affordability of 
22 
gas utility service rendered by the Companies to a potentially large number of low-income 
23 
households in their service areas would be further jeopardized by the delivery rate increases 
24 
requested in these proceedings, which for the average usage heating customers of National 
25 
Grid USA’s New York City and Long Island gas utilities (hereinafter “KEDNY” and 
26 
“KEDLI”, respectively) would amount to 29% and 31%, respectively; and for the average 
27

6 
 
usage non-heating customers of KEDNY and KEDLI would amount to 38% and 35%, 
1 
respectively. It is also to furnish recommendations that, if adopted, could help reduce the 
2 
scale of such requests and improve the customer service experience of some of KEDNY 
3 
and KEDLI’s most vulnerable customers. 
4 
Based on the most recent data available from the U.S. Census Bureau, I estimate that gas 
5 
energy burdens among low-income households in the KEDNY and KEDLI service areas 
6 
averaged 4.1% and 5.9%, respectively in 2021. I also estimate that overall energy burdens 
7 
(electric, gas and deliverable fuel) for these households averaged 10.0% in the KEDNY 
8 
service area and 13.8% in the KEDLI service area. To the extent that these households 
9 
represent KEDNY/KEDLI customers eligible for, but not enrolled in, the Companies’ 
10 
energy affordability programs (“EAPs”), the requested residential rate increases would 
11 
result in an unacceptable exacerbation of gas energy burdens for the affected households.  
12 
Adding weight to my affordability concerns is the persistent state of elevated arrears for 
13 
those KEDNY/KEDLI customers not enrolled in EAP. Collections Activity Reports 
14 
(“CARs”) for the “trailing twelve months”, or “TTM” ended June 30, 2023, show that the 
15 
average arrears of these “Non-EAP” customers were 150% more than pre-pandemic levels 
16 
at KEDNY while almost 130% higher at KEDLI. This was the case even after the 
17 
application of arrears relief under the Commission’s laudable efforts in 2022 and 2023 to 
18 
provide extensive assistance to residential customers impacted by the COVID-19 
19 
pandemic. 
20 
To the extent that high-energy burden households in the Companies’ service areas are EAP-
21 
eligible customers of the Companies who are not current participants, one of the best ways 
22 
to avert a further, harmful spike in their energy burdens is get them enrolled. In this regard, 
23 
while there has been incremental progress in EAP enrollment at KEDLI since 2019, 
24 
KEDNY participation, which averaged about 155,000 customers in 2019, dropped to 
25 
approximately 148,000 participants by June, 2023.   
26 
To further protect low-income households and disadvantaged communities (“DACs”) in 
27 
the KEDNY/KEDLI service areas from harmful rate increases they cannot afford, I 
28 
recommend that in these proceedings the parties and the Commission endeavor to limit 
29 
residential bill impacts to the minimum necessary to ensure safe, reliable service at just and

Findings 
4 
Q.  
What are the major findings of your testimony in these proceedings? 
5 
A. 
My testimony examines affordability and customer service in several areas, including but 
6 
not limited to evidence that: 
7 
1. For average usage residential ratepayers, the Companies’ requested residential gas 
8 
rate increases and accompanying increases in monthly fixed charges would impose 
9 
higher heating delivery bills of 29% at KEDNY and 31% at KEDLI. The equivalent 
10 
bill impacts proposed for non-heating customers would be 38% at KEDNY and 
11 
35% at KEDLI. Non-Heating EAP participants of both companies would 
12 
experience even higher percentage increases, since non-heating EAP discounts are 
13 
not updated to reflect the bill impacts of new rates in the same manner as are heating 
14 
discounts. 
15 
2. Low-income households in the KEDNY and KEDLI service areas were already 
16 
burdened by utility gas bills taking up a far higher percentage of their incomes in 
17 
2021 than the maximum 3% threshold for utility gas affordability targeted by the 
18 
New York State Public Service Commission (“Commission”), and its 6% burden 
19 
limit for overall electric and gas utility service, plus deliverable fuel costs. By 
20 
analyzing data provided by the U.S. Census Bureau, I estimate that in 2021 about 
21 
300,000 low-income households in the KEDNY service area spent an average of 
22 
4.1% of their incomes to meet their utility gas service needs, 37% higher than the 
23 
Commission’s gas policy goal. I further estimate that about 77,000 low-income 
24 
households in the KEDLI service area suffered average utility gas burdens of about 
25 
5.9%, 97% higher than Commission policy. Finally, I estimate that there were about 
26 
340,000 such households in the KEDNY service area whose overall electric, gas 
27 
and deliverable fuel energy burdens averaged 10.0% (67% higher than Commission 
28

A. 
I recommend several actions that should be taken to improve utility affordability and 
1 
customer service for many of the Companies’ customers, including but not limited to the 
2 
following: 
3 
Affordability 
4 
1. In these proceedings, the parties and the Commission should endeavor to limit 
5 
residential bill impacts to the minimum necessary to ensure safe, reliable service at 
6 
just and reasonable rates; and to achieve statewide policy objectives such as low-
7 
income energy affordability and the requirements of the CLCPA. This effort should 
8 
include, but not be limited to: 
9 
• Basing the Companies’ authorized return on equity (“ROE”) upon the 
10 
Commission’s “Generic Financing Methodology”2 in a one-year case. In 
11 
any multi-year settlement resulting from these proceedings, the Companies’ 
12 
ROE for each rate year should be set and trued-up based upon, but not 
13 
exclusively reliant upon, updated cost of equity calculations produced by 
14 
DPS Staff’s Generic Finance Model (“GFM”). Such a framework would 
15 
include an evidentiary process providing the opportunity for all parties to 
16 
submit interrogatories, file witness testimony and conduct cross-
17 
examination; and would incorporate separate consideration of all factors 
18 
affecting KEDNY/KEDLI’s ability to maintain their current credit ratings 
19 
and to raise capital most economically. 
20 
• Exploring alternative rate designs that would, at a minimum, freeze monthly 
21 
fixed charges for gas, reduce emissions which contribute to climate change 
22 
(especially in areas designated by the CLCPA as “disadvantaged 
23 
communities”) and, more generally, reward conservation and energy 
24
