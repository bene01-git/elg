I. 
UIU’S REPLY TO MISCHARACTERIZATION OF UIU’S NEUTRALITY ON 
OTHER PARTS OF THE JOINT PROPOSAL 
The Utility Intervention Unit (“UIU”) of the New York State Department of State’s 
Division of Consumer Protection submits this reply to one statement in opposition to the Joint 
Proposal filed by New York State Electric & Gas Corporation and Rochester Gas and Electric 
Corporation (“NYSEG and RGE” or the “Companies”) in the above-captioned proceedings on 
June 14, 2023. UIU submits this reply to address a mischaracterization of UIU’s intent when 
expressing neutrality on other parts of the Joint Proposal, for the purposes of clarifying the record 
before the Public Service Commission. 
On June 27, 2023, several parties submitted a joint Statement in Opposition of the Joint 
Proposal (“Statement in Opposition”). Those parties include Alliance for a Green Economy 
(“AGREE”), Fossil Free Tompkins (“FFT”), Ratepayer and Community Intervenors, Climate 
Solutions Accelerator, and Campaign for Renewable Energy (“AGREE et al.”). While the 
Statement in Opposition does not address the sole issue of UIU’s support—revenue allocation—
the statement seeks to imply that UIU takes a position on the Joint Proposal’s Climate Leadership 
and Community Protection Act (“CLCPA”) provisions. On June 16, 2023, AARP sent an 
interrogatory to all signatory parties requesting explanations on how the Joint Proposal is 
consistent with, supportive of, and in furtherance of the objectives of the CLCPA. On June 26, 
2023, UIU replied to those inquiries with the following objection, as the question was outside the 
scope of UIU’s support of the Joint Proposal: “UIU objects to this question as the subject matter 
falls outside the scope of UIU’s support of the Joint Proposal. UIU’s support is limited to NYSEG 
and RGE electric and gas revenue allocation, which is stated on UIU’s signature page to the Joint 
Proposal.”1  
When characterizing UIU’s response to this interrogatory, AGREE et al. characterizes 
UIU’s response as “hasten[ing] to distance” itself “from the responsibility or knowledge, 
particularly related to either the CLCPA or disadvantaged communities in responses to American

To clarify the record before the Commission, UIU asserts once again that, as stated in 
UIU’s Limited Statement in Support, “UIU does not take a position on any other part of the Joint 
Proposal or the Joint Proposal when taken together as a whole.”3 This is an important distinction 
because UIU has, in good faith, been consistent in this record that UIU’s issue areas of engagement 
in this matter are on the topics of rate design, revenue allocation, and customer service. UIU’s 
determination to support the revenue allocation outcome in the Joint Proposal represents a better 
outcome for consumers.  
II. 
CONCLUSION 
With this reply, UIU wishes to clarify that UIU’s support of the Joint Proposal is limited 
to NYSEG and RGE electric and gas revenue allocation, which is stated on UIU’s signature page 
to the Joint Proposal, as well as that UIU does not take a position on any other part of the Joint 
Proposal or the Joint Proposal when taken together as a whole.
