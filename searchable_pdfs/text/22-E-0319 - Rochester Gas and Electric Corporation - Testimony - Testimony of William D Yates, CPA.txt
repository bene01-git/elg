Purpose Of Testimony 
14 
Q.  
What is the purpose of your testimony in these proceedings? 
15 
A. 
The purpose of my testimony is to provide evidence demonstrating that the affordability of 
16 
electric and gas utility service rendered by the Companies to their low-income households 
17 
would be further jeopardized by the excessive rate increases requested in these 
18 
proceedings. It is also to furnish recommendations that, if adopted, could help reduce the 
19 
scale of such requests and improve the customer service experience of some of NYSEG 
20 
and RG&E’s most vulnerable customers. 
21 
The Companies’ requested residential rate increases, and accompanying double-digit 
22 
percentage increases in monthly fixed charges for all business units except RG&E electric, 
23 
would result in an unacceptable exacerbation of the energy burdens of the estimated 
24 
350,000 low-income households in the NYSEG and RG&E service areas already saddled 
25 
with energy bills taking up far higher percentages of their incomes than the maximum 6% 
26 
threshold for low-income affordability targeted by the New York State Public Service 
27 
Commission (“Commission”). Indeed, for a majority of these households, 43 – 44% of 
28 
whom have annual incomes under $20,000, utility bills are not affordable now. For 
29

residential ratepayers generally, NYSEG’s proposals would result in 34.9% higher electric 
1 
and 17.4% higher gas delivery charges; while for the average RG&E residential customer, 
2 
electric delivery charges would increase 22.1% and gas delivery charges by 18.7%. 
3 
However, percentage increases for those low-income households enrolled in the 
4 
Companies’ energy affordability programs (“EAP”) would be much higher.1 Tier 3 EAP 
5 
participants—some 75,150 customers who account for over half of EAP enrollment—

Multiple additional factors also confront low-income households already having difficulty 
8 
paying their NYSEG and RG&E bills. The initial economic shock of the COVID-19 
9

11

12 
NYSEG arrears almost tripled, from $26.1 million to $74.0 million; while RG&E’s arrears 
13 
almost doubled, from $33.6 million to $66.6 million. Concurrently, costs for policy 
14 
initiatives such as energy efficiency and implementation of measures designed to achieve 
15 
the goals of the Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act (“CLCPA"), which are 
16 
beyond the scope of decisions to be made in these proceedings, together with ever-
17 
escalating deferred storm costs at NYSEG, have imposed, and will continue to impose at 
18 
an accelerating pace, material additional burdens on the Companies’ low-income 
19 
households for the foreseeable future. 
20 
The Commission’s recent efforts to increase bill discounts and implement arrears reduction 
21 
programs for NYSEG and RG&E energy affordability program (“EAP”) participants, 
22 
when combined with significant federal relief administered through OTDA’s Home Energy 
23 
Assistance Program (“HEAP”)-related programs, can be expected to have a positive, if 
24 
largely temporary, impact on utility affordability for EAP participants, depending on the 
25

26 
assistance (all but the increase in EAP discounts) is one-time in nature and thus does not 
27 
address the persistent affordability challenges faced by these and other low-income 
28 
households in the Companies’ service areas.

4 
A. 
My testimony examines affordability and customer service in several areas, including but 
5 
not limited to evidence that: 
6 
1. The Companies’ requested residential rate increases and accompanying double-
7 
digit percentage increase in monthly fixed charges for all business units except 
8

9 
low-income households and DACs in the NYSEG and RG&E service areas. For 
10

11

12
