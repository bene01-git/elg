adopted a Joint Proposal that, among other things, established a 
Capital Reconciliation Mechanism.  KEDNY’s actual net plant 
balance was to be reconciled downward only to the Joint 
Proposal’s established net plant target.1  The Capital 
Reconciliation Mechanism established a cumulative reconciliation

BACKGROUND 
 
 
By Order issued December 21, 2007 in Case 06-G-1185, 
the Commission adopted a five-year rate plan for KEDNY for 
January 1, 2008 and ending December 31, 2012.2  In its June 13 
Order, the Commission extended KEDNY’s rate plan with 
modifications beginning January 1, 2013.  The modified rate plan 
established capital investment levels of $320.1 million for 2013 
and $293.7 million for 2014.  The modified rate plan did not 
allow for the recovery of any incremental expenditure after 
December 31, 2014.   
 
 
The Capital Reconciliation Mechanism requires KEDNY to 
establish a regulatory liability for the future benefit of 
customers to the extent that its net utility plant, inclusive of 
allocated common plant and depreciation expense, falls below the 
Joint Proposal’s combined target average net utility plant and 
depreciation expense revenue requirement.  The Capital 
Reconciliation Mechanism is a downward only reconciliation 
mechanism; therefore, KEDNY is not allowed to establish a 
regulatory asset for any increase in revenue requirement that 
results from the actual net utility plant and depreciation 
expense exceeding the set targets.

PETITION 
KEDNY claims that it largely completed its 2013–2014 
capital program as forecast.  KEDNY, however, states its ability 
to launch the program was severely hampered by Superstorm Sandy 
and its aftermath and, as a result, many capital projects were 
significantly delayed.  KEDNY’s capital program in the fourth 
quarter of 2012 was approximately $60 million under forecast.  
Delays continued through the first eight months of 2013 ($55.6 
million under forecast) because KEDNY claims it was forced to 
devote resources that would have been used to complete capital 
projects to restoring service, maintaining basic reliability 
with the storm-damaged system and otherwise remediating the 
impacts of the storm.  Due to Superstorm Sandy, KEDNY was in 
total $36.131 million behind schedule in capital spending in 
2013.  In 2014, KEDNY claims it attempted to complete the 
capital projects originally forecast to be placed in service in 
2013 as well as those forecast for 2014.  In 2014, KEDNY states 
that it over-spent its budget by $16.159 million.  KEDNY also 
states that its cumulative capital spending was within $20 
million or 3.3 percent of the cumulative two year forecast for 
2013–2014 reflected in its delivery rates.  Ultimately, it was 
both the delays in getting projects completed in late 2012 as 
well as booking projects as completed that led to the $58.069 
million deferred credit balance.   
The project delays attributable to Superstorm Sandy 
and accounting delays in completing the process of closing 
completed capital projects to plant in service created a 
significant difference between the targeted revenue requirement 
associated with the forecast capital expenditures for 2013 and 
2014 and the actual revenue requirement associated with the 
actual expenditures.  KEDNY calculates, and Staff has confirmed,

Pipeline Integrity Expenditures 
In response to the Pipeline Safety Improvement Act of 
2002, KEDNY developed its transmission pipeline Integrity 
Management Plan (IMP) to identify and address defects affecting 
the physical soundness of the Company’s facilities before they 
become safety/performance problems.  The IMP requires the 
Company to conduct baseline and periodic assessments of its 
transmission facilities.  Although the costs of tests and 
inspections under the IMP are operating expenses, the Company 
plans capital work to support in-line inspections, such as for 
the installation of pig launchers and receivers, for pipe 
reconfiguration/removal, and to resolve issues discovered during 
physical inspections.  KEDNY forecasts capital expenditures on 
these programs of $6.555 million in 2015 and $6.800 million in 
2016.   
System Reinforcement, Reliability and Pressure Regulation 
Projects 
As a result of growth in peak demand, KEDNY plans to 
complete a number of projects to ensure its ability to meet its 
firm customer requirements is maintained throughout the gas 
distribution network during peak periods.  KEDNY also proposes 
to perform replacement and modernization work at its Greenpoint 
Liquefied Natural Gas (LNG) facility,4 such as updating the 
electrical switchgear and breakers of which some equipment dates 
back to the 1920s; replacing the existing foundation heating 
system; replacing vaporizers 3 and 4; stabilizing the dike at 
the facility to prevent erosion; and repaving the facility.  
KEDNY forecasts capital expenditures on these projects of 
$45.096 million in 2015 and $47.209 million in 2016.

Growth-Related Spending 
With the low cost of natural gas commodity coupled 
with New York City mandates to phase out the use of No. 4 and 
No. 6 oil, KEDNY claims it is experiencing significant growth 
and must make significant capital investments in mains, services 
and system reinforcements.  The customer growth investments in 
Northern Queens alone are forecast at $72.124 million in 2015 
and $33.250 million in 2016 to upgrade its transmission and 
distribution facilities.  The Northern Queens Project will 
involve the installation of over six miles of 20-inch 
transmission main, 2,000 feet of distribution main and other 
system upgrades that will increase capacity and reliability in a 
constrained area of the Company’s system to support future load 
growth and reduce service interruptions for non-firm customers.

DISCUSSION 
 
 
The project delays attributable to Superstorm Sandy 
and accounting delays in closing completed capital projects to 
plant in service created a significant difference between the 
targeted revenue requirement associated with the forecast 


--- Page 12 ---
CASE 12-G-0544 
 
 
-12- 
capital expenditures for 2013 and 2014 and the actual revenue 
requirement associated with the actual expenditures.  As a 
result of the current Capital Reconciliation Mechanism, KEDNY 
calculated a deferred customer credit balance of $58.069 million 
as of December 31, 2014.  We believe it is reasonable to use 
this deferred credit balance to offset the revenue requirement 
associated with increased capital expenditures in 2015 and 2016. 
 
 
KEDNY’s proposal is in the public interest as it will 
provide customers with enhanced system safety and reliability 
without an immediate rate impact.  We therefore approve, with 
modifications, KEDNY’s request to modify its Capital 
Reconciliation Mechanism.  KEDNY’s petition should be limited to 
growth, mandated, reliability, and policy related capital 
expenditures.  Therefore, in granting relief to the Company, we 
modify KEDNY’s proposed capital investment plan for 2015 and 
2016. 
 
 
We find KEDNY’s proposed projects and programs in the 
overall 2015 and 2016 capital expenditure budget to be 
reasonable; however, we have the following modifications to the 
Company’s proposed capital program as shown in the attached 
Appendix.  First, KEDNY proposed spending of $9.000 million for 
Cast Iron Lining.  We are supportive of this technology; 
however, we are not confident that this program can be ramped up 
to the level of spending proposed by the Company.  We, 
therefore, allow funding up to $2.0 million annually for the 
Cast Iron Lining program.  We would like to further review this 
program based on the utility’s performance in 2015 and 2016 and 
then determine the appropriate funding for this program in the 
Company’s upcoming rate case.       
 
 
Second, KEDNY proposed spending of $17.025 million for 
the Newtown Creek Renewable Natural Gas Project.  The projected 
equivalent cost of gas from this process will be approximately

$12.14 per decatherm versus the otherwise current weighted 
average cost of gas for KEDNY of approximately $3.37 per 
decatherm.  A full rate case review of this project better 
provides all parties with the opportunity to examine and explore 
potential additional cost saving measures to further improve the

Company is invited and encouraged to propose new technologies, 
such as these projects, in its next rate filing.   
 
 
Third, we believe there is a need to accelerate the 
fire suppression system upgrade at the Greenpoint LNG Plant.  As 
a result of Superstorm Sandy, the fire suppression system at 
this facility was inoperable because of severe flooding.  In 
compliance with New York City fire code, KEDNY is unable to 
produce LNG if the fire suppression system is not properly 
functioning.  The lack of LNG production could pose a major 
reliability threat to KEDNY’s system as LNG has played a 
significant role in the Company’s ability to supply 
unprecedented volumes of gas during record breaking cold spells 
over the past two winters.  We, therefore, require that KEDNY 
reprioritize spending at the Greenpoint LNG Plant in order to 
enhance storm resiliency of the fire suppression system.

Enhanced Proactive Main Replacement 
 
 
We accept KEDNY’s proposal to replace 40 miles of leak 
prone pipe in 2015 and 47 miles of leak prone pipe in 2016 in 
order to further reduce leak rates and methane emissions on its 
system; however, we believe there is a need for further 
acceleration of leak prone pipe replacement in KEDNY’s service 
territory.  Due to our adjustments to the allowed capital 
expenditure budgets in 2015 and 2016, the Company may include 
additional expenditures for incremental leak prone pipe 
replacement beyond the targets previously stated, as long as the 
Company demonstrates that the incremental investments were made 
at reasonable costs prioritized by the risk ranking model. 
Moreover, if the Company does not file for rates 
before April 1, 2016, we will require a target of 50 miles of 
leak prone pipe removal for 2017 with a ramp up of 5 miles per 
year every calendar year thereafter until modified by the 
Commission. 
Earnings Sharing Mechanism 
 
 
There are no changes to KEDNY’s earnings sharing 
provisions that are contained in the Joint Proposal and approved 
in June 13 Order for the 2015 and 2016 rate years.  The KEDNY 
rate plan provided for sharing of 80 percent to customers and 20 
percent to shareholders when exceeding a 9.40 percent return on 
equity for the rate years 2013 and 2014.  Consistent with the 
Joint Proposal, because the Company did not file for new base 
rates that would have become effective no later than January 1, 
2016, the earnings sharing mechanism is required to be modified 
as of calendar year 2015 to require 100 percent of any return on 
equity above 9.40 percent be credited against the Site

Investigation and Remediation (SIR) deferral balance, and will 
continue prospectively until new base rates are in effect.6   
 
 
KEDNY’s proposed treatment of capital expenditure 
revenue requirements should not result in the Company earning a 
return on equity that exceeds its allowed return of 9.4 percent.  
In the event that its return on equity does exceed the 9.4 
percent threshold in any year, the Company will be required to 
limit the revenue requirement recovery so that the allowed 
return on equity is not exceeded.  
 
CONCLUSION 
 
 
Extending the Capital Reconciliation Mechanism period 
from two years to four years will facilitate the Company’s plan 
to increase investment in its distribution system infrastructure 
during calendar years 2015 and 2016 without creating any 
immediate rate impacts.  The modified capital investments will 
increase safety and reliability, enable the Company to modernize 
its gas system, promote gas network enhancement, and enhance 
storm resiliency in a manner consistent with our policy 
objectives. 
 
The Commission orders: 
1. KEDNY’s petition requesting modification to its 
Capital Expenditures and Net Utility Plant and Depreciation 
Expense Reconciliation Mechanism is granted as modified 
consistent with the Discussion and Conclusion in this Order.   
2. If the Company does not file for rates prior to 
April 1, 2016, the leak prone pipe removal target will be set at 
50 miles for calendar year 2017 with an increase of 5 miles per 
year every calendar year thereafter.
