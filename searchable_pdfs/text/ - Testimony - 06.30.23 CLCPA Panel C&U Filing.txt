The KEDLI GIOP panel describes hydrogen production and delivery to 844 customers in the 
Point Lookout section of Hempstead, NY.
a.
What greenhouse gas emission reductions are projected with this project? Please provide
the methodology involved in calculating the emissions reductions and please include all
greenhouse gases, not just carbon reductions.
b.
How much will this project cost in terms of dollars per ton of greenhouse gas emissions
reduced?
c.
Has the company compared the projected costs of GHG emission reductions to the cost of
GHG emissions that could be achieved through full electrification for the 844 customers
in the Point Lookout section of Hempstead, NY?
Response: 
a.
The Direct Testimony of Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act Panel
provides the Companies’ GHG analysis methodology.  The equations and emission
factors used to calculate greenhouse gas emissions were sourced from the Energy Sector
Greenhouse Gas Emission Under the New York State Climate Act: 1990-2020, Table 25
and 26.  The estimated GHG reductions for the HyGrid demonstration project are 518
MT annually based on the 20% blend rate.  In preparing this response, the Company
identified an inadvertent error in the initial greenhouse gas emissions reduction
calculation originally provided in Exhibit ___ (CLCPA-4).  The Company will reflect
this revision in its Corrections and Updates filing.

b.
The project is a proof-of-concept demonstration project and, therefore, the costs are not 
representative of a fully developed market and the potential for federal incentives to 
reduce costs.  The estimated cost of the demonstration project based on the current 
pricing of electrolysis and related components is $13.291 million and assumes an 
electrolysis cost of $1,742 per/kW plus the cost to install which is indicative of low 
volume production.   
The U.S. Department of Energy (“DOE”) has adopted the Hydrogen Shot initiative and 
established a technology target of $1 per/kg of hydrogen in one decade. See 
https://www.energy.gov/eere/fuelcells/hydrogen-shot.  In addition, DOE set a target of 
$300 per/kW for electrolysis by 2025 and $150 per/kW by 2030.  If the HyGrid project 
were priced at targeted 2025 prices, the total project cost would be less than $7 million or 
$1,350 capital per metric ton of greenhouse gas emissions reductions over ten years at 
20% blending before tax credits.  In addition, the HyGrid project is expected to be 
eligible for a Production Tax Credit of $3 per/kg, or approximately $140,000 per/year, 
which has a twenty-year present value of $2.0 million. 
c.
As mentioned, the HyGrid project is a proof-of-concept demonstration project on a local 
scale and, therefore, it would not be instructive to compare the estimated per-unit costs of 
this project to electrification costs; however, future cost comparisons could be developed 
based on learnings from these types of demonstration projects.  The Point Lookout 
neighborhood was developed in the 1920s as a community of summer bungalows and has 
since developed into full-year residences heated primarily with gas hydronic or steam 
heating systems.  Multiple estimates have shown that the cost to retrofit a hydronically 
heated single-family residence with an air-source heat pump in this region can exceed 
$9,275 per home and be as high as $32,800 when including required weatherization to 
accommodate the air-source heat pumps.  See Pathways to Carbon-Neutral NYC, at page 
43, available at the NYC.gov website.  The cost of a ground source system would be 
much higher and is not included for comparison purposes.     
Assuming each of the 844 gas customers simultaneously replaced their natural gas 
heating system with a minimally compliant air-source heat pump, and assuming no 
building envelope improvements, the Company estimates customers would collectively 
incur an equipment cost of at least $7.8 million excluding costs of weatherization and 
appliance replacement.  If the costs of weatherization needed to accommodate the heat 
pump are included, the Company estimates customer-sided costs increase to $27.68 
million.  These costs do not include any associated electric distribution system upgrades 
that may be required, and a complete comparison would require a detailed analysis of 
those upgrades and their estimated costs.  The associated greenhouse gas emissions 
reductions and relative costs will be primarily dependent on the broader and quicker 
availability of renewable power than the HyGrid project requires and will also be 
dependent on future economies of scale.
