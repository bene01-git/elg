I. 
INTRODUCTION 
 
This initial brief is submitted by the Long Island Power Authority (“LIPA” or 
“Authority”).  The Authority, a political subdivision of the State of New York, is a publicly-
owned utility operated solely for the benefit of its customers.  It has no shareholders.  As a 
public power utility, it is formed, owned, and operated for the benefit of the people of Long 
Island.  The Authority provides electric service to 1.1 million customers in Nassau and 
Suffolk Counties and the Rockaway Peninsula.   
The Authority acquired the electric transmission and distribution system of the Long 
Island Lighting Company (“LILCO”) in 1998 and has since supplied the crucial electric 
infrastructure for the people of Long Island.  While the Authority is the successor in 
ownership to LILCO’s electric system, National Grid Generation, LLC is the successor in 
ownership to LILCO’s legacy generating plants, and KeySpan Gas East Corp. d/b/a National 
Grid (“KEDLI”) is the local gas distribution company (“LDC”).

The Authority is responsible for providing the fuel used by many of the electric 
generators under contract to it, and all the natural gas used by those plants is delivered by 
KEDLI.  The cost of local gas transportation is part of the cost of electric service to the 
Authority’s customers.  The Authority’s testimony in these cases (and prior KEDLI rate 
cases) focused on the propriety of the Companies’ transportation rates for electric 
generation.1  The Authority’s testimony requested that the rates for gas transportation to 
electric generators be reformed in this proceeding, and not continue frozen waiting for a 
resolution of the ongoing generic case devoted to this question.  However, DPS Staff just 
recently issued its white paper on rate design for electric generators in the generic case.2  
Given this welcome development, the Authority now urges that all necessary steps be taken 
in this proceeding to allow KEDLI to implement any Public Service Commission 
(“Commission”) order issued in the generic proceeding as quickly as possible.

determined in an initial findings report “that a single component of delivery service to electric
