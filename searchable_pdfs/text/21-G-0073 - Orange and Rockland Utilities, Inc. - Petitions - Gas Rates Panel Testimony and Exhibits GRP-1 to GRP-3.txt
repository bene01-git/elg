Q. 
Please describe the functionalization and 
1 
classification step. 
2 
A. 
The functionalization and classification step assigns 
3 
the broad accounting-based cost categories to the more 
4 
detailed categories used in the ECOS study.  This 
5 
breakdown is required, for example, to differentiate 
6 
distribution-demand related costs from distribution-
7 
customer related costs.  During the process of 
8 
functionalization, all costs are classified as being 
9 
demand-related, commodity-related, and customer-
10 
related.  Demand-related costs are fixed costs caused 
11 
by the peak hourly loads placed on the various 
12 
components of the gas system.  Commodity-related costs 
13 
are variable costs caused by the total quantities of 
14 
gas delivered during the year.  Customer-related costs 
15 
are fixed costs caused by the presence of customers 
16 
connected to the system, regardless of usage.   
17 
Q.

18 
A. 
This step allocates the functionalized and classified 
19 
costs to the customer classes based on the appropriate 
20 
demand, commodity, customer or revenue allocation 
21 
factors, which are shown on Table 7 of the ECOS study.

I – SUMMARY 
 
This Embedded Cost of Service (ECOS) study allocates Orange and Rockland Utilities, Inc. Gas 
Department costs among service classifications (SC's) 1 and 2, based on an analysis of the rate 
base, including book cost of plant, and the operating expenses, including operation and 
maintenance expenses for the calendar year 2019. The ECOS study methodology is based on a 
two-step procedure.  First, the costs are functionalized and classified to Operating Functions, as 
shown on Tables 2 through 5. Along with the traditional functions, the study also includes fully 
unbundled competitive functions, i.e., the merchant function and billing and payment processing. 
The costs within each function are then allocated to Customer Classes based on appropriate 
physical quantities, such as CCF of gas sales, or other appropriate bases of allocation, such as book 
cost of meters and customer bills.  The bases of cost allocation are shown in detail on Table 7 as 
Allocation Factors and are further described below.  The details of allocations by customer class 
are shown on Tables 2 through 5.  The results of the cost allocation study are combined with class 
revenues to yield the rate of return statement by class shown on Table 1.  For use in rate design, 
the monthly average Customer Costs by class are shown on Table 6. The costs allocated in this 
study were adjusted for the following items: (1) the revenues reflect current rates, i.e. rates 
effective January 1, 2021; (2) revenues have been reduced to exclude the Gas Supply Charge 
(GSC), the Monthly Gas Adjustments (MGA), the System Benefit Charge (SBC) and the 
uncollectible component of the merchant function charge (“MFC”). The corresponding gas 
Exhibit___GRP-1

expenses have also been excluded; (3) interruptible customers are not represented in the study; (4) 
firm transportation customers have been priced as sales customers; and (5) for purposes of 
unbundling, total revenues were used to allocate customer-care related expenses, i.e., credit & 
collection to competitive functions as determined by the Public Service Commission and explained 
further.  
 
II - DESCRIPTION OF OPERATING FUNCTIONS: 
The operating functions shown on Table 2, Rate Base, Table 3, Operating Expenses, and Table 
5, Income Taxes (State and Federal) as described below. Where applicable, these functions 
include fixed costs including an allocation of common plant, associated operating expenses, 
administrative & general expenses (A&G), state income taxes (SIT) and federal income tax  
(FIT). 
Lines 1 - 3, The Gas Supply Function  
The Gas Supply Function is divided into the following three sub-functions: 
Line 1, Gas Supply-Demand  
Gas Supply-Demand consists of the demand costs of peaking and storage plus the demand charges 
relating to the storing and transporting of firm gas by the pipeline companies.  All costs for this 
function and corresponding revenues have been excluded from this study. 
Line 2, Gas Supply-Purchased Gas Cost  
Gas Supply–Purchased Gas Cost represents the commodity cost of gas purchased.  
All costs for this function and corresponding revenues have been excluded from this study. 
Line 3, Gas Supply-Other  
Gas Supply-Other consists of the costs for the Gas Control Center, recovered through rates.

Line 4, Merchant Function 
 
The Merchant function includes the costs associated with gas commodity procurement & 
scheduling, planning, and commodity billing & analysis.  Also included is a revenue-based 
allocation of credit & collection, theft, IT and education & promotional advertising.  This function 
is allocated to classes based on a hybrid allocator, which includes procurement costs being 
allocated 100% on annual full service sales, while the revenue based adders of credit & collection, 
IT and education & promotional advertising are allocated 25% on annual full service sales and 
75% on number of full service customers.   
Line 5, Storage  
Storage includes all Orange & Rockland storage expenses. 
Line 6, Transmission  
Transmission mains were re-functionalized to distribution mains.  Therefore, there are no 
transmission related costs identified in this study.  
Lines 7-11, Distribution  
The distribution function is divided into the following five sub-functions: 
Line 7, Distribution Demand (“Demand Component”) 
The Distribution Demand (“Demand Component”) consists of the balance of the distribution mains 
system not allocated to the customer component, and represents fixed costs related primarily to 
mains. It also includes distribution pressure governors and regulating equipment, used in 
distributing gas from the sellers to the firm classes of services. These costs are allocated to the firm 
classes in proportion to their maximum one-hour non-coincident use on a zero-degree day.

The Distribution Customer (“Customer Component”) consists of the distribution mains system that 
would be required to connect gas customers with a minimum predominant size pipe, regardless of 
their demand for gas. It is apportioned to the classes based on the number of services for each 
class.  
Line 9, Services  
Services, consisting of the fixed costs of connecting firm customers to the distribution system, are 
considered customer-related costs and have been apportioned, by class, based on the class

across O&R’s service territory.
