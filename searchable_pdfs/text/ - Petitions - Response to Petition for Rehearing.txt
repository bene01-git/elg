Background Statement 
 
In its December 16 Order, the Commission adopted with minor modification the 
terms of a well-supported Joint Proposal1 (“JP”) that provides for a comprehensive three-
year rate plan for the Companies to be in effect for the period January 1, 2017 through 
December 31, 2019.  While PULP seeks rehearing of the December 16 Order and 
requests the Commission to “rescind, annul or modify said Order to rectify the errors 
committed thereby,”2 PULP never states precisely the relief that it seeks but instead 
presents a hodgepodge of arguments that appear to (i) contest the Commission’s decision 
to reject PULP’s claim that the Companies should have been required to absorb a greater 
percentage of their Site Investigation and Remediation (“SIR”) costs, and (ii) assail the 
Commission’s failure to take notice of an increase in commodity gas costs above the 
level that the proponents of the JP relied upon in identifying the total rate impacts 
associated with the JP.  PULP’s rehearing request provides no basis for the Commission 
to reconsider or revise its December 16 Order.

A. 
PULP’s Claims Concerning The Treatment Of The Company’s SIR Costs 
Are Nothing More Than Unfounded Speculation Previously Raised By PULP 
And Rejected By The Commission  
 
The remarkable aspect of PULP’s petition for rehearing concerning the 
Commission’s decision to adopt the JP’s provisions concerning the recovery of SIR costs 
is that PULP does not provide a single citation to either the factual record or any 
meaningful legal authority that would support a finding that the Companies’ SIR costs 
are excessive, imprudent or should otherwise be subject to disallowance.  PULP’s 
petition for rehearing concerning the SIR issue is based on nothing more than its 
conjecture that a disallowance of SIR costs would motivate the Companies to achieve 
further SIR cost savings.  In this regard, while PULP (Rehearing at 6) acknowledges the 
Commission’s unassailable finding that “DEC has primary control over the scope and 
timing of MGP site clean-up and KEDNY and KEDLI have been complying with their 
obligations to remediate the contaminated sites they are responsible for,”3 PULP’s 
alleged error of fact appears to be based on its completely speculative assertion that “a 
cost sharing incentive could act as an additional consideration the Companies would 
weigh before requesting an extension from the DEC.”4 
 
Similarly, while PULP does not dispute the Commission’s finding that the 
Companies have followed best practices in conducting MGP remediation, (PULP 
Rehearing at 7), and while PULP does not identify any specific SIR-related action that it 
believes the Companies should have undertaken in addition to those identified in the

B. 
PULP’s Belated Effort To Introduce Evidence Of Recent Increases In The 
Commodity Cost Of Gas Should Be Rejected, And, In Any Event, Provides 
No Support For PULP’s Rehearing Request 
 
In its petition for rehearing, PULP attempts (at 4, 5) to introduce evidence that 
commodity gas prices have increased as compared to the forecasts reflected in the JP.  
PULP claims that the increase in commodity costs “should, arguably, have been taken 
into account in the December 16 Order, and should be at least part of a basis for rehearing 
the Order.”13  PULP’s position should be rejected for several reasons. 
 
First, it is procedurally deficient.  To the extent that PULP believed that changes 
in the commodity cost of gas were relevant to the Commission’s rate determinations, it 
had the opportunity to introduce evidence concerning such changes in both its direct and 
rebuttal testimony and in the hearings in which the JP was considered.  PULP offers no 
justification for its failure to present previously the evidence that it now wishes to 
introduce. 
 
When questions have arisen as to whether the Commission should grant rehearing 
on the basis of new evidence the Commission has determined that the standard of review 
for rehearing is similar to the requirements for relief from a judicial order under New 
York’s Civil Procedural Law and Rules (“CPLR”) § 5015(b).14  Under that statute, a new 
trial may be ordered if the moving party can establish the existence of newly discovered

C. 
PULP’s Reference To The Commission’s Orders In The Reforming The 
Energy Vision (“REV”) Proceeding Provides No Support For Its Claim That 
The Commission Must Disallow SIR Costs 
 
Despite PULP’s claims to the contrary,18 the Commission’s orders issued to date 
in Case 14-M-0101, the REV proceeding, in no way support PULP’s proposal to require 
shareholders to absorb prudently incurred SIR costs under the guise of “sharing” or 
creating a “market incentive.”  PULP cites no explicit provision of any order that 
supports its position.  Moreover, the Commission’s May 19, 2016 “Order Adopting A 
Ratemaking And Utility Revenue Model Policy Framework” makes it clear that the 
immediate subject of REV ratemaking policy relates to “financial incentives for REV 
activities such as DER integration and grid modernization.”19  To the Companies’ 
knowledge the Commission has nowhere indicated that the REV ratemaking framework 
does or should apply to the recovery of SIR costs.20  PULP’s unsupported claim to the 
contrary provides no basis for the Commission to modify its December 16 Order.
