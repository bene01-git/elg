STATEMENT OF NEUTRALITY  
A. CLCPA Section 7 Applies to the Commission’s Review of this Joint Proposal 
As is clear from the statutory text, the Commission’s own statutory interpretation, and the 
Commission’s prior practice of conducting such reviews, Sections 7(2) and 7(3) of the CLCPA 
(collectively, “Section 7”) apply to rate case orders.  
CLCPA Section 7(2) requires that “all state agencies,” in “considering and issuing 
permits, licenses, and other administrative approvals and decisions,” “shall consider whether 
such decisions are inconsistent with or will interfere with the attainment of the statewide 
greenhouse gas emissions limits.”7 The CLCPA mandates that New York adopt measures to 
reduce statewide greenhouse gas (“GHG”) emissions by 40% by 2030 and 85% by 2050 (from 
1990 levels), with an additional goal of achieving net zero emissions across all sectors of the 
economy by 2050 (the remaining 15% can come from carbon offsets).8 It also requires adoption 
of a state energy plan to produce 70% of electricity from renewable sources by 2030, and to 
achieve a zero-GHG-emissions electricity sector and increase energy efficiency 23% (from 2012 
levels) by 2040.9  
CLCPA Section 7(3) requires that “all state agencies,” in “considering and issuing 
permits, licenses, and other administrative approvals and decisions,” “shall not

B. The Existing Rate Case Paradigm is Inadequate to Ensure Consistency with the 
CLCPA 
Niagara Mohawk filed its rate application in this proceeding in late July 2020, more than 
one year after the CLCPA was enacted by the New York legislature and seven months after the 
CLCPA came into effect on January 1, 2020. Yet this rate case has largely been conducted under 
a business-as-usual approach, with no specific instruction from the Commission guiding the 
Company’s rate filing to require alignment with the objectives of the CLCPA. EDF neither 
opposes nor supports the Joint Proposal filed in this proceeding, recognizing the efforts to reduce 
GHG emissions (see Section E supra) by signatory parties, but also recognizing the lack of an 
essential guiding paradigm to ensure that the process and outcome of this utility rate case are 
consistent with the GHG reduction and environmental justice objectives of the CLCPA. The 
Commission must establish clear standards for utilities, and clear standards for utility rate cases, 
that will ensure New York’s climate goals can be met and facilitate decarbonization of the 
energy system, which will require dramatic GHG emissions reductions. To mitigate climate 
disaster, transformational change is required in the energy sector, and New York utilities must 
move towards a new norm. 
1. A Comprehensive Section 7 Analysis Requires Clear Guidelines for Utility 
Rate Cases  
CLCPA Section 7 requires consistency with New York’s GHG emission goals and 
protection of disadvantaged communities, but the Commission has instituted no standards to 
guide these analyses.

3. Delaying Effective Utility Climate Planning Will be Costly to Ratepayers, 
Particularly Low-Income Communities    
The existing rate case paradigm for gas utilities involves approval of long-term gas 
infrastructure that customers will be paying for in rates for decades to come, but that may not be 
used or useful for all of that time. There is a fundamental disconnect between state climate policy 
and the operational and cost recovery assumptions employed by many New York gas utilities—
and approved by the Commission in rate cases. New York has a vision for a clean energy future 
that significantly reduces reliance on natural gas, but many gas utilities continue to rely on 
traditional assumptions that they will maintain and expand their existing gas distribution systems 
and depreciate assets at historic rates.

D. The Commission Must Institute A Clear Process for CLCPA-Aligned Electric 
Utility Long-Term Planning and Rate Approvals 
The Commission must also align its electric utility planning requirements with the 
CLCPA and state electrification targets. As further discussed below, existing planning 
requirements arising from the REV proceeding are too short term and do not provide for the 
requisite alignment with CLCPA goals and related transition goals, which have a longer horizon. 
Without CLCPA-aligned planning that covers a time horizon commensurate with the energy 
transition policies of the CLCPA and related legislation, the result will be slower, costlier 
electrification of vehicles, buildings, and other sectors currently reliant on fossil fuels. EDF has 
previously asked the Commission to require electric utilities to perform distribution grid impact 
studies, which would look at the long-term, incremental impacts on electric load expected to 
result from vehicle electrification, including at the feeder level, and inform infrastructure 
investments accordingly.40 The Commission should require such studies, or similar planning 
requirements, not just in the EV context but in all sectors undergoing electrification. 
The Commission’s current planning requirements are insufficient to ensure that its 
regulated utilities are laying the groundwork for meeting the economy-wide emissions reduction 
targets of the CLCPA. In addition to planning for the integration of renewable and other non-
emitting generation and storage resources, utilities must plan for potential increases in electricity 
demand and consumption in New York as the transportation and building heating sectors 
electrify. Such planning is necessary to assess how electrification will impact the electric system,

E. Although the Joint Proposal is a Product of an Outdated Gas Utility Planning 
Paradigm, It Contains Provisions that Contribute to the CLCPA Objectives  
 
The Joint Proposal contains some notable provisions that indicate effort by the parties to 
align with the objectives of New York climate law. Although these provisions do not rise to the 
level of transformational change necessary to align gas utility programs with the CLCPA targets, 
they are indicative that the Company, Staff, and parties are attentive to these issues and seeking 
progress. In the Joint Proposal, the Company commits to “endeavor to achieve” a net-zero 
increase in billed gas usage in Rate Years 2 and 3, by endeavoring to achieve “weather-adjusted 
reductions in billed gas usage of one percent as compared to the forecast levels of non-generator 
gas use in Rate Years Two and Three.”46 In order to achieve that topline objective, the Company 
commits to pursue numerous pathways to reduce gas use, including to: “(i) implement 
Commission-approved energy efficiency (NE:NY) and incremental demand response programs, 
(ii) pursue non-gas NPA projects for certain LPP replacements and customer connections, (iii) 
promote geothermal/electric heating options with prospective customers and through the 
Company’s energy efficiency program offerings, (iv) provide a new, shareholder-funded 
incentives for gas-to-heat pump conversions (including both air- and ground-source heat pumps),

III. 
CONCLUSION  
EDF neither supports nor opposes the Joint Proposal. The Commission should establish a 
clear standard for its CLCPA Section 7 review of rate case orders to bring further clarity to the 
rate case process, and the Commission should move forward with establishing a comprehensive 
long-term planning process for utilities to ensure alignment with New York climate policy.
