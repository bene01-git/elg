PRELIMINARY STATEMENT 
Roger Caiazza, Richard Ellenbogen, and Francis Menton (Independent 
Intervenors) hereby submit this Statement in Opposition of the Joint Proposal (JP) 
filed with the New York State Public Service Commission (Commission) by 
Consolidated Edison Company of New York (Company) and signatory parties in 
these proceedings on November 5, 2025.  The Independent Intervenors respectfully 
submit this filing as ratepayers directly impacted by this proceeding and/or 
professionals with extensive backgrounds in New York State energy matters. 
The Independent Intervenors object to the JP because it seeks to implement 
large rate increases for projects not necessary for or germane to reliability and safety 
of the electrical system and that are instead intended to support extraneous goals that 
are impossible and infeasible.  The bases for the objections of the Independent 
Intervenors include: 
• The JP supports spending for purposes of building electrification and 
vehicle electrification, but there is not enough zero-emissions 
electricity generation capacity available to the downstate region to 
accomplish those goals reliably, and therefore any spending on 
Company delivery infrastructure for these purposes is wasteful.  The 
Independent Intervenors object to spending ratepayer money 


--- Page 6 ---
 
6 
 
supposedly for the delivery of electricity, when the electricity does not 
in fact exist. 
• Spending by Company to support building and vehicle electrification is 
actually counterproductive for Climate Leadership & Community 
Protection Act (CLCPA) goals because there is and will be little zero-
emission generation available to the Company service territory during 
and after the JP period.  Therefore, building and vehicle electrification 
will increase rather than decrease GHG emissions. 
• The JP supports large amounts of funding to achieve the “climate” goals 
of the State’s CLCPA, but the relevant State agencies have already 
conceded and admitted that those goals are not achievable and will not 
be achieved in any time frame relevant to the spending in the JP. 
• The State has admitted that achieving the CLCPA mandates, and in 
particular the 2030 emission-reduction mandates, are “infeasible.” 
• The CLCPA mandates for reduction of GHG emissions from the 
electricity system are in fact impossible to achieve, as is easily 
demonstrated by publicly available data from other jurisdictions, and 
therefore ratepayers should not be charged for wasteful spending to 
pursue impossible and unachievable goals.

Intervenors filed for party status in early June 2025.  On June 13, 2025, the

STATEMENT OF FACTS 
A. CURRENT GHG EMISSIONS IN NEW YORK STATE ARE FAR IN 
EXCESS OF THE MANDATES OF THE CLCPA FOR 2030, AND 
THERE IS NO POSSIBILITY THAT EITHER THE 2030 CLCPA 
MANDATES, OR SUBSEQUENT CLCPA MANDATES, WILL BE 
ACHIEVED BY THE ESTABLISHED DEADLINES OR ANY 
REASONABLE TIME THEREAFTER 
At this point New York State has no idea how it is going to generate 
sufficient “renewable” or “zero emissions” electricity to meet the 2030 and 
subsequent mandates of the CLCPA.  Generation of the electricity is outside of the 
Company’s purview, but the JP contains large amounts of spending by the 
Company to support the receipt and distribution of this non-existent electricity.  
This spending should not be allowed by the Commission until we know that the 
electricity is going to exist.  Such spending should not occur during this three-year 
rate period.   
The Draft New York State Energy Plan issued in July 2025 concludes that 
there is no scenario that will hit the 40% CLCPA-mandated reduction before 2036.  
In 2022, statewide emission reductions since 1990 were only down 9.3% far short 
of the 40% reduction required by 2030. 


--- Page 10 ---

The CLCPA established two Greenhouse Gas (GHG) emission reduction 
targets.  The ultimate limit is “Net Zero” - 85% reduction in GHG emissions and 
15% offset of emissions by 2050.  There also is an interim reduction target of a 
40% GHG reduction by 2030. The numerical statewide greenhouse gas (GHG) 
emission limits for a 40% reduction from 1990 levels by 2030 were established by 
the New York State Department of Environmental Conservation (DEC) in 
regulation 6 NYCRR Part 496, titled "Statewide Greenhouse Gas Emission 
Limits". According to Environmental Conservation Law (ECL) § 75-0107(1), the 
official 2030 limit is set at 245.87 million metric tons of CO₂-equivalent statewide 
GHG emissions. 
The Department of Environmental Conservation's 2024 Statewide GHG 
Emissions Report3, covering data through 2022, revealed that New York emissions 
as of 2022 had achieved only a 9.3% reduction in gross GHG emissions from 1990 
levels—far short of the 40% reduction theoretically required by 2030.

The closest thing in the world to a demonstration of the feasibility of a

El Hierro is an island of about 10,000 people.  It has average electricity 
demand of about 6 MW.  To attempt to meet that demand without fossil fuel backup, 
they built five large wind turbines with total capacity of 12.5 MW (that is, more than

MWh of energy (that is, about 45 hours of average demand).  El Hierro is fortunate 
to have an extinct volcano rising straight up from the ocean with a large crater at the 
top at an elevation of about 2000 feet, ideal for use as a pumped storage reservoir.

meaningfully-large pumped storage system.). The idea for the El Hierro system was
