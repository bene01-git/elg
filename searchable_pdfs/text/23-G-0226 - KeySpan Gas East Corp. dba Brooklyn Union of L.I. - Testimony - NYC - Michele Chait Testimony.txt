Q. 
HOW ARE ECOSS COSTS CLASSIFIED? 
12 
A. 
KEDNY Rate Design Panel Direct Testimony, lines 9-18 on page 23 of 75, explains that 
13 
the Company’s gas distribution system is designed to meet three primary objectives: (a) 
14 
connect customers to the Company’s distribution system; (b) meet the aggregate peak 
15 
design day capacity requirements of all customers entitled to service on the peak day; and 
16 
(c) deliver natural gas commodity to customers.  Corresponding to these objectives, each 
17 
functionalized cost element is classified as being customer-related, demand-related, or 
18 
commodity-related.  Customer-related costs include metering, billing, and the costs of 
19 
connecting customer premises to the distribution system.    
20

Q. 
HOW DOES THE ECOSS CLASSIFY DISTRIBUTION MAINS COSTS?  
1 
A.  
KEDNY Rate Design Panel Direct Testimony, page 25 lines 10-16, explains that because 
2 
distribution mains both connect customers to the distribution system and provide capacity 
3 
to meet customer demand, distribution mains costs are classified into customer-and 
4 
demand-related components.   
5 
Q. 
IS CLASSIFYING A SHARE OF DISTRIBUTION MAINS COSTS AS 
6 
CUSTOMER-RELATED A GENERALLY ACCEPTABLE APPROACH? 
7 
A. 
Yes.  The National Association of Regulatory Utility Commissioners (NARUC) discuss 
8 
this approach in their June 1989 Gas Distribution Rate Design Manual (“NARUC 
9 
Manual”).  Regarding cost classification, the NARUC Manual states “[a] portion of the 
10 
costs associated with the distribution system may be included as customer cost.”  
11 
Q. 
IS A MINIMUM-SYSTEM STUDY A GENERALLY ACCEPTED METHOD OF 
12 
DETERMINING THE CUSTOMER-RELATED SHARE OF DISTRIBUTION 
13 
MAINS COSTS? 
14 
A. 
Yes.  The NARUC Manual states “[o]ne argument for inclusion of distribution related 
15 
items in the customer cost classification is the ‘zero or minimum size main theory.’ This 
16 
theory assumes that there is a zero or minimum size main necessary to connect the customer 
17 
to the system and thus affords the customer an opportunity to take service if [they] so 
18 
desire.”   
19

5

6 
A. 
The customer portion is allocated based on the number of customers. The demand portion 
7

8 
Q. 
WHAT IS A TOLERANCE BAND? 
9 
A. 
The tolerance band is a mechanism employed to support grouping service classifications 
10 
into categories based on the  degree to which each class over- or under-contributes revenues 
11 
relative to the costs that are allocated to them.  The tolerance band therefore informs to 
12 
what degree the pure cost-based allocation of the distribution revenue change to individual 
13 
service classifications should be adjusted.  KEDNY Rate Design Panel Direct Testimony, 
14 
page 35 of 75 lines 10 – 11, states that using a tolerance band is intended to avoid disputes 
15 
about minor issues in the ECOSS. 
16 
Q. 
PLEASE DESCRIBE THE PROCESS THE COMPANIES UTILIZE TO 
17 
DETERMINE 
WHETHER 
MODIFICATIONS 
TO 
THE 
PURE 
COST 
18 
ALLOCATION ARE NECESSARY. 
19 
A. 
The process consists of five steps.  First, delivery revenue at present rates is calculated per 
20 
the product of ECOSS billing determinants and current rates.  Second, net operating income 
21 
is calculated per total operating revenues minus ECOSS expenses.  Third, return at present 
22 


--- Page 14 ---
Cases 23-G-0225 / 23-G-0226 
 
MICHELE CHAIT 
13 
 
rates (%) is calculated by dividing net operating income by rate base.  The resulting system 
1 
rate of return at present rates for KEDNY is 2.75%; for KEDLI it is 2.94%.  Fourth, the 
2 
rate of return at present rates for each customer class is unitized by dividing each class’s 
3 
rate of return by the system rate of return.  This step produces the Relative Return at Present 
4 
Rates result.  Fifth, the Relative Rate of Return is evaluated versus the tolerance band.  For 
5 
each service classification, if the Relative Return at Present Rates falls outside of the 
6 
tolerance band, the class is deemed to be over- or under-contributing; if the Relative Return 
7 
at Present Rates does not exceed the tolerance band values, the class is deemed to be within 
8 
the tolerance band.
