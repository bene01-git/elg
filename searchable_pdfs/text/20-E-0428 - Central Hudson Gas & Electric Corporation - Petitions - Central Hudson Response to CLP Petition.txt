INTRODUCTION 
 
On May 3, 2022, Citizens for Local Power (“CLP”) filed its Petition to 
Postpone Central Hudson Gas & Electric Company’s1 Gas and Electric Rate 
Increases, Scheduled for July 1, 2022, and Future Scheduled Rate Increases 
Pending the Outcome of Investigations into the Company’s Billing Problems and 
Response to Storm Damage (“Petition”).  The bases for the Petition are a series 
of misrepresentations concluding with the serious request that the New York State 
Public Service Commission (“Commission”) indefinitely suspend Central Hudson’s

JP in its Order Adopting Terms of a Joint Proposal and Establishing Electric and 
Gas Rate Plan issued and effective November 18, 2021 (“Rate Order”) in Cases 
20-E-0428 and 20-G-0429. 
 
Further, CLP does not suggest that the Commission suspend the Rate 
Order, just a suspension of future rate increases without regard for services 
provided by Central Hudson for the benefit of customers and the need for rates to 
support the ability for Central Hudson to provide safe and reliable service.  As a 
Party to the Rate Case that did not oppose the JP, CLP is attempting to avoid the 
rate increase but keep the benefits set forth in the JP and the Rate Order.  The 
Petition is not reasonable and would cause harm to customers, including the very 
customers that CLP ostensibly seeks to assist. 
DISCUSSION 
 
CLP seeks from the Commission the drastic remedy of rate increase 
suspension for three reasons.  Each reason is unfounded, lacks a factual basis

and suggests that Central Hudson should be punished before facts are known,

Petition is unreasonable and should be rejected. 
I. 
The Petition suggests that the Commission should punish Central 
Hudson merely for being under investigation without regard to the 
outcome of each investigation. 
  
CLP believes that the Commission should suspend Central Hudson’s future 
rate increases because it is under investigation.2  CLP references three 
investigations. 
A. 
The investigation by the New York State Senate Committee 
on Investigations and Government Operations 
 
All large New York utilities, including Central Hudson, are currently under 
investigation by the New York State Senate Committee on Investigations and 
Government Operations (“Senate Investigation”). Central Hudson is one of many 
New York State Utilities that are the subject of the Senate Investigation.  The 
Senate Investigation is centered on gas and electric commodity price increases 
over which neither Central Hudson, nor any other New York State Utility has 
control. 
It is unclear why CLP believes that the Senate Investigation should result in 
punishment to Central Hudson.  The Senate Investigation primarily concerns gas 
and electric commodity price increases which Central Hudson does not control.  
The Senate has also inquired about billing issues associated with Central 
Hudson’s transition from its forty-year-old legacy Customer Information System 
(“CIS”) system to a new SAP system, but no wrongdoing has been alleged.  These

CLP also raises Matter 22-00666, In the Matter of Staff's Investigation into 
Central Hudson's Customer Information System Implementation and Resulting 
Billing Errors (“Billing Investigation”), as the basis for its request.  The Billing 
Investigation was announced during the Commission’s March session and at that 
time Staff stated that it knew of no rules that have been broken by Central Hudson.  
Staff and Central Hudson agree that certain of Central Hudson’s customers have 
been inconvenienced by the transition from Central Hudson’s legacy CIS billing 
system to its new SAP system.  Central Hudson is cooperating with, and will 
continue to cooperate with, the Department of Public Service Office of 
Investigations and Enforcement, and it is providing the Office of Investigations and 
Enforcement with the information that it has requested. 
Central Hudson has taken responsibility for the issues that have arisen, and 
Central Hudson expects to voluntarily spend significant shareholder dollars to 
remedy the issues, and, in addition, it is possible that Central Hudson may pay 
negative revenue adjustments during the first year of its current rate plan.  The

C. 
Matter 22-00497, In the Matter of Central Hudson Gas & 
Electric Corporation's Response to the February 4, 2022 
Winter Storm. 
 
CLP raises a third investigation, the Commission’s standard investigation 
into Winter Storm Landon, Matter 22-00497, In the Matter of Central Hudson Gas 
& Electric Corporation's Response to the February 4, 2022 Winter Storm.  CLP 
implies that Central Hudson somehow caused 67,000 customer outages and failed 
to prepare and respond properly to the storm.  This is simply untrue. 
The Commission investigates every major storm event as a matter of 
routine.  Nothing about the opening of an investigation implies that Central Hudson 
has improperly prepared for or responded to a storm.   
Central Hudson, and all other utilities, is not responsible for the weather and 
does not cause outages that result from the weather.  Central Hudson has a history 
of making capital investments to minimize storm impacts, and exceptional storm 
preparation and storm response.  In the particular storm referenced, Landon, there 
were more than 67,000 outages during the event, with approximately 1,374 outage 
cases, 109 broken poles, and 2,796 reports of downed wires. The damage was 
largely caused by heavy icing which broke off limbs and toppled trees throughout 


--- Page 7 ---
6 
 
Ulster County, with smaller impacts in Orange and Dutchess Counties.  Landon hit 
Central Hudson’s service territory at approximately 4:00 P.M. on February 4, 2022.  
Despite the immense damage in a concentrated area, which made restoration 
more difficult, Central Hudson restored service to 90% of those effected by 2:00 
P.M. on February 7, 2022, and restored 100% of customers to service by 7:30 P.M. 
on February 8, 2022.   
Central Hudson was uniformly praised for its response by Governor Hochul 
and the Ulster County Executive.  Central Hudson’s employees and the more than 
1,000 mutual assistance crews that Central Hudson brought in to help, deserved 
the praise for their extraordinary restoration efforts.  Central Hudson has filed its 
reports in the investigation and expects no negative finding from the Commission.  
CLP’s assertion that Central Hudson should be punished in the most severe way 
possible without any adverse finding is irrational and should be denied. 
II. 
The Petition unreasonably suggests that Central Hudson should be 
punished for the recent electric commodity price surge that was not 
caused by Central Hudson because Central Hudson does not control 
electric or gas commodity prices. 
 
CLP falsely raises the issue of “wildly inconsistent rates, which, in the case 
of electricity, increased by 46% in February, only to be followed by a company 
announcement that rates would decrease 46% in March.”3  In the first instance 
CLP mischaracterizes the February and March electric commodity price changes.  
The 46% February change was with respect to total bill4 while the 46% March 
change was with respect to the supply rate only.5

III. 
The Petition irresponsibly asserts that the Commission should punish 
Central Hudson due to an increase in customer arrearages. 
 
The Petition raises the entirely irrelevant issue of customer arrearages.9  
While it is true that customer arrearages have grown, primarily due to the Covid 
epidemic and the mandatory and voluntary moratoria that accompanied Covid, 
there have been no customer disconnections resulting from customer arrearages.  
Central Hudson has not started disconnections for non-payment, regardless of the 
reason for the non-payment.  Central Hudson has actively sought payment 
assistance for customers, offered payment plans to customers and worked 
diligently with the Commission and other stakeholders to fashion reasonable
