BACKGROUND 
 
 
In its 2021 Rate Order, the Commission expanded the 
electric RDM to include S.C. 13 customers and expanded the gas 
RDM to include S.C. 11 customers, subject to conditions that 
were intended to shield these customers from excessive RDM 
surcharges and exclude them from excessive RDM credits.1  The 
Commission authorized the RDM conditions because of the small 
population of electric S.C. 13 and gas S.C. 11 customers.  
Changes in usage or removal of one or more customers could 
result in large RDM credits or surcharges.  Some of the 
conditions applicable to the expansion of the electric RDM to 
S.C. 13 customers include: (1) combining S.C. 13 revenue with 
S.C. 3 revenue to determine over or under collection of revenue; 
(2) excluding from RDM reconciliations the loss or gain of 
revenues due to the loss or gain of one or more S.C. 13 
customers; (3) excluding from RDM reconciliations the loss or 
gain of revenues due to the decline or increase in electric 
demand by one or more S.C. 13 customers of 40 percent or more 
compared to the customer’s forecasted electric demand; (4) 
utilizing a five percent deadband around S.C. 13 revenue 
reconciliations within which no monthly reconciliations would be 
implemented; and (5) limiting both S.C. 13 RDM revenue excesses

JOINT PETITION AND TARIFF FILING 
According to the Joint Petitioners, the proposed 
changes to the electric and gas RDMs, and the related tariff 
amendments, address an unintended effect of the initial 
application of the RDMs.  The Joint Petitioners explain that the 
initial application of the RDMs for electric S.C. 13 and gas 
S.C. 11 resulted in larger than anticipated surcharges 
applicable to customers served under these service 
classifications.  The Joint Petitioners contend that unintended 
consequence resulted from applying the 2.5 percent cap only 
prior to combining the over and under collection for each of 
these service classifications with the other non-residential 
class(es) but not also after the combined over and under 
collection was calculated to determine the electric S.C. 13 and 
gas S.C. 11 RDM adjustment factor.2

To address this unintended outcome, the Joint

for the electric RDM, capping the total S.C. 13 credit/surcharge

capping the total S.C. 11 credit/surcharge at 2.5 percent of the

On April 5, 2022, Central Hudson filed proposed tariff 
amendments that would apply the 2.5 percent cap after combining 
electric S.C. 13 revenues with S.C. 3 revenues and gas S.C. 11 
revenues with S.C. 2, 6 and 13 revenues, to effectively limit 
the surcharge or credit for the electric S.C. 13 and gas S.C. 11 
customers, as intended by the Joint Proposal adopted by the 2021 
Rate Order.  Central Hudson requests waiver of the requirements 
of newspaper publication due to the minor nature of the proposed 
modifications and because it plans to individually notify 
affected customers of the changes.

LEGAL AUTHORITY 
 
 
Under Public Service Law §§5, 65, and 66, the 
Commission has legal authority to regulate the general operation 
of the utilities under its jurisdiction, to review proposed 
tariff amendments, and to modify, reject, or approve those 
tariff amendments.   
 
DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION 
 
 
One of the intended purposes of the proposed 
conditions related to expanding the RDM-eligible service classes 
to include electric S.C. 13 and gas S.C. 11, as adopted in the 
2021 Rate Order, was to limit the excesses and shortfalls that 
these customers could be subject to during an RDM reconciliation 
period.  Because the electric S.C. 13 and gas S.C. 11 classes 
have few customers, these customers could be exposed to 
considerable rate volatility if certain limitations were not 
properly established.  The inclusion of additional service 
classes that would be allowed to share the excess and shortfall 
for electric S.C. 13 and gas S.C. 11 was essential in limiting 
the impacts these customers could experience.   
 
 
The Joint Proposal adopted by the 2021 Rate Order 
established the RDM conditions for electric S.C. 13 and gas S.C. 
11.  Appendix O to the Joint Proposal states that the 2.5 
percent cap was to be applied prior to the combination of 
service classifications to determine the RDM credit or surcharge 
and stated that the combined service classifications would share 
a single RDM adjustment factor.3  However, as explained by the 
Joint Petitioners, the 2.5 percent cap should also be applied

after combining electric S.C. 13 with S.C. 3 revenues and gas 
S.C. 11 with S.C. 2, 6, and 13 revenues, to effectively limit 
the surcharge or credit for the electric S.C. 13 and gas S.C. 11

Petition’s Exhibit B, which is a revised Appendix O to the Joint 
Proposal, accurately modifies the language in the Joint Proposal 
by specifically including the 2.5 percent cap in the development 
of the RDM adjustment factors.  For electric, Central Hudson 
would use separate RDM adjustment factors for S.C. 3 and S.C. 
13.  For gas, S.C. 11 would have its own RDM adjustment factor 
whereas S.C. 2, S.C. 6, and S.C 13 will have a shared RDM 
adjustment factor.  Without these modifications, the recovery or 
credit would be based on the total combined over or under 
revenue amount for all applicable service classifications and 
not limited by a 2.5 percent cap for the electric S.C. 13 and 
gas S.C. 11 customers.  To minimize any future financial 
exposure to the electric S.C. 13 and gas S.C. 11 customers, it 
is essential to adjust this in the present Order so the proper 
reconciliation procedures can be applied in the next RDM 
adjustment period that begins August 1, 2022.4  Accordingly, the 
relief requested in the Joint Petition is granted, and the 
tariff modifications to change the RDM to its original intended 
purpose are approved. 
Finally, the Commission finds Central Hudson’s request 
to waive the requirements of newspaper publication reasonable 
and grants its request.  In lieu of newspaper publication, the 
Company is directed to individually notify affected customers of 
the changes.
