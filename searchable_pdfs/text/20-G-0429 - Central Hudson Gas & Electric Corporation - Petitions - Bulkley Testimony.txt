Please describe the guiding principles to be used in establishing the cost of capital for 
7 
a regulated utility. 
8 
A. 
The United States Supreme Court’s precedent-setting Hope and Bluefield cases established 
9 
the standards for determining the reasonableness of a utility’s allowed ROE.  Among the 
10

11

12

13 
means of arriving at a fair return are not controlling, only that the end result leads to just 
14 
and reasonable rates.2 
15 
Based on those standards, the Commission’s order in these cases should provide the 
16 
Company with the opportunity to earn a ROE that is:  (1) adequate to attract capital at 
17 
reasonable terms, thereby enabling it to continue to provide safe, reliable service; (2)

Is flexibility of approach and judgment important to an ROE determination? 
1 
A. 
Yes, it is.  When faced with the task of estimating the cost of equity, analysts are inclined 
2 
to gather and evaluate as much relevant data (both quantitative and qualitative) as can be 
3 
reasonably analyzed.  Analysts and academics understand that ROE models are tools to be 
4 
used in the ROE estimation process and that strict adherence to any single approach, or the 
5 
specific results of any single approach, can lead to flawed conclusions.  No model can 
6 
exactly pinpoint the correct return on equity; rather, each model brings its own perspective 
7 
and set of inputs that inform the estimate of ROE.  That position is consistent with the Hope 
8 
finding that “[u]nder the statutory standard of ‘just and reasonable,’ it is the result reached, 
9 
not the method employed, which is controlling.”12  
10 
Though each model brings a different perspective and adds different information to the 
11 
analysis based on the underlying capital market conditions, each model also has its own set 
12 
of inherent weaknesses and should not be relied upon individually without corroboration 
13 
from other approaches.  Changes to inputs as a result of changes in economic conditions 
14 
could have widely different effects on the results of the various analyses.   
15 
Regardless of which analyses are performed to estimate the investor’s required return on 
16 
equity, the analyst must apply judgment to assess the reasonableness of results and to 
17 
determine the best weighting to apply to results under prevailing capital market conditions.  
18

4

5 
A.

6

11 
the utility and its investors for these additional risks over and above interest rate risk.
