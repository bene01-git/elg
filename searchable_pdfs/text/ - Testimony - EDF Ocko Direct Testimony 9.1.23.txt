IV.  Pathways to Reduce GHG Emissions Associated with Natural Gas Use  
4 
Q. 
How does natural gas use contribute to climate change?  
5 
A. 
The production, transportation, and combustion of natural gas releases GHGs and other 
6 
climate pollutants that contribute to the climate crisis. Natural gas is composed primarily 
7 
of methane, a potent GHG with an especially strong warming impact over shorter time 
8 
periods. Compared with carbon dioxide of equal mass, fossil fuel methane’s warming 
9 
potential is 83 times higher over a 20-year period and 30 times higher over a 100-year 
10 
period.27 The oil and gas supply chain—upstream production, to transportation, to local 
11 
distribution—is responsible for around one quarter of human-caused methane emissions 
12 
globally because methane is emitted through leakage and operational releases.28 Human-
13 
caused methane emissions are responsible for around 30% of today’s warming.29  
14 
Once produced and delivered, natural gas is combusted to heat homes and 
15 
buildings, facilitate heavy industrial processes, and generate power in appliances and
