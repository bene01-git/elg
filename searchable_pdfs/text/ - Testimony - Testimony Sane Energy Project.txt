Q. What is Sane Energy Project  
 
A. Sane Energy Project is a non-profit organization representing 8,500 New Yorkers. We are 
based in New York City, and work with communities across New York State to stop the buildout 
of fracked gas infrastructure and hasten the just transition to renewable energy. Since 2011, we 
have worked directly with scientists, health professionals, and impacted communities across the 
state to raise awareness about the health and climate impacts of fracked gas infrastructure, and 
we work with communities to identify and remove barriers to community-owned renewable 
energy. We’ve worked with thousands of New Yorkers to prepare and provide public comment 
and participate in the regulatory process for over a dozen fracked gas infrastructure projects 
across New York.

II.
GAS ISSUES 
 
A. Introduction and Background 
 
Q: What will you address relating to the Company’s business activities?  
 
A. We have reviewed the Company’s policy overview of its gas business activities, and this 
testimony addresses four​ i​ssues relating to these activities. 
 
First, we oppose the Company’s proposals to spend hundreds of millions of ratepayer dollars on 
gas capital investments to replace Leak Prone Pipeline (LPP) instead of retire it and convert 
customers from oil to gas because these proposals conflict with state and city climate goals when 
the unique climate impacts of fracked gas are taken into account.

Q.What are New York State greenhouse gas reduction goals?  
 
A:The New York State Energy plan sets a 40% greenhouse gas reduction target for 2030 
and 80% reduction by 2050. The recently passed and signed into law Climate Leadership and 
Community Protection Act (CLCPA) also requires 40 percent emissions reduction by 2030 and 
an increased requirement of 85 percent emissions reductions by 2050.  
 
Q:What are the city’s goals greenhouse gas emission reduction goals? 
 
A: Intro 1253 passed by the New York City Council requires buildings over 25,000 square feet 
to reduce greenhouse gas emissions by 40% by 2030 and 80% by 2050.  
 
Q: Can the Company expand gas capacity and meet state and city greenhouse gas 
emissions reduction goals?  
 
A.No. When the full life cycle of methane leakage is taken into account, gas expansion goes 
against city and state goals to reduce greenhouse gas emissions. Methane, the main component of 
gas is a powerful greenhouse gas that is 86-106 times more potent a warming agent than C02 for 
the first 20 years it’s in the atmosphere. FLIR footage has shown high leakage rates of methane 
at the wellhead and all along the transportation route at compressor and metering stations. 
Cornell scientist Robert Howarth estimates that when looking at upstream and downstream 
emissions from shale gas, leakage rates range between 3.8% and 12% . Even if upstream 
4
emissions were not taken into account Howarth estimates the downstream emissions associated 
with storing gas and delivering shale gas to market to be 2.5%. With leakage rates like this it is 
impossible to meet city and state goals to reduce greenhouse gas emissions and is more likely 
taking us in the wrong direction by increasing emissions.

Q.How are the Company’s assertions about meeting greenhouse gas emission goals 
inadequate?  
 
A. When it comes to oil to gas conversions through the Customer Connections Program, the 
company primarily bases its claims of reducing greenhouse gas emissions on the fact that when 
burned, gas emits less CO2 than oil, but ignores upstream and downstream methane leakage. In 
doing so they are presenting an incomplete and misleading representation of greenhouse gas 
emissions associated with their projects and their environmental impact. Additional when talking 
about reducing greenhouse gas emissions the Company proposes projects to reduce methane 
leaks  within their system while at the same time expanding gas capacity and extending the 
5
overall life use of pipelines through replacements. Expanding and extending gas capacity will 
lead to more fracking and fracking infrastructure needed to transport the gas, thus increasing 
overall methane emissions.  
 
Q: Does the Company’s project proposals to limit methane address the issue?  
 
A: No. While identifying and fixing leaks is important, the technologies proposed to identify 
leaks do not address the upstream emissions especially when paired with pipeline and 
infrastructure enhancements that will overall increase gas use.  
 
Q:What should the Company do instead of investing in gas capital project to replace 
pipeline and convert customers?  
 
A:The Company should not invest any new ratepayer funds in expanding gas capacity and 
extending the life of pipelines and should instead invest in gas demand reduction and beneficial 
electrification. The company should look at retiring LPP instead of replacing it and convert 
customers to non-gas alternatives.

D: Need for Radon Testing

benefits” is inaccurate?

11

III. PROCEDURAL RECOMMENDATION: PUBLIC PARTICIPATION 
 
Q:What barriers have you experienced in participating in this proceeding​? 
 
A: As two part-time employees working for an organization, it has been very difficult for Sane 
Energy Project to have the capacity to draft our own testimony and financially impossible for us 
to hire our own experts. Additionally the public we work with has had no opportunity to bring 
evidence to the table or ask questions of the Company.  
 
Q:What is your procedural recommendation? 
 
A:​ We recommend Intervenor Funds available for the public and since it is our rate payer dollars 
National Grid is proposing to spend, the PSC should require utilities to fund the public to hire 
our own experts. We also recommend multiple public hearings in locations where energy 
burdens are greatest earlier in the process so members of the public can provide evidence before 
settlement negotiations begin. We also recommend discovery be available to all members of the 
public not just individuals who have the capacity to intervene as a party. The climate crisis and 
unwanted expansion of fracked gas infrastructure have made New Yorkers more aware of and 
wanting to shape the energy system of New York than ever before.
