Pursuant to the ruling of Administrative Law Judges (“ALJ”) Maureen Leary and James

I. 
THE COMMISSION SHOULD REJECT SECTION 7.8 OF THE JOINT 
PROPOSAL.  
 
The Joint Proposal, if approved, would allow rate recovery for four biomethane 
interconnections that will allow the Companies to purchase biomethane that is produced by 
private developers and inject it into their distribution system.1 WE ACT explained in its 
Statement in Opposition to the Joint Proposal and Reply to Statements in Support of the Joint 
Proposal that rate recovery would violate Sections 7(2) and 7(3) of the Climate Leadership and 
Community Protection Act (“CLCPA”) for a number of reasons, including that the Companies 
have not provided sufficient information about the interconnections’ greenhouse gas and co-
pollutant emissions and resulting impacts on disadvantaged communities (“DACs”).2 Testimony 
elicited at the May 20, 2024 evidentiary hearing sheds further light on the Companies’ failure to 
estimate the interconnections’ emissions and confirms that rate recovery must be rejected.  
A. The Record Cannot Support a Finding that the Biomethane Interconnections are 
Consistent with CLCPA Section 7(2).  
 
The Companies have not demonstrated—and could not demonstrate—that the 
interconnections comply with CLCPA Section 7(2). As detailed in WE ACT’s Statement in

The Companies might argue that emissions resulting from the biomethane projects should

RATEPAYERS SHOULD NOT FUND THE COMPANIES’ MATERIALS 
PROMOTING GAS, WHICH, AS STAFF CONFIRMED AT THE EVIDENTIARY 
HEARING, HAS BECOME A POLITICAL ISSUE.  
 
WE ACT’s Statement in Opposition to the Joint Proposal details the Companies’ unlawful 
use of ratepayer money to fund activities that advance National Grid’s policy agenda, including 
but not limited to materials that aim to sway public opinion about the role of natural gas in New 
York’s energy future.36 WE ACT also explained that the Companies’ stated goal of encouraging

III. 
THE FLOUNDERING NPA PROGRAM ILLUSTRATES THE NEED FOR THE 
COMMISSION TO CREATE BENCHMARKS IN ORDER FOR THE 
COMPANIES TO FUNDAMENTALLY CHANGE THEIR PRACTICES. 
 
WE ACT renews its concerns that the Companies will fall short of implementing the Joint 
Proposal’s provisions on non-pipeline alternatives (“NPAs”). While WE ACT is pleased that the 
Companies are exploring new NPA programs to address load relief, main replacement, and 
service line replacements,41 the Companies, as Mr. Patric O’Brien explained, have not been able 
to develop a successful NPA to date.42 The Companies have stated they are committed to 
developing and implementing successful NPAs, yet the Joint Proposal reflects minimal changes 
to the Companies’ patterns and practices.
