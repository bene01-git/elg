I.  INTRODUCTION 
 
The New York Power Authority (“NYPA”) files this limited statement to reply to an 
inaccurate characterization of NYPA made in the Statement in Opposition of the Joint Proposal 
(“JP”) submitted on behalf Alliance for a Green Economy (“AGREE”), Fossil Free Tompkins, 
Ratepayer and Community Intervenors, Climate Solutions Accelerator, and Campaign for 
Renewable Energy (collectively, “AGREE et al.”) on June 27, 2023, in this proceeding.  The 
goal of this reply is to clarify that NYPA’s support for the JP does not represent a diminished 
commitment to achieve the state’s goals under the Climate Leadership and Community 
Protection Act (“CLCPA”)1 and to reiterate its support for the Commission’s adoption of the JP 
in full as related to the electric service provisions contained therein.  
 
II. LIMITED REPLY  
In its comments criticizing the JP as heavily contested or that numerous signatory parties 
emphasized their agreement with only certain aspects of the JP, AGREE et al. states as follows: 
Even among the signatories, there are now efforts to distance themselves from 
the Joint Proposal. Indeed, as recently as yesterday (June 26, 2023) 
[characterizations of other signatories not relevant here]. . . in responses to the 
American Association of Retired Persons’ IRs 1-8[,] [t]he New York Power 
Authority similarly distanced itself from any element of the CLCPA or 
disadvantaged communities other than streetlight dimming. 
AGREE et al. at 27. 
The Commission should not accept this comment about NYPA at face value.  For NYPA, 
JP represents the advancement of CLCPA goals through street lighting dimming strategies that it 
argued for and—as a result of negotiations—were adopted in the JP in the form of a street

requirement to expressly support each and every position of a JP.  Thus, it was appropriate to 
answer the remainder of #IR-1 and all of #sIR2-8 by referring to the Companies and Department 
of Public Service Staff or other parties that would have information on, or have taken positions 
on, how the JP addresses the CLCPA with respect to disadvantaged communities.  For these 
reasons, there is no cause for AGREE et al. to characterize NYPA as “distancing” itself from the 
CLCPA or the JP.
