LEAK PRONE PIPE REMOVAL 
11 
Q. 
What type of pipe is considered leak prone? 
12 
A. 
LPP generally includes unprotected steel, cast 
13 
iron, wrought iron, and some early vintages of 
14 
plastic pipe.  On pages 30 and 31 of its initial 
15 
testimony, the Gas Infrastructure and Operations 
16 
Panel, referred to as the GIOP, states that 
17 
O&R’s LPP inventory consists of 90 miles of 
18 
Aldyl plastic, 119 miles of unprotected steel, 
19 
and 390 miles of pre-1971 ineffectively coated 
20 
steel mains. 
21 
Q. 
What is meant by the term unprotected? 
22 
A. 
Unprotected means that the pipe lacks adequate 
23 
cathodic protection, thus rendering it 
24 


--- Page 11 ---
Case 21-G-0073 
  Staff Gas Safety Panel 
 
10 
susceptible to corrosion.  Unprotected steel 
1 
often has inadequate or no coating, which makes 
2 
efforts to cathodically protect such pipe 
3 
ineffective and uneconomical. 
4 
Q. 
How do LPP removal programs improve the safety 
5 
of natural gas systems? 
6 
A. 
Leaks on underground piping can create safety 
7 
risks to the public and potentially lead to 
8 
serious incidents, resulting in injuries to 
9 
persons or property damage.  The removal of LPP 
10 
reduces these safety risks. 
11 
Q. 
Please explain the importance of removing 
12 
unprotected steel pipe. 
13 
A. 
Data collected by the United States Department 
14 
of Transportation, Pipeline and Hazardous 
15 
Materials Safety Administration, or PHMSA, shows 
16 
that corrosion is a leading cause of pipeline 
17 
failures, and that unprotected steel is the more 
18 
susceptible material to experience corrosion. 
19 
Q. 
Please explain the importance of removing Aldyl 
20 
plastic. 
21 
A. 
Aldyl plastic can become brittle with time due 
22 
to slow crack growth propagation.  Earlier 
23 
vintages of Aldyl plastic have similar 
24 


--- Page 12 ---
Case 21-G-0073 
  Staff Gas Safety Panel 
 
11 
characteristics to that of cast-iron, which 
1 
renders it susceptible to leakage and the 
2 
potential for failure. 
3 
Q. 
Are there other benefits associated with 
4 
removing LPP? 
5 
A. 
Yes, removing LPP should reduce the number of 
6 
leaks, lead to a decline in leakage rates, 
7 
reduce methane emissions, and reduce the 
8 
operating, maintenance, and overtime costs 
9 
associated with surveying leaks and responding 
10 
to emergency reports. 
11 
Q. 
Please describe the LPP removal component of the 
12 
pipeline safety performance measures. 
13 
A. 
This component is designed to ensure that the 
14 
Company continues to proactively remove LPP from 
15 
its system.  Typically, the Company would only 
16 
remove LPP to ensure compliance with the 
17 
pipeline safety regulations, to respond to 
18 
customer complaints, or through coordinated 
19 
municipal or state projects where a portion of 
20 
the associated costs are shared. 
21

Yes.

A. 
The pipe to be removed from service is 
1 
identified and ranked using a risk assessment 
2 
model-based approach. 
3 
Q. 
What is a risk assessment model? 
4 
A. 
A risk assessment model prioritizes all segments 
5 
of LPP according to attributes that pose the 
6 
highest associated risk.  The model uses several 
7 
weighted factors, including, but not limited to, 
8 
material type, diameter, pressure, date of 
9 
installation, and leak history, to determine a 
10 
ranking.  This allows the Company to provide a 
11 
higher level of public safety by focusing its 
12 
efforts on the pipeline segments with higher 
13 
risk.  Although there are commonalities between 
14 
the LDCs, it is important to note that the model 
15 
has been tailored to the Company’s unique mix of 
16 
assets and may not be appropriate for other 
17 
LDCs.  A uniform approach cannot accommodate the 
18 
characteristics that must be considered for each 
19 
system.
