Question No. : 64 
Following up to the Company’s response to UIU-2-15 and the attachment, “UIU-2-15-2016- 
2020 Customer Service Performance Incentive Mechanisms”, provided in the Company’s 
response. 
a. Referring to the 2016-2020 Customer Satisfaction Survey tab of the Excel spreadsheet, it
appears that the monthly result for August 2020 was omitted. Please update and provide the
Company’s performance for August 2020.
b. Referring to the 2016-2020 Call Answer Rate tab of the Excel spreadsheet:
i. Can the Company provide a detailed explanation as to why in 2018 the Company’s annual
performance, only 20.0% of calls being answered within 30 seconds, was so tremendously poor?
ii. Again referring to the Company’s performance in 2018, particularly in August (9.3%),
September (4.4%) and October (6.5%), can the Company please explain why their performance
was so exceptionally low in these months?
iii. However, the Company’s performance rebounded in 2019 and was even better in 2020. Can
the Company explain what accounted for such an increase in performance? (Did the Company
conduct more training? Increase staffing? Was there a drastic decrease in call volume?)
Response 
a.
In its response to UIU-2-15, the Company excluded the survey results for August 2020 in
compliance with the terms of the Joint Proposal adopted by the Commission in its Order
Adopting Terms of Joint Proposal and Establishing Electric and Gas Rate Plans issued March
14, 2019 in Case 18-E-0067.  Section b) of Appendix 15 to the Joint Proposal specifically
provides:
Except for the Residential Termination/Uncollectibles metric, for measurement purposes, 
results from months having abnormal operating conditions will not be considered. 
Abnormal operating conditions are deemed to occur during any period of emergency, 
catastrophe, strike, natural disaster, major storm, or other unusual event not in the 
Company’s control affecting more than 10 percent of the customers in an operating area 
during any month. A “major storm” will have the same definition as set forth in 16 
NYCRR Part 97.

Tropical Storm Isaias, which struck the Company’s service territory on August 4, 2020, qualified 
as a “major storm.”  Accordingly, the Company excluded the monthly survey results for August 
2020. 
 
b. The Company objects to this interrogatory on the grounds that it is argumentative.  Subject to 
this objection, the Company refers to DPS Staff’s 2019 Customer Service Performance 
Report issued in June 2020 in Case 20-M-0046, In the Matter of Utility Customer Service 
Quality Performance.  Specifically, page 10 of this Report states: 
 
In 2018, Orange and Rockland failed to meet its target for this measure and was  
assessed an NRA of $450,000. The Company informed Staff that higher call volumes 
related to customer inquiries on credit and bill payments, solar billing, high bills, 
estimated bills, scams, AMI meters, and social media inquiries resulted in its 
underperformance of this measure. O&R made additional steps to prevent 
underperforming in 2019, such as hiring additional personnel, coaching customer service 
representatives (CSRs), and drafting talking points for CSRs to utilize when customer 
call into the Company. The Company has made significant improvements by increasing 
its performance from averaging at 20% in 2018 to 64.6% in 2019.

Company ceased its terminations for residential customers as a result of the mandated 
moratorium, provide an explanation as to why the Company is proceeding with the recovery of 
the PRA from customers.

As reported in the Company’s 2020 Customer Service Performance Report, the Company did 
meet the residential terminations metric (“Metric”).  The Metric, which is included in the 
Company’s Commission-approved rate plan, does not allow for the modification of results due to 
extenuating circumstances (such as the COVID-19 pandemic).  Given its performance in prior 
years, and how it was tracking at the end of the first quarter of 2020, the Company is confident 
that it would have met the Metric even absent the COVID-19 pandemic.  By the terms of its rate

19 pandemic on O&R within the context of these electric and gas base rate cases.  Further, the 
Company notified Staff that in order to facilitate such review, O&R is willing to extend the 
commencement of its recovery of the PRA from June 1, 2021 until January 1, 2022.
