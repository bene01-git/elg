I. 
INTRODUCTION / PRELIMINARY STATEMENT 
New York State Electric & Gas Corporation (“NYSEG”) and Rochester Gas and Electric 
Corporation (“RG&E,” and together with NYSEG, the “Companies”), hereby submit this Post-
Evidentiary Hearing Brief (“Brief”) in further support of the Joint Proposal (“JP”).1  At the July 
17 and 18, 2023 evidentiary hearing, witnesses from the Companies and New York State 
Department of Public Service Staff (“Staff”) testified in support of the JP and were subject to 
cross-examination by the Alliance for a Green Economy (“AGREE”), the Public Utility Law 
Project (“PULP”), AARP New York (“AARP”), Multiple Intervenors (“MI”), Fossil Free 
Tompkins (“FFT”), and Ratepayer and Community Intervenors (“RCI”) (collectively, the 
“Intervenors”).  Numerous questions were also posed by Administrative Law Judge Bergen and 
Chief Administrative Law Judge Lecakes (“ALJs”).

(collectively, the “Statements in Support”).2   
The record clearly demonstrates that the JP will allow the Companies to provide safe and 
adequate service at just and reasonable rates, consistent with their well-settled obligations under 
the Public Service Law.3  The JP outlines a comprehensive, multi-year rate settlement that will, 
among other things, provide resources for the safe and reliable operation of the electric and gas 
systems, provide for energy affordability and economic development programs, fund increased 
energy efficiency and heat pump programs, enhance vegetation management, and improve 
customer service.  Despite ample opportunity for cross-examination, even a cursory review of 
the hearing transcript reveals the Intervenors’ failure to bring forth relevant factual evidence that 
would support the Commission’s rejection or modification of the JP.  Accordingly, the 
Companies request expeditious approval of the JP to mitigate rate impacts for customers 
resulting from delay.  For the reasons set forth below, as well as described in the Statements in 
Support, the JP is reasonable, in the public interest, and should be adopted by the Commission 
without modification.

B. The Evidentiary Record Demonstrates that the Joint Proposal is Consistent with the 
CLCPA 
 
The evidentiary record firmly establishes that the JP is consistent with Section 7(2) of the 
Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act (“CLCPA”).  AGREE focused its cross-
examination on whether the Companies and Staff had quantified the greenhouse gas (“GHG”) 
emissions reductions associated with the provisions of the JP.  As the Companies’ witness Ms. 
Ancel and Staff witness Mr. Lowery testified, the Companies and Staff are awaiting guidance 
from the Commission in Case 22-M-0149, which is expected to detail how utilities should 
quantify, account for and report GHG emissions (TR 39-40, 44-45).  Quantification of GHG 
emissions is a time-intensive process, and as Ms. Ancel testified, using significant resources to 
develop an independent GHG accounting methodology and perform a study before the 
Commission issues its modeling assumptions and guidance may require the Companies to repeat 
the exercise and incur additional costs, which is not in the best interest of customers (TR 52-53).  
While specific GHG emission reductions may not have been fully quantified, the hearing record 
demonstrates that the JP includes a number of provisions that will lead to reduced GHG 
emissions, and the JP is therefore consistent with the goals of the CLCPA.  For example, 
Companies’ witness Ms. LaBombard testified that the Companies’ leak prone main replacement

purposes, and desire to continue receiving natural gas service, the Companies are obligated to 
provide it (TR 101, 105).  The hearing record is clear that increased electrification of heating, 
which the Companies support, does not mean that the Companies can simply discontinue their 
gas distribution system when a subset of customers leave but others remain (TR 100-107).7

C. The Evidentiary Record Supports the Joint Proposal’s Revenue Requirement  
 
The JP’s proposed rate increases are fully supported by the evidentiary record in these 
proceedings.  While Intervenors focused on the size of the proposed rate increases during the 
hearing, their cross-examination failed to generate relevant evidence to contradict the fact that 
the proposed rate increases are necessary for the Companies to continue to invest in their 
systems, maintain financial integrity, and provide safe and adequate service.  The evidentiary 
record does, however, demonstrate that the JP takes affordability into account and includes 
adjustments made by Staff and the Companies to mitigate the rate increases (TR 152-153).  As 
Staff witness Mr. Leary testified, the proposed total revenue requirement increases for Rate Year 
1 (not levelized) are lower than either the Companies’ or Staff’s litigation position (TR 155-156) 
and the JP compares favorably with the likely result of a litigated outcome.   
Intervenors may assert the JP’s recommended return on equity (“ROE”) of 9.2% is not 
fully supported by the record.  Intervenors, including AARP, MI, and PULP, conducted ROE-

industry average and if approved by the Commission would be lower than any authorized ROE

record and any arguments to the contrary should be rejected.  
Turning next to NYSEG Electric vegetation management, the evidentiary record 
demonstrates that the proposed spending levels are in the public interest.  The Companies’

D. The Evidentiary Record Supports the Joint Proposal’s Earnings Adjustment 
Mechanisms 
 
The earnings adjustment mechanisms (“EAMs”) included in the JP incentivize the 
Companies to overachieve clean energy policy goals and/or historical performance in furtherance 
of the CLCPA, and are therefore in the public interest.10  As Staff witness Mr. Lowery testified, 
the EAMs use statewide policy goals (or historical performance in the absence of a statewide 
policy goal) to set a baseline, and the targets for the EAMs exceed that baseline (TR 233-234).
