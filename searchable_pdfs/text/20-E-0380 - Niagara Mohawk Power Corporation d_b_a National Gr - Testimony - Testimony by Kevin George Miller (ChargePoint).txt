IV. 
Response to the Company’s Proposed Programs 
5 
Q.  
Is the Company’s EV Charging program in the public interest?  
6 
A.  
The Company’s proposed EV Charging Program is in the public interest. The program 
7 
would generally increase the availability of and access to EV charging throughout the 
8 
Company’s service territory, which would be in the public interest. Supporting the 
9 
transition to an electrified fleet would further the achievement of New York’s Zero-
10 
Emission Vehicle (“ZEV”) targets and New York’s greenhouse gas (“GHG”) reduction goals 
11 
under the Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act (“CLCPA”), which is in the 
12 
public interest. Increasing access to electric transportation for public and private fleets, 
13 
which can benefit communities disproportionately impacted by poor air quality, is also in 
14 
the public interest. In addition, the program is consistent with the goals laid out by the 
15 
Commission in its Make Ready Program Order in 18-E-0138. 
16 
 
17 
 
The public interest is also served by supporting the continued growth of an open and 
18 
competitive market that brings innovative products and services to consumers and 
19 
efficiently manage risk and capital. Site hosts install EVSE for a variety of reasons and with 
20 
different goals in mind, and are best positioned to understand the needs of and provide 
21 
charging solutions to EV drivers, who are also their customers, employees, tenants, and 
22 
residents. When site hosts can choose the EV charging solution that best meets their 
23 
needs, vendors strive to develop the innovative products and compete to meet site hosts’ 
24 
needs. This competition encourages continual product differentiation, reduces customer 
25 
costs, and improves customer satisfaction. 
26

12

1 
 
The proposed Fleet Program, as designed, will address several unique barriers to fleet 
2 
electrification. A 2018 study conducted by UPS and GreenBiz identified the top barriers to 
3 
fleet electrification as a lack of EV charging infrastructure at facilities, a lack of product 
4 
availability, and prohibitive purchase prices.6 The Program will directly help overcome two 
5 
of these barriers by reducing total cost of stations and installation, as well as increasing 
6 
awareness of EV fleet product availability.  The Program will indirectly help mitigate the 
7 
third barrier (prohibitive purchase prices) by otherwise reducing total upfront cost with 
8 
respect to EV fleet charging infrastructure.    
9 
 
 
10 
In addition, no matter their type or function, fleets need vehicles that are reliable and 
11 
ready when needed.  The decision to electrify a fleet cannot be made without taking into 
12 
consideration the operational needs of the fleet.  Fleet operators must examine virtually 
13 
every aspect of their operations and address a number of issues:  1) is the vehicle 
14 
operating all day; 2) does the vehicle have a set or variable route; 3) how and where is the 
15 
vehicle being garaged; and, 4) if the vehicle is parked overnight, how long does it need to 
16 
be charged before it is needed again?   
17 
 
18 
Once these and other operational questions are answered, decisions can be made about 
19 
the best vehicle and charging solution for the specific operational needs of the fleet.
