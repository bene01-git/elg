Executive Summary
This paper requests Sanction of S008105 in the amount of $7.000M with a tolerance of +/-10% for the 
purposes of retrofitting the second floor of existing office Reservoir Woods to higher density seating.

        $6.400M Capex
        $0.100M Opex
        $0.500M Removal
Project Summary
National Grid’s Reservoir Woods location in Waltham, MA is filled to capacity under its current configuration.
Growth projections and change in business model from IT and the Transformation Office require an 
increased seating requirement.   This project will provide additional seating capacity and improvements to 
the utilization of existing space.
Background
The Reservoir Woods building layout was designed for approximately 1800 workers.  A supplemental space 
at Wyman St. was leased to accommodate the Gas Business Enablement (“GBE”) group (400 people).   The 
Reservoir Woods building was upgraded in 2019 to add collaboration seating and incorporate smart 
workspace principles.  This upgrade was based on providing workstations for an average occupancy of 70% 
indicated by employee usage data.  Supplemental open collaborative seating and conference space 
allows additional occupancy.   IT has a different business model which requires team members to work 
together in the office 100% of the time.
Project Description
This project includes reconfiguring the workspaces to accommodate an additional 315 people.  The 
workstations on the second floor will be replaced to provide a higher density of task seating and

This memorandum, and the papers filed concurrently herewith, constitute the
application of THE NIAGARA MOHAWK COMPANY, a National Grid Company (hereinafter,
"Niagara Mohawk") for obsolescence ("Application") relative to the valuation of its special
franchise real property, or advisory appraisals of its real property not located in the public
right-of-way, by the State Board or State Office of Real Property Tax Services ("State Board"
or "ORPTS") for the 2021 final assessment rolls, and thereafter.
I. Introduction and Summary
The State of New York Office of Real Property Tax Services is the sole determinant of
the special franchise assessments that are used by the local municipalities and taxing
authorities on their final assessment roles. See Real Property Tax Law ("RPTL") Article 6.
In addition, ORPTS is authorized to provide advisory appraisals to municipalities in limited
circumstances. See RPTL §1544 (permits ORPTS to provide advisory appraisals upon request
for taxable public utility properties). ORPTS offers its valuation services with respect to
utility property not located in the right-of-way (i.e., non-special franchise property) ,
pursuant to 9 NYCRR §195-2. In recognition of ORPTS' dual authority, this application
requests that ORPTS incorporate the identified obsolescence (set forth herein) into its
determination of Niagara Mohawk's special franchise assessments and advisory appraisals.
Niagara Mohawk reserves the right to apply other methodologies and factors to
calculate its functional and economic obsolescence. These additional methods and factors
are likely to result in an even higher computed obsolescence.

III.
Applicable Law
This Application is made in accordance with the rules and regulations of the State
Board, 9 NYCRR §197-2.8 (see also 9 NYCRR Part 195 for advisory appraisals), as well as
the administrative procedures adopted by the State Board for special franchise
valuations.
Niagara Mohawk's special franchise property must all be valued by the reproduction
cost new less depreciation methodology ("ReproCNLD") (the cost approach). As recognized
by the State Board in its regulations, in applying ReproCNLD there are three forms of
depreciation physical depreciation, functional obsolescence and economic obsolescence. See
Procedures of ORPS, Special Franchise Assessments and Values: City, Town and Village
Assessment Rolls (Final November 2001) ("ORPS Procedures"), §2.4-2.6. See also, Long
Island Lighting Company v. Assessor for Town of Brookhaven, Index Nos. 76/15628, et seq.
(Sup. Ct. Nassau Co.), aff'd, 202 A.D.2d 32, 616 N.Y.S.2d 375 (2d Dep't 1994); AMERICAN
INSTITUTE OF REAL ESTATE APPRAISERS, The Appraisal of Real Estate (10th Ed. 1992), p. 312. The
State Board routinely and unilaterally considers physical depreciation in determining the
value of special franchise property and in conducting its advisory appraisals (as the same
valuation methodology is used for both) (mainly incurable physical depreciation; see 9 NYCRR
§197-2.8; ORPS Procedures, §2.4). Conversely, for functional and economic obsolescence, the
State Board requires an application from the taxpayer before it considers either. See 9 NYCRR
§197-2.8; ORPS Procedure, §§2.5 and 2.6.
Pursuant to New York case law, functional obsolescence "is a loss of value resulting
from defects in design. It can also be caused by changes that, overtime, have made some
aspect of a structure, such as its materials or design, obsolete by current standards"
(emphasis added). See Appraisal Institute, The Appraisal of Real Estate, Tenth Edition,
©1992, p. 352. Functional obsolescence is the "loss of operating efficiency." Onondaga County
Water District v. Board of Assessors, 39 N.Y.2d 601, 605, 385 N.Y.S.2d 13, 17 (1976) (citing,
Valuation of Property, Volume 1, pp. 187-188); Piazza v. Assessor of Town of Porter, 16
A.D.2d 863 (4th Dep't 1962)(functional obsolescence includes "inadequacies of design"). That
is, "[f]unctional obsolescence includes the loss in value within the property as a result of
development of new technology,...changes in design, materials or processes resulting [in]
overcapacity, inadequacy, excess construction, lack of utility or excess fixed or variable
operating costs" (emphasis added). Long Island Lighting Company v. Assessor for Town of
Brookhaven, supra, slip op. at p. 33.
Functional obsolescence is demonstrated by comparing the existent construction,
design, materials and technologies of existing assets, for example, with the design and
technological advances employed in a state-of-the-art facility or equipment. Long island
Lighting Company v. Assessor for Town of Brookhaven, supra; Matter of Consolidated
Edison Company of New York, Inc. v. City of New York, Index No. 8564/98 (Sup.Ct. Kings Co.
