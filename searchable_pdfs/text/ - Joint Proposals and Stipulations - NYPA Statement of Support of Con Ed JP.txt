INTRODUCTION AND DESCRIPTION OF NYPA’S INTEREST 
 
The New York Power Authority (“NYPA”) hereby files this statement in support of the 
Joint Proposal (“JP”) filed with the New York Public Service Commission (“Commission”) on 
November 5, 2025.  NYPA is a signatory to the JP with respect to the electric service issues set 
forth therein by Consolidated Edison Company of New York, Inc. (the “Company” or “Con 
Edison”) in Case 25-E-0072 and the gas service issues set forth therein by the Company in Case 
25-G-0073. 
NYPA is a corporate municipal instrumentality and a political subdivision of New York 
State (“the State”) whose mission is to provide low cost and reliable electricity consistent with 
safety and a clean environment, while promoting economic growth and energy efficiency for the 
benefit of its customers.1  NYPA is authorized under its statute to serve as the full requirements 
power supplier to approximately 11,000 governmental customer accounts (collectively, the 
“NYPA Governmental Customers”) in the Company’s service territory.2  These customers, 
which NYPA serves under long-term supply contracts, include the City of New York, County of 
Westchester, Metropolitan Transportation Authority (“MTA”), Port Authority of New York and 
New Jersey, New York City Housing Authority, and the New York State Office of General 
Services.  The NYPA Governmental Customers comprise a separate service class under the 
Company’s tariffs.3

PRELIMINARY STATEMENT  
 
NYPA supports the electric and gas service provisions of the JP as a fair and reasonable 
result consistent with the public interest.  NYPA filed testimony on operational flow orders, 
electric service customer operations, and the earnings adjustment mechanism (“EAM”) for 
improving transportation and building electrification project timelines. These items were 
addressed in the JP, as discussed in more detail below.   
With respect to overall rates, the JP achieved compromise resolutions addressing, inter 
alia, electric revenue requirements and bill impacts, rate of return on equity, revenue allocation 
and rate design, electric reliability and resiliency, and the myriad other provisions affecting the 
electric and gas businesses of the Company and its customers, which are fair and reasonable and 
consistent with the public interest.

In particular, the JP’s resolutions on operational flow orders, customer operations, and 
the transportation and building electrification EAM satisfy the standards of review set forth in 
the Commission’s settlement guidelines.5  The JP reflects a compromise of competing interests 
that, in the context of setting rates, terms, and conditions for three years (rather than for a single 
year that would emerge from a litigated case), produces an outcome that is superior to litigation.   
 
For NYPA, the JP represents the advancement of NYPA’s policy goals in a manner that 
may not have been achievable in a litigated rate case.  The outcomes achieved in the JP related to 
operational flow orders, customer operations, and the transportation and building electrification 
EAM reflect NYPA’s recognition that while it had a high degree of confidence that its litigation 
positions would be successful in an administrative trial, these efforts could have fallen short, and 
the compromises contained in the JP are a reasonable outcome.  
In sum, as demonstrated below, the JP represents a settlement in the public interest as 
understood by the Commission, as it (1) fairly balances the interests of ratepayers, investors and 
the long-term viability of the Company; (2) fosters consistency with sound environmental, social, 
and economic policies of the Commission and New York State; and (3) produces results that are 
within the range of reasonable results that would likely have arisen from a Commission decision 
in a litigated proceeding.6  Accordingly, the Commission should approve the JP in full and 
without modification.

A. Operational Flow Orders  
NYPA’s testimony sought modifications to the Company’s practices and procedures 
relating to operational flow orders (“OFOs”), which are restrictions on natural gas usage.  NYPA 
owns or oversees on behalf of its customers eight generation facilities that use natural gas 
delivered by the Company’s pipeline system, and the Company’s issuance of an OFO can disrupt 
NYPA’s ability to make accurate gas purchases, reduce revenue opportunities, and increase 
operational costs.  OFOs are to be issued only when the Company needs to alleviate conditions 
that threaten the integrity of the Company’s system or prevent short-term interruptions, but the 
prevalence of OFOs has increased in recent years, to NYPA’s detriment.7   
In testimony, NYPA sought additional transparency and reforms to the Company’s OFO 
notice procedures to enable NYPA’s fuel supply team to make more informed gas purchases 
under OFO conditions.  In the JP, the Company commits to increasing transparency by providing 
as much notice of OFOs as possible, providing information on cold weather operating guidelines 
and operational restrictions, and providing additional information on the causes of OFOs.8,9  
Additionally, the Company commits to convening bi-annual seasonal meetings with NYPA and 
other interruptible generation market participants to review the previous season’s performance 
and to discuss specific issues encountered, potential improvements to be considered, and the 
overall system plan for the upcoming season.10

It is NYPA’s position that the Company’s commitments relating to OFOs, as expressed in 
the JP, are within the range of reasonable outcomes that would have arisen from a litigated 
proceeding before the Commission.  The OFO provisions in the JP stand to reduce costs to

settlements balance ratepayer and Company interests while being consistent with sound 
environmental, social, and economic policy.  As the OFO provisions are thus consistent with the 
Commission’s settlement guidelines, they should be adopted in full. 
B. Customer Operations  
NYPA’s testimony also sought support for the NYPA Governmental Customers with 
regard to several customer operations issues, including billing delays, slow response times to 
requests for estimated reads, which are calculated estimations of customer usage for a particular 
billing period where an actual meter reading is not available, and non-billing of accounts, leading 
to large monthly adjustments.  The causes of these billing issues include, but are not limited to, 
broken meters, the failure of the Company’s automated metering infrastructure connectivity 
functions, and meter access issues, such as when the Company cannot physically access a meter 
but fails to relay this issue to NYPA to work with the relevant Governmental Customer to 
resolve the access issue.11 
The Company’s initial rate filing requested funding for the creation of a dedicated NYPA 
customer operations team within the Company to deal with these issues.  Instead, in response to 
answering testimony and other feedback from NYPA and the NYPA Governmental Customers, 
Con Edison commits in the JP to create two engagement teams which will meet monthly and will

CONCLUSION 
In sum, the JP balances utility and ratepayer interests and is consistent with sound 
environmental, social and economic policy, in accordance with the Commission’s settlement 
guidelines.  For these reasons, and the other reasons set forth in detail in this statement, the 
Commission should find that the JP is in the public interest and adopt it in full and without 
modification.
