Quantitative Profiles 
  
 
Whipsaw risk is elevated and imminent 
 
 
  
18 October 2019   
 
We’re at the trickiest part of the cycle 
Our US Regime Indicator (p. 56) puts us in month seven of the “Downturn” phase, which 
has lasted eight months on average. The only way to exit this phase is by moving into 
“Early Cycle”. And factors that do well during the “Downturn” phase (quality, large caps, 
low risk, anti-value) are opposite to those that lead during “Early Cycle” (beta, small size, 
deep value). Institutional investors today have been funneled into growth and 
momentum stocks and are firmly underweight value and beta. We analyzed prior 
inflection points in our regime indicator, from one month prior to inflection through 
three months into “Early Cycle”, and found that Value fared best (+14.6% avg; 86% hit 
rate vs. benchmark), but Momentum was weakest (+5.5%, 43% hit rate). And Value beat 
Momentum during all seven inflection point periods since 1991. In our view, neutralizing 
Momentum and tilting toward Value is advisable for this reason as well as those below. 
Value rotation: not just a head-fake 
Our Regime Indicator is just one of several reasons to avoid an anti-Value bias. A violent 
rotation from Momentum to Value started in early September but largely ran its course 
by mid-month, at which point Momentum stocks resumed leadership. Value held onto 
relative gains for the month (6.2%, leading the index by the largest post-crisis monthly 
margin of +3.1ppt.) But in the year-to-date, Value still lags other factors, and investors 
may have been lulled back into complacency on high momentum stocks. But an 
anticipated re-acceleration of S&P 500 EPS growth in 1Q 2020, unusually low 
correlations between Momentum and Value, plus high valuation dispersions all warrant a 
tilt toward Value. Value stocks are deeply underweight by active managers (0.89 avg. 
relative weight in the factor) and our Value factor screens as inexpensive, trading at a 
near 25% discount to history on Price to Book Value. 
4Q playbook: Value over Growth  
The calendar provides further support for a Value tilt. Based on historical relative 4Q 
performance back to 1989 (Chart 1), Value factors tend to outperform most in the 4Q, 
where Free Cash Flow to Enterprise Value led. Cash Return factors also fare well. But 
Growth factors were unequivocally weakest in the 4Q (Long Equity Duration and High 
EPS Momentum lagged most). Among Risk factors, High Beta has outperformed most 
consistently in the last quarter of the year, providing some evidence for the notion of a 
year-end beta chase led by institutional investors.

Seven points in favor of Value 
 
 
  
12 November 2019   
 
Why this Value run could continue 
Despite struggling in early October, Value recovered and ultimately outperformed, 
advancing 0.9% in October, and remains ahead in November. Inexpensive stocks could 
continue outperform for a multitude of reasons, including that (1) 3Q is likely the trough 
in S&P 500 profits, (2) valuation dispersion has reached post-crisis highs, (3) the 
correlation between value and momentum has hit near record lows, (4) Financials (a 
value sector) is our highest conviction sector pick, (5) Value has never been this cheap 
relative to Momentum stocks, and is underowned (6) our US Regime Model (Chart 2) 
indicates that “Early Cycle” may be near, and other macro indicators indicate a trough.  
#7: Value stocks have shrunk to near record lows 
The median market cap of the lowest P/E decile has closed in on record lows - 0.6x the 
median index market cap (cover chart). When Value has shrunk below 0.7x, the factor has 
outperformed by 6.5% on avg. (67% hit rate) over the next 6 months. But Value 
dominance doesn’t mean market gains: when Value’s shrank to all-time lows in Sep. 
2000, Value outperformed by 24ppt in next six months but the S&P 500 sported losses. 
Quality led in October and for the year 
Despite rising equities and a falling VIX, factor returns were defensive, with Quality 
factors (ROC, ROE, ROA, +2.8% on avg.) ahead, and Risk factors behind (-0.5% on avg., 
more below). Year-to-date (YTD) Quality leads all other factor groups with +22.3% gain, 
2ppt ahead of the index. But Quality is now expensive and crowded (Table 3 on p. 5). Our 
US Regime Indicator (p. 55) puts us in month eight of the “Downturn” phase, which has 
lasted eight months on average. The next phase is “Early Cycle”, during which Quality 
has historically lagged. We have been proponents of Quality for the year, but risks are 
building in this style which may lead to underperformance if macro data inflects. 
Risk lagged; MNCs outperformed 
Risk was the weakest group last month with a 0.5% loss. Energy-heavy High EPS 
Estimate Dispersion (-4.2%) lagged most. But High Beta (+2.4%) has handily 
outperformed YTD, and this factor remains inexpensive and underowned by active funds. 
Multinational corporations (MNCs), which make up our Foreign Exposure factor (+2.0% 
in Oct. / +26.6 YTD), benefited from optimism around a trade deal and could continue to 
benefit if a trade deal is eventually reached.

The case for Value remains 
 
  
 
  
 
 
 
 
Value delivered in November – and we see more ahead 
In our Year Ahead, we outlined nine reasons the Value “bounce” may be more than a 
bounce, and could have room to run. Reasons include a pickup in profits in 2020, 
extreme positioning of institutional investors, and the relative valuation of Value – cheap 
stocks are really cheap vs. Momentum. Our US Regime Indicator (p. 8) has been in a 
“downturn” phase for longer than average (9 mos. vs. 8 mos. on avg.), and we believe it 
is likely to move into a “recovery” rather than a further downturn—last month’s 
deterioration suggests the smallest m/m decline of the current downturn. November 
performance corroborated the case - Value factors (+4.7%) beat all other factor groups, 
and the best five factors in November were Value factors with returns ranging from 
+5.2% (Low P/E trailing) to +6.8% (Low P/E forward). All Value factors except our 
Dividend Discount Model cleared the equal-weighted S&P 500 benchmark last month. 
Over the past three months, the period which covers the recent Value run, Value factors 
outperformed Growth factors by 4.6ppt and Momentum factors by 10.2ppt. But year-to-
date (YTD), Value (+16.0%) remains behind all other factor groups. 
Massive short squeeze likely helped (but isn’t over) 
Our High Short Interest (SI) factor (stocks with the most upward-trending SI as 
measured by 12-mth Z-score) beat our Low SI factor by 5.6ppt, the fifth-largest monthly 
performance spread since at least 1994. A short squeeze in beaten-down Value stocks 
likely helped Value’s performance last month, where the best-performing Value factor, 
Low P/E (Fwd), has seen increasing short interest since April (Chart 1). But short interest 
remains near recent highs and should still support Value’s performance in the near term. 
MTD (as of Dec. 9), Value (unch. on avg.) trails only Risk factor group (+0.2%). 
Still room for growth and cash return (tied for 2nd) 
All is not lost for growth and income investors. Both Growth and Cash Deployment 
factors advanced 4.0%, on avg. in November. Share Repurchase (+4.6%) and High 
Dividend Growth (+4.4%) outperformed, while High Dividend Yield (+3.1%) suffered from 
a tick-up in interest rates (10-yr Tsy yield rose 8bp for the month). All Growth factors 
outperformed the benchmark, with returns from +3.3% (High EPS Momentum) to +4.9% 
(High Upward EPS Estimate Revisions). YTD, Cash Deployment (+25.3%) leads the index 
by 1.3ppt, while Growth (+21.8%) has underperformed by 2.3ppt. 
Momentum lagged again; Beta, MNCs led  
Momentum ranked among the laggards, with our 5wk/30wk Relative Strength (+1.2%) 
and 12m Return + 1m Price Reversal (+1.5%) factors ending among the bottom five 
factors overall. YTD, Momentum (+19.2%) is among the three weakest factor groups. 
Risk factors performed in-line with the index in November, but performance dispersion 
among individual factors was wide - High Beta (+4.7%) was among the top 10 factors, 
but High EPS Estimate Dispersion (+1.3%) was among bottom five factors overall. Our 
High Foreign Exposure factor (multinational corporations (MNCs), +4.2%) may have 
benefited from improved sentiment around US-China trade in November. Oddly enough, 
despite a backdrop of trade friction/protectionism and weakening global growth this 
year, high Foreign Exposure leads all other factors YTD with a 32% gain.

Regulatory Analysis and Ratemaking 
Ms. Bulkley has provided a range of advisory services relating to regulatory policy analysis and many 
aspects of utility ratemaking.  Specific services have included: cost of capital and return on equity 
testimony, cost of service and rate design analysis and testimony, development of ratemaking 
strategies; development of merchant function exit strategies; analysis and program development to 
address residual energy supply and/or provider of last resort obligations; stranded costs assessment 
and recovery; performance-based ratemaking analysis and design; and many aspects of traditional 
utility ratemaking (e.g., rate design, rate base valuation).   
Cost of Capital  
Ms. Bulkley has provided expert testimony on the cost of capital in more than 30 regulatory 
proceedings before regulatory commissions in Arizona, Arkansas, Colorado, Connecticut, Kansas, 
Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Missouri, New Jersey, New Mexico, New York, North Dakota, 
Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Texas, South Dakota, West Virginia, and the Federal Energy Regulatory 
Commission.  In addition, Ms. Bulkley has prepared and provided supporting analysis for at least 
forty Federal and State regulatory proceedings in which she did not testify.  
Ms. Bulkley has more than two decades of management and economic consulting experience 
in the energy industry.  Ms. Bulkley has extensive state and federal regulatory experience on 
both electric and natural gas issues including rate of return, cost of equity and capital structure 
issues. Ms. Bulkley has provided expert testimony on the cost of capital in more than 30 
regulatory proceedings before regulatory commissions in Arizona, Arkansas, Colorado,
