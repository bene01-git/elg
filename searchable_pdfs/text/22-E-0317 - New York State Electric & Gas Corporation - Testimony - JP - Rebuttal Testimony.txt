Q 
WOULD SHIFTING CUSTOMER DEMAND BE A SUFFICIENT REASON TO 
1 
EITHER REJECT RG&E’S ELECTRIC ECOS STUDY OR SUBSTITUTE THE RATE 
2 
YEAR 1 RDM PROJECTED REVENUES FOR THE ACTUAL TEST-YEAR 
3 
REVENUES? 
4 
A 
No.  The purpose of an ECOS study is to determine whether the revenues recovered 
5 
from each class are sufficient to recover its allocated costs.  The allocated costs are 
6 
directly related to each class’s coincident and non-coincident (class) peak demands.  
7 
The corresponding revenues reflect the actual use of delivery services during the test 
8 
year.  Further, substituting the revenues projected in a prior case for actual test-year 
9 
revenues would violate the Matching Principle.   
10

11 
PEAK DEMAND? 
12

13 
demands used in the electric ECOS studies were abnormally low for the more weather-
14 
sensitive classes.  Specifically:

18

5

6

7

8

11

12 
costs — contrary to any cost-of-service evidence — from small gas consumers within 
13 
SC 1T to large gas consumers within SC 1T.
