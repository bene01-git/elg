VI. 
GAS CAPITAL PROGRAM – “REGULATOR STATIONS” 
1 
Q.    
What types of projects are included in the “Regulator Stations” category? 
2 
A.    
The category “Regulator Stations” addresses needed replacement and 
3 
modernization of critical natural gas pressure control stations.  These 
4 
stations safely reduce transmission pressure to distribution or utilization 
5 
pressure.  Noteworthy projects within this category include replacement of 
6 
antiquated regulator stations which date to the time of manufactured gas 
7 
plants; pressure control upgrades and conditioning equipment to provide 
8 
enhanced safety and reliability; installation of coating systems to extend 
9 
the useful life of existing assets; and increased utilization of the 
10 
Company’s internal communication network system for monitoring system 
11 
pressures remotely and in real time for enhanced situational awareness.  
12 
Funding for this category totals $19.4 million in the Five-Year Gas Capital 
13 
Plan.   
14 
Q.    
How does the Company prioritize regulator station projects?  
15 
A.    
The Company uses a prioritization methodology to rank the regulator 
16 
stations and assign a risk score.  This risk score takes into account 
17 
different factors, including output pressure stability, feedback received 
18 
from the mechanics that work on the station, and antiquated/obsolete 
19 
equipment. Based on the risk score stations are prioritized and projects 
20 
are engineered to modernize and improve the stations.  These regulator

Q.    
Why is output pressure stability important for prioritizing regulator station 
3 
projects?  
4 
A.    
Maintaining steady output pressure from regulator stations allows for 
5 
predictable performance of end use appliances.  This is critical for 
6 
utilization pressure systems, but also on upstream systems as well 
7 
because they are directly feeding utilization pressure systems and 
8 
elevated pressure customers. 
9 
Q.    
Please describe how antiquated and obsolete equipment factors into the 
10 
regulator station risk score.  
11 
A.    
Regulator station equipment is continuously maintained, once equipment 
12 
is not supported by the equipment manufacturer there is an increased risk 
13 
to the gas system by not having repair materials or support available in the 
14 
event of an equipment malfunction.  Typically, because equipment will 
15 
become unsupported by the manufacturer based on age of equipment, 
16 
station age is factored into the risk score.    
17 
Q.    
Is the Company adding any safety enhancements to Regulator Stations?   
18 
A.    
Yes, Central Hudson has recently improved the standard design for 
19 
regulator stations to increase resiliency and public safety by adding 
20 
additional safety devices for pressure control, a monitoring system which 
21 
is to be used in conjunction with a relief valve.  A monitoring system has
