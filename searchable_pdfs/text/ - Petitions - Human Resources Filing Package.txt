Q. 
Please explain why Willis Towers Watson’s study compares the 
1 
relative value of National Grid’s total compensation to the relative 
2 
value of the total compensation provided by the peer group. 
3 
A. 
The purpose of performing an overall compensation study is to determine 
4 
whether the total level of cash compensation and benefits is sufficient to 
5 
attract a highly qualified workforce.  From this perspective, cash 
6 
compensation – fixed and variable – is best analyzed by comparing the 
7 
cash compensation provided to various positions at National Grid to cash 
8 
compensation provided by other companies with comparable positions.  In 
9 
this case, Willis Towers Watson analyzed the cash compensation provided 
10 
by members of the peer group to management positions that fall within 
11 
various salary bands to the equivalent cash compensation provided to the 
12 
equivalent positions at National Grid.  The comparison of total 
13 
compensation for various benchmarked positions produces an overall 
14 
comparison of cash compensation that permits National Grid to evaluate 
15 
whether management positions within various salary bands are receiving a 
16 
reasonable (i.e., within the 10 percent corridor of the median) level of cash 
17 
compensation that will ensure that National Grid remains competitive in 
18 
the labor market.  The results of these analyses are set forth in Schedule 2 
19 
of Exhibit __ (HRP-2). 
 
20 
 
21

In contrast, acquiring information concerning peer companies’ costs of 
1 
various employee benefits and comparing those costs is very difficult, if 
2 
not impossible.  The reason for this is that the cost of various benefits can 
3 
vary dramatically from year to year and from company to company.  For 
4 
example, the cost of pension and OPEBs can change because of changes 
5 
in the funded status of the plans established to provide those benefits, the 
6 
investment performance of the plans, and the mortality rate of those 
7 
participating in the plan.   
8 
 
9 
Similarly, the cost of health insurance can vary because of the claims 
10 
experience of individual companies and the age and health of individual 
11 
employees in the workforce and their dependents.  Changes in costs 
12 
associated with these factors do not determine whether the benefits being 
13 
provided by a particular company are competitive.  Instead, it is the 
14 
relative value of the benefits that provides the proper measurement of 
15 
whether a benefits package is sufficient to attract, retain and engage highly 
16 
qualified personnel.  The analyses performed by Willis Towers Watson 
17 
properly consider the relative value of the benefits being provided by 
18 
National Grid and the peer group.

Q. 
How did Willis Towers Watson determine the relative value of the 
9 
benefits package provided by National Grid and the peer group? 
10 
A. 
The relative value of the various benefits is established based on a 
11 
common set of actuarial assumptions and a single employee population.  
12 
This establishes a controlled environment in which differences in value are 
13 
exclusively a function of the differences in benefit plan provisions.  The 
14 
relative value of various benefits is determined by dividing the value of 
15 
the particular benefit for individual companies by the average value of all 
16 
the companies in the peer group.  Relative values are developed for each 
17 
benefit and for the entire benefit plan.

11

13

14 
retirement involves assessing one’s financial preparedness provided 
15

16 
fixed benefits payable under National Grid’s pension plan, and the 
17 
potential for significant health care expense as participants’ age, retiree 
18 
health insurance benefits are one of the key factors in reaching the 
19

20 
National Grid has reached the conclusion that changes to OPEB benefit

– Scores are determined on a dollar value basis relative to the average value
• This report includes the following:
– List of peer companies included in the study
– Executive summary of study results
– Overview of the benefits environment
– Relative value comparison for the total program and individual benefit programs
– Breakdown of benefit values by component programs

grandfathered plans are not valued for the peer group
• Throughout this document, “total value” refers to the positioning of the benefit programs, including the value of 
employee contributions; “employer value” refers to the value after employee contributions have been deducted

Overview of Competitive Assessment
• Willis Towers Watson maintains a U.S. database of benefit provisions for over 1,000 organizations
• BENVAL determines a value for these benefits by applying a standard methodology to a standard employee 
population
• Benefit programs are:
– Compared on a relative value basis between employers — a score of 100 represents the average value for the peer group
– Scores are determined on a dollar value basis relative to the average value
• This report includes the following:
– List of peer companies included in the study
– Executive summary of study results
– Overview of the benefits environment
– Relative value comparison for the total program and individual benefit programs
– Breakdown of benefit values by component programs
• Benefit plans are those that were in force as of 2022 and were applicable for new employees. Closed or 
grandfathered plans are not valued for the peer group
• Throughout this document, “total value” refers to the positioning of the benefit programs, including the value of 
employee contributions; “employer value” refers to the value after employee contributions have been deducted
• The Entire Benefit Program total and employer values are primarily driven by the value of the retirement plans, 
active and post-retirement medical plans, as well as vacation and holiday programs, which comprise the vast 
majority of the program values

– Inflation: 2.5%

BENVAL Valuation Methodology
© 2023 WTW. Proprietary and confidential. For WTW and WTW client use only.
Relative values are determined based on a representative employee group and a standardized set of valuation 
assumptions/methods; results are not intended to represent actual program costs
• Plan provisions valued are those applicable to newly hired employees; benefits available only to grandfathered groups
of employees are not reflected
• Standard population
– Average age: 44 years
– Average service: 12 years
– Average base salary : $79,825
