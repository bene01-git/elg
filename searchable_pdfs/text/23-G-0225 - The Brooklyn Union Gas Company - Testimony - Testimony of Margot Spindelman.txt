A.	
Thermal energy networks extract, deliver, balance, and recycle heat among buildings 
and other sources and sinks— heat that would otherwise be wasted. Heat recovery 
and recycling makes these systems extremely efficient. Heat is not created in these 
systems, but simply transferred. Thermal energy networks are able to provide both 
heating and cooling using a fraction of the energy that is used when we burn fossil 
fuels. New York State passed the Utility Thermal Energy Network and Jobs Act in 
June, 2022, and the Governor signed it into law on July 5, to move ahead quickly with 
this energy saving, greenhouse gas emission slashing, life enhancing technology. 
The seven largest utilities in the State were tasked with creating and implementing 
pilots to provide important learnings on how to build these systems at scale.
 
National Grid’s initial submission for a pilot in their KEDNY territory, specifically 
at the New York City Housing Authority (“NYCHA”) complex at Vandalia Avenue, 
fails in several ways. 
1. 	KEDNY’s preferred design is for heating only. 
 
KEDNY proposes a pilot at a NYCHA complex at 17, 47, and 77 Vandalia 
Avenue in Brooklyn, and suggests possibly including nearby commercial 
sites, to date unnamed in their filings. The total connected load is estimated 
to serve 417,000 square feet. As pointed out in the Egg Geo “Case 22-M-
0429 Scoping Study / PEER Reveiw of Proposed U-TEN for Brooklyn 
NYCHA”, “Grid’s consultant’s analysis reported, as noted above, that the 
cost of providing heating and cooling to the NYCHA buildings is estimated 
at over $67.7 million, compared to an estimate of slightly over $38 million 
if heating only is provided to the NYCHA buildings. The Company states

Q.	
Recognizing the difference in scale of a proposal for a single U-TEN project vs a 
rate case filing for the entire downstate territory, how do the requests compare?
A.	
The meager effort of KEDNY to rise to the challenges and opportunities presented 
by the state mandated U-TEN proposal is in vivid contrast to their detailed and 
comprehensive request for massive amounts of money ($3,801,356,821) to expand 
and extend the life of their gas system in myriad ways. By law and public demand in 
the face of the climate emergency, it is inevitable that gas systems will be phased out. 
KEDNY should recognize that its survival depends on shifting its business model 
from piping methane to piping water for thermal energy networks. The UTENJA 
law threw gas utilities a lifeline: the Company should seize it and release the gas 
infrastructure that will pull it under.
