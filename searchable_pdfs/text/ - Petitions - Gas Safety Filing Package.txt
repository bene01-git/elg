A. Operator Qualification Program 
12 
Q. 
Please describe the Operator Qualification Program and the Commission’s recent 
13 
changes to OQ requirements in New York. 
14 
A. 
Operator qualifications require that utilities develop and maintain training and testing 
15 
programs for individuals performing “covered tasks” governed by both PHMSA’s 
16 
regulations (49 CFR § 192.805) and the Commission’s gas safety regulations (16 NYCRR 
17 
Part 255).  On March 18, 2022, in Case 19-G-0736, the Commission issued a 
18 
“Memorandum and Resolution Adopting Amendments” to its gas safety regulations in 16 
19 
NYCRR Part 255 (the “OQ Order”).  Among other items, the OQ Order expanded the 
20 
definition of a “covered task” to include additional tasks performed on pipeline facilities 
21 
connected to the gas system; modified requirements for trainings and evaluations; 
22 
standardized OQ requirements for utility employees and contractors; required program 
23 
Testimony of the Gas Safety Panel
Page 10 of 48


--- Page 13 ---
effectiveness reviews; and added records retention requirements.  The OQ changes are 
1 
intended to ensure that individuals working on a pipeline have the knowledge, skills, and 
2 
ability to perform a covered task and recognize and react to any abnormal operating 
3 
conditions they may experience.  The regulatory changes in the OQ Order materially 
4 
impacted the ways that utilities are required to conduct their operations and OQ programs. 
5 
 
6 
Pursuant to 16 NYCC § 255.604(c), utilities must have written OQ programs that comply 
7 
with the revised regulations in place by April 1, 2023, and the Commission noted that it 
8 
was “imperative for operators to implement the required changes, as discussed above, in 
9 
an expeditious manner to ensure that the implementation and training of personnel, as 
10 
defined by the operators OQ programs, is not delayed.”  Given the need to begin 
11 
implementing the required changes immediately after issuance of the order and recognizing 
12 
that the OQ program changes would result in incremental costs beyond what was included 
13 
in then effective utility rate plans, the OQ Order stated that “[a]ny incremental costs 
14 
[incurred to implement the OQ changes] may be deferred pursuant to the terms of the 
15 
individual utility’s rate plan.”
