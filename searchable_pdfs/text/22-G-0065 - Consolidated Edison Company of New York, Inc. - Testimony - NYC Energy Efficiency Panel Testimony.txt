Q. 
WHY IS THE COMPANY’S APPROACH PROBLEMATIC? 
16 
A. 
While the installation of beneficial electrification technologies is a critical step 
17 
toward transitioning New Yorkers off the natural gas system, without a focus on 
18 
energy efficiency, beneficial electrification measures could drive up electricity use, 
19 
thereby increasing near-term reliance on fossil-fired units.  In addition, if 
20 
electrification is not pursued strategically, with attention to energy efficiency, it is 
21 
expected to result in large increases in system peaks, which would require 
22

expensive investments in additional distribution and supply infrastructure, the cost

2 
 
 
It is critical that steps be taken to simultaneously reduce electricity usage by 
3 
combining energy efficiency retrofits with efficient electrification to the greatest 
4 
extent possible.  While efficiency measures have an upfront cost, they can reduce 
5 
costs elsewhere.  First, energy efficiency allows buildings to “right-size” their 
6

9 
reduce the need for investments in additional distribution and supply infrastructure.  
10 
Fourth, in the event of a power outage, efficient envelopes can help maintain safe 
11 
indoor temperatures during extreme weather events such as heat waves.   
12 
 
 
As is discussed in more detail in the City’s Policy Panel Testimony, 
13 
maintaining energy affordability should be a priority consideration as an analysis

15 
burdens over six percent if the Company’s proposed rate increases are adopted.  
16 
Further, energy efficiency measures are important from a climate perspective

because they reduce the need for generation and, at present, harmful air emissions.

Q. 
WHY ARE OUTCOME-BASED EAMS PROBLEMATIC? 
3 
A. 
EAMs should be used to reward exceptional utility performance.  However, when 
4 
EAMs reward the Company for actions taken by others, then the EAMs are not 
5 
justified because they are not related to utility performance.  The Company’s 
6 
proposed outcome-based EAMs do precisely that by rewarding the Company’s 
7 
shareholders for efforts undertaken by third parties and not necessarily attributable 
8 
to any actions by the Company itself.  For example, customers in the Company’s 
9 
service territory now install energy efficient lighting because virtually all lighting 
10 
sold at retail is energy efficient.  In other words, the marketplace has evolved.  
11 
Rewarding the Company’s shareholders when the market has changed serves no 
12 
useful purpose and instead unnecessarily burdens customers with higher utility 
13 
costs.
