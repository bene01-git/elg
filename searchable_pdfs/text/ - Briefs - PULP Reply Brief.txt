I. 
INTRODUCTION 
 
The Public Utility Law Project, Inc.  (“PULP”) submits this Reply Brief to refute and/or 
respond to questions raised or conclusions made by other parties in their initial briefs.  In 
addition, pursuant to 16 NYCRR § 4.7(d), PULP is using this Reply Brief to object to Your 
Honors’ April 7, 2020 denial of PULP’s April 2, 2020 letter motion (hereinafter “Motion”).  
PULP’s Reply Brief does not address every attempt by the Brooklyn Union Gas 
Company d/b/a National Grid NY (“KEDNY”) and KeySpan Gas East Corporation d/b/a 
National Grid (“KEDLI”) (collectively, the “Companies”) to satisfy their burden to prove that 
each substantive element individually or as a whole is just and reasonable and is consistent 
with sound environmental, social and economic policies of the Public Service Commission 
(“PSC” or “Commission”) and of New York State.  PSL, § 66(12)(i). Nor does this brief 
contest every argument made by the Department of Public Service (“DPS”) Staff or argue that 
DPS’ positions are not in the public interest, except where expressly so stated.  
PULP argues here however that no claim or argument presented by KEDNY/KEDLI or 
by DPS Staff has met the burden of proof that a rate increase of any kind in these cases, and 
particularly in these troubled economic times where the economic impact of rate cases must 
also be scrutinized, is in the public interest. Nor have any such arguments dissuaded PULP 
from the opinions and conclusions expressed in its initial brief.  Now is not the time to proceed 
with a rate increase of any kind. Instead, rate relief for residential consumers and small 
businesses would be more appropriate despite DPS Staff not seeking such goals in these cases.  
As discussed below, the other intervenors’ initial briefs reinforce and supplement much 
of the reasoning and recommendations made by PULP.  For many sections of the table of 
contents therefore, PULP has chosen to rely on its initial brief or on the initial briefs of the other 
intervenors and thereby does not address all subjects herein. PULP notes here that the absence of 
any discussion of specific topics in this reply is not and should not be construed as support for 
other parties’ or the Companies’ positions. PULP notes further that it maintains all of its 
positions, both testimonial and the initial brief, as if restated herein in detail, despite eliding the 
repetition of such testimony here in favor of concision. Finally, this Reply Brief agrees with DPS 
Staff and/or the Companies’ positions as set forth here in summary, and stated with specificity 
throughout this brief:

• The need for the interest rates that are to be agreed upon or recommended in this 
case should be updated based upon interest rates and yield spreads in existence

II. 
PROCEDURAL HISTORY 
 
On March 30, 2020 the Public Utility Law Project, Inc.  (“PULP”) filed a Motion 
requesting that the Companies refresh the calculations and projections that underly their 
proposed revenue requirement (i.e., including but not limited to, projections of an inflation 
rate, calculations underlying a proposed ROE, calculations/projections of forward-looking 
commodity prices and sales, and other elements of a revenue requirement) due to the economic 
effects of COVID-19.  On April 6, 2020 PULP filed its Initial Brief, which included fourteen 
specific conditions PULP believes are necessary to avert the Companies’ substantial 
undermining of affordability during the evolving economic, political, and societal struggles 
that the Companies’ ratepayers will face.   
PULP continues to believe that the fourteen conditions are necessary to avert the 
Companies’ substantial undermining of affordability during the evolving economic, political, 
and societal climate that the Companies’ ratepayers face in the upcoming year(s) of the rate 
plan arising from this case, and beyond.  Indeed, considering the economic impacts of the 
worsening economy and labor outlook caused by COVID-19, PULP advocates in our Reply 
Brief that no rate increase of any kind should be approved in this case.  In the alternative, if the 
Commission decides to allow an increase, PULP respectfully suggests that it be limited to total 
bill impacts no greater than 2%. Further, PULP requests that any increase amount be “stayed” 
through a rate freeze or temporary rates until an austerity proceeding is completed, non-
essential expenses are removed from the Companies’ rate plans, the ROE is substantially 
reduced, and the debt to equity ratio is adjusted to lower revenues.   
  On April 7, 2020, Your Honors issued the denial of PULP’s motion.  In the denial, 
Your Honors discussed how,

III. 
SUMMARY OF REVENUE REQUIREMENT AND RATE DRIVERS  
 
PULP acknowledges DPS Staff’s efforts to lower the Companies’ annual gas revenue 
requirements for KEDNY to approximately $30.588 million, or an increase of approximately 
4.46% in delivery revenues and 3.08% in total revenues, and for KEDLI to $39.737 million, or a 
decrease of approximately 2.08% in delivery revenues and 1.43% in total revenues.  DPS Staff, 
Initial Brief (“IB”), at pages 6-7.  However, PULP remains opposed to the Commission 
approving or implementing any rate increase of any amount during the economic downturn 
created by the COVID-19 pandemic.  In the alternative, if a smaller increase is to be ordered 
with total bill impacts no greater than 2%, PULP might not challenge such an increase, if the 
increase amount  is “stayed” through a rate freeze or temporary rates until an austerity 
proceeding is completed in which parties are allowed to adduce economic impact evidence and 


--- Page 7 ---
 
 
 
 
- 7 - 
conduct discovery, and the Commission takes due account of the economic impacts of proposed 
rate increases, or rate increases already assured through multi-year rate plans. 
Moreover, while the Companies provided rounds of revised revenue requirements before 
the end of 2019, and DPS Staff updated their recommendations during this rate case, it does not 
change the fact that New York State is now facing unprecedented financial harm and such 
updates were “stale” by the time the parties submitted briefs.  Id. at 6.  PULP consequently 
restates our belief that the public interest requires that all of the underlying financial figures, 
studies and analysis of this rate case should be updated before a Recommended Decision and/or 
Final Order are issued, due to the economic reality New York State now faces.  This includes but 
is not limited to: economic and sales forecasts and calculations; testimony regarding future 
economic conditions and commodity costs; returns upon equity; ratios between equity and debt; 
potential staffing shortages and utility usage infrastructure necessary to for “safe and adequate 
service and just and reasonable rates;” and proposed “Earnings Incentive Mechanisms,” 
“Earnings Adjustment Mechanisms,” “Positive Revenue Adjustments,” and “Negative Revenue 
Adjustments. Furthermore, parties must be able to provide economic impact evidence, so that 
they might be able to “identify a nexus between the evidence presented and specific problems at 
issue in the case at hand.” Cohalan v. Gioia, 451 N.Y.S.2d 275, 276 (1982). 
PULP also notes DPS Staff’s acknowledgment in their Initial Brief that site investigation 
and remediation (“SIR”) costs are one of the major rate drivers in this rate case.  Id. at page 7.  
PULP reiterates our concerns as addressed in our Testimony, cross-examination, and Initial Brief 
that the SIR costs are extremely high and have ballooned since the 2016 rate case. PULP further 
reiterates its position from 2016 and in this case that requiring the Companies to bear a 
reasonable portion of those costs is necessary and would have caused KEDNY to work to avert 
or at least slow the ballooning of the cleanup costs.  Instead, the allocation of 100% of SIR 
cleanup costs to ratepayers removed a strong incentive to control costs, in PULP’s opinion. 
PULP discusses these concerns in greater detail below.

9. Site Investigation and Remediation (“SIR”) (KEDNY and KEDLI) 
 
PULP disagrees with the Companies’ arguments rejecting PULP’s recommendation that 
SIR costs be shared by the Companies.  Companies, IB, at 60-61. The Commission’s “Generic 
SIR Order,” 11-M-0034, is clear that the choice was made with the Order’s issuance, not to  
“adopt a generic policy requiring allocation of SIR expenses between ratepayers 
and shareholders.... we retain the flexibility to tailor the rate treatment of SIR 
costs to the concrete conditions of each utility.” See, Generic SIR Order at 2-3.   
 
As a result, DPS staff has specifically received discretion to recommend alternative sharing of 
SIRs.  
Moreover, the two “circumstances” the Companies point to in justifying their position 
that sharing may be appropriate but not applicable to KEDNY, were never meant by the 
Commission to be exclusive.  Instead, the Generic SIR Order specifically states:  
 
“We do not shut the door to sharing under specific company and rate case 
circumstances. We anticipate at least two such circumstances. First, sharing of 
some proportion may serve as an incentive to constrain SIR costs for utilities that 
appear to need such an incentive. Second, some stockholder responsibility for SIR 


--- Page 11 ---
 
 
 
 
- 11 - 
costs could be included as part of the negotiation for rate plans where an earnings 
sharing mechanism is contemplated. In these cases, an earnings sharing 
mechanism may funnel some percentage of shareholder excess earnings to SIR 
costs. We expect Staff and other parties to explore opportunities aggressively to 
allocate some proportion of excess earnings to pay down deferred SIR costs.” 
(Emphasis added, Generic SIR Order at 3).   
 
DPS staff has the flexibility to be aggressive and look for cost sharing in any rate case.  This is 
especially important for KEDNY due to its involvement with the massively polluted Newtown 
Creek and the Gowanus Canal superfund sites.  PULP’s Testimony, cross examination during the 
Evidentiary Hearing and our Initial Brief clearly lay out a history of skyrocketing SIR costs over 
many years of inaction or limited action by KEDNY; which strongly suggests that costs are only 
going to continue to increase for both the Newtown Creek and Gowanus Canal sites.  PULP, IB 
at 16-19.  Now is the time for the DPS Staff to use the flexibility the Generic SIR Order provides 
to rein in out of control cost increases.  As a result, PULP urges the ALJs to recommend to the 
Commission that KEDNY share in the SIR costs.   
When PULP filed its initial testimony on August 31, 2019, there was an assumption that 
this case would result in a multi-year settlement.  As a result, William Yates proposed that the 
Companies allocate 100% of KEDNY’s share of earnings-sharing to reduce deferred SIR costs.  
Hearing Transcript (“HT”), 1629, lines 6-9.  Due to the fact that this case was fully litigated and 
will result in a one-year rate plan wherein earnings sharing mechanisms are not often proposed, 
PULP supports DPS Staff’s proposal that an earnings sharing mechanism containing an 
allocation of SIR costs to KEDNY be imposed, if the Companies decided to stay out without 
filing a new rate case after one year.  DPS Staff, IB at 162-163.  Such an action would 
incentivize the companies to file their new case soon and provides a framework for the ALJ’s to 
recommend that the Commission order KEDNY to share in the SIR costs it is producing, rather 
than placing this financial burden solely on their ratepayers.

The excess unprotected EDFIT is ratepayer money. It is inequitable and unreasonable to 
withhold it from being used now to dispel ratepayer harms caused by the COVID-19 health 
pandemic, instead of being saved to moderate future escalating rates to which Staff may agree.   
PULP advocated in our Initial Brief for the unprotected EDFIT monies from unprotected 
plant and non-plant balances to be split in two ways.  First, one-half of the monies should be

- 13 -

EDFIT monies in that manner would also provide such customers with greater discounts within

The second half of the unprotected EDFIT monies should then be divided in half, with 
50% being used as added rate moderation in the one-year rate plan resulting from this case. The

the arrears/uncollectibles accrued during the moratoria.  Id.   
Ordering the Companies to divide and return the EDFIT monies during the rate plan not 
only protects the Companies’ most vulnerable current customers and customers newly entering 
low-income status, and protects the Companies at least in part from the effects of ballooning 
arrears and uncollectibles, but is also consistent with a rational and equitable use of the funds 
while returning net ratepayer funds as rapidly as is practicable.  As a result, PULP urges the 
ALJs to recommend this plan for division and return of the EDFIT monies to the Commission as 
a measure to assist the Companies’ most vulnerable customers during this unprecedented 
economic downturn.

XIX.      CONCLUSION  
 
As discussed in this Reply Brief, PULP respectfully requests that the ALJs recommend to 
the Commission that it deny the Companies’ requested rate increases.  In the alternative, if the 
Commission determines it should allow a rate increase, PULP respectfully requests such an 
increase be limited to a rate increase with no more than a 2% total bill impact, with such increase

use of the Commission’s temporary rate making powers under § 114 of the PSL, or such other

opportunity to present economic impact evidence to the Commission, or DPS Staff should be 
directed to do so along with a refresh of the record, before the Commission acts.

are based upon numbers that are no longer accurate and of “rate case quality,” and requests the 
ALJs to order the Company and Staff to refresh at a minimum the underpinnings of the revenue

the Companies to take such actions as will improve their customer service procedures to account 
for the aftermath of the COVID-19 health pandemic.  There needs to be a plan in place now that 
will adequately help residential customers pay their utility bills without the immediate risk of 
termination of service.  And, there needs to be a plan that will protect ratepayers from the tidal 
wave of terminations coming at the end of the moratoria. PULP therefore suggests here that 
KEDNY/KEDLI, DPS Staff, PULP and other interested parties need to work together to see that 
a plan of action is in place to help curb the financial harm ahead.
