(Petition) to change the 2024 Earnings Adjustment Mechanism 
(EAM) baseline for its Commercial Managed Charging Program 
(CMCP).  In the Petition, the Company seeks authorization to 
modify certain aspects of its CMCP EAM for calendar year 2024, 
which was approved by the Commission in the 2023 Rate Order.1  
The CMCP EAM, as approved in the 2023 Rate Order, provides

BACKGROUND 
 
 
Appendix 22 of the Joint Proposal filed on February 
16, 2023 (Joint Proposal), and adopted by the Commission in the 
2023 Rate Order, provides that details of one or more Managed 
Charging EAM metrics would be developed through a collaborative 
process to be commenced shortly after the Joint Proposal was 
filed, and would be completed within 60 days of its 
commencement.  The Joint Proposal reserved up to a maximum of 
ten basis points of financial award for any such EAMs.  The 
Joint Proposal states that, in the event the collaborative 
process reaches consensus on the Managed Charging EAMs, the 
Company would prepare a consensus report to be filed with the 
Commission providing a detailed description of the EAM metrics, 
targets, and associated financial awards.  However, if the 
collaborative process fails to reach consensus, the Joint 
Proposal provided that parties would be able to file comments 
and recommendations to the Commission regarding the EAMs, with 
an opportunity for reply comments.  In either scenario, the 
outcome of the collaborative process envisioned in the Joint 
Proposal was intended to be provided to the Commission for 
consideration alongside the Joint Proposal itself.

commercial charging capability across the Company’s service 
territory, regardless of whether such charging capability 
participates in the CMCP; resulting in a unitless percentage.

For each year, this ratio would be measured during two time

December 31 of the prior year, and a Performance Period 
consisting of June 1 through September 30 of the present year 
(Performance Ratio).5  The ratio computed during the Baseline

during the Baseline Period, as a percentage, and the observed 
growth rate of total charging capacity in the Company’s service 
territory through the end of the Performance Period of the 
applicable Rate Year – referred to as the Baseline Ratio.6 
 
 
The CMCP EAM is awarded based on meeting targets 
expressed as a percent improvement between Baseline Ratio and 
Performance Ratio.  For example, to earn a financial award 
related to the minimum achievement level during 2024, the 
Performance Ratio would have to exceed the Baseline Ratio by 
five percent.  Similarly, to earn a financial incentive at the 
maximum level of achievement during 2024, the Performance Ratio 
would have to exceed the Baseline Ratio by at least 17 percent.

PETITION 
 
 
In its Petition, Con Edison requests that the 
Commission adjust the time period used to set the baseline for 
the 2024 CMCP EAM, noting that this proposed change would modify 
the timing of the 2024 CMCP EAM in the Rate Plan but would not 
otherwise modify the CMCP EAM metric.  The Company notes that 
without approval of its proposed modifications, a CMCP EAM in 
2024 would not be possible to compute, therefore the Company 
would not be incentivized to overperform in its CMCP during 
2024.  Con Edison further argues that the Commission should 
approve its proposed modifications since overperformance during 
2024, incentivized through the CMCP EAM, would benefit customers 
by result in more difficult targets to attain during 2025 since 
the CMCP EAM metric is adjusted based on the prior year’s 
performance. 
 
 
The Company notes that the baseline time period 
included in the Collaborative Report was set based on the 
assumption that the Commission would approve a CMCP on a 
timeframe sufficient to begin program operations during the Fall 
of 2023; however, it acknowledges that the precise amount of 
time that would be within the baseline period by the end of 2023 
was unknown when the Collaborative Report was filed.  The 
Company further states that the Collaborative Report had not 
specified a baseline duration or program start time beyond the 
“fall time period” because the Commission had not yet approved 
the CMCP by the time the Collaborative Report was filed, but 
that it had anticipated a baseline period of approximately four 
months at the end of 2023.  Con Edison notes that it needed to 
complete substantial work to implement the CMCP once it was 
approved by the Commission in the Demand Charge Alternatives 
Immediate Solutions Order, resulting in a program go-live date 
of January 19, 2024.

while the title of the Petition states that it seeks to change

modify both the Baseline Period and the Enrollment Baseline 
Growth Period which would be used to calculate the performance 
and potential financial awards for the CMCP EAM applicable

DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION 
 
 
Con Edison’s request to modify its CMCP EAM for 2024 
is denied for three reasons.  First, Con Edison knew or should 
have known when it submitted the Collaborative Report that the 
timeline for approval of the CMCP was uncertain.  The Company 
references this uncertainty approximately five times throughout 
its Petition.  Although the Demand Charge Alternatives Immediate 
Solutions Order was issued after the Company and Parties to the

Collaborative Report had anticipated, an outcome of that Order 
resulting in delays to the implementation of the CMCP was not 
entirely unforeseeable.  Con Edison was aware of the risks 
associated with the timeframes proposed in the Collaborative 
Report, and the Commission chooses not to re-open the 
determinations made in the 2023 Rate Order solely to enable the 
Company to earn this EAM. 
 
 
Second, The Joint Proposal and Collaborative Report 
approved by the Commission represent the rules for EAMs during 
the term of the Rate Plan.  The Commission has upheld these 
rules, once approved, throughout the lifecycle of EAM metrics 
since the first EAMs were approved in 2017, including during 
COVID-19 pandemic years when it would have been beneficial to 
customers to pause achievement, awarding, and collection of 
EAMs.13  Con Edison argues that the CMCP EAM would be roughly as 
easy or difficult to earn – within plus-or-minus 10 to 15 
percent.  However, NYECC claims that the proposed modifications 
would, on balance, benefit the Company and notes that Con 
Edison’s proposal provides no assurance that the ultimate result 
of the modifications to the CMCP EAM baseline period would be at 
least neutral compared to the theoretical results of an 
unmodified baseline had the Company been able to implement the 
CMCP earlier.  While it is not clear whether the modifications 
to the CMCP EAM would result in easier or more difficult targets
