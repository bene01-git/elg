A. 
The basic concept underlying the CAPM is that 
20

21 
assuming additional risk.  In other words, 
22 
higher-risk securities are priced to yield 
23

Q. 
How does the CAPM measure risk? 
1 
A. 
The CAPM measures risk based upon the premise 
2 
that an investor who diversifies his security 
3 
portfolio is only exposed to systematic or 
4 
market risk.  The systematic risk represents the 
5 
risk of an investment within a well-diversified 
6 
portfolio.  The remaining (or unsystematic) 
7 
risks are not assumed by rational investors 
8 
since these risks can be eliminated through 
9 
diversification.  In the CAPM, systematic risk 
10 
is measured by beta.  The beta of a stock 
11 
measures the volatility of that stock in 
12 
relation to the volatility of the stock market 
13 
as a whole.  It is produced by a regression 
14 
analysis and represents the covariance of a 
15 
stock with the market.  A beta above 1.0 means 
16 
the stock has greater risk than the market; a 
17 
beta below 1.0 means that a stock has less risk 
18 
than the market.  A beta of 1.0 means the risk 
19 
of the stock is equivalent to the risk of the 
20 
market.
