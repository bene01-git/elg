submits Statement in Support of the Joint Proposal. 
Although Walmart did not address every issue raised by the Company or other parties in 
the course of this proceeding or resolved by the Joint Proposal and the settlement among the

and fair resolution of the issues in the electric case that Walmart believes is in the best interest of

II. 
ARGUMENT 
As noted, Walmart did not address every issue presented by the Company in their initial 
filing or raised by other parties in the evidence presented in the course of this case. Rather, 
Walmart presented evidence and arguments in the form of the Direct Testimony of Alex J. 
Kronauer, addressing distinct and discreet issues: (1) the Company's proposed return on equity 
("ROE"); and (2) the customer charge for SC-3. See Direct Testimony of Alex J. Kronauer 
("Walmart Kronauer DT"), pp. 4-5. This Statement in Support of the Joint Proposal accordingly 
does not address each and every issue resolved by the settlement. 
Walmart did not oppose the Company's as-filed class cost of service study or proposed 
revenue allocation at the requested overall revenue requirement. That said, Walmart did submit 
testimony noting that the Company's requested ROE was well outside the norm for vertically-
integrated utilities nationwide, and therefore recommended that the Commission carefully 
consider recent ROEs awarded both in New York and nationwide and the potential impact that the 
ROE determination would have on NIMO's customers. Additionally, noting that the Company's 
proposed design of rates for schedule SC-3 (and specifically Walmart's main schedule, SC-3 
Secondary) would create intra-class subsidies by recovering substantial amounts of customer-
related fixed costs through demand charges, Walmart recommended that the Commission instead 
set the customer charge for the SC-3 rate schedule at a full cost-based level with any balance of 
the revenue increase applied to the demand charge.

After many months of settlement discussions addressing extensive issues, the settling 
parties achieved the filed Joint Proposal. Regarding cost of equity, the Joint Proposal recommends 
that the Commission approve an ROE of 9.0 percent. See Joint Proposal§ W.2.1.a. From 
Walmart's perspective, this reflects significant compromise on the part of the both the Company 
and other parties that presented evidence on this issue and serves to mitigate some of the impact 
on customers through a lower overall settled revenue increase. 
Regarding the rate design, the Joint Proposal incorporates an important adjustment to the 
Company's as-filed proposal. Specifically for the SC-3 class, and particularly the SC-3 Secondary 
sub-class, the Joint Proposal reflects an agreement among the parties to increase the customer 
charge at a greater percentage than originally proposed by NIMO. See Joint Proposal Appendix 
2, Schedule 5.6. While this movement does not reflect a fully cost-based customer charge, as 
recommended by Walmart, the resulting rates do ensure that a greater percentage of the Company's 
fixed cost to serve this class are incorporated in the fixed customer charge, thus reducing intra-
class subsidization. Additionally, this modification to rates is also revenue neutral, impacting only 
the SC-3 class and individual sub-classes without harming other classes. 
From a broader perspective, the avoidance of ongoing time and expenses related to 
litigation of these issues is a valuable and important component of achieving settlements, which 
this Commission has previously acknowledged. See "Procedural Guidelines for Settlement," 
Cases No. 90-M-0255 and 92-M-0138 (1992), p. 1. The fact that this case has already been 
pending for well over a year supports the substantial benefit provided to the parties, and the 
Commission, by the resolution through settlement of the issues embodied by the Joint Proposal.

III. 
CONCLUSION 
For these reasons, Walmart believes that the Joint Proposal represents a fair, just, and 
reasonable resolution of issues. The Joint Proposal for settlement also reflects many hours of 
negotiation between traditionally adverse parties representing numerous public interests within the
