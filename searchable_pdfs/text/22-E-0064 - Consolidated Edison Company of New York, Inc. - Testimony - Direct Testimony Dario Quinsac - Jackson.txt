Case 22-E-0064 Direct Testimony of Dario Quinsac 
 
I. Introduction  
• 
Q - Please state your name, business address, and occupation. 
A - Dario Quinsac, my business address is 5030 Broadway, Suite 701. New York NY. 
I am employed by the New York State Senate as the Special Assistant for State Senator Robert 
Jackson. 
 
• 
Q - On whose behalf are you testifying in this case? 
A – I will be testifying on behalf of State Senator Robert Jackson. 
 
• 
Q- Have you previously submitted testimony before other state regulatory commissions? 
A- Yes, I have in the State of New York on behalf of other legislative bodies. 
 
• 
Q- Are you providing exhibits in your testimony? 
A- I will not be providing charts nor exhibits in this testimony. 
 
• 
Q – What kind of entity do you represent? 
A - The New York State Senate is the upper house of the New York State Legislature. The 
legislature’s primary purpose is to draft and approve changes to the laws of New York. 
 
• 
Q - What is the purpose of your testimony? 
A - My testimony has been prepared to review the rate case testimony and exhibits presented 
by Consolidated Edison Company of New York, Inc. (“Con Edison”) with regards to the overall 
rate increase and concerns regarding the electric infrastructure in Northern Manhattan. 
 
• 
Q – What part of the increase is the most worrisome for the constituents of Senator Robert 
Jackson’s district? 
A - These increases across the board are bad for my constituents especially those in rates sc-1, 
sc-3, sc-6. All my constituents are affected including those not directly paying utility bills 
because the public services they received require electricity that is supplied by NYPA but is 
delivered over (“Con Edison”) electric infrastructure. 
 
• 
Q – Do you have any Accounting Panel specific programmatic changes that you think need to be 
reviewed and discussed with the regulators? 
 
A - Yes, the “Credit Modeling Software,” and “Replevin and Collection Agency Fees,” need to be 
adjusted to account for the actual economic landscape and the different indirect cost savings 
(“Con Edison”) as been able to accomplish during the moratorium. The investments in these 
programs should be designed to assist customers both residential and commercial to be able to 
pay off their arrears while still be able to balance their wallets.

• 
Q – Do you have Climate leadership and community protection act programmatic changes that 
you think need to be reviewed and discussed with the regulators? 
A – Yes, but not specific programs, but the overall fact that ratepayers will be paying (“Con 
Edison”), for “improvements” that should be already factored into the decisions they make and 
as such shouldn’t be separated in the rate formula. 
 
 
• 
Q – Do you think using the ISO market “spread” for Clean Energy Credit 18 for Low-Income 
Customers Program is the best choice? 
A - No, (“Con Edison”) should have instead built a green generation plant for that specific 
customer group instead and delivered that energy directly without depending on the market 
fluctuations? 
 
• 
Q- Do you think the current Ratchet Demand Charge pricing structure for demand-based tiers 
should be revised? 
 
A – The current brackets don’t reflect the overall demand curve especially in the SC-6, SC-9 rate 
groups. I propose that (“Con Edison”) shall provide a study prior to filling their next rate case. 
 
• 
Q – Do you think the rate increases currently proposed will cause legislative review? 
 
A – Yes, multiple legislative bills have been introduced to regulate the industry because the 
projected increases will cause pain and suffering to the residents and businesses of New York 
especially in (“Con Edison”) territory.  
 
• 
Q – Would you consider (“Con Edison”) to be a rare constituent concern in Senator Robert 
Jackson’ s office? 
 
A - No, on the contrary, over 200 constituents have contacted his office because they have 
problems with (“Con Edison”). These problems range from Smart Meter installation problems to 
massive arrears that they need help getting help with or the more technical concerns that 
require the Department of Public Service to investigate and send staff on-site to review. 
 
• 
Q – Do you think the level of public anger to this pending rate case would be lower if the 
compensation of the top executives at (“Con Edison”) was in-line with the compensation of an 
executive at a governmental agency?  
A – I can’t tell you how much lower, but the savings accomplished in the overall rate formula by 
reducing executive pay, would trickle down to (“Con Edison”)’s customer base.  
 
• 
Q - Does this conclude your update testimony?  
A. Yes, it does.
