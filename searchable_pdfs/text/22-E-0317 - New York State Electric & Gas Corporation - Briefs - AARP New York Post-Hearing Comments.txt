AARP New York disagrees that factual information such as the historical costs of capital

proposals, comments, even body language and facial expressions must remain confidential.

litigated outcome when there is no ability to see, yet alone analyze, that relevant factual 
information that allegedly supports that provision in the JP.  Merely asking the Companies 
and DPS Staff for their opinion is neither sufficient nor appropriate.11

Beliefs That The JP’s Return on Equity Is The Result Of Robust Negotiations Have No 
Factual Support. 
AARP New York takes issue with claims that the JP’s ROE reflects a major concession on the 
part of the Companies that demonstrates a commitment to affordable rates.  The 
Companies asserted in their initial brief: 
The Joint Proposal’s recommended ROE of 9.2% represents the bare minimum 
for what is required for the Companies to both access debt/capital markets 
and establish just and reasonable rates that provide them the opportunity to 
earn a fair return. The Companies’ willingness to accept an ROE significantly 
lower than its litigation position therefore represents a significant concession 
on the part of the Companies and reflects the Companies’ desire to minimize 
rate impacts.12 
It is undisputed that the Commission and the Department have been using since the early 
1990s what is commonly known as the Generic Finance Model (GFM) to calculate what 
they conclude is an appropriate authorized return on equity.13   DPS Staff’s Finance Panel 
confirmed this in its direct testimony: 
Q. Given the Commission’s approved ROEs in recent  rate proceedings, does 
the Panel believe that a rational investor would expect the Commission to 
authorize the 10.20 percent ROE requested by the Companies in these 
proceedings?  
A. No. Rational investors are aware of the Commission’s time-tested 
formulaic approach to the cost of common equity, as presented in our 
analysis.14

rebuttal testimony:

Proposal provides for an equity ratio of 48% which is consistent with Staff’s

recently approved by the Commission for O&R and Corning and recommended

Joint Proposal therefore has a rational basis and is well within the range of 
reasonable outcomes of a fully litigated one-year case.”19  
 
The cost of capital terms contained in the Joint Proposal and, in particular, the 
9.2 percent ROE, are a reasonable outcome given the current economic 
environment. Equity return requirements have generally increased, as is 
demonstrated by Staff’s testimony in the Consolidated Edison steam rate 
proceeding. Furthermore, the Joint Proposal’s recommended 9.2 percent ROE 
is slightly below the 9.25 percent ROE that was agreed to in the most recent 
joint proposal in the Consolidated Edison Electric and Gas rate proceeding 
which is currently pending before the Commission. It is also identical to that 
which was adopted by the Commission in the Liberty Utilities- St Lawrence 
Gas (SLG) Rate Order issued in June 2023.20
