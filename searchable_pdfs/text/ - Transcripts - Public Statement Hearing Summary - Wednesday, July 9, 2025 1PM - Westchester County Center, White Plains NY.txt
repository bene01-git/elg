My name is James Costello and with me is Nicholas Planty.  We 
are two of three Administrative Law Judges for the Department of 
Public Service assigned to these rate cases and we are presiding 
over this public statement hearing, held on behalf of the New 
York State Public Service Commission, regarding the proposed 
changes in the electric and gas delivery rates and practices of 
the Consolidated Edison Company of New York.  
 
I will begin by providing some background on these proceedings. 
 
Con Edison filed amendments to its electric and gas tariff 
schedules on January 31, 2025, proposing to increase its annual 
electric and gas delivery revenues for the 12-month period 
ending December 31, 2026.  As of its April 10, 2025 updated 
testimony, Con Edison proposes to increase its electric delivery 
revenues by approximately $1.6 billion (an 11.3 percent increase 
in total revenues or 17.9 percent increase in base delivery 
revenues), and its natural gas delivery revenues by 
approximately $349 million (a 10.5 percent increase in total 
revenues or 14.9 percent increase in base delivery revenues).  
The actual bill impacts of these proposed changes on any

By letter dated June 24, 2025, Con Edison filed a notice of 
impending settlement negotiations.  If settlement negotiations 
are successful, the parties to the settlement would file a Joint 
Proposal to the Commission.  There would then be further 
briefing, public comment, and an evidentiary hearing before the 
case is submitted to the Commission for decision.  The 
Commission can adopt the Joint Proposal, modify the Joint 
Proposal, or reject the Joint Proposal.  
 


--- Page 4 ---

If settlement negotiations are not successful, the 
administrative law judges would issue a new procedural schedule 
for an evidentiary hearing and post-hearing briefs.  The cases 
then may be presented to the Commission for decision or the 
administrative law judges may issue a Recommended Decision.  If 
we issue a Recommended Decision, the parties would then have an 
opportunity to brief their positions on those recommendations, 
and then the cases would be decided by the Commission. 
 
Under New York State law, the Public Service Commission must 
consider the utility's proposals and may adopt or reject them in 
whole or in part or modify it.   
 
At this time, the Commission must decide these cases by December 
30, 2025.  However, that date may be subject to further 
extension.  
 
Regardless of how these cases proceed, the Public Service 
Commission will make the final decision regarding the Company’s 
terms and conditions for providing electric and gas service.  
This hearing is being held so you can provide your comments on 
Con Edison’s proposals for the Commission’s consideration.  
 
In making its decision, the Commission will consider the 
evidentiary record in these cases and the comments made at this 
and other public statement hearings.   
 
The Public Service Commission has seven members -- the 
Chairperson, Rory Christian, and six Commissioners - James 
Alesi, David Valesky, John Maggiore, Uchenna Bright, Denise
