B.

measured primarily with the SAIFI and CAIDI indices.9  Central Hudson explained that

to its failure to meet the reliability performance targets due to “these factors and others beyond the 
Company’s control.”12  Next, the Company stated that it “may” seek Commission approval to

2. 
Property Taxes 
 
Central Hudson agrees with Multiple Intervenors that it “would be more appropriate to 
functionalize property taxes on net plant” in its electric ECOS studies in the same manner as it 
does in the Company’s gas studies.26  The Company’s Cost of Service (“COS”) Panel conceded 
on cross-examination that (i) the method recommended by Multiple Intervenors would be “more 
appropriate” because functionalizing property taxes based on net plant would better align with cost 
causation principles,”27 and (ii) the COS Panel would choose to functionalize property taxes based 
on net plant as Mr. Pollock recommends if it were to update the electric cost studies.28

Notwithstanding these statements, Central Hudson asserts in its Initial Brief that it is not 
necessary to adopt this change in this proceeding because “the overall electric ECOS studies, and

These arguments should be rejected.  Central Hudson effectively argues that it does not 
matter whether the methodologies used for the ECOS studies are consistent and appropriately 
rooted in cost causation principles so long as it believes that the model outputs are reasonable.  
This is an astonishing and results-oriented argument that would undermine the reliability of utility 
cost studies if accepted in this (or any other) proceeding.   
Cost studies consist of three steps – functionalization, classification, and allocation – each 
of which should be conducted based on cost causation principles to the greatest extent 
practicable.30  Completing these steps may require the exercise of discretion, but those subjective 
judgments should be guided by the cost causation principles on which the cost study is based.  If 
Central Hudson is allowed to pick functionalization methodologies based on the model outputs 
that it would prefer to result from those choices, then the resulting cost study would lack probative 
value because its results would reflect only the utility’s subjective opinion of the delivery revenue 
increase that should be assigned to each rate class.  This is not how a cost study should be 
conducted and it would not provide the Commission with any cost-based justification to make 
decisions regarding revenue allocation and rate design.

Multiple Intervenors acknowledges that gradualism is a widely-accepted concept that is 
used to adjust movements towards class costs of service in order to mitigate the resulting rate 
impacts.  Critically, however, gradualism is applied after the cost study is completed in order to 
adjust the revenue allocations indicated by the outputs of an objective cost-of-service study.  It is 
not typically used to justify changing model inputs for the sake of generating preferred model 
outputs.  The Company’s results-oriented approach would be inequitable, it would corrode the 
reliability of cost studies in this and future proceedings, and it should be rejected unequivocally.

4. 
Legacy Generation Fleet  
 
In its Initial Brief, Staff agreed with the Central Hudson proposal to impute $3.9 million of 
legacy hydro benefits in the Rate Year.39  Staff argued that the smaller imputation based on older 
data should be utilized because the amount is modestly less than the $4.4 million indicated by more 
recent data and the Company expects future energy prices to decline.40  Notably, however, Staff 
asserted that “should the actual legacy hydro benefits exceed the $3.9 million imputation level in 
the Rate Year, any excess revenues would be passed back to customers through the Miscellaneous 
Charge.”41 
Staff’s argument is irrelevant and should be rejected.  This dispute relates to the amount of 
legacy generation market revenues that should be reflected in the pro forma (Rate Year) ECOS 
study.42  It does not relate to whether revenues actually earned during the Rate Year will be passed 
back to customers.  Whether or not market revenues earned by the generation fleet are returned 
through the Miscellaneous Charge thus has no bearing on the amount of benefits that should be 
reflected in an ECOS study to match the generation fleet costs that give rise to those revenues.
